# Security & Compliance: RBI Guidelines for Payment Systems (India) — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: The Regulatory Landscape

**Q1. What are the primary RBI frameworks that govern digital payment systems in India, and how do they relate to each other?**
A: RBI's payment regulation operates through a layered set of frameworks — each addressing a different dimension of payment system security, operations, and consumer protection. As a backend engineer at a fintech, you interact with at least four of these directly through what you build:

**The core frameworks:**

```
1. Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (PSS Act)
   → The legislative foundation. Gives RBI the authority to regulate payment systems.
   → Requires authorization to operate a payment system in India.
   → Defines "payment system", "payment obligation", "system provider".

2. Master Direction — Payment Aggregators and Payment Gateways (PA/PG Guidelines)
   Issued: March 2020 (effective March 2021, updated 2023)
   → Governs entities that facilitate online payments (Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree, CCAvenue)
   → Merchant onboarding norms, escrow account requirements, data storage rules
   → Baseline security framework mandates for PAs

3. Master Direction — Security Framework for Digital Payments
   (formerly the "Cyber Security Framework for Banks" extended to payment systems)
   → Mandates specific technical controls: encryption, network security, fraud monitoring
   → Applies to banks, NBFCs, payment system operators, PAs

4. Card-on-File Tokenisation (CoFT) Framework
   Circular: September 2021, effective October 2022
   → Prohibits merchants and PAs from storing actual card data (PAN, CVV)
   → Mandates replacement of stored card data with network tokens
   → Directly impacts any fintech that stores cards for recurring payments

5. RBI Regulations on Recurring Payments (e-NACH / e-mandates)
   Circular: August 2019 (AFA for recurring), multiple amendments through 2021-2023
   → Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA) requirements for recurring debits
   → Pre-debit notification requirements (24 hours before charge)
   → Maximum amount limits for auto-debit without OTP (₹15,000)

6. Data Localisation Mandate
   Circular: April 2018, confirmed September 2018
   → All payment data pertaining to Indian customers must be stored exclusively in India
   → Defines "payment system data" broadly — end-to-end transaction details
   → Cross-border transaction data: foreign leg can be stored abroad; Indian leg must be in India

7. DPDP Act 2023 (Digital Personal Data Protection Act)
   → Broad data protection law, not payments-specific, but applies to all financial data
   → Intersects with RBI guidelines on data retention, consent, purpose limitation
   → Operationally relevant alongside RBI mandates
```

**How they relate:**
```
PSS Act
  └── Enables RBI to issue Master Directions
        ├── PA/PG Guidelines → who can operate, how merchants are onboarded
        ├── Security Framework → what technical controls are required
        ├── CoFT Framework → how card data must be handled
        └── Recurring Payments → how auto-debits must work

All frameworks apply simultaneously — compliance with one does not
satisfy the others. A payment aggregator must comply with ALL of them.
A fintech building on top of a PA must comply with the PA/PG merchant
obligations and CoFT even if the PA handles the PCI-DSS layer.
```

**Q2. What is the PA/PG (Payment Aggregator / Payment Gateway) distinction, and what are the specific obligations that apply to each under the RBI March 2020 Master Direction?**
A: The RBI distinguishes between Payment Aggregators and Payment Gateways based on whether they touch funds — a critical distinction that determines the regulatory requirements:

**The distinction:**
```
Payment Aggregator (PA):
  Definition: An entity that facilitates e-commerce merchants in accepting payments
              by pooling together payment instruments (cards, netbanking, UPI, wallets)
              and settling funds to merchants.
  Key characteristic: PA RECEIVES customer funds into its own/nodal account
                      before passing to merchant
  Examples: Razorpay (PA licence), PayU, Cashfree, Instamojo, CCAvenue
  RBI treatment: Requires RBI authorization under PSS Act
  Net worth requirement: ₹25 crore at time of application, ₹25 crore maintained ongoing
                         (increased to ₹100 crore net worth by April 2028 per 2023 circular)

Payment Gateway (PG):
  Definition: A technology infrastructure provider that routes payment messages
              between merchant, bank, and card networks — does NOT touch funds.
  Key characteristic: PG NEVER holds customer funds; purely a message routing layer
  Examples: TechProcess (before acquisition), some banks' own gateways
  RBI treatment: Must comply with security framework but does NOT need PA authorization
                 Must register with RBI (mandatory since 2023 circular)

In practice: most entities called "payment gateways" by merchants (Razorpay, Stripe India)
are actually Payment Aggregators because they settle funds. The term "gateway" is
colloquially used for both.
```

**Key PA obligations from the March 2020 Master Direction:**

```
Obligation 1 — Escrow / Nodal account for funds:
  PA must maintain funds collected from customers in a designated Escrow Account
  with a scheduled commercial bank in India.
  Settlement to merchants: T+1 for credit card, T+1 for debit card/UPI/netbanking
  (T = transaction date)
  PA cannot use float from the escrow for its own operations — ring-fenced.
  Engineering implication: reconciliation system must match transactions
  in the escrow account against settlements to merchants daily.

Obligation 2 — Merchant onboarding / KYC:
  PAs must conduct due diligence on merchants before onboarding.
  Risk categorization: Low, Medium, High risk merchants.
  Low risk:  video KYC optional; basic document verification
  Medium risk: KYC within 30 days of onboarding
  High risk:  full KYC before onboarding; ongoing monitoring
  Engineering implication: merchant onboarding flow, KYC integration (Video KYC API,
  CKYC registry lookup), risk scoring system.

Obligation 3 — Prohibited merchant categories:
  PAs cannot onboard merchants dealing in:
  Drugs/narcotics, weapons, gambling/betting (unless licensed), multi-level marketing
  schemes, businesses deemed illegal under Indian law.
  Engineering implication: merchant category code (MCC) validation at onboarding;
  monitoring for prohibited activities in transaction patterns.

Obligation 4 — Security requirements (baseline):
  PA must comply with PCI-DSS for card data handling.
  PA must implement 2FA for merchant logins to PA dashboard.
  PA must conduct periodic security audits.
  PA must have incident response policy for data breaches.

Obligation 5 — Data storage (CoFT linkage):
  PA must not store actual card data (PAN, CVV) after transaction authorization.
  PA must tokenize card-on-file — this predates CoFT but CoFT formalized it.
  PA must ensure its systems comply with RBI data localisation.

Obligation 6 — Reporting obligations:
  PA must report security incidents to RBI within 6 hours of detection.
  PA must submit periodic returns to RBI (quarterly, annual financial statements).
  PA must report significant business changes (ownership change, technology changes).
```

## Section 2: Card-on-File Tokenisation (CoFT)

**Q3. What is the RBI's Card-on-File Tokenisation (CoFT) mandate, what does it require technically, and how is it different from the PCI-DSS tokenization requirement?**
A: CoFT is an RBI-specific mandate that goes beyond PCI-DSS in scope and specificity. PCI-DSS is a card industry standard focused on protecting cardholder data through various means (encryption, tokenization). CoFT is a regulatory mandate that specifically requires network-level tokenization of all saved card credentials by every entity in the Indian payment ecosystem — merchants, aggregators, and intermediaries:

**The CoFT mandate — what changed:**
```
Before October 1, 2022:
  A customer shopping on an e-commerce site (Flipkart, Zomato, Swiggy, fintech)
  could save their card: "Remember my card for future payments"
  The merchant or payment aggregator stored: PAN, expiry, sometimes CVV (illegally)
  On next purchase: pre-filled card details used → customer provides CVV again
  Millions of merchants stored millions of PANs → massive breach surface

After October 1, 2022 (CoFT effective date):
  NO merchant, payment aggregator, or any entity in the payment chain can store
  the actual PAN or any card credential after transaction authorization.
  The ONLY entity that may store the PAN is the card-issuing bank or card network.
  Merchants must replace stored PANs with network tokens issued by card networks
  (Visa Token Service, Mastercard Digital Enablement Service, RuPay Token Service).
```

**How network tokens differ from payment aggregator tokens (PCI tokenization):**
```
PCI Tokenization (what Razorpay/Stripe do):
  Token: pm_8x9mR2pL (Razorpay's internal token)
  Vault: Razorpay's vault maps pm_8x9mR2pL → PAN
  Scope: only Razorpay can use this token; not portable to other gateways
  Issuer involvement: none — the bank doesn't know about this token
  PAN storage: Razorpay stores the PAN (they are PCI Level 1 certified)

Network Tokenization (CoFT mandate):
  Token: 4000 0000 0000 0001 (a token PAN — looks like a card number)
  Vault: Visa Token Service / Mastercard MDES maps token PAN → actual PAN
  Scope: portable across any merchant, acquirer, or PA who receives the token
  Issuer involvement: issuing bank participates in token lifecycle
  PAN storage: NO ONE stores the PAN except the issuer and card network
  The token PAN looks like a real card number — can be processed by any system
  that handles cards without knowing it's a token (backward compatible)

Network token flow:
  Customer: "Save this card on Swiggy"
  Swiggy (via PA) → requests token from card network (Visa Token Service):
    POST /token/create
    { card_number: "4111 1111 1111 1111", expiry: "09/27", merchant: "Swiggy" }
  Visa Token Service → issues token:
    { token: "4000 0000 0000 0001", token_expiry: "09/27" }
    (Token is bound to Swiggy — cannot be used on Zomato)
  Swiggy stores: { customerId: "C123", networkToken: "4000 0000 0000 0001" }
  Swiggy does NOT store: 4111 1111 1111 1111

  Next purchase on Swiggy:
  Swiggy sends: { token: "4000 0000 0000 0001", amount: 500 }
  → Acquirer → Visa network → Visa Token Service: detokenizes internally
  → Issues authorization to issuer with the real PAN
  → Merchant and PA never see the real PAN at any point
```

**What PAs and merchants must do technically to comply:**
```
Step 1: Purge all stored PANs (for entities that stored them pre-Oct 2022)
  → One-time mass tokenization: for each stored PAN, request a network token
  → After token received: delete the PAN from storage
  → Confirm deletion and document for audit

Step 2: Integrate with card network tokenization APIs
  → Visa Token Service (VTS)
  → Mastercard Digital Enablement Service (MDES)
  → RuPay Token Service (via NPCI)
  Most PAs handle this integration; merchants use PA's token management API

Step 3: Modify recurring charge flows
  → Instead of charging PAN, charge network token
  → Handle token lifecycle events: token expiry, card replacement, token suspension
  → Token refresh: when underlying card is renewed, issuer can update token
                   (network token may remain same even when card is replaced)

Step 4: Update stored payment method records
  Engineering: migration from { pan: "4111..." } to { networkToken: "4000...", tokenExpiry: "09/27" }
```

**Q4. What are the technical requirements for implementing a CoFT-compliant card-on-file flow in a Node.js fintech backend?**
A: The implementation spans three layers: the initial card saving flow (tokenization), the recurring charge flow (using the token), and the token lifecycle management (expiry, updates, suspension):

```typescript
// types/payment.ts — CoFT-compliant data model
interface NetworkToken {
  tokenPan:         string;   // The network token — looks like a card number
  tokenExpiry:      string;   // MM/YY — may differ from actual card expiry
  tokenRequestorId: string;   // PA's registered Token Requestor ID with card network
  tokenReferenceId: string;   // Unique reference from card network for this token
  cardNetwork:      'VISA' | 'MASTERCARD' | 'RUPAY' | 'AMEX';
  lastFour:         string;   // Last 4 of the ACTUAL card (for display)
  cardBrand:        string;   // For display in UI
  isActive:         boolean;
}

// Database schema — CoFT compliant
// customer_payment_methods table:
// id, user_id, token_pan, token_expiry, token_reference_id,
// token_requestor_id, card_network, last_four, card_brand,
// is_active, created_at, updated_at
// NOT stored: actual PAN, CVV, full magnetic stripe data

// ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// Flow 1: Initial card saving (tokenization request)
// ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

@Injectable()
export class CoFTService {
  constructor(
    private readonly razorpayService: RazorpayService,
    private readonly paymentMethodRepo: PaymentMethodRepository,
  ) {}

  async saveCardWithCoFT(
    userId: string,
    cardData: CardInputDto, // Received from hosted fields — short-lived
  ): Promise<SavedPaymentMethod> {

    // Step 1: Request network token from card network via PA
    // (Razorpay handles the actual VTS/MDES API call)
    const tokenResponse = await this.razorpayService.tokenize({
      pan:           cardData.pan,     // Sent ONLY for tokenization; never stored
      expiry:        cardData.expiry,
      cvv:           cardData.cvv,     // Required for initial tokenization; never stored
      customerId:    userId,
      consentGiven:  true,             // Customer explicitly consented to save card
      consentTimestamp: new Date().toISOString(),
    });

    // tokenResponse from Razorpay (wrapping Visa Token Service response):
    // {
    //   networkToken:      "4000 0000 0000 0001",
    //   networkTokenExpiry: "09/27",
    //   tokenReferenceId:  "VISA_TOKEN_REF_ABC",
    //   last4:             "1111",    // Last 4 of real card — for display
    //   cardBrand:         "Visa",
    //   cardNetwork:       "VISA",
    // }

    // Step 2: Store ONLY the network token — never the actual PAN
    const savedMethod = await this.paymentMethodRepo.create({
      userId,
      tokenPan:         tokenResponse.networkToken,
      tokenExpiry:      tokenResponse.networkTokenExpiry,
      tokenReferenceId: tokenResponse.tokenReferenceId,
      lastFour:         tokenResponse.last4,
      cardBrand:        tokenResponse.cardBrand,
      cardNetwork:      tokenResponse.cardNetwork,
      isActive:         true,
    });

    // Step 3: Explicit memory scrub (best-effort)
    cardData.pan = '0'.repeat(cardData.pan.length);
    cardData.cvv = '000';

    return savedMethod;
  }

// ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// Flow 2: Recurring charge using network token
// ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  async chargeWithNetworkToken(
    userId: string,
    paymentMethodId: string,
    amount: number,           // in paise
    description: string,
    idempotencyKey: string,
  ): Promise<ChargeResult> {

    const paymentMethod = await this.paymentMethodRepo.findOne({
      where: { id: paymentMethodId, userId, isActive: true },
    });

    if (!paymentMethod) {
      throw new NotFoundException('Payment method not found');
    }

    // Check token expiry before attempting charge
    const [expMonth, expYear] = paymentMethod.tokenExpiry.split('/');
    const tokenExpiry = new Date(2000 + parseInt(expYear), parseInt(expMonth) - 1);
    if (tokenExpiry < new Date()) {
      await this.handleExpiredToken(paymentMethod);
      throw new UnprocessableEntityException('Card expired — please update payment method');
    }

    // Charge using network token — PA sends to card network
    const chargeResult = await this.razorpayService.chargeToken({
      networkToken:    paymentMethod.tokenPan,       // The network token PAN
      tokenExpiry:    paymentMethod.tokenExpiry,
      tokenReferenceId: paymentMethod.tokenReferenceId,
      amount,
      currency:       'INR',
      description,
      idempotencyKey,
    });

    return chargeResult;
  }

// ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// Flow 3: Token lifecycle — handle token updates from card network
// ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  // Card networks send webhook events when tokens change
  async handleTokenLifecycleEvent(event: TokenLifecycleEvent): Promise<void> {
    switch (event.type) {
      case 'TOKEN_SUSPENDED':
        // Card reported lost/stolen → token suspended → cannot charge
        await this.paymentMethodRepo.update(
          { tokenReferenceId: event.tokenReferenceId },
          { isActive: false, suspensionReason: event.reason },
        );
        await this.notifyUser(event.userId, 'CARD_SUSPENDED');
        break;

      case 'TOKEN_DELETED':
        // Customer removed card from all merchants / card closed
        await this.paymentMethodRepo.softDelete({ tokenReferenceId: event.tokenReferenceId });
        break;

      case 'TOKEN_UPDATED':
        // Card renewed (new expiry) — token may stay same or get new expiry
        await this.paymentMethodRepo.update(
          { tokenReferenceId: event.tokenReferenceId },
          {
            tokenPan:    event.newTokenPan ?? undefined,  // Token PAN may change
            tokenExpiry: event.newTokenExpiry,
          },
        );
        break;

      case 'TOKEN_REQUIRES_CRYPTOGRAM':
        // Dynamic cryptogram required for this charge (high-risk transaction)
        // Must fetch a fresh cryptogram from VTS/MDES before charging
        await this.refreshCryptogram(event.tokenReferenceId);
        break;
    }
  }
}
```

## Section 3: Recurring Payments and AFA Requirements

**Q5. What does RBI's Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA) requirement mean for recurring payment systems, and what are the specific rules for e-mandates and auto-debits?**
A: RBI's AFA framework mandates that card-not-present transactions above certain thresholds require a second factor of authentication — typically an OTP sent to the registered mobile number. For recurring payments (EMIs, subscriptions, loan repayments), RBI has specific rules that balance security with user convenience:

**The AFA rules for recurring payments (as of 2023):**
```
Transactions ≤ ₹15,000 (Auto-debit without AFA):
  First registration:  MUST have AFA (OTP) to register the mandate
  Subsequent debits:   NO AFA required if:
    → Amount ≤ ₹15,000 per transaction
    → Pre-debit notification sent to customer 24 hours before debit
    → Customer has option to cancel/pause before the debit executes
    → Mandate registered with bank for this specific merchant/purpose
    → Debit within the mandate parameters (amount, frequency, end date)

Transactions > ₹15,000:
  EVERY transaction requires AFA regardless of mandate registration
  No exception for recurring even if mandate exists
  OTP must be sent and verified before the debit is processed

AFA methods accepted by RBI:
  OTP via SMS to registered mobile number (most common)
  OTP via bank's mobile app (push notification-based)
  Biometric (fingerprint, face) via mobile banking app
  NOT accepted for card transactions: security questions, static passwords
```

**The e-mandate registration flow (technical):**
```
Customer: "Set up auto-debit for my loan EMI — ₹8,500/month"

Step 1: Mandate Registration (requires AFA)
  Fintech → PA → Customer's bank:
    {
      mandateType:    "RECURRING_DEBIT",
      amount:         850000,        // ₹8,500 in paise
      frequency:      "MONTHLY",
      startDate:      "2025-10-05",
      endDate:        "2028-10-05",
      merchantName:   "LoanCo",
      purpose:        "LOAN_REPAYMENT",
      customerMobile: "+91XXXXXXXXXX",
    }

  Bank sends OTP to customer's registered mobile
  Customer enters OTP → mandate registered
  Bank confirms: { mandateId: "NACH_MANDATE_XYZ", status: "ACTIVE" }
  Fintech stores: mandateId, mandateStatus, registrationTimestamp

Step 2: Pre-debit Notification (mandatory, 24 hours before)
  Fintech must send notification at T-24 hours before each debit:
    Channels: SMS + email (at minimum); in-app notification (recommended)
    Content: amount, date, merchant name, how to cancel
    Required by: RBI circular; failure = regulatory violation
    Engineering: scheduled job runs daily, identifies next-day debits,
                 sends notifications, logs delivery confirmation

  Example notification:
  "Dear Customer, ₹8,500 will be auto-debited from your account ending XXXX
  on 05-Oct-2025 for Loan EMI. To cancel, call 1800-XXX-XXXX or click [link].
  — LoanCo"

Step 3: Debit Execution (T+0, no AFA needed for ≤₹15,000)
  Fintech → PA → NACH (National Automated Clearing House):
    {
      mandateId:  "NACH_MANDATE_XYZ",
      amount:     850000,
      debitDate:  "2025-10-05",
      purpose:    "LOAN_REPAYMENT_OCT_2025",
    }
  NPCI processes via NACH → bank debits customer → sends back status
  Fintech receives: SUCCESS / FAILURE (insufficient funds, mandate cancelled, etc.)

Step 4: Failure handling
  Insufficient funds: retry logic — RBI allows limited retries (typically 3 within
                       the presentation period); each retry must also have notification
  Mandate cancelled: alert customer service; flag loan account; do not retry
  Technical failure: retry after confirmation from NPCI; use idempotency key
```

**Q6. What is NACH (National Automated Clearing House) and how does it differ from UPI AutoPay for recurring debits in a fintech context?**
A: Both NACH and UPI AutoPay enable recurring automatic debits from customer bank accounts, but they operate through different rails, have different limits, and have different registration experiences:

```
NACH (National Automated Clearing House):
  Operated by: NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India)
  Type: Batch processing (debits submitted in batches; not real-time)
  Settlement: T+1 business day (debit happens on the mandate date; settlement next day)
  Amount limit: No upper limit specified by NPCI (bank-level limits may apply)
               Practically used for amounts from ₹100 to crores (home loan EMIs)
  Registration: Physical NACH form (legacy) OR e-NACH (electronic, via netbanking/debit card)
  Mandate with: Customer's bank account (account number + IFSC)
  Used for: Loan EMIs, insurance premiums, SIPs (mutual funds), utility bills, rent
  Failure rate: ~5-10% (insufficient funds, account closed, mandate not honoured)
  Retry: Presentment can be made multiple times within the clearance window
  Engineering: Integration with sponsor banks or aggregators like Razorpay NACH,
               digiO, NBFC-specific bank partnerships

UPI AutoPay (UPI Recurring Payments):
  Operated by: NPCI (via UPI rails)
  Type: Real-time processing (debit happens instantly, not batch)
  Settlement: Real-time (instant credit to beneficiary account)
  Amount limit: ₹1 lakh per transaction (as of 2023; may be revised)
               ₹15,000 without AFA per transaction; > ₹15,000 requires OTP each time
  Registration: Via UPI app (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM) — fully digital, instant
  Mandate with: Customer's UPI VPA (Virtual Payment Address) or bank account via UPI
  Used for: Streaming subscriptions, utility bills, insurance, small-ticket EMIs
  Failure rate: Lower than NACH (real-time balance check before debit)
  Engineering: Integration via UPI Autopay API (PA/bank-specific implementation)
               Customer must have UPI app; mandate registered in-app

Comparison in fintech lending context:

Feature              NACH                          UPI AutoPay
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Setup experience     Netbanking login / debit card  UPI app (GPay, PhonePe)
Settlement           T+1 (batch)                   Real-time
Max amount           No hard limit                  ₹1 lakh
AFA for setup        Yes (OTP or netbanking)        Yes (UPI PIN in app)
AFA for recurrence   No (if ≤₹15,000)              No (if ≤₹15,000)
Pre-notification     24 hours mandatory             24 hours mandatory
Bounce handling      Complex (return memo process)  Simpler (instant failure notification)
Coverage             All bank account holders       UPI app users (~350M+)
Best for             Large EMIs (home loan, vehicle) Small EMIs, subscriptions

RBI mandate: Both rails must send 24-hour pre-debit notification.
             Both rails must allow customer to cancel mandate at any time.
             Both must send post-debit notification (SMS) regardless of outcome.
```

## Section 4: Data Localisation

**Q7. What does RBI's data localisation mandate require, how broad is the definition of "payment system data," and what are the architecture implications for a fintech with global cloud infrastructure?**
A: The RBI circular of April 6, 2018 (later confirmed September 2018) mandates that all data related to payment systems operated in India must be stored exclusively within India. This is one of the most operationally impactful requirements for fintechs using AWS, GCP, or Azure:

**What "payment system data" covers — RBI's broad definition:**
```
The RBI circular defines payment system data to include:
  1. Customer data: name, mobile number, email, Aadhaar number, PAN number,
                   bank account details, card details
  2. Payment sensitive data: customer and beneficiary account details,
                             credentials, PINs, passwords, card numbers, CVVs
  3. Payment credentials: passwords, PINs, OTPs, security questions
  4. Transaction data: originating and destination system information,
                      transaction reference, amount, date/timestamp,
                      currency, merchant details
  5. END-TO-END TRANSACTION DETAILS: all data collected, processed, transmitted,
                                      or stored as part of the message/payment instruction

The "end-to-end" clause makes this extremely broad:
  → Includes metadata: IP addresses, device IDs, session IDs of payment transactions
  → Includes logs related to payment transactions
  → Includes fraud analytics data derived from payment transactions
  → Includes even aggregated / anonymized payment data if it can be linked back
```

**What is permitted for cross-border transactions:**
```
Cross-border transaction exception:
  The foreign leg of a cross-border transaction CAN be stored abroad.
  The Indian leg of the transaction MUST be stored in India.

Example — Indian customer paying a foreign merchant:
  Transaction data:
    Indian side: customer's bank details, UPI VPA, transaction initiation = INDIA ONLY
    Foreign side: merchant's bank details, foreign currency = can be abroad
  The ENTIRE end-to-end transaction record: must have a COPY stored in India

Practical implication: even if the transaction processes through servers abroad,
the complete transaction record must be mirrored to India-based storage.
Many global payment processors (Visa, Mastercard) maintain India data centers
specifically for this requirement.
```

**Architecture implications for AWS-based fintechs:**
```
AWS region selection:
  ap-south-1 (Mumbai): Primary region for ALL payment data
  ap-south-2 (Hyderabad): Secondary region for DR (disaster recovery) — still India ✓
  PROHIBITED for payment data: us-east-1, eu-west-1, ap-southeast-1 (Singapore)

Services that must be India-region only:
  RDS / Aurora:        All databases storing customer or transaction data → ap-south-1
  S3:                  Transaction logs, payment documents, audit trails → ap-south-1
  Elasticache/Redis:   Session data including payment session state → ap-south-1
  Elasticsearch:       Transaction search/analytics indices → ap-south-1
  CloudWatch Logs:     Payment transaction logs → ap-south-1
  SQS/SNS:             Payment event queues/notifications → ap-south-1
  Secrets Manager:     Encryption keys for payment data → ap-south-1

Services that CAN use global regions (with care):
  CloudFront CDN:      Static assets (JS, CSS, images) — no payment data
  Route 53:            DNS — no payment data
  IAM:                 Global service — credentials, not payment data
  GitHub/Bitbucket:    Source code (never commit real payment data) — OK

The problematic patterns:
  Using a US-based analytics platform that receives raw transaction data:
  → Violation: payment data leaving India
  → Fix: aggregate/anonymize before sending to global analytics; or use India region

  Centralised logging in a Singapore region for global observability:
  → Violation: transaction logs must stay in India
  → Fix: India-specific log pipeline that stays in ap-south-1;
          forward only non-payment-linked logs to global aggregation

  Backup to S3 in a non-India region:
  → Violation: payment DB backups contain payment data
  → Fix: S3 backup bucket strictly in ap-south-1 with S3 replication to ap-south-2 only
```

**Q8. What are the RBI's requirements around data retention and the right to erasure for payment data, and how do you implement a retention policy that satisfies both RBI and the DPDP Act?**
A: Payment data retention involves navigating a tension between RBI's minimum retention mandates (data must be kept for regulatory purposes) and the DPDP Act's purpose limitation and erasure obligations (data should not be kept longer than necessary):

**RBI retention requirements for payment data:**
```
Transaction records:
  Minimum retention: 5 years from the date of transaction
  Rationale: RBI inspection, audit, dispute resolution, fraud investigation
  Who this applies to: banks, PAs, NBFCs, any regulated entity handling payments

KYC records:
  Minimum retention: 5 years after business relationship ends (PMLA requirement)
  Extended to 10 years if the relationship involves suspicious transaction reports (STRs)

Audit logs:
  Minimum: 5 years
  Must be tamper-proof (append-only, hash-chained or equivalent)

Chargeback / dispute records:
  Keep for duration of dispute + 18 months for card disputes
  (Visa/Mastercard chargeback time limits go up to 18 months for some categories)

PCI-DSS alignment:
  Transaction logs: 1 year readily accessible + 1 year total (PCI minimum)
  RBI overrides with 5 years → follow RBI (more stringent wins)
```

**DPDP Act 2023 obligations on retention:**
```
Section 8(7) — Storage Limitation:
  Personal data must not be retained beyond the period necessary for the purpose
  for which it was collected.
  Once purpose is fulfilled: erase or anonymize.

The conflict:
  DPDP says: erase when purpose is served
  RBI says:  retain for 5 years minimum

Resolution — regulatory purpose creates its own retention justification:
  "Compliance with regulatory obligations" is a legitimate basis for retention
  under the DPDP Act even after the original transaction purpose is served.
  A fintech can retain payment transaction data for 5 years under the RBI
  mandate — this satisfies DPDP because the purpose (regulatory compliance)
  is ongoing.

What DPDP DOES require even within the 5-year window:
  Access controls: only personnel with legitimate need access retained data
  Purpose limitation: retained data used ONLY for regulatory purposes,
                      not for marketing or product improvement
  Data minimization: retain only what is required; not the entire customer record
  Right to erasure exceptions: customer can request erasure; fintech can refuse
                                ONLY for the portion required by law (transaction record);
                                must erase the rest (preferences, marketing data)
```

**Implementation — retention policy with automated lifecycle:**
```typescript
// Data retention categories and TTLs
const RETENTION_POLICY = {
  TRANSACTION_RECORD: {
    retentionDays:  5 * 365,  // 5 years — RBI mandate
    basis:          'REGULATORY',
    category:       'PAYMENT_DATA',
    erasureEligible: false,   // Cannot be erased on customer request — regulatory hold
  },
  KYC_DOCUMENT: {
    retentionDays:  5 * 365,  // 5 years after relationship ends
    basis:          'REGULATORY',
    category:       'KYC_DATA',
    erasureEligible: false,
  },
  MARKETING_PREFERENCE: {
    retentionDays:  365,      // 1 year after last interaction
    basis:          'CONSENT',
    category:       'PREFERENCE_DATA',
    erasureEligible: true,    // DPDP right of erasure applies
  },
  PAYMENT_METHOD_TOKEN: {
    retentionDays:  null,     // Keep while active; delete when customer removes card
    basis:          'CONTRACT',
    category:       'PAYMENT_TOKEN',
    erasureEligible: true,    // Delete when card removed
  },
  SESSION_LOGS: {
    retentionDays:  90,       // 90 days — fraud investigation window
    basis:          'LEGITIMATE_INTEREST',
    category:       'LOG_DATA',
    erasureEligible: false,   // Fraud investigation basis
  },
  AUDIT_TRAIL: {
    retentionDays:  5 * 365,  // 5 years
    basis:          'REGULATORY',
    category:       'AUDIT_DATA',
    erasureEligible: false,
  },
};

// Automated lifecycle management
@Injectable()
export class DataRetentionService {
  // Runs daily — purges data past its retention period
  @Cron('0 2 * * *')  // 2 AM daily
  async enforceRetentionPolicy(): Promise<void> {
    for (const [category, policy] of Object.entries(RETENTION_POLICY)) {
      if (!policy.retentionDays) continue;

      const cutoffDate = subDays(new Date(), policy.retentionDays);

      const purgedCount = await this.dataStore.purge({
        category,
        olderThan: cutoffDate,
      });

      await this.auditLog.record({
        event:      'DATA_PURGE',
        category,
        cutoffDate,
        purgedCount,
        basis:      policy.basis,
      });
    }
  }

  // DPDP right of erasure — partial erasure respecting regulatory holds
  async processErasureRequest(userId: string): Promise<ErasureResult> {
    const results: Record<string, string> = {};

    for (const [category, policy] of Object.entries(RETENTION_POLICY)) {
      if (policy.erasureEligible) {
        await this.dataStore.eraseForUser(userId, category);
        results[category] = 'ERASED';
      } else {
        // Cannot erase — provide the legal basis to the customer
        results[category] = `RETAINED — ${policy.basis} (${policy.retentionDays / 365} years)`;
      }
    }

    // Log the erasure request and outcome for DPDP compliance
    await this.auditLog.record({
      event:   'ERASURE_REQUEST',
      userId,
      results,
      timestamp: new Date(),
    });

    return { userId, results };
  }
}
```

## Section 5: UPI, BBPS, and Specific Payment Rail Requirements

**Q9. What are the technical and compliance obligations when integrating UPI into a fintech application — specifically around transaction limits, failure handling, and the TPAPs' role?**
A: UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is operated by NPCI and is accessed by fintechs through a layered structure of participants. Understanding this hierarchy determines what obligations apply to the fintech directly versus what is handled upstream:

**The UPI participant hierarchy:**
```
NPCI (regulator/operator of UPI network)
    │
    ├── PSP Banks (Payment Service Provider Banks)
    │   Examples: Axis Bank, HDFC Bank, SBI, Yes Bank
    │   Role: Issue UPI handles (@axisbank, @hdfc), operate UPI switch interface
    │   Obligation: Direct NPCI membership; full UPI compliance
    │
    └── TPAPs (Third-Party Application Providers)
        Examples: PhonePe, Google Pay (GPay), Paytm, BHIM, WhatsApp Pay
        Role: Consumer-facing UPI apps; build on top of a PSP Bank's UPI membership
        Must have: RBI authorization as Payment Aggregator OR operate via a licensed PA
        Obligation: NPCI's TPAP guidelines + RBI PA/PG guidelines

Fintech (e.g., a lending app that accepts UPI payments):
  The lending app is a MERCHANT in UPI terminology.
  It does NOT need its own UPI membership.
  It integrates with a PA (Razorpay, Cashfree) which handles UPI collection.
  The PA's UPI integration covers the NPCI/PSP bank compliance layer.
  The fintech's obligation: merchant-level compliance (merchant onboarding norms).
```

**UPI transaction limits (NPCI mandated, 2023):**
```
Standard P2P (person-to-person):
  Per transaction:  ₹1,00,000
  Per day:          ₹1,00,000 across all UPI apps

P2M (person-to-merchant) — higher limits for specific categories:
  Capital markets:  ₹2,00,000 per transaction
  IPO/RBI retail:   ₹5,00,000 per transaction
  Insurance:        ₹2,00,000 per transaction
  Medical:          ₹2,00,000 per transaction
  Education:        ₹2,00,000 per transaction
  Loan repayment:   ₹2,00,000 per transaction (highly relevant for fintechs)

UPI Lite (small transactions, no PIN required):
  Per transaction:  ₹500
  Daily wallet load: ₹2,000
  No OTP/PIN required for UPI Lite — reduces friction for small payments

Engineering implication:
  Validate transaction amounts against limits before submitting to PA.
  Offer split-payment if loan EMI exceeds UPI limit (₹1 lakh for standard).
  For loan repayments > ₹1 lakh: offer NACH or netbanking as alternative.
```

**UPI failure handling — the technical requirements:**
```
UPI transactions have defined response codes and timing windows:

Success:   RRN (Reference Reference Number) returned; settlement T+1
Pending:   Transaction in processing — must query status; do NOT retry immediately
Failure:   Error code returned; may or may not be retriable

Common failure codes and handling:
  U30 — Transaction declined:        Do NOT retry; notify customer; offer alternative
  U43 — Transaction in process:      Query status after 30 seconds; retry if still pending
  U69 — Limit exceeded:              Notify customer of UPI daily limit; offer alternative
  U40 — Connection refused by PSP:   Retry after 60 seconds (up to 3 times)
  YB  — Your bank is down:           Retry during next bank window; notify customer
  Z9  — Beneficiary bank offline:    Retry after bank comes online; notify customer

Idempotency — critical for UPI:
  Every UPI transaction must have a unique merchant transaction ID.
  If a network failure occurs AFTER submission but BEFORE response:
    Check the status using the original merchant transaction ID.
    DO NOT generate a new transaction ID and retry without checking first.
    Double-payment risk: customer's account debited; merchant gets success on both.

  // Idempotency pattern for UPI payments
  async initiateUpiPayment(params: UpiPaymentParams): Promise<UpiResult> {
    // Generate idempotency key BEFORE attempting
    const merchantTxnId = `${params.loanId}-${params.emiMonth}-${params.userId}`;
    // Same key used if retrying

    // Check if already processed (from a previous attempt in same session)
    const existing = await this.txnRepo.findByMerchantTxnId(merchantTxnId);
    if (existing?.status === 'SUCCESS') return existing;
    if (existing?.status === 'PENDING') {
      return this.checkAndReturnStatus(merchantTxnId);
    }

    // Initiate new transaction
    const result = await this.upiGateway.collect({
      merchantTxnId,
      customerVpa:  params.vpa,
      amount:       params.amount,
      description:  params.description,
    });

    await this.txnRepo.upsert({ merchantTxnId, ...result });
    return result;
  }
```

**Q10. What is the BBPS (Bharat Bill Payment System) framework, and what obligations does an RBI-authorized BBPS Operating Unit face when building bill payment functionality?**
A: BBPS is NPCI's interoperable bill payment network that standardizes the collection of recurring bills (utilities, insurance, loan EMIs, education fees, etc.). It is directly relevant to fintechs building loan repayment or utility payment features:

**BBPS structure:**
```
NPCI Bharat BillPay Limited (NBBL)
  Operates the central BBPS platform; sets standards; handles clearing and settlement

BBPS Operating Units (BBPSOUs):
  Banks and non-bank entities authorized by RBI to participate in BBPS
  Two roles:
    Agent Institution (AI): consumer-facing; accepts bill payments from customers
    Biller Operating Unit (BOU): biller-side; connects billers to the BBPS network

Billers (in the context of fintech):
  A fintech lender IS a biller — customers pay their loan EMIs via BBPS
  The lender registers with a BOU; the BOU connects them to BBPS
  Customers can then pay EMIs via ANY BBPS-compatible channel:
    Their bank's BBPS interface, GPay, PhonePe, BHIM, Post Office, etc.

Obligations for a fintech registering as a BBPS biller:
  1. API integration with a licensed BBPS Operating Unit
  2. Provide a fetch-bill API (BBPS network queries fintech for customer's bill details)
  3. Receive payment notifications (BBPS sends confirmation when payment is made)
  4. Settlement reconciliation (T+1 or T+2 depending on biller category)
  5. Customer care integration (mandatory customer support number for BBPS disputes)
  6. Biller category registration: Loan Repayment is a defined BBPS category
```

**The BBPS bill fetch and payment flow:**
```
Customer: "Pay my LoanCo EMI via GPay"

Step 1 — Bill Fetch:
  Customer enters their loan account number in GPay
  GPay → BBPS network → LoanCo's Fetch Bill API:
    GET /bbps/bill-fetch?customerRef=LOAN_ACCOUNT_12345
  LoanCo responds:
    {
      customerName:    "Amit Kumar",
      billAmount:      8500,
      dueDate:         "2025-10-05",
      billPeriod:      "October 2025",
      loanAccountId:   "LOAN_ACCOUNT_12345",
      minimumPayment:  8500,
      billerId:        "LOANCOPRIVLTD",
    }
  GPay displays: "EMI due: ₹8,500 by Oct 5, 2025"

Step 2 — Payment:
  Customer confirms in GPay → UPI debit from customer's bank
  GPay → BBPS → LoanCo's Payment Notification API:
    POST /bbps/payment-notification
    {
      transactionId:   "BBPS_TXN_XYZ",
      customerRef:     "LOAN_ACCOUNT_12345",
      amountPaid:      8500,
      paymentTimestamp: "2025-10-04T14:32:00+05:30",
      paymentChannel:  "UPI",
    }
  LoanCo: mark EMI as paid, update loan ledger, send confirmation SMS

Engineering requirements:
  Fetch Bill API must respond in < 5 seconds (BBPS SLA)
  Payment Notification API must be idempotent (BBPS may retry on timeout)
  Both APIs must be available 24×7 (BBPS operates round the clock)
  API authentication: BBPS uses client certificates (mTLS) for BBPSBOU communication
  Rate: handle volume spikes on due dates (many customers pay simultaneously)
```

## Section 6: Incident Reporting and Operational Requirements

**Q11. What are RBI's mandatory incident reporting requirements for payment system operators, and what does a compliant incident response process look like for a fintech?**
A: RBI mandates specific reporting timelines that are significantly tighter than general data protection laws. A fintech that fails to report a security incident within the prescribed window faces regulatory action independent of the technical impact of the incident:

**RBI incident reporting mandates:**
```
Cybersecurity incidents affecting payment systems:
  Initial report:  Within 6 hours of incident detection (or becoming aware)
                   Sent to: RBI's CSITE (Cyber Security and Information Technology Examination)
                   and the relevant Department of Regulation
  Detailed report: Within 24 hours of incident detection
                   Includes: incident timeline, impact assessment, systems affected,
                             data potentially compromised, initial containment steps
  Closure report:  Within 30 days of resolution
                   Includes: root cause analysis, remediation actions, preventive measures

Reportable incidents (RBI definition — broad):
  Any attack on banking/payment infrastructure: DDoS, SQL injection, ransomware
  Data breach involving customer financial data (including payment data)
  Unauthorized access to customer accounts or funds
  Fraud involving payment systems
  System outage affecting payment processing for > 2 hours
  Vulnerability exploitation that compromises payment data confidentiality/integrity
  Social engineering attacks targeting payment processing staff

Who receives the report:
  RBI: mandatory
  CERT-In (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team): mandatory for data breaches
        (CERT-In has its own 6-hour reporting mandate under CERT-In directions 2022)
  NPCI: if the incident affects UPI, NACH, or other NPCI-operated systems
  Card networks: if card data is compromised (Visa/Mastercard have their own breach protocols)
```

**Compliant incident response process:**

**Q12. What is the RBI's framework around customer grievance redressal for payment failures, and what technical systems must a fintech have to comply?**
A: RBI's framework for payment dispute resolution is governed by the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021 (for banking and payment grievances) and specific turnaround time (TAT) mandates for different types of payment failures:

**Mandatory turnaround times for payment failures:**
```
Failed transaction — automatic refund TATs (RBI Circular on TAT):

UPI transaction failures:
  Bank account debited, beneficiary not credited:
    Credit within: T+1 business day (T = transaction date)
    If not: auto-reversal by T+1; if delay, interest at savings rate

  Payment declined at merchant but account debited:
    Refund within: 5 business days
    If not reversed: customer complaint triggers bank liability

NACH/mandate-based debit failures:
  Debit executed but service not delivered:
    Merchant must refund within 5 business days of complaint

Card transactions:
  Failed card transaction (declined but amount held):
    Authorization hold released: within 3 business days
    Dispute raised: resolution within 45 business days (chargeback process)
    Provisional credit: within 10 business days if liability established

Engineering implications:
  Automatic refund engine: if a payment is debited but loan disbursement fails,
  the refund must trigger automatically without manual intervention
  Monitoring: track all transactions where debit succeeded but service delivery failed
  Reconciliation: match payment receipts against service delivery events daily
  SLA alerting: alert operations team when TAT is at risk (e.g., T+0.5 alert for T+1 refunds)
```

**The Integrated Ombudsman — what it means for engineering:**
```
RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021:
  Customers can escalate unresolved payment disputes to the RBI Ombudsman
  The Ombudsman can award compensation up to ₹20 lakh for a single complaint
  The entity (bank/PA/fintech) must respond to Ombudsman within 21 days
  Repeated Ombudsman complaints = regulatory scrutiny of the entity

What the engineering system must produce for an Ombudsman response:
  Complete transaction audit trail: every API call, every status change, every timestamp
  Evidence of pre-debit notification: SMS/email delivery receipts with timestamps
  Evidence of refund attempt: refund initiation timestamp, refund reference, bank response
  Customer communication logs: every interaction with the customer about the dispute
```
