# Coinsurance and Reinsurance Concepts — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is coinsurance, and how does it differ from reinsurance?**
A: Coinsurance and reinsurance are both mechanisms for distributing insurance risk across multiple parties, but they operate at different layers of the insurance structure and serve different purposes.

**Coinsurance** is the sharing of a single risk among two or more insurers at the primary (direct) level — each insurer issues its own policy or participates in a single policy for its proportionate share. The policyholder knows about and may interact with all coinsurers directly. Example: a large commercial fire policy for a ₹500 crore factory is coinsured by four insurers each taking 25%.

**Reinsurance** is insurance purchased by an insurer from another insurer (the reinsurer) to protect itself against large or concentrated losses. The policyholder has no contractual relationship with the reinsurer — they interact only with the primary (ceding) insurer. Example: an insurer writes ₹100 crore of motor policies and buys reinsurance so that any single catastrophic event loss above ₹20 crore is covered by the reinsurer.

Key distinctions:

| Property | Coinsurance | Reinsurance |
|---|---|---|
| **Parties** | Multiple primary insurers + policyholder | Primary insurer (cedant) + reinsurer; policyholder not involved |
| **Policyholder awareness** | Yes — knows all coinsurers | No — reinsurance is "behind the scenes" |
| **Purpose** | Distribute one large risk at the point of underwriting | Protect insurer's portfolio / balance sheet after underwriting |
| **Policy document** | One policy with multiple insurers, or separate policies per insurer | No new policy for policyholder; reinsurance treaty/certificate between insurers |
| **Claim payment** | Each coinsurer pays its proportionate share directly | Primary insurer pays full claim; recovers its share from reinsurer |
| **Regulatory view** | IRDAI regulates coinsurance arrangements | IRDAI regulates cession limits; GIC Re has right of first refusal |
| **Typical use** | Large property, marine, aviation, corporate risks | All lines of business at portfolio level |

Both mechanisms exist because no single insurer can or should bear unlimited risk. Coinsurance distributes risk at origination; reinsurance redistributes it after the primary insurer has written it.

**Q2. What is the coinsurance clause in retail insurance, and how does it penalise underinsurance?**
A: In retail and commercial property insurance, the coinsurance clause (also called the "average clause" in Indian market practice) is a policy condition that penalises the insured for insuring a property at less than its full replacement value.

The principle: if you insure a property for only 60% of its true value (to save on premium), the insurer will only pay 60% of any partial loss claim. This is called "applying the average."

**Formula:**

```
Claim payout = (Sum insured / True value of property) × Loss amount
```

**Example:**
- Property replacement value: ₹1,00,00,000 (₹1 crore)
- Sum insured: ₹60,00,000 (60% — underinsured)
- Fire loss: ₹30,00,000
- Claim payout = (60,00,000 / 1,00,00,000) × 30,00,000 = **₹18,00,000** (not ₹30,00,000)

The insured bears ₹12,00,000 as their own share of the loss — they acted as a "co-insurer" for the uninsured portion.

```typescript
function computeAverageClausePayout(
  sumInsured: bigint,
  truePropertyValue: bigint,
  lossAmount: bigint,
): { payout: bigint; insuredBears: bigint; averageApplied: boolean } {
  // Average clause only applies if underinsured (sum insured < true value)
  if (sumInsured >= truePropertyValue) {
    return {
      payout: lossAmount > sumInsured ? sumInsured : lossAmount,
      insuredBears: 0n,
      averageApplied: false,
    };
  }

  // Apply average (pro-rata reduction)
  const payoutDecimal = Number(sumInsured) / Number(truePropertyValue) * Number(lossAmount);
  const payout = BigInt(Math.round(payoutDecimal));
  const insuredBears = lossAmount - payout;

  return { payout, insuredBears, averageApplied: true };
}
```

**Indian market context:**
The average clause is standard in Indian commercial fire, property, and engineering policies under the Standard Fire and Special Perils policy wordings filed with IRDAI. It is one of the most common sources of claim disputes — policyholders are often unaware that their underinsurance will reduce claim payouts proportionally. IRDAI mandates that insurers clearly explain the average clause at the time of policy issuance.

**Q3. What is the coinsurance structure in large commercial risks, and how does the lead insurer model work?**
A: For large commercial risks (industrial plants, airports, infrastructure projects) where no single insurer wants full exposure, a coinsurance arrangement is structured with a lead insurer and followers.

**Lead insurer model:**

```
Risk: ₹500 crore industrial plant
      ↓
Lead insurer (30% share) ← appointed by the policyholder / broker
  ├── New India Assurance: 30%  ← LEAD
  ├── United India Insurance: 25%
  ├── Oriental Insurance: 25%
  └── Bajaj Allianz: 20%
```

**Lead insurer responsibilities:**
- Underwrites and agrees the policy terms on behalf of all coinsurers.
- Issues the master policy (the lead's policy covers the entire risk; co-insurers issue "following" policies).
- Handles all policyholder correspondence, surveys, and endorsements.
- Coordinates claim settlement — assessor is appointed by the lead; all coinsurers ratify.
- Collects and distributes premium to co-insurers according to shares.

**Following insurer responsibilities:**
- Follow the lead's terms and conditions exactly ("follow the leader" principle).
- Pay claims in proportion to their share once the lead has assessed and approved.
- Cannot independently renegotiate terms or dispute claims that the lead has settled.

**Q4. What is reinsurance, and why do primary insurers buy it?**
A: Reinsurance is a contractual arrangement in which a primary insurer (the cedant) transfers a portion of its insurance risk to a reinsurer in exchange for a proportionate premium. The reinsurer agrees to pay the cedant a share of claims according to the terms of the reinsurance treaty.

Primary insurers buy reinsurance for four reasons:

**1. Capacity** — an insurer's ability to write large risks is limited by its capital and solvency requirements. Reinsurance frees up capacity by transferring risk off the balance sheet.

**2. Stabilisation** — individual large claims or catastrophic events can cause extreme earnings volatility. Reinsurance smooths the P&L by capping the insurer's net loss on any single event or in any single year.

**3. Catastrophe protection** — a single earthquake or flood can generate thousands of simultaneous claims. Without reinsurance, a regional insurer could be insolvent after a single natural catastrophe. Cat reinsurance protects against this.

**4. Arbitrage / expertise** — large global reinsurers (Munich Re, Swiss Re, Gen Re) have actuarial expertise and diversified global portfolios. They can price and carry risk more efficiently than a domestic insurer with concentrated local exposure.

**The reinsurance relationship:**

```
Policyholder pays premium → Primary insurer (cedant)
                                    ↓
                         Cedes portion of premium to Reinsurer
                         (reinsurance premium = ceded premium)
                                    ↓
                              Reinsurer
                         (pays claims recoveries back to cedant
                          when cedant pays claims to policyholder)
```

The cedant always pays the policyholder first — then recovers from the reinsurer. The policyholder has no claim against the reinsurer directly (with very limited exceptions under cut-through clauses in some jurisdictions).

**Q5. What is the difference between treaty reinsurance and facultative reinsurance?**
A: The two fundamental types of reinsurance differ in how they are placed and what risks they cover:

| Property | Treaty Reinsurance | Facultative Reinsurance |
|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Automatically covers all risks within a defined class (e.g., all motor policies, all fire policies) | Covers one specific risk, negotiated individually |
| **Placement** | Negotiated once per year; applies to all qualifying policies automatically | Negotiated separately for each risk; cedant offers, reinsurer can accept or decline |
| **Underwriting** | Reinsurer does not assess individual risks — trusts cedant's underwriting | Reinsurer fully underwrites each individual risk |
| **When used** | Standard portfolio risks (retail motor, health, personal lines) | Unusual, large, or complex risks outside treaty parameters |
| **Binding** | Automatic — no cedant action needed per policy | Requires specific acceptance from reinsurer before coverage attaches |
| **Documentation** | Reinsurance treaty (annual contract) | Reinsurance certificate (per risk) |
| **Cedant obligation** | Must cede all qualifying risks (obligatory treaty) | Cedant chooses which risks to offer (optional fac) |

**Engineering implications:**
- Treaty reinsurance: your system automatically applies the cession percentage to every policy written — no per-policy decision needed. Premium cession and claim recovery are computed as part of the standard policy and claim workflow.
- Facultative reinsurance: each risk needs a separate facultative placement workflow — offer to reinsurer, acceptance tracking, certificate issuance, and independent premium and claim processing.

## Section 2: Reinsurance Structures

**Q6. What is proportional reinsurance, and what are its two main variants?**
A: Proportional (pro-rata) reinsurance means the reinsurer shares both premium and losses in the same proportion. If the reinsurer takes 40% of the premium, it also bears 40% of every loss.

**Variant 1 — Quota Share Treaty:**
The reinsurer takes a fixed percentage of every risk in the portfolio. The cedant cedes 40% of every policy's premium and sum insured; the reinsurer pays 40% of every claim.

```
Policy written: ₹1,00,000 sum insured; ₹5,000 premium
Quota share (40% cession):
  Cedant retains:   ₹60,000 sum insured; ₹3,000 premium; pays 60% of claims
  Reinsurer takes:  ₹40,000 sum insured; ₹2,000 premium; pays 40% of claims
```

**Ceding commission:** The reinsurer pays the cedant a "ceding commission" (typically 25–35% of the ceded premium) to compensate the cedant for acquisition costs (agent commissions, admin expenses). This makes the cedant's economics work — they earn commission income even on the ceded premium.

**Variant 2 — Surplus Treaty:**
The reinsurer takes the amount of the risk in excess of the cedant's "retention" (the amount it keeps). If the cedant's retention is ₹1 crore and the policy sum insured is ₹4 crore, the surplus (₹3 crore) is ceded.

```
Risk: ₹4 crore sum insured; premium ₹2,00,000
Cedant retention: ₹1 crore (25% of risk)
Surplus ceded: ₹3 crore (75% of risk)

Premium split: Cedant: ₹50,000 (25%); Reinsurer: ₹1,50,000 (75%)
Claim split (for a ₹2 crore loss): Cedant: ₹50,00,000 (25%); Reinsurer: ₹1,50,00,000 (75%)
```

Surplus treaties have variable cession percentages — each risk is ceded in proportion to how much of it exceeds the retention. Your system must compute the cession percentage per policy at the time of binding.

```typescript
function computeSurplusCession(
  sumInsured: bigint,
  cedantRetention: bigint,
  maxTreatedLines: number,  // treaty typically covers up to N "lines" (multiples of retention)
): { cedantShare: number; reinsurerShare: number; cededAmount: bigint } {
  if (sumInsured <= cedantRetention) {
    // Risk is within retention — no cession
    return { cedantShare: 1.0, reinsurerShare: 0.0, cededAmount: 0n };
  }

  const surplus = sumInsured - cedantRetention;
  const maxCessible = cedantRetention * BigInt(maxTreatedLines);

  if (surplus > maxCessible) {
    // Risk exceeds treaty capacity — facultative placement required for the excess
    throw new ExceedsTreatyCapacityError(
      `Surplus ${surplus} exceeds treaty capacity ${maxCessible}. Facultative placement required.`
    );
  }

  const reinsurerShare = Number(surplus) / Number(sumInsured);
  const cedantShare = 1 - reinsurerShare;

  return {
    cedantShare,
    reinsurerShare,
    cededAmount: surplus,
  };
}
```

**Q7. What is non-proportional reinsurance, and what are its main variants?**
A: Non-proportional (excess of loss) reinsurance does not share every loss proportionally. Instead, the cedant pays all losses up to a "retention" (deductible), and the reinsurer pays losses above that retention up to a "limit." The reinsurer's premium is not proportional to the sum insured — it is a fixed amount determined by actuarial analysis of expected large losses.

**Variant 1 — Per Risk Excess of Loss (Risk XL):**
Applies to each individual claim or risk separately.

```
Cedant retention: ₹1 crore per risk
Treaty layer: ₹1 crore xs ₹1 crore (covers up to ₹2 crore per risk)

Claim of ₹1.5 crore:
  Cedant pays: ₹1 crore (retention)
  Reinsurer pays: ₹50 lakh (above retention)

Claim of ₹80 lakh:
  Cedant pays: ₹80 lakh (below retention — no reinsurance recovery)
```

**Variant 2 — Per Occurrence / Catastrophe Excess of Loss (Cat XL):**
Applies to the aggregate of all claims arising from a single event (earthquake, flood, cyclone). Protects against catastrophe accumulation.

```
Cat XL layer: ₹50 crore xs ₹20 crore (covers catastrophe losses between ₹20–70 crore)

Cyclone hits; 10,000 motor claims totalling ₹65 crore:
  Cedant pays: ₹20 crore (retention)
  Reinsurer pays: ₹45 crore (above retention, within limit)

Catastrophe of ₹100 crore (exceeds layer):
  Cedant pays: ₹20 crore (retention)
  Reinsurer pays: ₹50 crore (layer limit — exhausted)
  Cedant pays: ₹30 crore (above the layer — cedant's own retention for excess)
```

**Variant 3 — Aggregate Excess of Loss (Stop Loss):**
Applies to the cedant's total annual losses across a class of business. Protects against a bad underwriting year even without a single catastrophic event.

```
Stop loss: ₹30 crore xs ₹20 crore annual aggregate (covers when annual losses exceed ₹20 crore)

Annual motor losses: ₹28 crore
  Cedant pays: ₹20 crore
  Reinsurer pays: ₹8 crore

Annual motor losses: ₹55 crore (exceeds layer limit)
  Cedant pays: ₹20 crore + ₹5 crore above layer = ₹25 crore
  Reinsurer pays: ₹30 crore (layer limit — exhausted)
```

```typescript
interface XlTreaty {
  type: 'PER_RISK' | 'CAT_XL' | 'STOP_LOSS';
  retention:    bigint;   // cedant's deductible per risk/event/year
  limit:        bigint;   // reinsurer's maximum pay per risk/event/year
  reinstatements: number; // how many times the layer can be reinstated (paid out) in a year
}

function computeXlRecovery(
  loss: bigint,
  treaty: XlTreaty,
  layerUtilisedToDate: bigint,  // for stop loss: aggregate already used
): { cedantPays: bigint; reinsurerPays: bigint; layerExhausted: boolean } {
  const remainingLayer = treaty.limit - layerUtilisedToDate;

  if (remainingLayer <= 0n) {
    // Layer already exhausted for the year
    return { cedantPays: loss, reinsurerPays: 0n, layerExhausted: true };
  }

  if (loss <= treaty.retention) {
    // Loss below retention — no recovery
    return { cedantPays: loss, reinsurerPays: 0n, layerExhausted: false };
  }

  const lossAboveRetention = loss - treaty.retention;
  const reinsurerPays = lossAboveRetention > remainingLayer
    ? remainingLayer
    : lossAboveRetention;
  const cedantPays = loss - reinsurerPays;

  return {
    cedantPays,
    reinsurerPays,
    layerExhausted: reinsurerPays >= remainingLayer,
  };
}
```

**Q8. What is GIC Re (General Insurance Corporation of India), and what is its mandatory role in Indian reinsurance?**
A: GIC Re (General Insurance Corporation of India) is India's national reinsurer — a public sector entity owned by the Government of India. It holds a unique statutory position in Indian insurance regulation.

**Mandatory cession (Obligatory Cession):**
IRDAI regulations historically required every general insurer operating in India to cede a specified percentage of their premium to GIC Re — this is called the "obligatory cession." This gave GIC Re a right of first refusal on all Indian insurance business.

Historical mandatory cession rates:
- Pre-2012: 20% compulsory cession to GIC Re.
- 2012 onwards: Reduced progressively; eventually IRDAI moved to an "order of preference" model rather than fixed percentage.
- Current (2024): IRDAI's Reinsurance Regulations (2018, amended) require that Indian insurers first offer reinsurance to Indian reinsurers (GIC Re + other registered Indian reinsurers) before placing with foreign reinsurers. This "right of first refusal" for Indian reinsurers is maintained.

**GIC Re's role in engineering terms:**
- Every reinsurance placement must first be offered to GIC Re at the same terms as foreign reinsurers.
- GIC Re has 30 days to accept or decline.
- If declined, business can be placed with cross-border reinsurers.
- Your reinsurance accounting system must track GIC Re separately from foreign reinsurers for IRDAI reporting.

## Section 3: Reinsurance Accounting

**Q9. How does reinsurance affect the ledger, and what are the key accounting entries?**
A: Reinsurance creates a parallel set of accounting entries alongside the direct insurance entries. The primary insurer's P&L shows the net position (gross premium minus ceded premium; gross claims minus recovered claims).

**Account structure:**

```sql
-- Reinsurance-specific GL accounts
INSERT INTO accounts (code, name, type) VALUES
  ('4001', 'Gross Premium Income',              'REVENUE'),   -- direct business
  ('4002', 'Ceded Premium Expense',             'EXPENSE'),   -- premium paid to reinsurers
  ('4003', 'Ceding Commission Income',          'REVENUE'),   -- commission from reinsurers
  ('5001', 'Gross Claims Expense',              'EXPENSE'),   -- claims paid to policyholders
  ('5002', 'Reinsurance Claims Recovery',       'REVENUE'),   -- claims recovered from reinsurers
  ('1010', 'Reinsurance Premium Receivable',    'ASSET'),     -- ceded premium not yet remitted
  ('2010', 'Reinsurance Claims Payable',        'LIABILITY'), -- recoveries not yet received
  ('1011', 'Ceding Commission Receivable',      'ASSET');
```

**Entry 1 — Premium cession (when policy is written under a quota share treaty at 40%):**
```
Gross premium received from policyholder: ₹10,000

Dr  Bank Account (Asset)                     ₹10,000
  Cr  Gross Premium Income (Revenue)           ₹10,000

Cede 40% to reinsurer + receive ceding commission (30% of ceded):
  Dr  Ceded Premium Expense (Expense)          ₹4,000   ← 40% of ₹10,000
  Cr  Reinsurance Premium Payable (Liability)  ₹2,800   ← net payable (₹4,000 − ₹1,200)
  Cr  Ceding Commission Income (Revenue)       ₹1,200   ← 30% of ₹4,000
```

**Entry 2 — Claim payment and reinsurance recovery:**
```
Claim paid to policyholder: ₹25,000 (40% recoverable from reinsurer)

Dr  Gross Claims Expense (Expense)           ₹25,000
  Cr  Bank Account (Asset)                    ₹25,000

Reinsurance recovery (40% × ₹25,000 = ₹10,000):
Dr  Reinsurance Recovery Receivable (Asset)  ₹10,000
  Cr  Reinsurance Claims Recovery (Revenue)   ₹10,000

When reinsurer pays:
Dr  Bank Account (Asset)                     ₹10,000
  Cr  Reinsurance Recovery Receivable (Asset) ₹10,000
```

**Net position on P&L:**
```
Net premium income = Gross premium (₹10,000) − Ceded premium (₹4,000) + Ceding commission (₹1,200) = ₹7,200
Net claims expense = Gross claims (₹25,000) − RI recovery (₹10,000) = ₹15,000
Net loss ratio = ₹15,000 / ₹7,200 = 208% (this risk is loss-making)
```

**Q10. How do you implement the reinsurance accounting module in a system that supports both proportional and non-proportional treaties?**
A: The reinsurance module sits between the policy/claim system and the ledger. Its job is to compute the correct cession/recovery amounts for every transaction and post the corresponding ledger entries.

```typescript
// Reinsurance accounting service
class ReinsuranceAccountingService {

  async processPolicyCession(policyId: string): Promise<void> {
    const policy = await db('policies').where({ id: policyId }).first();
    const applicableTreaties = await this.getApplicableTreaties(policy);

    for (const treaty of applicableTreaties) {
      const cession = await this.computeCession(policy, treaty);

      await db('policy_reinsurance_cessions').insert({
        policy_id:         policyId,
        treaty_id:         treaty.id,
        cession_type:      treaty.treaty_type.startsWith('QUOTA') || treaty.treaty_type === 'SURPLUS'
                             ? 'PROPORTIONAL' : 'NON_PROPORTIONAL',
        cedant_share:      cession.cedantShare,
        reinsurer_share:   cession.reinsurerShare,
        ceded_sum_insured: cession.cededSumInsured,
        ceded_premium:     cession.cededPremium,
        ceding_commission: cession.cedingCommission,
        effective_from:    policy.inception_date,
        effective_until:   policy.expiry_date,
      });

      await postLedgerTransaction({
        idempotencyKey: `ri-cession:${policyId}:${treaty.id}`,
        entries: [
          { account: 'CEDED_PREMIUM_EXPENSE',        type: 'DEBIT',  amount: cession.cededPremium },
          { account: 'CEDING_COMMISSION_INCOME',     type: 'CREDIT', amount: cession.cedingCommission },
          { account: 'REINSURANCE_PREMIUM_PAYABLE',  type: 'CREDIT',
            amount: cession.cededPremium - cession.cedingCommission },
        ],
      });
    }
  }

  async processClaimRecovery(claimId: string): Promise<void> {
    const claim = await db('claims').where({ id: claimId }).first();
    const policy = await db('policies').where({ id: claim.policy_id }).first();
    const cession = await db('policy_reinsurance_cessions')
      .where({ policy_id: claim.policy_id })
      .first();

    if (!cession) return;  // No reinsurance on this policy

    const recovery = this.computeRecovery(claim.settlement_amount, cession);

    await db('claim_reinsurance_recoveries').insert({
      claim_id:         claimId,
      treaty_id:        cession.treaty_id,
      gross_claim:      claim.settlement_amount,
      cedant_retains:   recovery.cedantRetains,
      recovery_amount:  recovery.recoveryAmount,
      recovery_status:  'PENDING',
    });

    await postLedgerTransaction({
      idempotencyKey: `ri-recovery:${claimId}`,
      entries: [
        { account: 'REINSURANCE_RECOVERY_RECEIVABLE', type: 'DEBIT',  amount: recovery.recoveryAmount },
        { account: 'REINSURANCE_CLAIMS_RECOVERY',     type: 'CREDIT', amount: recovery.recoveryAmount },
      ],
    });
  }

  private computeRecovery(
    grossClaim: bigint,
    cession: PolicyReinsuranceCession,
  ): { cedantRetains: bigint; recoveryAmount: bigint } {
    if (cession.cession_type === 'PROPORTIONAL') {
      const recoveryAmount = BigInt(
        Math.round(Number(grossClaim) * Number(cession.reinsurer_share))
      );
      return { cedantRetains: grossClaim - recoveryAmount, recoveryAmount };
    }
    // XL: handled separately with retention/limit logic
    throw new Error('XL recovery requires treaty-specific computation');
  }
}
```

**Q11. What is a reinsurance bordereau, and how do you generate it?**
A: A bordereau is a detailed schedule submitted by the cedant to the reinsurer, listing all the risks or claims ceded under a treaty during a given period. It is the reinsurer's primary source of data about what they are covering and what claims to expect.

**Types:**
- **Premium bordereau** — lists all policies ceded in the period with their premium, sum insured, and cession details. Submitted monthly or quarterly.
- **Claims bordereau** — lists all claims on ceded policies, their status, and recovery amounts. Submitted with each claim or monthly.

```typescript
interface PremiumBordereau {
  cedantName:       string;
  treatyName:       string;
  reportingPeriod:  { from: Date; to: Date };
  records: Array<{
    policyNumber:     string;
    inceptionDate:    Date;
    expiryDate:       Date;
    riskDetails:      string;
    grossSumInsured:  bigint;
    cededSumInsured:  bigint;
    grossPremium:     bigint;
    cededPremium:     bigint;
    cedingCommission: bigint;
  }>;
  totals: {
    totalGrossPremium:    bigint;
    totalCededPremium:    bigint;
    totalCedingCommission: bigint;
    netPayable:           bigint;  // ceded premium − ceding commission
  };
}

async function generatePremiumBordereau(
  treatyId: string,
  periodFrom: Date,
  periodTo: Date,
): Promise<PremiumBordereau> {
  const treaty = await db('reinsurance_treaties').where({ id: treatyId }).first();

  const cessions = await db('policy_reinsurance_cessions as prc')
    .join('policies as p', 'p.id', 'prc.policy_id')
    .where({ 'prc.treaty_id': treatyId })
    .where('p.inception_date', '>=', periodFrom)
    .where('p.inception_date', '<=', periodTo)
    .select('p.*', 'prc.*');

  const records = cessions.map(c => ({
    policyNumber:     c.policy_number,
    inceptionDate:    c.inception_date,
    expiryDate:       c.expiry_date,
    riskDetails:      buildRiskDescription(c),
    grossSumInsured:  c.sum_insured,
    cededSumInsured:  c.ceded_sum_insured,
    grossPremium:     c.annual_premium,
    cededPremium:     c.ceded_premium,
    cedingCommission: c.ceding_commission,
  }));

  const totals = records.reduce(
    (acc, r) => ({
      totalGrossPremium:     acc.totalGrossPremium + r.grossPremium,
      totalCededPremium:     acc.totalCededPremium + r.cededPremium,
      totalCedingCommission: acc.totalCedingCommission + r.cedingCommission,
      netPayable:            acc.netPayable + r.cededPremium - r.cedingCommission,
    }),
    { totalGrossPremium: 0n, totalCededPremium: 0n, totalCedingCommission: 0n, netPayable: 0n }
  );

  return { cedantName: CEDANT_NAME, treatyName: treaty.treaty_name,
           reportingPeriod: { from: periodFrom, to: periodTo }, records, totals };
}
```

## Section 4: Indian Fintech & Regulatory Context

**Q12. What are IRDAI's key regulations on reinsurance, and how do they affect system design?**
A: IRDAI's Reinsurance Regulations, 2018 (and subsequent amendments) govern all aspects of reinsurance placement by Indian insurers.

**Order of preference for reinsurance placement:**
IRDAI mandates the following order when an Indian insurer places reinsurance:
1. **GIC Re** — must be offered first; 30-day right of first refusal.
2. **Other Indian reinsurers** (Cross-border reinsurance branches in GIFT City).
3. **Lloyd's of London India operations** (if applicable).
4. **Foreign reinsurers with credit rating A- (S&P) or equivalent** — only after Indian reinsurers have had the opportunity.

**Retention norms:**
IRDAI sets minimum and maximum retention levels for Indian insurers to prevent excessive cession abroad (which drains foreign exchange):
- Life insurers: must retain at least 50% of sum at risk domestically.
- General insurers: minimum retention varies by line of business (motor: higher retention mandated; catastrophe-prone lines: lower retention allowed).
- Net retention must be backed by actual capital — solvency margin computation uses net premium (after cession).

**Foreign exchange implications:**
Reinsurance premium paid to foreign reinsurers is an outward foreign exchange payment — subject to FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act) compliance. Each outward payment must:
- Be through an authorised dealer bank.
- Carry an appropriate purpose code (P0601 for reinsurance premiums under FEMA).
- Be reported to RBI via the AD bank.

**Q13. How does coinsurance work in the Indian PSGI (Public Sector General Insurance) context, and what are the regulatory constraints?**

**Q15. How do you compute the key reinsurance performance metrics: loss ratio, combined ratio, and cession rate?**
A: These metrics measure whether the reinsurance program is delivering value and whether the portfolio is profitable.

```typescript
interface ReinsurancePortfolioMetrics {
  // Premiums
  grossPremiumIncome:   bigint;
  cededPremium:         bigint;
  cedingCommission:     bigint;
  netPremiumIncome:     bigint;   // gross - ceded + commission

  // Claims
  grossClaimsIncurred:  bigint;
  reinsuranceRecoveries: bigint;
  netClaimsIncurred:    bigint;   // gross - recoveries

  // Ratios
  grossLossRatio:       number;   // grossClaims / grossPremium
  netLossRatio:         number;   // netClaims / netPremium
  cessionRate:          number;   // cededPremium / grossPremium
  recoveryRate:         number;   // recoveries / grossClaims (how much RI is paying)
  reinsuranceEfficiency: number;  // recoveryRate / cessionRate (> 1 = RI paying more than cost)
}

async function computePortfolioMetrics(
  lineOfBusiness: string,
  periodFrom: Date,
  periodTo: Date,
): Promise<ReinsurancePortfolioMetrics> {
  const premiums = await db('policies as p')
    .join('policy_reinsurance_cessions as prc', 'prc.policy_id', 'p.id')
    .where('p.line_of_business', lineOfBusiness)
    .where('p.inception_date', '>=', periodFrom)
    .where('p.inception_date', '<=', periodTo)
    .select(
      db.raw('SUM(p.annual_premium) as gross_premium'),
      db.raw('SUM(prc.ceded_premium) as ceded_premium'),
      db.raw('SUM(prc.ceding_commission) as ceding_commission'),
    ).first();

  const claims = await db('claims as c')
    .join('claim_reinsurance_recoveries as crr', 'crr.claim_id', 'c.id')
    .where('c.line_of_business', lineOfBusiness)
    .where('c.intimation_date', '>=', periodFrom)
    .where('c.intimation_date', '<=', periodTo)
    .select(
      db.raw('SUM(c.settlement_amount) as gross_claims'),
      db.raw('SUM(crr.recovery_amount) as recoveries'),
    ).first();

  const grossPremium     = BigInt(premiums.gross_premium);
  const cededPremium     = BigInt(premiums.ceded_premium);
  const cedingCommission = BigInt(premiums.ceding_commission);
  const netPremium       = grossPremium - cededPremium + cedingCommission;
  const grossClaims      = BigInt(claims.gross_claims);
  const recoveries       = BigInt(claims.recoveries);
  const netClaims        = grossClaims - recoveries;

  return {
    grossPremiumIncome:    grossPremium,
    cededPremium,
    cedingCommission,
    netPremiumIncome:      netPremium,
    grossClaimsIncurred:   grossClaims,
    reinsuranceRecoveries: recoveries,
    netClaimsIncurred:     netClaims,
    grossLossRatio:    Number(grossClaims)  / Number(grossPremium) * 100,
    netLossRatio:      Number(netClaims)    / Number(netPremium)   * 100,
    cessionRate:       Number(cededPremium) / Number(grossPremium) * 100,
    recoveryRate:      Number(recoveries)   / Number(grossClaims)  * 100,
    reinsuranceEfficiency:
      (Number(recoveries) / Number(grossClaims)) /
      (Number(cededPremium) / Number(grossPremium)),
  };
}
```

**Q16. What is profit commission in reinsurance, and how do you compute it?**
A: Profit commission is a contractual feature of proportional reinsurance treaties where the reinsurer shares a portion of its underwriting profit with the cedant if the ceded business performs well. It incentivises the cedant to cede good quality business and discourages adverse selection.

**Profit commission formula:**

```
Profit = Ceded premium − Ceding commission − Losses recoverable − Reinsurer's expenses

Profit commission = Profit × Profit commission rate (if Profit > 0)
```

Example:
- Ceded premium: ₹1,00,00,000
- Ceding commission (30%): ₹30,00,000
- Claims recoveries: ₹45,00,000
- Reinsurer's expenses (5% loading): ₹5,00,000
- Reinsurer's profit: ₹20,00,000
- Profit commission rate: 20%
- Profit commission payable to cedant: ₹4,00,000

```typescript
function computeProfitCommission(
  cededPremium: bigint,
  cedingCommissionRate: number,
  claimsRecoverable: bigint,
  reinsurersExpenseRate: number,  // typically 3–7% of ceded premium
  profitCommissionRate: number,   // typically 10–25%
  deficitCarryForward: bigint,    // prior year deficit to offset (some treaties carry forward losses)
): { profit: bigint; profitCommission: bigint } {
  const cedingCommission = BigInt(Math.round(Number(cededPremium) * cedingCommissionRate));
  const reinsurersExpenses = BigInt(Math.round(Number(cededPremium) * reinsurersExpenseRate));

  const profit = cededPremium - cedingCommission - claimsRecoverable
                 - reinsurersExpenses - deficitCarryForward;

  if (profit <= 0n) {
    return { profit, profitCommission: 0n };
  }

  const profitCommission = BigInt(Math.round(Number(profit) * profitCommissionRate));
  return { profit, profitCommission };
}
```

The deficit carry-forward provision means that if the reinsurer has a loss one year, the cedant must first offset that deficit before becoming eligible for profit commission in subsequent years. This protects the reinsurer from paying profit commission in a good year while still holding a cumulative loss position.

**Q17. "Design the reinsurance module for an NBFC-backed digital motor insurer with a ₹500 crore annual premium target."**
A: At ₹500 crore annual gross premium with an average motor premium of ₹5,000 per policy, the insurer writes ~10 lakh (1 million) policies per year. The reinsurance program must handle proportional cession at scale and catastrophe protection.

**Typical reinsurance program structure:**

```
Gross Premium: ₹500 crore

Layer 1 — Quota Share Treaty (40% cession to GIC Re + foreign reinsurers):
  ├── GIC Re: 20% (offered first; right of first refusal)
  ├── Munich Re India: 10%
  └── Swiss Re: 10%
  Ceded premium: ₹200 crore
  Ceding commission received: ₹60 crore (30% of ₹200 crore)
  Net premium after QS: ₹360 crore

Layer 2 — Per Risk Excess of Loss:
  ├── Retention per risk: ₹50 lakh
  ├── Limit: ₹2 crore xs ₹50 lakh (covers ₹50L–₹2.5 crore per risk)
  └── Annual premium: ₹15 crore

Layer 3 — Catastrophe XL:
  ├── Retention: ₹25 crore per event
  ├── Layer 1: ₹50 crore xs ₹25 crore (covers ₹25–75 crore per cat event)
  ├── Layer 2: ₹50 crore xs ₹75 crore (covers ₹75–125 crore per cat event)
  └── Annual premium: ₹20 crore
```

**System architecture:**

```
Policy Binding
      ↓
Reinsurance Cession Engine
  ├── Applies Quota Share (40% auto-cession)
  ├── Checks if risk exceeds per-risk XL retention (₹50L sum insured × 40% net = ₹30L net; below XL)
  ├── Adds to cat accumulation register (by pincode / peril zone)
  └── Posts ledger entries (ceded premium, ceding commission payable)
      ↓
Monthly Bordereau Generation
  ├── Premium bordereau → GIC Re, Munich Re, Swiss Re
  └── Net settlement calculation

Claim Settlement
      ↓
Reinsurance Recovery Engine
  ├── Proportional recovery: 40% of any claim
  ├── Per risk XL: if claim > ₹50L (net of QS), trigger XL recovery
  ├── Cat aggregation: accumulate event losses; trigger Cat XL if > ₹25 crore
  └── Posts recovery receivable ledger entries

Catastrophe Accumulation Monitor (real-time)
  ├── Tracks all policies in affected pincodes
  ├── Estimates gross loss using damage factors
  ├── Computes expected reinsurance recovery per layer
  └── Alerts if approaching Cat XL trigger
```

```
Net retained by cedant:        ₹25 crore  ← equals Cat XL retention (as expected)

Cat XL reinsurers pay: ₹23 crore
Total: ₹80 crore ✓
```

**Q19. "How would you detect and alert on reinsurance counterparty credit risk — the risk that a reinsurer cannot pay?"**
A: Reinsurance counterparty credit risk (the reinsurer defaults and cannot pay recoveries) is one of the most dangerous risks for a primary insurer. An insurer that has relied heavily on a weak reinsurer may find itself fully exposed after a major catastrophe.

**Detection signals:**
- Credit rating downgrade (S&P, AM Best, Moody's) of the reinsurer.
- Public financial distress signals (missed debt payments, regulatory action).
- Unusual delay in paying claim recoveries (more than 30–60 days past due).
- Concentration: more than 20–25% of total ceded premium with a single reinsurer.

```typescript
async function monitorReinsurerCreditRisk(): Promise<void> {
  const reinsurers = await db('reinsurance_placements')
    .groupBy('reinsurer_name', 'reinsurer_id')
    .select(
      'reinsurer_id',
      'reinsurer_name',
      db.raw('SUM(share_premium) as ceded_premium_ytd')
    );

  const totalCededPremium = reinsurers.reduce((s, r) => s + BigInt(r.ceded_premium_ytd), 0n);

  for (const reinsurer of reinsurers) {
    const concentration = Number(reinsurer.ceded_premium_ytd) / Number(totalCededPremium);
    const latestRating = await fetchRatingFromProvider(reinsurer.reinsurer_name);
    const outstandingRecoveries = await getOutstandingRecoveries(reinsurer.reinsurer_id);
    const oldestUnpaidDays = await getOldestUnpaidRecoveryAge(reinsurer.reinsurer_id);

    let riskFlag = 'GREEN';
    const alerts: string[] = [];

    if (!isInvestmentGrade(latestRating)) {
      riskFlag = 'RED';
      alerts.push(`Below investment grade: ${latestRating}`);
    } else if (isDowngrade(latestRating, reinsurer.prior_rating)) {
      riskFlag = 'AMBER';
      alerts.push(`Downgraded: ${reinsurer.prior_rating} → ${latestRating}`);

    if (concentration > 0.25) {
      riskFlag = riskFlag === 'RED' ? 'RED' : 'AMBER';

    }

    if (oldestUnpaidDays > 60) {
      riskFlag = 'AMBER';
      alerts.push(`Delayed recovery: oldest unpaid ${oldestUnpaidDays} days`);
    }

    await db('reinsurer_credit_monitoring').insert({
      reinsurer_id: reinsurer.reinsurer_id,

      rating_agency: 'SP',
      rating_date: new Date(),

      outstanding_recoveries: outstandingRecoveries,
      concentration_pct: concentration,
      risk_flag: riskFlag,
      flagged_at: riskFlag !== 'GREEN' ? new Date() : null,
    });

    if (riskFlag !== 'GREEN') {
      await alertRiskTeam(reinsurer.reinsurer_name, riskFlag, alerts);

  }
}
```

**Q20. "Walk through the complete accounting entries for a motor insurance policy — from premium receipt to claim settlement — showing both the direct and reinsurance sides."**
A: End-to-end double-entry for a ₹10,000 premium motor policy, 40% quota share, claim of ₹25,000.

**Step 1 — Premium received from policyholder:**
```
Dr  Bank Account (Asset)                        ₹10,000
  Cr  Gross Premium Income (Revenue)              ₹8,475   ← net of 18% GST
  Cr  GST Payable — Output (Liability)            ₹1,525   ← 18% of ₹8,475
```

**Step 2 — Quota share cession (40% of net premium):**
```
Net premium: ₹8,475
Ceded premium (40%): ₹3,390
Ceding commission (30% of ₹3,390): ₹1,017

Dr  Ceded Premium Expense (Expense)             ₹3,390
  Cr  Ceding Commission Income (Revenue)          ₹1,017
  Cr  Reinsurance Premium Payable (Liability)     ₹2,373   ← net remittable to reinsurer
```

**Step 3 — Unexpired risk reserve / unearned premium reserve (at month-end, pro-rata):**
```
If policy is 6 months old — 50% of net premium is "earned"
Earned premium = 50% × (₹8,475 − ₹3,390 + ₹1,017) = 50% × ₹6,102 = ₹3,051

Dr  Unearned Premium Reserve (Liability)        ₹3,051
  Cr  Net Premium Earned (Revenue)                ₹3,051
```

**Step 4 — Claim intimated (₹25,000 loss):**
```
Dr  Gross Claims Incurred (Expense)            ₹25,000
  Cr  Claims Outstanding (Liability)            ₹25,000
```

**Step 5 — Reinsurance recovery recognised:**
```
Recovery = 40% × ₹25,000 = ₹10,000

Dr  Reinsurance Recovery Receivable (Asset)    ₹10,000
  Cr  Reinsurance Claims Recovery (Revenue)    ₹10,000
```

**Step 6 — Claim paid to policyholder:**
```
Dr  Claims Outstanding (Liability)             ₹25,000
  Cr  Bank Account (Asset)                      ₹25,000
```

**Step 7 — Reinsurance recovery received:**
```
Dr  Bank Account (Asset)                       ₹10,000
  Cr  Reinsurance Recovery Receivable (Asset)  ₹10,000
```

**Net P&L position for this policy:**
```
Gross premium income:      ₹8,475
Ceded premium:            −₹3,390
Ceding commission:        +₹1,017
Net premium income:        ₹6,102

Gross claims:             −₹25,000
RI recovery:              +₹10,000
Net claims:               −₹15,000

Net underwriting result:  −₹8,898  (loss ratio: 246% — this policy lost money)
```

## Quick Reference Table

| Concept | Detail | Engineering action |
|---|---|---|
| **Coinsurance vs reinsurance** | Coinsurance: multiple insurers at primary level; Reinsurance: insurer buys protection after writing risk | Different data models — coinsurance_shares; ri_cessions |
| **Average clause** | Underinsurance penalty: payout = (SI / true value) × loss | `computeAverageClausePayout`; warn at policy binding |
| **Lead insurer** | Underwrites, coordinates claims, distributes premium for coinsurance | One `is_lead = TRUE` per arrangement; lead is accountable |
| **Proportional RI** | Quota share (fixed %) or Surplus (excess of retention) | Auto-cession on bind; variable % for surplus per policy |
| **Ceding commission** | Reinsurer pays cedant ~25–35% of ceded premium for acquisition costs | `CREDIT` to Ceding Commission Income on cession |
| **Non-proportional RI** | Risk XL (per claim); Cat XL (per event); Stop Loss (per year) | `computeXlRecovery`; retention + limit + reinstatements |
| **Cat XL applies to net** | Cat XL operates on net retained loss (after proportional cession) | Apply QS first; then apply Cat XL to residual |
| **GIC Re right of first refusal** | Offer GIC Re first; 30-day window; then foreign reinsurers | `first_refusal_offered_at` + deadline tracking |
| **Bordereau** | Monthly/quarterly schedule of ceded risks (premium) or claims (recoveries) | Generate from `policy_reinsurance_cessions`; submit to RI |
| **Profit commission** | RI pays back % of its profit to cedant in good years | Computed annually; deficit carry-forward reduces future entitlement |
| **FEMA compliance** | Outward RI premium to foreign reinsurers = foreign exchange payment; FEMA purpose code P0601 | AD bank reference + RBI reporting per payment |
| **Cat accumulation** | Track policy exposures by pincode/peril zone; estimate event loss | `cat_accumulation_register`; real-time monitor |
| **Counterparty credit risk** | Reinsurer default → cedant bears full loss | Rating monitoring; concentration limit (max 25%); recovery aging |
| **Reinstatement** | After Cat XL layer is exhausted, cedant pays a reinstatement premium to restore capacity | `reinstatement_count` on treaty; additional premium posting |
| **IRDAI retention norms** | Minimum % of risk must be retained in India | Net retention check on treaty setup; solvency margin uses net premium |
| **Stop accrual (RI)** | Outstanding RI recoveries on NPA-equivalent reinsurers must be provisioned | Counterparty credit provision when reinsurer rating < BBB |

**Tip for the interview:** Coinsurance and reinsurance questions at the 5 YOE level appear as "how does your insurance platform handle risk distribution," "design the reinsurance accounting module," and "a major flood hits — walk me through the claim and recovery process." The detail that separates senior answers is understanding that Cat XL applies to the net retained loss (after quota share), not the gross loss — getting the order of recovery wrong is a common mistake that changes the recovery amounts significantly. Know the distinction between proportional (premium and loss shared in same ratio) and non-proportional (reinsurer only triggered above a retention) — these are conceptually different and implemented differently in code. The GIC Re right of first refusal is India-specific regulatory knowledge that immediately signals IRDAI domain depth. The bordereau — many candidates know reinsurance conceptually but have never thought about how the data flows between cedant and reinsurer operationally; describing bordereau generation and the monthly net settlement calculation shows you have thought about the operational reality.
