# Insurance Policy Lifecycle: Quote → Bind → Issue → Endorse → Renew / Cancel — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is the insurance policy lifecycle, and what are the distinct states a policy passes through?**
A: The insurance policy lifecycle is the end-to-end sequence of states and events from the moment a customer requests a price to the moment the policy is no longer in force. Unlike a payment (which has a short, linear lifecycle), an insurance policy is a long-lived contract that can be modified, suspended, and terminated in multiple ways.

The canonical state machine:

```
QUOTE_REQUESTED → QUOTED → BOUND → ISSUED → IN_FORCE
                                               ↓          ↓         ↓
                                          ENDORSED    RENEWED   CANCELLED
                                               ↓                    ↓
                                           IN_FORCE           LAPSED / EXPIRED
                                                               SURRENDERED (life)
```

State definitions:

| State | Meaning | Key trigger |
|---|---|---|
| `QUOTE_REQUESTED` | Customer has submitted details; rating engine computing premium | Customer form submission |
| `QUOTED` | Premium computed; offer presented to customer; not yet accepted | Rating engine output |
| `BOUND` | Customer accepted the quote; insurer committed to coverage; policy not yet formally issued | Payment or digital acceptance |
| `ISSUED` | Policy document generated and delivered; coverage active with a policy number | Document generation |
| `IN_FORCE` | Active policy; premium paid; coverage in effect | Payment confirmation |
| `ENDORSED` | Mid-term change applied (address, sum insured, vehicle details) | Customer / underwriter request |
| `LAPSED` | Premium not paid by due date; coverage suspended | Premium due date missed |
| `CANCELLED` | Policy terminated before natural expiry — by customer (free look / cancellation) or insurer | Cancellation request / regulatory trigger |
| `EXPIRED` | Policy reached its natural end date without renewal | Policy end date |
| `RENEWED` | A new policy period started, linked to the prior policy | Renewal payment |
| `SURRENDERED` | Life insurance only — policy terminated early with surrender value paid | Policyholder request |

Each state transition must be: logged with timestamp and actor, immutable once recorded, and in some cases reported to IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India) within mandated timeframes.

**Q2. What is the difference between binding and issuing a policy, and why does the gap between them matter?**
A: Binding and issuing are two distinct events with different legal and operational significance:

**Binding** is the moment the insurer commits to coverage. From the moment of binding:
- The insurer is legally obligated to pay valid claims, even if the policy document hasn't been generated yet.
- The customer is legally obligated to pay the premium.
- The "inception date" (coverage start) is set.
- In motor insurance in India, IRDAI mandates that a Cover Note (temporary proof of insurance) be issued immediately on binding — before the formal policy document.

**Issuing** is when the formal policy document (the contract) is generated and delivered. It contains:
- The policy number (unique, IRDAI-compliant format).
- Schedule of insurance (insured name, asset details, sum insured, premium).
- Policy wording (terms and conditions).
- Certificate of Insurance (CoI) for motor and health policies.

**Why the gap matters:**
- Claims can arrive during the gap. A customer who binds a motor policy at 10:00 AM and is in an accident at 11:00 AM before the policy document is issued at 3:00 PM is still covered — binding is the legal trigger.
- The Cover Note issued at binding must be valid for the gap period (IRDAI allows Cover Notes valid for up to 60 days; most digital insurers issue them immediately and generate the formal document within minutes or hours).
- Premium not collected during the gap creates a credit risk — the insurer has liability but no premium.

```sql
CREATE TABLE policies (

```

**Q3. What is the quote, and what does the rating engine do to produce it?**
A: A quote is a time-limited offer from the insurer to provide coverage at a specified premium. The rating engine is the actuarial/algorithmic system that computes the premium from the risk parameters of the customer and the insured object.

**Inputs to the rating engine (motor insurance example):**

| Input | Why it matters to premium |
|---|---|
| Vehicle make, model, year | IDV (Insured Declared Value) base; repair cost profile |
| RTO (registration office) | Geographic risk — theft rates, accident frequency vary by city |
| Fuel type (petrol/diesel/EV) | Repair cost and risk profile differ |
| NCB percentage (0–50%) | No Claim Bonus discount for claim-free years |
| Add-ons selected | Zero Dep, Engine Protection, RSA add to base premium |
| Prior claims history | Claims in last 3 years increase premium loading |
| Driver age and experience | Under 25 or over 65 attract loading |
| Coverage type (OD only / TP only / Comprehensive) | Third Party premium is IRDAI-mandated fixed rate |

**Rating engine output:**

```typescript
interface QuoteResult {
  quoteId:        string;
  validUntil:     Date;              // quote expires (typically 30 days)
  basePremium:    bigint;            // own damage premium in paise
  tpPremium:      bigint;            // third-party premium (IRDAI-fixed)
  addOnPremiums:  Record<string, bigint>;  // per add-on
  ncbDiscount:    bigint;            // No Claim Bonus discount
  loadings:       Record<string, bigint>;  // age loading, claims loading, etc.
  netPremium:     bigint;            // base + addons + loadings - discounts
  gst:            bigint;            // 18% on net premium
  stampDuty:      bigint;            // state-specific; ₹25–₹200 typically
  totalPremium:   bigint;            // netPremium + gst + stampDuty
  idv:            bigint;            // Insured Declared Value
  ratingFactors:  Record<string, any>;  // full factor breakdown for audit
}
```

Quotes must be reproducible — given the same inputs, the rating engine must produce the same output. This is a testability and audit requirement. The `ratingFactors` object is the complete input snapshot stored alongside the quote — if a customer disputes the premium, you can reproduce the exact calculation from it.

**Q4. What is IDV (Insured Declared Value), and how is it computed for motor insurance?**
A: IDV is the maximum amount the insurer will pay in the event of a total loss (theft or beyond-repair accident) of the vehicle. It is the "sum insured" for motor Own Damage (OD) coverage.

**IRDAI IDV formula:**

```
IDV = (Ex-showroom price − Depreciation) + Accessories value (if separately insured)
```

IRDAI-mandated depreciation schedule by vehicle age:

| Vehicle age | Depreciation % |
|---|---|
| < 6 months | 5% |
| 6 months – 1 year | 15% |
| 1–2 years | 20% |
| 2–3 years | 30% |
| 3–4 years | 40% |
| 4–5 years | 50% |
| > 5 years | Mutually agreed (no fixed rate) |

```typescript
function computeIdv(
  exShowroomPrice: bigint,
  vehicleAgeMonths: number,
  accessories: bigint = 0n,
): bigint {
  const depreciationRate = getDepreciationRate(vehicleAgeMonths);
  const depreciation = BigInt(Math.round(Number(exShowroomPrice) * depreciationRate));
  return exShowroomPrice - depreciation + accessories;
}

function getDepreciationRate(ageMonths: number): number {
  if (ageMonths < 6)   return 0.05;
  if (ageMonths < 12)  return 0.15;
  if (ageMonths < 24)  return 0.20;
  if (ageMonths < 36)  return 0.30;
  if (ageMonths < 48)  return 0.40;
  if (ageMonths < 60)  return 0.50;
  return 0.50;  // > 5 years: use 50% as a floor; actual IDV negotiated
}
```

IDV directly determines the Own Damage premium — higher IDV means higher premium. Customers sometimes understate vehicle value to reduce premium, but this results in proportional underinsurance at claim time (under-insurance penalty applies).

**Q5. What is the difference between Own Damage (OD), Third Party (TP), and Comprehensive motor insurance, and how does IRDAI regulate TP premium?**
A: Motor insurance in India has two mandatory components with very different regulatory treatment:

**Third Party (TP) Liability:**
- Covers damage or injury to third parties (other vehicles, persons, property) caused by the insured vehicle.
- Legally mandatory under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 — driving without TP insurance is a criminal offence.
- Premium is **fully fixed by IRDAI** — every insurer charges the same TP premium for the same vehicle category. No insurer can deviate.
- IRDAI publishes revised TP premium rates annually (effective April 1).
- Example (FY 2024–25): private cars up to 1000cc — TP premium ₹2,094/year; 1000–1500cc — ₹3,416/year.

**Own Damage (OD):**
- Covers damage to the insured vehicle (accident, fire, natural calamity, theft).
- Optional (but required if vehicle is under loan — lender insists on comprehensive).
- Premium computed by the insurer's rating engine — each insurer can set their own rates.
- Base rate expressed as a percentage of IDV.

**Comprehensive:**
- OD + TP bundled in one policy.
- Most retail customers buy comprehensive.

```typescript
function computeMotorPremium(params: MotorRatingParams): MotorPremiumBreakdown {
  // TP premium: fixed by IRDAI, lookup from regulatory table
  const tpPremium = getIrdaiTpPremium(params.vehicleCategory, params.engineCC);

  // OD premium: insurer's own rating
  const baseOdRate = getBaseOdRate(params.vehicleCategory);  // e.g., 2.5% of IDV
  const baseOdPremium = BigInt(Math.round(Number(params.idv) * baseOdRate));

  const ncbDiscount = BigInt(Math.round(Number(baseOdPremium) * params.ncbRate));
  const loadings = computeLoadings(params);
  const addOnPremiums = computeAddOnPremiums(params.addOns, params.idv);

  const netOdPremium = baseOdPremium - ncbDiscount + loadings + addOnPremiums;
  const netPremium = netOdPremium + tpPremium;

  const gst = BigInt(Math.round(Number(netPremium) * 0.18));
  const stampDuty = getStampDuty(params.state);

  return {
    tpPremium, baseOdPremium, ncbDiscount, loadings, addOnPremiums,
    netOdPremium, netPremium, gst, stampDuty,
    totalPremium: netPremium + gst + stampDuty,
  };
}
```

## Section 2: Policy Issuance and Document Generation

**Q6. What is the policy document generation pipeline, and what must it contain to be IRDAI-compliant?**
A: Policy document generation is triggered immediately after binding (or after payment clears, depending on the insurer's flow). It must produce three artefacts:

1. **Policy Schedule** — the customer-specific page showing: policy number, insured name, vehicle/asset details, coverage period, sum insured, premium breakdown, NCB, add-ons.
2. **Certificate of Insurance (CoI)** — the portable proof of insurance (required by traffic police for motor; hospitals for cashless health claims). Issued as a QR-code-enabled PDF.
3. **Policy Wording** — the standard terms and conditions document (usually a reference to the approved wording filed with IRDAI; not generated per-policy but attached).

**IRDAI-mandated policy number format:**
- Unique across all policies of the insurer.
- Typically: `<insurer code>/<line of business>/<year>/<sequence>`.
- Example: `BAJAJ/2024/MOT/00123456` — Bajaj Allianz motor policy, 2024.

**Document generation pipeline:**

```typescript
async function generatePolicyDocuments(policyId: string): Promise<PolicyDocuments> {
  const policy = await db('policies').where({ id: policyId }).first();
  const quote = await db('quotes').where({ id: policy.quote_id }).first();
  const customer = await db('customers').where({ id: policy.customer_id }).first();

  // Step 1: Assign policy number (idempotent — only if not already assigned)
  if (!policy.policy_number) {
    const policyNumber = await assignPolicyNumber(policy.line_of_business);
    await db('policies').where({ id: policyId }).update({ policy_number: policyNumber });
    policy.policy_number = policyNumber;
  }

  // Step 2: Render policy schedule from template
  const scheduleHtml = await renderTemplate('policy-schedule', {
    policyNumber:   policy.policy_number,
    customerName:   customer.name,
    inceptionDate:  policy.inception_date,
    expiryDate:     policy.expiry_date,
    sumInsured:     formatCurrency(policy.sum_insured, 'INR'),
    premium:        formatCurrency(policy.total_premium, 'INR'),
    ratingFactors:  quote.rating_factors,
    // ... all other schedule fields
  });

  // Step 3: Generate QR code for CoI verification
  const qrData = JSON.stringify({
    policyNumber: policy.policy_number,
    insurer: INSURER_CODE,
    verifyUrl: `https://verify.insurer.com/${policy.policy_number}`,
  });
  const qrCode = await generateQRCode(qrData);

  // Step 4: Render PDF (using headless Chrome / Puppeteer or WeasyPrint)
  const schedulePdf = await htmlToPdf(scheduleHtml);
  const coiPdf = await renderCoiPdf(policy, customer, qrCode);

  // Step 5: Store in S3 (immutable; versioned)
  const s3Keys = await Promise.all([
    s3.putObject({ Bucket: POLICY_DOCS_BUCKET,
                   Key: `policies/${policyId}/schedule_v${policy.version}.pdf`,
                   Body: schedulePdf }),
    s3.putObject({ Bucket: POLICY_DOCS_BUCKET,
                   Key: `policies/${policyId}/coi_v${policy.version}.pdf`,
                   Body: coiPdf }),
  ]);

  // Step 6: Update policy status to ISSUED
  await db('policies').where({ id: policyId }).update({
    status: 'ISSUED',
    issued_at: new Date(),
  });

  // Step 7: Send to customer via email + WhatsApp
  await notificationService.sendPolicyDocuments(customer, {
    policyNumber: policy.policy_number,
    scheduleUrl: generateSignedUrl(s3Keys[0]),
    coiUrl: generateSignedUrl(s3Keys[1]),
  });

  return { policyNumber: policy.policy_number, scheduleUrl: s3Keys[0], coiUrl: s3Keys[1] };
}
```

**IRDAI e-Insurance Account (eIA) requirement:**
IRDAI mandates that all life insurance policies and long-term non-life policies be held in an e-Insurance Account (eIA) — a demat-like account for insurance policies, maintained by Insurance Repositories (IR) like NSDL, CDSL, CAMS, and Karvy. Insurers must submit policy data to the relevant IR within 30 days of issuance. Your system must integrate with the IR APIs for this submission.

**Q7. How do you implement idempotent policy number assignment to prevent gaps and duplicates?**
A: Policy numbers must be unique, gap-free (for audit), and assigned exactly once. Race conditions in policy issuance (two concurrent requests for the same policy) can cause duplicates or gaps.

**Pattern — database sequence with advisory lock:**

```sql
-- Dedicated sequence per line of business per year
CREATE SEQUENCE policy_number_seq_motor_2024 START 1;
CREATE SEQUENCE policy_number_seq_health_2024 START 1;

-- Policy number assignment function — atomic, no gaps
CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION assign_policy_number(
  p_line_of_business TEXT,
  p_policy_id UUID
) RETURNS TEXT AS $$
DECLARE
  v_seq BIGINT;
  v_policy_number TEXT;
BEGIN
  -- Advisory lock on this policy — prevents two concurrent calls for same policy
  PERFORM pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(p_policy_id::TEXT));

  -- Idempotent: return existing number if already assigned
  SELECT policy_number INTO v_policy_number FROM policies WHERE id = p_policy_id;
  IF v_policy_number IS NOT NULL THEN
    RETURN v_policy_number;
  END IF;

  -- Assign next sequence value
  EXECUTE format('SELECT nextval(''policy_number_seq_%s_%s'')',
    lower(p_line_of_business), extract(year FROM now()))
  INTO v_seq;

  v_policy_number := format('%s/%s/%s/%s',
    'INSURER', extract(year FROM now()), p_line_of_business,
    lpad(v_seq::TEXT, 8, '0'));

  UPDATE policies SET policy_number = v_policy_number WHERE id = p_policy_id;
  RETURN v_policy_number;
END;
$$ LANGUAGE plpgsql;
```

**Cover Note number (assigned at BIND):**
Cover Note numbers follow the same idempotency pattern but use a separate sequence — they are assigned earlier than policy numbers and are always present, even if policy issuance is delayed.

## Section 3: Endorsements

**Q8. What is a policy endorsement, and what are the different types?**
A: An endorsement is a formal, documented mid-term change to an in-force policy. It modifies the terms of the original contract without replacing it. Every endorsement creates a new version of the policy and may result in a premium adjustment.

**Endorsement types:**

| Type | Example | Premium impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Addition endorsement** | Adding a driver, adding an add-on cover | Additional premium collected |
| **Deletion endorsement** | Removing an add-on, removing a named driver | Pro-rata refund issued |
| **Modification endorsement** | Change of address, vehicle colour, engine number (after repair) | Usually no premium change |
| **Sum insured change** | Increasing IDV after vehicle modification, increasing health sum insured | Additional premium (increase) or refund (decrease) |
| **Name change** | Change of ownership (vehicle sold), legal name change | Endorsement fee + re-underwriting |
| **Cancellation endorsement** | Policy cancelled mid-term | Refund per short-rate or pro-rata table |
| **Compulsory endorsement** | IRDAI mandates a change (e.g., new clause required by regulator) | Usually nil premium |

**Endorsement data model:**

```sql
CREATE TABLE policy_endorsements (
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  policy_id         UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES policies(id),
  endorsement_number TEXT NOT NULL,       -- sequential within policy (E001, E002, ...)
  endorsement_type  TEXT NOT NULL,
  effective_date    DATE NOT NULL,         -- when the change takes effect
  requested_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  approved_at       TIMESTAMPTZ,
  approved_by       TEXT,                  -- underwriter ID or 'SYSTEM' for auto-approve
  status            TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PENDING',
  -- PENDING, APPROVED, REJECTED, APPLIED
  changes           JSONB NOT NULL,        -- before/after snapshot of changed fields
  premium_adjustment BIGINT,              -- positive = additional premium; negative = refund
  gst_adjustment    BIGINT,
  total_adjustment  BIGINT,

  applied_at        TIMESTAMPTZ,
  policy_version_before INT NOT NULL,
  policy_version_after  INT,
  UNIQUE (policy_id, endorsement_number)
);
```

**Q9. How do you compute the premium adjustment for a mid-term endorsement?**
A: Mid-term premium adjustments use either pro-rata or short-rate calculation depending on the type of change and the insurer's rules.

**Pro-rata calculation** (used for additions — customer gets coverage for exact remaining days):

```
Pro-rata additional premium = Annual premium adjustment × (Remaining days / 365)
```

**Short-rate calculation** (used for cancellations — customer gets a slightly less than pro-rata refund; insurer retains a short-rate penalty):

```
Short-rate refund = Annual premium × Short-rate factor × (Remaining days / 365)
Short-rate factor: typically 0.75–0.90 (insurer retains 10–25% as a penalty)
```

```typescript
function computeEndorsementPremiumAdjustment(
  endorsement: EndorsementRequest,
  policy: Policy,
  effectiveDate: Date,
): PremiumAdjustment {
  const remainingDays = differenceInDays(policy.expiry_date, effectiveDate);
  const totalDays = differenceInDays(policy.expiry_date, policy.inception_date);
  const proRataFactor = new Decimal(remainingDays).div(totalDays);

  switch (endorsement.type) {
    case 'SUM_INSURED_INCREASE': {
      const oldPremium = computeAnnualPremium(endorsement.oldSumInsured, policy);
      const newPremium = computeAnnualPremium(endorsement.newSumInsured, policy);
      const annualDelta = newPremium - oldPremium;
      const adjustment = BigInt(
        new Decimal(annualDelta.toString()).mul(proRataFactor).toFixed(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_EVEN)
      );
      const gst = BigInt(Math.round(Number(adjustment) * 0.18));
      return { adjustment, gst, total: adjustment + gst, direction: 'COLLECT' };
    }

    case 'ADD_ON_ADDITION': {
      const addOnAnnualPremium = getAddOnPremium(endorsement.addOn, policy);
      const adjustment = BigInt(
        new Decimal(addOnAnnualPremium.toString()).mul(proRataFactor)
          .toFixed(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_EVEN)
      );
      const gst = BigInt(Math.round(Number(adjustment) * 0.18));
      return { adjustment, gst, total: adjustment + gst, direction: 'COLLECT' };
    }

    case 'CANCELLATION': {
      const SHORT_RATE_FACTOR = new Decimal('0.80');  // customer gets 80% of pro-rata
      const refund = BigInt(
        new Decimal(policy.annual_premium.toString())
          .mul(proRataFactor)
          .mul(SHORT_RATE_FACTOR)
          .toFixed(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_EVEN)
      );
      const gstRefund = BigInt(Math.round(Number(refund) * 0.18));
      return { adjustment: refund, gst: gstRefund, total: refund + gstRefund, direction: 'REFUND' };
    }

    default:
      return { adjustment: 0n, gst: 0n, total: 0n, direction: 'NIL' };
  }
}
```

**Q10. How do you apply an endorsement atomically and maintain policy version integrity?**
A: An endorsement changes the policy record and must be applied in a single transaction — partial application (endorsement record created but policy not updated) corrupts the audit trail.

```typescript
async function applyEndorsement(endorsementId: string): Promise<void> {
  await db.transaction(async (trx) => {
    const endorsement = await trx('policy_endorsements')
      .where({ id: endorsementId, status: 'APPROVED' })
      .forUpdate()
      .first();

    if (!endorsement) throw new InvalidStateError('Endorsement not approved or not found');

    const policy = await trx('policies')
      .where({ id: endorsement.policy_id })
      .forUpdate()
      .first();

    // Validate version hasn't changed since endorsement was approved
    if (policy.version !== endorsement.policy_version_before) {
      throw new ConcurrentEndorsementError(
        `Policy version changed: expected ${endorsement.policy_version_before}, got ${policy.version}`
      );
    }

    // Apply changes from endorsement.changes (JSON patch)
    const updatedFields = applyJsonPatch(policy, endorsement.changes);
    const newVersion = policy.version + 1;

    // Update policy
    await trx('policies').where({ id: policy.id }).update({
      ...updatedFields,
      version: newVersion,
      annual_premium: endorsement.premium_adjustment
        ? policy.annual_premium + endorsement.premium_adjustment
        : policy.annual_premium,
    });

    // Mark endorsement as applied
    await trx('policy_endorsements').where({ id: endorsementId }).update({
      status: 'APPLIED',
      applied_at: new Date(),
      policy_version_after: newVersion,
    });

    // Post ledger entries for premium adjustment
    if (endorsement.total_adjustment && endorsement.total_adjustment !== 0n) {
      await postEndorsementPremiumEntry(trx, policy.id, endorsement);
    }

    // Generate endorsement document
    await generateEndorsementDocument(policy.id, endorsementId, newVersion);
  });
}
```

## Section 4: Renewal

**Q11. What is the policy renewal lifecycle, and what are the engineering components of a renewal engine?**
A: Renewal is the process of extending an expiring policy for another term — typically 1 year. It is operationally distinct from new business: the insurer already knows the customer, the risk parameters are largely unchanged, and the primary engineering challenge is automation at scale with accurate NCB and continuity tracking.

**Renewal lifecycle:**

```
T-60 days: Renewal notice sent to customer
T-45 days: Follow-up notice with updated premium (if rate revision)
T-30 days: Final notice; renewal quote generated
T-0 (expiry): Policy expires; grace period begins
T+30 (grace end): Policy lapses if not renewed; NCB forfeited
```

**Renewal engine components:**

```typescript
// Renewal orchestration — runs nightly
async function runRenewalEngine(runDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  // 1. Identify policies expiring in the next 60 days (start notification cycle)
  const expiringIn60 = await getPoliciesExpiringBetween(runDate, addDays(runDate, 60));

  for (const policy of expiringIn60) {
    const daysToExpiry = differenceInDays(policy.expiry_date, runDate);

    if (daysToExpiry === 60) await sendRenewalNotice(policy, 'FIRST_NOTICE');
    if (daysToExpiry === 30) await generateRenewalQuote(policy);
    if (daysToExpiry === 15) await sendRenewalNotice(policy, 'FINAL_NOTICE');
    if (daysToExpiry === 7)  await sendRenewalNotice(policy, 'URGENT_NOTICE');
  }

  // 2. Handle expired policies in grace period (0 to 30 days post-expiry)
  const inGrace = await getPoliciesExpiredBetween(subDays(runDate, 30), runDate);
  for (const policy of inGrace) {
    if (!await hasBeenRenewed(policy.id)) {
      await sendRenewalNotice(policy, 'GRACE_PERIOD_NOTICE');
    }
  }

  // 3. Lapse policies that have exceeded grace period without renewal
  const toRapse = await getPoliciesExpiredBefore(subDays(runDate, 30));
  for (const policy of toRapse.filter(p => p.status !== 'CANCELLED' && p.status !== 'LAPSED')) {
    await lapsePolicy(policy.id);
  }
}
```

**Q12. How does No Claim Bonus (NCB) work, and how do you compute and carry it forward at renewal?**
A: NCB (No Claim Bonus) is a discount on the Own Damage (OD) premium given to policyholders who did not make a claim in the prior year. It rewards safe driving with progressive discounts.

**IRDAI NCB scale (motor insurance):**

| Claim-free years | NCB discount on OD premium |
|---|---|
| 1 year | 20% |
| 2 years | 25% |
| 3 years | 35% |
| 4 years | 45% |
| 5+ years | 50% (maximum) |

**NCB rules:**
- NCB is earned by the policyholder, not the vehicle. If the vehicle is sold, NCB transfers to the new vehicle.
- A single claim resets NCB to 0% — regardless of how many claim-free years preceded it.
- NCB lapses if the policy is not renewed within 90 days of expiry.
- NCB transfer: if the customer switches insurers, they must provide an NCB retention certificate from the prior insurer.

```sql
CREATE TABLE policy_ncb_history (
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  policy_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES policies(id),
  customer_id     UUID NOT NULL,
```

```typescript
async function computeRenewalNcb(
  customerId: string,
  expiringPolicyId: string,
): Promise<NcbAssessment> {
  const expiringPolicy = await db('policies').where({ id: expiringPolicyId }).first();

  // Check if any claim was made during the expiring policy period
  const claimMade = await db('claims')
    .where({ policy_id: expiringPolicyId })
    .whereIn('status', ['SETTLED', 'PAID', 'PARTIALLY_SETTLED'])
    .first();

  if (claimMade) {
    // Claim made — NCB resets to 0%
    return {
      ncbRate: 0,
      ncbResetReason: 'CLAIM_MADE',
      claimId: claimMade.id,
      priorNcbForfeited: expiringPolicy.ncb_rate,
    };
  }

  // No claim — advance NCB to next tier
  const currentNcb = Number(expiringPolicy.ncb_rate);
  const NCB_PROGRESSION = [0, 0.20, 0.25, 0.35, 0.45, 0.50];
  const currentIndex = NCB_PROGRESSION.indexOf(currentNcb);
  const nextNcb = NCB_PROGRESSION[Math.min(currentIndex + 1, NCB_PROGRESSION.length - 1)];

  return {
    ncbRate: nextNcb,
    ncbResetReason: null,
    claimId: null,
    priorNcbForfeited: 0,
  };
}

async function generateRenewalQuote(expiringPolicy: Policy): Promise<Quote> {
  const ncbAssessment = await computeRenewalNcb(
    expiringPolicy.customer_id, expiringPolicy.id
  );

  // Rating with updated NCB, revised IRDAI TP rates, and updated vehicle age/IDV
  const renewalPremium = computeMotorPremium({
    ...extractRatingParams(expiringPolicy),
    vehicleAgeMonths: differenceInMonths(new Date(), expiringPolicy.vehicle_registration_date),
    ncbRate: ncbAssessment.ncbRate,
  });

  const quote = await db('quotes').insert({
    customer_id:      expiringPolicy.customer_id,
    quote_type:       'RENEWAL',
    prior_policy_id:  expiringPolicy.id,
    rating_factors:   renewalPremium.ratingFactors,
    total_premium:    renewalPremium.totalPremium,
    ncb_rate:         ncbAssessment.ncbRate,
    valid_until:      addDays(new Date(), 30),
    status:           'ACTIVE',
  }).returning('*').then(r => r[0]);

  return quote;
}
```

## Section 5: Cancellation, Lapse, and Free Look

**Q13. What is the free look period, and how does it differ from a mid-term cancellation?**
A: The free look period is an IRDAI-mandated right allowing policyholders to cancel a newly issued policy within a specified window and receive a full refund (minus certain deductions). It is a consumer protection measure.

**IRDAI free look rules:**
- **15 days** for policies sold through traditional (offline) channels.
- **30 days** for policies sold through distance marketing (online, phone, email) — IRDAI circular 2024.
- Life insurance: free look is mandatory; full premium refunded less stamp duty, medical examination costs, and proportionate risk premium for days of coverage.
- Non-life insurance: typically full refund if no claim has been made; pro-rata deduction for period of coverage if claim made.

**Free look vs mid-term cancellation:**

| Property | Free Look | Mid-term Cancellation |
|---|---|---|
| **Window** | 15/30 days from policy receipt | Any time after free look expires |
| **Refund** | Full premium (minus minimal deductions) | Short-rate refund (insurer retains penalty) |
| **Effect on NCB** | NCB not affected (policy treated as if never issued) | NCB resets to 0% if claim was made; otherwise maintained |
| **IRDAI reporting** | Must be reported as free look cancellation | Reported as mid-term cancellation |
| **Trigger** | Policyholder only | Policyholder or insurer (for fraud / non-disclosure) |

```typescript
async function processCancellation(
  policyId: string,
  cancellationType: 'FREE_LOOK' | 'MID_TERM_CUSTOMER' | 'MID_TERM_INSURER',
  reason: string,
  effectiveDate: Date,
): Promise<CancellationResult> {
  const policy = await db('policies').where({ id: policyId }).forUpdate().first();

  if (policy.status !== 'IN_FORCE' && policy.status !== 'ISSUED') {
    throw new InvalidStateError(`Cannot cancel policy in ${policy.status} state`);
  }

  // Validate free look window
  if (cancellationType === 'FREE_LOOK') {
    const daysSinceIssuance = differenceInDays(new Date(), policy.issued_at);
    const freeLookDays = policy.channel === 'ONLINE' ? 30 : 15;
    if (daysSinceIssuance > freeLookDays) {
      throw new FreeLookExpiredError(`Free look period of ${freeLookDays} days has expired`);
    }
  }

  // Check for active claims — cannot cancel if open claim
  const activeClaim = await db('claims')
    .where({ policy_id: policyId })
    .whereIn('status', ['REGISTERED', 'UNDER_INVESTIGATION', 'APPROVED'])
    .first();
  if (activeClaim) {
    throw new ActiveClaimError('Cannot cancel policy with an active claim');
  }

  // Compute refund
  const refund = computeCancellationRefund(policy, cancellationType, effectiveDate);

  await db.transaction(async (trx) => {
    // Update policy status
    await trx('policies').where({ id: policyId }).update({
      status: 'CANCELLED',
      cancelled_at: new Date(),
      cancellation_reason: reason,
    });

    // Record cancellation event
    await trx('policy_events').insert({
      policy_id: policyId,
      event_type: 'CANCELLATION',
      event_subtype: cancellationType,
      effective_date: effectiveDate,
      details: { reason, refundAmount: refund.total },
    });

    // Post refund to ledger and initiate payment
    if (refund.total > 0n) {
      await postCancellationRefundEntry(trx, policyId, refund);
      await initiateRefundPayment(policy.customer_id, refund.total, `Policy ${policy.policy_number} cancellation`);
    }
  });

  // Cancel any pending NACH debits for future premium installments
  await cancelFuturePremiumDebits(policyId);

  // Notify IRDAI (if required by line of business)
  await reportCancellationToIrdai(policy, cancellationType);

  return { policyNumber: policy.policy_number, refundAmount: refund.total, effectiveDate };
}
```

**Q14. What is a policy lapse, and how does it differ from cancellation? What is the reinstatement process?**
A: A lapse occurs when the policy's premium is not paid by the due date (and the grace period expires) — coverage ceases automatically, without any action by the insurer. This is distinct from cancellation, which is an active decision.

**Lapse characteristics:**
- Happens automatically when premium is unpaid after the grace period.
- In motor insurance: grace period is typically 0 (no grace — coverage ceases on the due date).
- In health insurance: IRDAI mandates a 30-day grace period for annual policies.
- In life insurance: IRDAI mandates a 30-day grace period for annual premium; 15 days for monthly.
- A lapsed policy cannot pay claims — the insured is unprotected.

**Reinstatement:**
- The process of restoring a lapsed policy to in-force status.
- Requires: payment of all outstanding premiums + late payment penalty + evidence of continued insurability (health declaration for life/health; inspection for motor).
- IRDAI limits the reinstatement window: typically up to 2 years from lapse date for life insurance.
- Motor insurance: reinstated policies require a vehicle inspection if lapsed > 90 days (NCB also forfeits).

```sql
CREATE TABLE policy_lapse_events (

  grace_period_end    DATE NOT NULL,
  reinstatement_deadline DATE,              -- null = not reinstateable (expired window)
  reinstated_at       TIMESTAMPTZ,
  reinstatement_premium_paid BIGINT,
  late_fee_paid       BIGINT,
```

## Section 6: Indian Fintech & Regulatory Context

**Q15. What are the key IRDAI regulations that affect the engineering design of an insurance platform?**
A: IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India) is the insurance sector regulator. Key regulations with direct engineering implications:

**IRDAI (Insurance Web Aggregators) Regulations, 2017:**
- Web aggregators (PolicyBazaar, Coverfox) must be IRDAI-licensed.
- They cannot collect premium directly — premium must go to the insurer.
- They must display standardised product comparison formats.

**IRDAI Sandbox Regulations, 2019:**
- Insurtech startups can test innovative products in a regulatory sandbox for up to 3 years.
- Allows testing of parametric insurance, usage-based insurance, and new distribution models.

**IRDAI (Issuance of e-Insurance Policies) Regulations, 2016:**
- All new life insurance policies and long-term (> 1 year) non-life policies must be issued electronically.
- Customers must be linked to an eIA (e-Insurance Account) at one of the four Insurance Repositories (IR).
- Engineering requirement: IR API integration for policy creation, update, and query.

**IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests) Regulations, 2017:**
- Free look period mandatory.
- Claim settlement within 30 days of document completion (non-life) or 30 days of death certificate (life).
- Renewal notice mandatory 30 days before expiry.
- All policy communications must be in the policyholder's chosen language.

**IRDAI guidelines on data localisation (2022):**
- All policyholder data must be stored in India.
- No transfer of sensitive policyholder data outside India without explicit consent.
- Engineering: AWS `ap-south-1` or Azure India Central for all policy data.

**Premium collection regulations:**
- Premium must be collected before coverage inception for non-life insurance (except cover notes in motor).
- For life insurance: 30-day grace period allowed for premium collection.
- Premium refund on cancellation: must be processed within 15 days of cancellation request.

**Q16. How does GST apply to insurance premiums, and how does it vary by product line?**
A: Insurance premiums attract GST, but the rate varies significantly by product line — unlike the uniform 18% on most financial services.

| Insurance type | GST rate | SAC code |
|---|---|---|
| Life insurance (term plan — pure risk) | 18% on premium | 9971 |
| Life insurance (endowment / ULIP — 1st year premium) | 4.5% on premium | 9971 |
| Life insurance (endowment / ULIP — renewal premium) | 2.25% on premium | 9971 |
| Health insurance (individual / family) | 18% on premium | 9971 |
| Senior citizen health insurance | 5% on premium | 9971 |
| Motor — Third Party (TP) | 18% on TP premium | 9971 |
| Motor — Own Damage (OD) | 18% on OD premium | 9971 |
| Crop / Agricultural insurance | Exempt (0%) | — |
| Government welfare schemes (PMJJBY, PMSBY) | Exempt (0%) | — |

```typescript
function computePremiumGst(
  netPremium: bigint,
  lineOfBusiness: string,
  subType: string,
  policyYear: number,
): { gst: bigint; rate: number } {
  const gstRate = getGstRate(lineOfBusiness, subType, policyYear);
  const gst = BigInt(Math.round(Number(netPremium) * gstRate));
  return { gst, rate: gstRate };
}

function getGstRate(lob: string, subType: string, policyYear: number): number {
  if (lob === 'LIFE') {
    if (subType === 'TERM') return 0.18;
    if (subType === 'ENDOWMENT' || subType === 'ULIP') {
      return policyYear === 1 ? 0.045 : 0.0225;
    }
  }
  if (lob === 'HEALTH' && subType === 'SENIOR_CITIZEN') return 0.05;
  if (lob === 'CROP') return 0;
  if (['MOTOR', 'HEALTH', 'FIRE', 'TRAVEL', 'MARINE'].includes(lob)) return 0.18;
  return 0.18;  // default
}
```

**Stamp duty:**
In addition to GST, stamp duty is levied on insurance policies under the Indian Stamp Act. The rate is state-specific and typically ₹25–₹200 depending on the state and sum insured. Stamp duty is collected by the insurer and remitted to the state government.

```sql
-- Valid policy state transitions
CREATE TABLE policy_valid_transitions (
  from_status policy_status NOT NULL,
  to_status   policy_status NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (from_status, to_status)
);

INSERT INTO policy_valid_transitions VALUES
  ('QUOTED',   'BOUND'),
  ('BOUND',    'ISSUED'),
  ('ISSUED',   'IN_FORCE'),
  ('IN_FORCE', 'ENDORSED'),
  ('ENDORSED', 'IN_FORCE'),
  ('IN_FORCE', 'LAPSED'),
  ('IN_FORCE', 'CANCELLED'),
  ('IN_FORCE', 'EXPIRED'),
  ('LAPSED',   'IN_FORCE'),    -- reinstatement
  ('LAPSED',   'CANCELLED'),
  ('EXPIRED',  'IN_FORCE');    -- renewal creates new policy from expired

CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION enforce_policy_transition()
RETURNS TRIGGER AS $$
BEGIN
  IF OLD.status = NEW.status THEN RETURN NEW; END IF;
  IF NOT EXISTS (
    SELECT 1 FROM policy_valid_transitions
    WHERE from_status = OLD.status AND to_status = NEW.status
  ) THEN
    RAISE EXCEPTION 'Invalid policy transition: % → %', OLD.status, NEW.status;
  END IF;
  RETURN NEW;
END;
$$ LANGUAGE plpgsql;

CREATE TRIGGER enforce_policy_state
  BEFORE UPDATE ON policies
  FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION enforce_policy_transition();
```

**Q18. "A customer claims their NCB was incorrectly set to 0% at renewal even though they did not make a claim. How do you investigate?"**
A: NCB disputes are common and often arise from: a claim that was registered but later repudiated being incorrectly counted, a NCB transfer from a previous insurer not being recorded, or a data entry error linking the wrong policy to the renewal.

**Step 1 — Pull NCB history for the customer**
```sql
SELECT
  p.policy_number, p.inception_date, p.expiry_date, p.ncb_rate,
  n.claim_made, n.claim_id, n.ncb_at_renewal, n.certificate_no
FROM policies p
LEFT JOIN policy_ncb_history n ON n.policy_id = p.id
WHERE p.customer_id = '<customer_id>'
ORDER BY p.inception_date;
```

**Step 2 — Check claims for the prior policy period**
```sql
SELECT c.id, c.claim_number, c.status, c.intimation_date, c.settlement_amount
FROM claims c
WHERE c.policy_id = '<prior_policy_id>'
ORDER BY c.intimation_date;
```

If a claim exists with status `REPUDIATED` or `WITHDRAWN` — a repudiated claim should NOT reset NCB. IRDAI guideline: only settled/paid claims trigger NCB reset. If the system treated a repudiated claim as a settled claim, that is a bug.

**Step 3 — Check for NCB transfer certificate**
If the customer switched from another insurer and provided an NCB retention certificate, check whether it was recorded:
```sql
SELECT * FROM policy_ncb_history
WHERE customer_id = '<customer_id>'
  AND transferred_from_policy_id IS NOT NULL;
```

**Step 4 — Remediation**
If NCB was incorrectly reset:
1. Raise a correction endorsement: NCB change from 0% to the correct rate (e.g., 35%).
2. Compute the premium difference (credit to customer).
3. Issue a revised policy schedule and CoI.
4. If premium was already collected at wrong rate: initiate refund for the difference.

**Q19. "How would you design a parametric insurance product (e.g., flight delay insurance) where claims are auto-triggered?"**
A: Parametric insurance pays a fixed amount when a pre-defined trigger event occurs — no loss assessment required. Flight delay insurance pays ₹2,000 if a flight is delayed by more than 3 hours. The entire claim process is automated.

**Key differences from traditional insurance:**

| Property | Traditional (indemnity) | Parametric |
|---|---|---|
| **Claim trigger** | Actual loss + documentation | Objective event (flight delayed > 3h) |
| **Claim process** | Claim filing → investigation → settlement (days/weeks) | Auto-triggered → instant payment (minutes) |
| **Payout** | Actual loss amount (up to sum insured) | Fixed agreed amount regardless of actual loss |
| **Fraud risk** | High (exaggerated claims) | Low (trigger is externally verified) |
| **Basis risk** | None | Yes — trigger may not perfectly match actual loss |

**Architecture:**

```typescript
// Flight delay parametric policy lifecycle
interface ParametricPolicy {
  policyId:           string;
  flightNumber:       string;
  flightDate:         Date;
  triggerDelayHours:  number;     // e.g., 3 hours
  payoutAmount:       bigint;     // e.g., ₹2,000 in paise
  premiumPaid:        bigint;
  status:             'IN_FORCE' | 'TRIGGERED' | 'PAID' | 'EXPIRED_NO_TRIGGER';
}

// Data feed listener — flight status API (FlightAware, AviationStack)
async function checkFlightDelayTrigger(policy: ParametricPolicy): Promise<void> {
  const flightStatus = await flightStatusApi.get(policy.flightNumber, policy.flightDate);

  if (flightStatus.status === 'DEPARTED' || flightStatus.status === 'ARRIVED') {
    const delayMinutes = flightStatus.actualDepartureMinutes - flightStatus.scheduledDepartureMinutes;
    const delayHours = delayMinutes / 60;

    if (delayHours >= policy.triggerDelayHours) {
      // Trigger event confirmed — auto-settle
      await autoSettleParametricClaim(policy, {
        triggerEvent: 'FLIGHT_DELAYED',
        actualDelayHours: delayHours,
        verifiedBy: 'FLIGHTAWARE_API',
        verifiedAt: new Date(),
      });
    } else if (flightStatus.status === 'ARRIVED' && delayHours < policy.triggerDelayHours) {
      // Flight arrived with insufficient delay — policy expires without payout
      await db('policies').where({ id: policy.policyId }).update({
        status: 'EXPIRED_NO_TRIGGER',
      });
    }
  }
}

async function autoSettleParametricClaim(
  policy: ParametricPolicy,
  triggerDetails: TriggerDetails,
): Promise<void> {
  await db.transaction(async (trx) => {
    // Create claim record
    const claim = await trx('claims').insert({
      policy_id:    policy.policyId,
      claim_type:   'PARAMETRIC_AUTO_TRIGGER',
      trigger_data: triggerDetails,
      status:       'AUTO_APPROVED',
      claimed_amount: policy.payoutAmount,
      approved_amount: policy.payoutAmount,
    }).returning('*').then(r => r[0]);

    // Update policy
    await trx('policies').where({ id: policy.policyId }).update({ status: 'TRIGGERED' });

    // Post ledger entry
    await postClaimPaymentEntry(trx, claim);
  });

  // Initiate instant payout (UPI / bank transfer)
  await initiateClaimPayout(policy.customerId, policy.payoutAmount,
    `Flight delay claim — Policy ${policy.policyNumber}`);

  // Notify customer
  await notifyCustomer(policy.customerId, 'PARAMETRIC_CLAIM_AUTO_SETTLED', {
    amount: policy.payoutAmount,
    flightNumber: policy.flightNumber,
    delay: triggerDetails.actualDelayHours,
  });
}
```

**IRDAI sandbox requirement:** Parametric products must be approved by IRDAI (under the Sandbox framework or as a filed product). The trigger mechanism, data source, and payout terms must be disclosed in the approved product filing.

| **eIA submission** | IRDAI mandate: all life + long-term non-life to IR | IR API integration; 30-day deadline from issuance |
| **IRDAI compliance SLA** | Claim settlement ≤ 30 days; renewal notice ≥ 30 days before expiry | `irdai_compliance_events` with `is_breached` flag |
| **GST rates** | Motor/Health: 18%; Senior health: 5%; Crop: 0%; Endowment renewal: 2.25% | Product-type lookup; different from flat 18% |
| **Stamp duty** | State-specific; ₹25–₹200 | State lookup table; separate ledger entry |
| **Parametric trigger** | External data source (flight API, weather oracle) | Auto-approve on trigger; no human claim assessment |
| **Health portability** | No fresh waiting period if porting within 30 days of renewal | `continuity_from_policy_id`; carry-forward waiting credit |
| **Cumulative bonus** | Health: sum insured increases claim-free years (5–10%/year) | Separate from NCB; no premium discount |
| **Policy state machine** | DB trigger enforces valid transitions | `policy_valid_transitions` table + trigger |
| **Data localisation** | All policyholder data must stay in India | `ap-south-1` only; no cross-border transfer |

**Tip for the interview:** Insurance policy lifecycle questions at the 5 YOE level appear as "design a policy management system," "how do you handle mid-term changes to a policy," and "what happens to NCB when a customer makes a claim mid-year and then renews." The detail that separates senior answers is the distinction between binding and issuing — most candidates conflate them, but the gap between them is where the hardest engineering problems live (claims during the gap, Cover Note validity, IRDAI compliance). Know that TP premium is IRDAI-fixed (every insurer charges the same) while OD premium is insurer-determined — this is a fundamental Indian motor insurance fact that immediately signals domain depth. The NCB computation (repudiated claims do NOT reset NCB; only settled claims do) and the IRDAI free look period (30 days online, 15 days offline) are the two regulatory details most candidates miss. Tie the endorsement system to the version-controlled data model — an endorsement is not an update, it is a new version with a full audit trail of the before/after state.
