# EMI Calculation (Reducing Balance Method) — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is an EMI, and what does the reducing balance method mean?**
A: An EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is a fixed periodic payment made by a borrower to a lender on a specified date each month. Each EMI covers two components — the interest due for that period and a portion of the outstanding principal. The sum of these two components is constant across all instalments, but their internal composition changes every month.

The **reducing balance method** (also called the **diminishing balance method**) means that interest for each period is computed on the **outstanding principal after the previous payment**, not on the original loan amount. As the borrower repays principal each month, the base on which interest is charged shrinks — hence "reducing balance." This is the mathematically correct way to price a loan and the method mandated by the RBI for all retail lending products in India.

Contrast with the **flat rate method**: interest is computed on the original principal for the entire tenure and divided equally. A 10% flat rate loan is far more expensive than a 10% reducing balance loan — the effective annual rate (EAR) on a flat rate loan is roughly 1.8× the stated flat rate. RBI's Fair Practices Code requires lenders to disclose the annualised rate on a reducing balance basis (Annual Percentage Rate — APR) so borrowers can make informed comparisons.

**Q2. What is the mathematical formula for EMI under the reducing balance method?**
A: The EMI formula is derived from the present value of an annuity:

```
        P × r × (1 + r)^n
EMI = ─────────────────────
         (1 + r)^n − 1
```

Where:
- `P` = Principal loan amount (disbursed amount)
- `r` = Periodic interest rate = Annual interest rate / 12 (for monthly EMI)
- `n` = Total number of instalments (loan tenure in months)

**Derivation intuition:** The lender disburses `P` today. They want a stream of `n` equal payments whose present value, discounted at rate `r`, exactly equals `P`. The annuity formula solves for that equal payment.

**Example:**
- Principal: ₹5,00,000
- Annual interest rate: 12% → monthly rate r = 12% / 12 = 1% = 0.01
- Tenure: 24 months

```
        5,00,000 × 0.01 × (1.01)^24
EMI = ────────────────────────────────
              (1.01)^24 − 1

      5,00,000 × 0.01 × 1.26973
    = ──────────────────────────
             1.26973 − 1

      5,00,000 × 0.0126973
    = ─────────────────────
           0.26973

      6,348.65
    = ─────────
      0.26973

    ≈ ₹23,536.74
```

The borrower pays ₹23,536.74 every month for 24 months. Total payment = ₹5,64,881.76. Total interest paid = ₹64,881.76.

**Q3. What is an amortization schedule, and what does each row represent?**
A: An amortization schedule is a complete table showing how each EMI payment is split between interest and principal for every instalment in the loan tenure. It is the foundational artifact of a loan — required for accounting, borrower communication, prepayment calculation, and regulatory reporting.

Each row in an amortization schedule represents:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `instalment_no` | EMI sequence number (1 to n) |
| `due_date` | Calendar date on which this EMI is due |
| `opening_balance` | Outstanding principal at the start of this period |
| `emi_amount` | Fixed EMI amount (constant across all rows) |
| `interest_component` | Interest charged this period = opening_balance × monthly_rate |
| `principal_component` | Principal repaid this period = emi_amount − interest_component |
| `closing_balance` | Remaining principal after this payment = opening_balance − principal_component |

Key property: `closing_balance` of row `n` must equal `0` (or a rounding residual < ₹1 due to floating point). If it does not, the schedule has a calculation error.

In a reducing balance loan:
- Early EMIs have a **high interest component and low principal component** — the outstanding principal is large, so interest is large.
- Late EMIs have a **low interest component and high principal component** — the outstanding principal has reduced significantly.

This front-loading of interest is why prepayment in the early months of a loan saves disproportionately more interest than prepayment in the later months.

**Q4. Why does the reducing balance method matter from a regulatory and borrower fairness perspective in India?**
A: The reducing balance method is not a choice for regulated lenders in India — it is an RBI mandate:

- **RBI Master Direction on Interest Rate on Advances (2016):** All floating rate loans and most fixed rate retail loans must have interest computed on the daily/monthly reducing balance. Charging interest on the original principal (flat rate method) for retail borrowers is prohibited for banks and most NBFCs.
- **Annual Percentage Rate (APR) disclosure:** Under RBI's Fair Practices Code, lenders must disclose the APR — the cost of credit expressed as an annualised reducing balance rate — in the loan agreement and KFS (Key Facts Statement). This allows borrowers to compare loans across lenders on an apples-to-apples basis.
- **Key Facts Statement (KFS) mandate (2024):** RBI mandated that from October 2024, all retail and MSME loans must include a KFS document showing the EMI, amortization schedule summary, APR, and total interest cost in clear terms before loan sanction.

For engineers building a lending platform, these regulations mean:
- The amortization schedule generated by the system is a **legally binding document**. Rounding errors, floating-point inconsistencies, or off-by-one date bugs can create discrepancies between what the borrower signed and what the system bills — a regulatory and legal liability.
- The system must be able to regenerate the **exact same schedule** for a loan at any point in time (audit requirement). This means the schedule must be stored, not recomputed dynamically, because recomputation with updated rate or date assumptions could produce a different schedule.

**Q5. What is the difference between monthly reducing balance and daily reducing balance, and when is each used?**
A: The distinction is in the frequency at which the outstanding principal is updated for interest calculation:

**Monthly reducing balance:**
- Interest for month `m` = closing balance of month `m−1` × monthly rate.
- Principal is considered reduced only at the end of each month (when the EMI is posted).
- Standard for personal loans, home loans, auto loans, and most consumer credit in India.
- Simpler to compute; EMI formula gives the exact instalment.

**Daily reducing balance:**
- Interest accrues daily on the outstanding principal as of that day.
- Any payment (EMI, prepayment) reduces the principal from the day it is received, immediately reducing the next day's interest accrual.
- Used for overdraft (OD) facilities, credit cards, and some home loan products (where the borrower parks surplus funds in a linked OD account to reduce interest daily).
- More favorable to the borrower — if an EMI is paid 5 days early, the borrower saves 5 days of interest.
- Computationally more complex; the schedule cannot be generated upfront as a fixed table since actual payment dates affect accrued interest.

```typescript
// Monthly reducing balance — schedule is fully deterministic upfront
function monthlyEmi(principal: number, annualRate: number, tenureMonths: number): number {
  const r = annualRate / 12 / 100;
  if (r === 0) return principal / tenureMonths; // zero-interest loan edge case
  const factor = Math.pow(1 + r, tenureMonths);
  return (principal * r * factor) / (factor - 1);
}

// Daily reducing balance — interest computed day by day
function dailyAccruedInterest(outstandingPrincipal: number, annualRate: number, days: number): number {
  const dailyRate = annualRate / 365 / 100;
  return outstandingPrincipal * dailyRate * days;
}
```

## Section 2: EMI Calculation In Depth

**Q6. How do you implement an amortization schedule generator with correct rounding in TypeScript?**
A: Floating-point arithmetic is the hidden enemy of financial calculations. `0.1 + 0.2 === 0.30000000000000004` in JavaScript. A production amortization generator must handle rounding explicitly and adjust the final instalment to eliminate accumulated rounding residuals.

```typescript
// lending/emi/amortization.service.ts

export interface AmortizationRow {
  instalmentNo: number;
  dueDate: Date;
  openingBalance: number;
  emiAmount: number;
  interestComponent: number;
  principalComponent: number;
  closingBalance: number;
}

export interface AmortizationSchedule {
  loanId: string;
  principal: number;
  annualInterestRate: number;
  tenureMonths: number;
  emiAmount: number;
  totalInterest: number;
  totalPayment: number;
  rows: AmortizationRow[];
}

export class AmortizationService {
  /**
   * Rounds a number to 2 decimal places using "round half away from zero"
   * — the standard for financial calculations (not banker's rounding).
   */
  private round(value: number): number {
    return Math.round((value + Number.EPSILON) * 100) / 100;
  }

  calculateEmi(principal: number, annualRate: number, tenureMonths: number): number {
    if (annualRate === 0) return this.round(principal / tenureMonths);
    const r = annualRate / 12 / 100;
    const factor = Math.pow(1 + r, tenureMonths);
    return this.round((principal * r * factor) / (factor - 1));
  }

  generateSchedule(
    loanId: string,
    principal: number,
    annualRate: number,
    tenureMonths: number,
    firstDueDate: Date,
  ): AmortizationSchedule {
    const emi = this.calculateEmi(principal, annualRate, tenureMonths);
    const r = annualRate / 12 / 100;
    const rows: AmortizationRow[] = [];
    let balance = principal;

    for (let i = 1; i <= tenureMonths; i++) {
      const openingBalance = this.round(balance);
      const interest = this.round(openingBalance * r);

      let principal_comp: number;
      let closingBalance: number;

      if (i === tenureMonths) {
        // Final instalment — absorb any rounding residual
        // This ensures closing balance is exactly 0, not ±₹0.01
        principal_comp = openingBalance;
        closingBalance = 0;
      } else {
        principal_comp = this.round(emi - interest);
        closingBalance = this.round(openingBalance - principal_comp);
      }

      const dueDate = new Date(firstDueDate);
      dueDate.setMonth(dueDate.getMonth() + (i - 1));

      rows.push({
        instalmentNo: i,
        dueDate,
        openingBalance,
        emiAmount: i === tenureMonths ? this.round(openingBalance + interest) : emi,
        interestComponent: interest,
        principalComponent: principal_comp,
        closingBalance,
      });

      balance = closingBalance;
    }

    const totalPayment = this.round(rows.reduce((sum, r) => sum + r.emiAmount, 0));
    const totalInterest = this.round(totalPayment - principal);

    return { loanId, principal, annualInterestRate: annualRate, tenureMonths, emiAmount: emi, totalInterest, totalPayment, rows };
  }
}
```

**Key rounding decisions:**
- Round each component independently (interest, then principal = EMI − interest). Never compute principal first and derive interest — this reverses the error accumulation.
- The final EMI is computed as `openingBalance + interest` for the last period, not the fixed EMI amount. This absorbs all accumulated rounding errors in one place, ensuring the schedule closes to zero.
- Use `Number.EPSILON` guard in the round function to handle floating-point representation artifacts.

**Q7. How does the interest-to-principal ratio shift across the loan tenure, and what is the engineering significance of this?**
A: For a ₹5,00,000 loan at 12% per annum for 24 months (EMI ≈ ₹23,537):

| Instalment | Opening Balance | Interest | Principal | Closing Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₹5,00,000 | ₹5,000 | ₹18,537 | ₹4,81,463 |
| 6 | ₹4,05,234 | ₹4,052 | ₹19,485 | ₹3,85,749 |
| 12 | ₹2,95,618 | ₹2,956 | ₹20,581 | ₹2,75,037 |
| 18 | ₹1,78,245 | ₹1,782 | ₹21,755 | ₹1,56,490 |
| 24 | ₹23,303 | ₹233 | ₹23,303 | ₹0 |

The interest component drops from ₹5,000 (21.3% of EMI) in month 1 to ₹233 (1% of EMI) in month 24. The principal component correspondingly rises.

**Engineering significance:**

1. **Prepayment savings are front-loaded** — if a borrower prepays ₹1,00,000 in month 3, they eliminate the interest that would have accrued on that ₹1,00,000 for the remaining 21 months. The same prepayment in month 20 saves far less. The system must compute prepayment savings accurately for borrower-facing "prepayment benefit calculator" features.

2. **Foreclosure (full prepayment) calculations** — the outstanding principal at any point in time equals the sum of the `opening_balance` of all remaining instalments, or equivalently, the present value of all remaining EMIs discounted at the loan rate. The system must compute this on demand for foreclosure requests.

3. **NPA (Non-Performing Asset) classification** — the overdue principal (not the overdue EMI) is what RBI uses for NPA aging. If an EMI is unpaid, the overdue amount grows as each subsequent period's principal component goes unpaid. Systems must track the principal portion of each overdue EMI separately from the interest portion for correct NPA reporting.

4. **GST on interest** — processing fees attract GST, but interest itself does not. The system must store interest and principal components separately; a system that stores only the EMI amount cannot generate correct tax documents.

**Q8. How do you handle edge cases in EMI date generation — month-end dates, weekends, and holidays?**
A: EMI date generation is more operationally complex than it appears:

**Month-end problem:**
If the first EMI date is January 31, what is the second EMI date? February has 28/29 days — February 31 does not exist.

```typescript
// WRONG — naive month addition
const nextDue = new Date(currentDue);
nextDue.setMonth(nextDue.getMonth() + 1);
// If currentDue is Jan 31 → nextDue becomes Mar 2/3 (JS clamps to next valid date)

// CORRECT — preserve the intended day-of-month using the loan's anchor day
function addMonthsPreservingDay(date: Date, months: number, anchorDay: number): Date {
  const result = new Date(date);
  result.setMonth(result.getMonth() + months);
  // If the month doesn't have the anchor day, use the last day of that month
  const lastDayOfMonth = new Date(result.getFullYear(), result.getMonth() + 1, 0).getDate();
  result.setDate(Math.min(anchorDay, lastDayOfMonth));
  return result;
}

// Loan disbursed Jan 15, anchor day = 5 (EMI due on 5th of each month)
// Month 1: Feb 5, Month 2: Mar 5, Month 3: Apr 5 — consistent
// Anchor day = 31: Jan 31, Feb 28 (not March), Mar 31, Apr 30, May 31
```

**Weekend and holiday shifting:**
Many lenders specify that if an EMI due date falls on a Sunday or public holiday, the due date shifts to the next working day — but the interest calculation remains based on the original due date (the borrower is not penalized for a non-working day).

```typescript
const NATIONAL_HOLIDAYS_2026 = new Set([
  '2026-01-26', // Republic Day
  '2026-08-15', // Independence Day
  '2026-10-02', // Gandhi Jayanti
  '2026-11-01', // Diwali (varies by state — use RBI holiday calendar)
  // ...
]);

function getNextWorkingDay(date: Date): Date {
  let d = new Date(date);
  while (true) {
    const day = d.getDay();
    const iso = d.toISOString().split('T')[0];
    if (day !== 0 && day !== 6 && !NATIONAL_HOLIDAYS_2026.has(iso)) {
      return d; // working day
    }
    d.setDate(d.getDate() + 1);
  }
}

function generateEmiDates(
  anchorDay: number,
  startDate: Date,
  tenureMonths: number,
  shiftForHolidays: boolean,
): EmiDate[] {
  return Array.from({ length: tenureMonths }, (_, i) => {
    const scheduledDate = addMonthsPreservingDay(startDate, i, anchorDay);
    const presentationDate = shiftForHolidays
      ? getNextWorkingDay(scheduledDate)
      : scheduledDate;
    return {
      instalmentNo: i + 1,
      scheduledDate,        // for interest calculation — never shifts
      presentationDate,     // for NACH presentation / actual debit
    };
  });
}
```

**Q9. How do you calculate the outstanding principal at an arbitrary point in time — for prepayment or foreclosure?**
A: The outstanding principal at any point is the present value of all remaining EMIs, discounted at the loan's monthly rate. This is mathematically equivalent to the `opening_balance` of the next due instalment in the amortization schedule.

**Method 1 — Schedule lookup (preferred for stored schedules):**
```typescript
async function getOutstandingPrincipal(
  loanId: string,
  asOfDate: Date,
): Promise<number> {
  // Find the next unpaid instalment as of asOfDate
  const nextDueInstalment = await emiRepo.findNextUnpaid(loanId, asOfDate);
  if (!nextDueInstalment) return 0; // fully repaid

  // The opening balance of the next instalment IS the outstanding principal
  return nextDueInstalment.openingBalance;
}
```

**Method 2 — Formula-based (for dynamic computation or verification):**
```typescript
function outstandingPrincipalByFormula(
  originalPrincipal: number,
  annualRate: number,
  tenureMonths: number,
  paidInstalments: number,
): number {
  if (annualRate === 0) {
    return originalPrincipal * (1 - paidInstalments / tenureMonths);
  }
  const r = annualRate / 12 / 100;
  const remainingInstalments = tenureMonths - paidInstalments;
  const emi = calculateEmi(originalPrincipal, annualRate, tenureMonths);

  // PV of remaining EMI stream
  return (emi / r) * (1 - Math.pow(1 + r, -remainingInstalments));
}
```

**Foreclosure amount includes:**
- Outstanding principal
- Interest accrued from last EMI date to foreclosure date (pre-closure interest)
- Foreclosure charges (lenders may charge up to 2% on floating rate loans; RBI prohibits foreclosure charges on floating rate individual loans under certain categories)
- Less: any advance payments or credits on the loan account

```typescript
async function calculateForeclosureAmount(
  loanId: string,
  foreclosureDate: Date,
): Promise<ForeclosureBreakdown> {
  const loan = await loanRepo.findById(loanId);
  const lastEmiDate = await emiRepo.findLastPaidDate(loanId);
  const outstanding = await getOutstandingPrincipal(loanId, foreclosureDate);

  // Interest from last EMI date to foreclosure date
  const daysSinceLastEmi = dateDiffDays(lastEmiDate, foreclosureDate);
  const preClosureInterest = round(outstanding * (loan.annualRate / 365 / 100) * daysSinceLastEmi);

  // Foreclosure charge — only if applicable (check loan type and RBI rules)
  const foreclosureCharge = loan.isFloatingRate && loan.borrowerType === 'individual'
    ? 0  // RBI prohibits foreclosure charge on floating rate individual loans
    : round(outstanding * loan.foreclosureChargePct / 100);

  const gstOnCharge = round(foreclosureCharge * 0.18);

  return {
    outstandingPrincipal: outstanding,
    preClosureInterest,
    foreclosureCharge,
    gstOnCharge,
    totalPayable: round(outstanding + preClosureInterest + foreclosureCharge + gstOnCharge),
  };
}
```

## Section 3: Prepayment and Restructuring In Depth

**Q10. What are the two standard ways to handle a prepayment on a reducing balance loan?**
A: When a borrower makes a partial prepayment (not a full foreclosure), the lender has two options for applying it — and the borrower should typically get to choose:

**Option 1 — Reduce EMI, keep tenure constant:**
The outstanding principal is reduced. A new, lower EMI is calculated for the remaining tenure at the same interest rate. The borrower benefits from lower monthly outflow.

**Option 2 — Reduce tenure, keep EMI constant:**
The outstanding principal is reduced. The same EMI continues, but it now pays off the loan faster — resulting in fewer remaining instalments. The borrower benefits from less total interest paid (since the loan ends sooner).

**Which saves more interest?** Option 2 (reduce tenure) always saves more total interest because the reduced principal is paid off faster, reducing the interest-accruing period more aggressively.

```typescript
// lending/prepayment/prepayment.service.ts
export interface PrepaymentResult {
  prepaidAmount: number;
  newOutstandingPrincipal: number;
  option: 'reduce_emi' | 'reduce_tenure';
  newEmiAmount?: number;           // for reduce_emi option
  newRemainingInstalments?: number; // for reduce_tenure option
  interestSaved: number;
  newSchedule: AmortizationRow[];
}

async function applyPrepayment(
  loanId: string,
  prepaidAmount: number,
  option: 'reduce_emi' | 'reduce_tenure',
  prepaymentDate: Date,
): Promise<PrepaymentResult> {
  const loan = await loanRepo.findById(loanId);
  const currentOutstanding = await getOutstandingPrincipal(loanId, prepaymentDate);

  if (prepaidAmount >= currentOutstanding) {
    throw new PrepaymentExceedsOutstandingError(
      `Prepayment ₹${prepaidAmount} exceeds outstanding ₹${currentOutstanding}. Use foreclosure instead.`,
    );
  }

  const newPrincipal = round(currentOutstanding - prepaidAmount);
  const paidInstalments = await emiRepo.countPaid(loanId);
  const remainingInstalments = loan.tenureMonths - paidInstalments;
  const nextDueDate = await emiRepo.findNextUnpaidDate(loanId);

  // Original remaining interest (before prepayment)
  const originalRemainingInterest = computeRemainingInterest(
    currentOutstanding, loan.annualRate, remainingInstalments, loan.emiAmount,
  );

  let result: Partial<PrepaymentResult>;

  if (option === 'reduce_emi') {
    // Same tenure — new lower EMI
    const newEmi = calculateEmi(newPrincipal, loan.annualRate, remainingInstalments);
    const newRemainingInterest = computeRemainingInterest(
      newPrincipal, loan.annualRate, remainingInstalments, newEmi,
    );
    result = {
      newEmiAmount: newEmi,
      interestSaved: round(originalRemainingInterest - newRemainingInterest),
      newSchedule: generateSchedule(
        loanId, newPrincipal, loan.annualRate, remainingInstalments, nextDueDate,
      ).rows,
    };

  } else {
    // Same EMI — fewer instalments
    const newTenure = computeNewTenure(newPrincipal, loan.annualRate, loan.emiAmount);
    const newRemainingInterest = computeRemainingInterest(
      newPrincipal, loan.annualRate, newTenure, loan.emiAmount,
    );
    result = {
      newRemainingInstalments: newTenure,
      interestSaved: round(originalRemainingInterest - newRemainingInterest),
      newSchedule: generateSchedule(
        loanId, newPrincipal, loan.annualRate, newTenure, nextDueDate,
      ).rows,
    };
  }

  // Persist: update loan record, mark old schedule superseded, store new schedule
  await loanRepo.recordPrepayment(loanId, {
    prepaidAmount,
    newPrincipal,
    option,
    prepaymentDate,
    ...result,
  });

  return { prepaidAmount, newOutstandingPrincipal: newPrincipal, option, ...result } as PrepaymentResult;
}

function computeNewTenure(
  principal: number,
  annualRate: number,
  emiAmount: number,
): number {
  // Solve for n in EMI formula: n = -log(1 - P*r/EMI) / log(1+r)
  const r = annualRate / 12 / 100;
  if (r === 0) return Math.ceil(principal / emiAmount);
  const ratio = (emiAmount / (principal * r));
  if (ratio <= 1) throw new EmiTooLowError('EMI is too low to cover even the interest. Loan cannot be repaid.');
  return Math.ceil(-Math.log(1 - 1 / ratio) / Math.log(1 + r));
}

function computeRemainingInterest(
  principal: number,
  annualRate: number,
  remainingMonths: number,
  emi: number,
): number {
  return round(emi * remainingMonths - principal);
}
```

**Q11. How do you handle loan restructuring — interest rate change or tenure extension — on an existing loan?**
A: Loan restructuring arises from two scenarios: a borrower in financial distress (RBI's restructuring framework applies) or a benign repricing event (floating rate loan where the benchmark rate changes).

**Floating rate repricing (routine):**
When the benchmark rate (e.g., EBLR — External Benchmark Lending Rate, which is repo rate-linked under RBI's October 2019 mandate) changes, the loan's interest rate changes on the reset date. The lender has two options per RBI guidelines:
1. Adjust EMI, keep tenure.
2. Adjust tenure (up or down), keep EMI.

```typescript
async function repriceFloatingRateLoan(
  loanId: string,
  newAnnualRate: number,
  repriceDate: Date,
  option: 'adjust_emi' | 'adjust_tenure',
): Promise<void> {
  const loan = await loanRepo.findById(loanId);
  const outstanding = await getOutstandingPrincipal(loanId, repriceDate);
  const paidInstalments = await emiRepo.countPaid(loanId);
  const remainingInstalments = loan.tenureMonths - paidInstalments;
  const nextDueDate = await emiRepo.findNextUnpaidDate(loanId);

  // Mark old schedule as superseded — audit trail
  await scheduleRepo.supersede(loanId, repriceDate, {
    reason: 'rate_reset',
    oldRate: loan.annualRate,
    newRate: newAnnualRate,
  });

  if (option === 'adjust_emi') {
    const newEmi = calculateEmi(outstanding, newAnnualRate, remainingInstalments);
    const newSchedule = generateSchedule(loanId, outstanding, newAnnualRate, remainingInstalments, nextDueDate);
    await scheduleRepo.save(newSchedule);
    await loanRepo.update(loanId, { annualRate: newAnnualRate, emiAmount: newEmi });

  } else {
    // Keep EMI — compute new tenure
    const newTenure = computeNewTenure(outstanding, newAnnualRate, loan.emiAmount);
    const newSchedule = generateSchedule(loanId, outstanding, newAnnualRate, newTenure, nextDueDate);
    await scheduleRepo.save(newSchedule);
    await loanRepo.update(loanId, { annualRate: newAnnualRate, remainingTenure: newTenure });
  }

  // RBI requires notifying borrower of rate change and its impact within 30 days
  await notificationService.sendRateChangeNotice(loanId, {
    oldRate: loan.annualRate,
    newRate: newAnnualRate,
    effectiveDate: repriceDate,
    option,
  });
}
```

**RBI restructuring (distress scenario):**
Under RBI's prudential framework, a restructured loan must be classified as a Special Mention Account (SMA) or NPA and provisioned accordingly. The restructuring must be documented with a resolution plan, and the borrower must receive a revised repayment schedule. The system must:
- Flag the loan as restructured in the regulatory reporting module.
- Store the original schedule and the restructured schedule separately (cannot overwrite — audit requirement).
- Trigger provisioning calculation updates in the ALM (Asset Liability Management) module.

**Q12. What is a moratorium, and how does it affect the amortization schedule?**
A: A moratorium is a period — typically 1 to 6 months — during which the borrower is not required to pay EMIs. It is granted for various reasons: COVID-19 relief (RBI's March 2020 moratorium), natural disaster relief, or an agreed-upon holiday period at loan origination (e.g., a 6-month moratorium on a home loan before the first EMI).

**Critical distinction:** a moratorium is not an interest waiver. During the moratorium period, interest continues to accrue on the outstanding principal. This accrued interest is typically:
- **Capitalised (added to principal)** — the outstanding principal increases by the accrued interest; a new, higher EMI is computed for the post-moratorium tenure. This is compound interest on the deferred amount.
- **Spread as additional EMIs** — the original EMI stays the same, but additional months are added to the tenure to recover the accrued interest.

```typescript
function applyMoratorium(
  principal: number,
  annualRate: number,
  originalTenure: number,
  moratoriumMonths: number,
  originalFirstDueDate: Date,
  interestTreatment: 'capitalise' | 'extend_tenure',
): AmortizationSchedule {
  const r = annualRate / 12 / 100;

  // Interest accrued during moratorium (simple interest on original principal)
  const moratoriumInterest = round(principal * r * moratoriumMonths);

  let newPrincipal: number;
  let newTenure: number;
  const newFirstDueDate = addMonths(originalFirstDueDate, moratoriumMonths);

  if (interestTreatment === 'capitalise') {
    // Principal swells by accrued interest — higher EMI, same tenure
    newPrincipal = round(principal + moratoriumInterest);
    newTenure = originalTenure;
  } else {
    // Principal unchanged — same EMI, longer tenure
    newPrincipal = principal;
    const originalEmi = calculateEmi(principal, annualRate, originalTenure);
    // Additional instalments needed to recover moratorium interest at the same EMI
    const additionalInstalments = Math.ceil(moratoriumInterest / originalEmi);
    newTenure = originalTenure + additionalInstalments;
  }

  return generateSchedule('', newPrincipal, annualRate, newTenure, newFirstDueDate);
}
```

**Regulatory note:** RBI's COVID-19 moratorium (2020) required that accrued interest during the moratorium not be compounded — i.e., compound interest on the deferred interest portion was prohibited. The system had to implement a specific calculation that treated the moratorium interest as simple interest, then spread it across remaining instalments without further compounding. This is an example of regulation directly overriding the standard mathematical approach.

## Section 4: NPA, Overdue Tracking, and Penal Interest

**Q13. How do you track overdue amounts on a loan with a reducing balance schedule, and how does this map to NPA classification?**
A: Overdue tracking on a reducing balance loan must distinguish between the **overdue principal** and **overdue interest** components — they have different implications for NPA classification and provisioning.

```typescript
// lending/overdue/overdue-tracker.service.ts
export interface OverdueBreakdown {
  loanId: string;
  asOfDate: Date;
  overdueInstalments: OverdueInstalment[];
  totalOverduePrincipal: number;
  totalOverdueInterest: number;
  totalOverduePenalInterest: number;
  totalOverdueAmount: number;
  dpd: number;  // Days Past Due — key metric for NPA classification
  smaCategoryAsOf: 'standard' | 'SMA-0' | 'SMA-1' | 'SMA-2' | 'NPA';
}

export interface OverdueInstalment {
  instalmentNo: number;
  dueDate: Date;
  overdueDays: number;
  overdueEmiAmount: number;
  overduePrincipal: number;
  overdueInterest: number;
  penalInterest: number;
}

@Injectable()
export class OverdueTrackerService {
  async getOverdueBreakdown(
    loanId: string,
    asOfDate: Date = new Date(),
  ): Promise<OverdueBreakdown> {
    const unpaidEmis = await this.emiRepo.findUnpaid(loanId, { dueDate_lte: asOfDate });

    const overdueInstalments: OverdueInstalment[] = unpaidEmis.map(emi => {
      const overdueDays = dateDiffDays(emi.dueDate, asOfDate);
      const penalInterest = this.calculatePenalInterest(
        emi.principalComponent,
        overdueDays,
        emi.penalRate,  // typically 2-3% p.a. over normal rate, per RBI guidelines
      );

      return {
        instalmentNo: emi.instalmentNo,
        dueDate: emi.dueDate,
        overdueDays,
        overdueEmiAmount: emi.emiAmount,
        overduePrincipal: emi.principalComponent,
        overdueInterest: emi.interestComponent,
        penalInterest,
      };
    });

    const dpd = overdueInstalments.length > 0
      ? Math.max(...overdueInstalments.map(i => i.overdueDays))
      : 0;

    return {
      loanId,
      asOfDate,
      overdueInstalments,
      totalOverduePrincipal: round(sum(overdueInstalments.map(i => i.overduePrincipal))),
      totalOverdueInterest: round(sum(overdueInstalments.map(i => i.overdueInterest))),
      totalOverduePenalInterest: round(sum(overdueInstalments.map(i => i.penalInterest))),
      totalOverdueAmount: round(sum(overdueInstalments.map(i =>
        i.overdueEmiAmount + i.penalInterest,
      ))),
      dpd,
      smaCategoryAsOf: this.classifySma(dpd),
    };
  }

  private classifySma(dpd: number): OverdueBreakdown['smaCategoryAsOf'] {
    // RBI's SMA/NPA classification framework (November 2021 circular)
    if (dpd === 0) return 'standard';
    if (dpd >= 1 && dpd <= 30) return 'SMA-0';
    if (dpd >= 31 && dpd <= 60) return 'SMA-1';
    if (dpd >= 61 && dpd <= 90) return 'SMA-2';
    return 'NPA'; // dpd > 90
  }

  private calculatePenalInterest(
    overduePrincipal: number,
    days: number,
    annualPenalRate: number, // e.g., 2 (for 2% p.a. penal)
  ): number {
    // RBI October 2023 circular: penal charges must be on overdue principal only,
    // not on the full outstanding. Cannot be compounded.
    return round(overduePrincipal * (annualPenalRate / 365 / 100) * days);
  }
}
```

**RBI NPA classification rules (as per November 2021 and subsequent circulars):**
- **Standard** — DPD = 0 (no overdue)
- **SMA-0** — DPD 1–30
- **SMA-1** — DPD 31–60
- **SMA-2** — DPD 61–90
- **NPA (Sub-standard)** — DPD > 90

**Penal charges (RBI October 2023 circular):** Penal charges must be levied as a flat amount or as a rate on the overdue principal only — not on the entire outstanding loan balance. Penal charges cannot be compounded (i.e., charged on previously unpaid penal charges). The system must enforce this.

**Q14. How do you compute the total interest cost and APR for disclosure in the Key Facts Statement?**
A: The KFS (mandated from October 2024 for all retail loans) requires the lender to disclose:

```typescript
export interface KeyFactsStatement {
  loanAmount: number;
  disbursedAmount: number;           // after processing fee deduction
  annualInterestRate: number;        // the stated rate
  apr: number;                       // effective rate including all charges
  emiAmount: number;
  tenureMonths: number;
  firstEmiDate: Date;
  lastEmiDate: Date;
  totalInterestPayable: number;
  processingFee: number;
  gstOnProcessingFee: number;
  otherCharges: OtherCharge[];       // insurance, documentation, etc.
  totalCostOfCredit: number;         // total outflow - principal
  foreclosureCharges: string;        // textual disclosure per RBI
}

function computeApr(
  disbursedAmount: number,       // actual amount received by borrower (after fee deduction)
  emiAmount: number,
  tenureMonths: number,
  otherUpfrontCharges: number,   // processing fee + GST + insurance premium
): number {
  // APR is the IRR (Internal Rate of Return) of the cash flow stream
  // Cash flows: +disbursedAmount at t=0, -emiAmount at t=1..n, -otherCharges at t=0
  // Net outflow to borrower at t=0: disbursedAmount - otherUpfrontCharges
  const netDisbursed = disbursedAmount - otherUpfrontCharges;

  // Solve for monthly rate r such that NPV = 0:
  // netDisbursed = EMI/(1+r)^1 + EMI/(1+r)^2 + ... + EMI/(1+r)^n
  // Use Newton-Raphson iteration

  let r = 0.01; // initial guess: 1% per month
  for (let iteration = 0; iteration < 1000; iteration++) {
    const factor = Math.pow(1 + r, tenureMonths);
    const pv = (emiAmount * (factor - 1)) / (r * factor);
    const f = pv - netDisbursed;

    // Derivative of f with respect to r (numerical differentiation)
    const dr = 0.0001;
    const factorDr = Math.pow(1 + r + dr, tenureMonths);
    const pvDr = (emiAmount * (factorDr - 1)) / ((r + dr) * factorDr);
    const fPrime = (pvDr - pv) / dr;

    const rNew = r - f / fPrime;
    if (Math.abs(rNew - r) < 1e-10) {
      r = rNew;
      break;
    }
    r = rNew;
  }

  // Monthly rate → annualised APR
  const annualApr = r * 12 * 100;
  return round(annualApr * 100) / 100; // round to 2 decimal places
}

// Example:
// Loan: ₹1,00,000 | Rate: 18% p.a. | Tenure: 12 months
// EMI: ₹9,168 | Processing fee: ₹2,000 + GST ₹360 = ₹2,360
// Disbursed to borrower: ₹97,640 (₹1,00,000 - ₹2,360)
// APR: ~22.8% (higher than 18% because of fee deducted upfront)
```

The APR is almost always higher than the stated interest rate because it factors in upfront fees that the borrower pays but that reduce the actual funds received. RBI mandates that the APR be disclosed prominently so that a borrower comparing a 18% loan with ₹2,000 processing fee against a 19% loan with zero processing fee can make an informed choice.

## Section 5: Scenario / System Design Style Questions

**Q15. "Design a loan EMI management system for an NBFC that handles 50,000 active loans. What are the key architectural decisions?"**
A: A production EMI management system at 50K loans scale requires careful separation of concerns across schedule generation, collection, overdue tracking, and reporting.

```
Loan Origination Service
    │  loan sanctioned and disbursed
    ▼
Schedule Generation Service (synchronous — at disbursement)
    │  generates amortization schedule
    │  stores all N rows in emi_schedule table
    │  generates virtual account for collection
    ▼
emi_schedule table (N rows per loan — e.g., 24 rows for a 2-year loan)
    │
    ├── Collection Service (daily cron — presents NACH debits for due EMIs)
    │       │  T-1: present NACH debit for next day's EMIs
    │       │  T+0: NACH result received (success/failure)
    │       │  On success: mark EMI paid, update loan ledger
    │       │  On failure: classify return code, initiate dunning/VA fallback
    │
    ├── Overdue Tracker (daily cron — runs EOD)
    │       │  scans for EMIs with dueDate < today and status = 'unpaid'
    │       │  computes DPD, SMA classification, penal interest
    │       │  writes to overdue_summary table (one row per loan per day)
    │       │  triggers NPA flag if DPD crosses 90
    │
    ├── Prepayment Service (on-demand)
    │       │  recomputes schedule on prepayment
    │       │  supersedes old schedule rows; inserts new rows
    │       │  stores prepayment event in loan_events audit log
    │
    └── Regulatory Reporting Service (monthly)
            │  NPA aging report (SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2, NPA counts and amounts)
            │  Provisioning calculation per IRACP norms
            │  KFS generation on demand
```

**Key architectural decisions:**

1. **Store the schedule, never recompute on the fly** — the amortization schedule is a legal document. It must be stored at disbursement and retrievable exactly as generated. Any recomputation (even with identical inputs) risks floating-point divergence. Store all rows in the DB; serve from DB, never regenerate.

2. **Immutable schedule rows + supersession pattern** — when a prepayment or restructuring changes the schedule, mark old rows as `superseded` with a `superseded_at` timestamp. Insert new rows. Never delete or overwrite. Audit requires the ability to answer "what was the schedule for loan X on date Y?"

3. **Separate EMI schedule from EMI collection** — the schedule table is the source of truth for what is owed. A separate `emi_collections` table records what was actually paid (NACH debit result, VA payment, cash). Reconciliation between schedule and collections is a daily job.

4. **Penal interest as a separate ledger line** — penal interest must never be mixed with contractual interest in the ledger. RBI's October 2023 circular prohibits compounding of penal charges; a mixed ledger makes it impossible to verify compliance.

5. **Precision — use NUMERIC, not FLOAT in PostgreSQL** — `FLOAT` is subject to floating-point errors. Use `NUMERIC(18,2)` for all monetary amounts. In the application layer, use a decimal library for intermediate calculations if needed.

```sql
-- Core schema
CREATE TABLE emi_schedule (
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  loan_id           UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES loans(id),
  schedule_version  INT NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,      -- increments on prepayment/restructure

  due_date          DATE NOT NULL,
  opening_balance   NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  emi_amount        NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  interest_component NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  principal_component NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  closing_balance   NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,

  superseded_at     TIMESTAMPTZ,                 -- null if current; set if superseded

CREATE INDEX idx_emi_due ON emi_schedule (due_date, status)
  WHERE status = 'pending' AND superseded_at IS NULL;

CREATE TABLE emi_collections (
  id                  UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  emi_schedule_id     UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES emi_schedule(id),
  loan_id             UUID NOT NULL,
  collected_amount    NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  principal_collected NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  interest_collected  NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL,
  penal_collected     NUMERIC(18,2) NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  collection_mode     VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL,      -- nach | va_transfer | cash | upi

CREATE TABLE loan_overdue_summary (
```

**Q16. "A borrower calls in claiming their EMI amount changed without notice. How do you investigate?"**
A: An unexplained EMI change is a sensitive customer complaint — it could be a product feature (floating rate repricing), a bug (schedule overwritten incorrectly), or a miscommunication (prepayment changed the schedule but the borrower wasn't notified).

**Step 1 — Retrieve the full schedule history for the loan**
```sql
-- Check if multiple schedule versions exist
SELECT schedule_version, COUNT(*) AS instalment_count,
       MIN(due_date) AS start_date, MAX(due_date) AS end_date,
       MIN(emi_amount) AS min_emi, MAX(emi_amount) AS max_emi,
       MIN(superseded_at) AS superseded_at
FROM emi_schedule
WHERE loan_id = '<loan_id>'
GROUP BY schedule_version
ORDER BY schedule_version;
```

If multiple versions exist — a schedule change occurred. Check the `loan_events` table for the trigger:

```sql
SELECT event_type, event_data, created_at, created_by
FROM loan_events
WHERE loan_id = '<loan_id>'
  AND event_type IN ('prepayment', 'rate_reset', 'restructuring', 'moratorium_applied')
ORDER BY created_at;
```

**Step 2 — Root cause identification by finding event**

| Finding | Root Cause | Correct Action |
|---|---|---|
| Rate reset event on correct reset date | Floating rate repricing — legitimate | Verify notification was sent; resend if not |
| Prepayment event — borrower chose 'reduce_emi' | Prepayment applied correctly | Confirm borrower made prepayment and chose this option |
| No event — schedule version changed | Bug — schedule overwritten without event | Rollback to original schedule; file P1 bug |
| Final EMI adjusted slightly | Rounding residual absorbed in last EMI — normal | Explain to borrower; this is correct behavior |
| Rate change without reset date event | Bug — rate changed without proper repricing flow | Audit; may require regulatory disclosure |

**Step 3 — Verify notification delivery**
```typescript
const notifications = await notificationRepo.findByLoanAndType(
  loanId,
  ['rate_change_notice', 'prepayment_confirmation', 'schedule_updated'],
);
// Check delivery status — was the notification sent? Was it delivered?
```

**Step 4 — Borrower communication**
If the change was legitimate: send a revised KFS document with the updated schedule, explain the trigger (rate reset or prepayment), and confirm the new EMI amount and effective date.

If the change was a bug: immediately restore the correct schedule, issue an apology communication, and if the incorrect EMI was already collected, process a refund for the overcharged amount.

**Q17. "How would you build a unit-testable EMI calculation module with financial-grade precision guarantees?"**
A: Financial calculation modules must be tested with extreme rigour because a ₹1 rounding error across 50,000 loans is ₹50,000 of unexplained variance — a regulatory finding.

```typescript
// lending/emi/amortization.service.spec.ts
describe('AmortizationService', () => {
  const svc = new AmortizationService();

  describe('calculateEmi', () => {
    it('computes EMI correctly for a standard loan', () => {
      // Known reference: ₹5,00,000 @ 12% p.a. for 24 months
      const emi = svc.calculateEmi(500000, 12, 24);
      expect(emi).toBe(23536.74); // verified against Excel PMT function
    });

    it('handles zero-interest loans without division by zero', () => {
      const emi = svc.calculateEmi(120000, 0, 12);
      expect(emi).toBe(10000); // simple division
    });

    it('handles single-instalment loans', () => {
      const emi = svc.calculateEmi(10000, 12, 1);
      expect(emi).toBe(10100); // principal + one month's interest
    });
  });

  describe('generateSchedule — invariants', () => {
    const schedule = svc.generateSchedule('L1', 500000, 12, 24, new Date('2026-02-05'));

    it('produces exactly n rows', () => {
      expect(schedule.rows.length).toBe(24);
    });

    it('closing balance of last row is exactly 0', () => {
      expect(schedule.rows[23].closingBalance).toBe(0);
    });

    it('each row: opening_balance = previous closing_balance', () => {
      for (let i = 1; i < schedule.rows.length; i++) {
        expect(schedule.rows[i].openingBalance)
          .toBe(schedule.rows[i - 1].closingBalance);
      }
    });

    it('each row: principal + interest = emi_amount (±₹0.01 for last row)', () => {
      for (const row of schedule.rows) {
        const diff = Math.abs(row.principalComponent + row.interestComponent - row.emiAmount);
        expect(diff).toBeLessThanOrEqual(0.01);
      }
    });

    it('interest component = opening_balance × monthly_rate (±₹0.01)', () => {
      const r = 12 / 12 / 100;
      for (const row of schedule.rows) {
        const expectedInterest = Math.round(row.openingBalance * r * 100) / 100;
        expect(Math.abs(row.interestComponent - expectedInterest)).toBeLessThanOrEqual(0.01);
      }
    });

    it('total interest = total payment − principal', () => {
      expect(schedule.totalInterest).toBe(
        schedule.rows.reduce((s, r) => s + r.emiAmount, 0) - 500000,
      );
    });

    it('total interest matches pre-computed reference', () => {
      // Reference computed in Excel: SUM of interest column
      expect(schedule.totalInterest).toBe(64881.76); // ±₹0.02 acceptable
    });
  });

  describe('edge cases', () => {
    it('handles month-end anchor day correctly — Jan 31 loan', () => {
      const schedule = svc.generateSchedule('L2', 100000, 12, 3, new Date('2026-01-31'));
      expect(schedule.rows[0].dueDate).toEqual(new Date('2026-01-31'));
      expect(schedule.rows[1].dueDate).toEqual(new Date('2026-02-28')); // Feb has no 31st
      expect(schedule.rows[2].dueDate).toEqual(new Date('2026-03-31'));
    });

    it('prepayment: reduce_tenure produces fewer instalments', () => {
      const original = svc.generateSchedule('L3', 500000, 12, 24, new Date('2026-02-05'));
      // After 6 paid instalments, prepay ₹1,00,000 — reduce tenure
      const outstanding = original.rows[6].openingBalance; // ≈ ₹3,85,700
      const newPrincipal = outstanding - 100000;
      const newSchedule = svc.generateSchedule('L3', newPrincipal, 12, 18, new Date('2026-08-05'));
      expect(newSchedule.rows.length).toBeLessThan(18); // fewer months needed
    });

    it('floating point: no row has a negative closing balance due to rounding', () => {
      const schedule = svc.generateSchedule('L4', 99999.99, 11.5, 36, new Date('2026-03-01'));
      for (const row of schedule.rows) {
        expect(row.closingBalance).toBeGreaterThanOrEqual(0);
      }
    });
  });
});
```

**Cross-validation approach for production:**
- Always cross-validate the EMI formula output against Excel's `=PMT(rate, nper, pv)` for the same inputs. Store these reference values in a regression test fixture.
- Run a nightly job that picks 100 random active loans, recomputes the outstanding principal using the formula method, and compares against the stored schedule's next opening balance. Alert if any diverge by more than ₹1.
- For regulatory submissions (NPA reports, ALM returns), have a second independent computation path (e.g., a Python script using the `numpy-financial` library) that validates the outputs of the primary TypeScript calculation.

## Quick Reference Table

| Concept | Formula / Definition | Engineering Implication |
|---|---|---|
| EMI | `P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1)` | Use `NUMERIC(18,2)` in DB; never `FLOAT` |
| Monthly rate `r` | Annual rate / 12 / 100 | Watch for zero-rate edge case |
| Outstanding principal | PV of remaining EMIs = `(EMI/r) × (1 − (1+r)^−remaining)` | Store in schedule; don't recompute dynamically |
| Interest component | `opening_balance × r` | Front-loaded; shrinks each month |
| Principal component | `EMI − interest_component` | Back-loaded; grows each month |
| Final EMI | `opening_balance + last_interest` | Absorbs all rounding residuals |
| APR | IRR of net cash flows including fees | Always ≥ stated rate; KFS disclosure mandatory |
| Prepayment — reduce EMI | New EMI = `calculateEmi(newPrincipal, rate, remainingMonths)` | Lower outflow; less interest saved |
| Prepayment — reduce tenure | New tenure = `−log(1 − 1/ratio) / log(1+r)` | More interest saved; same outflow |
| Moratorium — capitalise | New principal = original + accrued interest | Higher EMI post-moratorium |
| DPD | Days Past Due = days since earliest unpaid EMI due date | NPA at DPD > 90 per RBI norms |
| Penal interest | `overdue_principal × penal_rate / 365 × days` | On overdue principal only; not compoundable (RBI Oct 2023) |
| SMA-0 / 1 / 2 / NPA | DPD 1–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / >90 | Drives provisioning; regulatory reporting mandatory |

The senior differentiator is understanding that the amortization schedule is a **legally binding document** — not a computed view. It must be stored immutably, versioned on any change, and regenerable on demand without recomputation. In the Indian lending context, layer in the RBI mandates: KFS and APR disclosure requirements, the October 2023 penal interest circular (no compounding on penal), the EBLR-linked floating rate reset framework, and the SMA/NPA classification DPD thresholds. Engineers who know the regulation that drives the schema design are the ones who get the offer.
