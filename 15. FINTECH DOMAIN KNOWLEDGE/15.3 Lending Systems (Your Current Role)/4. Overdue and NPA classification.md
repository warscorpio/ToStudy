# Overdue and NPA Classification — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is an overdue loan, and how does it differ from a defaulted loan?**
A: An overdue loan is one where any scheduled repayment — principal, interest, or fee — has not been received by the due date. A default is a more severe legal classification, typically triggered after a defined overdue period, that activates enforcement rights under the loan agreement and applicable law.

The distinction matters for both engineering and regulatory reasons:

| Property | Overdue | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Payment not received by due date | Overdue beyond a contractual or regulatory threshold (e.g., 90 days) |
| **System action** | Penalty interest accrual; bounce notification; re-presentation | NPA classification; provisioning; collections escalation; legal notice |
| **Customer impact** | Late fee charged; credit bureau reporting of missed payment | Credit bureau NPA flag; enforcement proceedings possible |
| **Regulatory label** | No specific RBI label below 30 days | SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2 (30/60/90 days); NPA at 90 days |
| **Reversibility** | Cured by payment | NPA can be upgraded to standard only after full regularisation |

Every NPA was first overdue; not every overdue becomes an NPA. Your system must track the precise number of days past due (DPD) continuously from the first missed payment — this is the primary input to both the SMA/NPA classification pipeline and the credit bureau reporting feed.

**Q2. What is the RBI's NPA classification framework, and what are the exact DPD thresholds?**
A: The Reserve Bank of India defines asset quality classification for banks and NBFCs under the Prudential Norms on Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning (IRACP). The framework:

**Standard Asset** — all dues paid on time; no overdue.

**Special Mention Account (SMA)** — precursor to NPA; early warning category:

| Category | DPD threshold | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **SMA-0** | 1–30 days past due | Principal or interest overdue between 1 and 30 days |
| **SMA-1** | 31–60 days past due | Overdue between 31 and 60 days |
| **SMA-2** | 61–90 days past due | Overdue between 61 and 90 days |

**Non-Performing Asset (NPA)** — asset where interest or principal is overdue for more than 90 days:

| Sub-category | Criteria | Provisioning requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-Standard** | NPA for ≤ 12 months | 15% of outstanding (secured); 25% (unsecured) |
| **Doubtful-1** | Sub-Standard for 12–24 months | 25% (secured); 100% (unsecured) |
| **Doubtful-2** | Sub-Standard for 24–36 months | 40% (secured); 100% (unsecured) |
| **Doubtful-3** | Sub-Standard > 36 months | 100% |
| **Loss Asset** | Identified as uncollectable by auditor / RBI | 100%; should be written off |

**Critical nuance — the "90 days" rule is not calendar days:**
For term loans with monthly installments, the 90-day clock starts on the first day after the due date of the installment that was not paid. If EMI is due March 1 and not paid, the DPD count starts March 2. The account becomes NPA on June 1 (day 91).

For overdraft / cash credit accounts, the NPA trigger is different: the account is NPA if it remains "out of order" (outstanding balance continuously exceeds the sanctioned limit, or there are no credits to cover interest debited) for more than 90 days.

**Q3. What is DPD (Days Past Due), and how is it computed correctly?**
A: DPD is the number of calendar days elapsed since the earliest unpaid due date. It is the single most important number in the loan servicing system — it drives NPA classification, provisioning, credit bureau reporting, and collections prioritisation.

**Correct DPD computation:**

```
DPD = MAX(0, today - earliest_unpaid_due_date)
```

Key rules:
- DPD resets to 0 only when **all** overdues are cleared — not just the oldest one. A partial payment that clears EMI-1 but leaves EMI-2 unpaid keeps DPD running from EMI-2's due date.
- DPD is computed per loan account, not per installment. If three installments are overdue, DPD is measured from the oldest unpaid installment.
- Prepayment does not reduce DPD. A customer who paid EMI-6 early but missed EMI-3 still has DPD from EMI-3's due date.

```sql
-- DPD computation per loan account
SELECT
  l.id AS loan_id,
  l.customer_id,
  MIN(rs.due_date) AS earliest_unpaid_due_date,
  CURRENT_DATE - MIN(rs.due_date) AS dpd,
  SUM(rs.total_emi) - COALESCE(SUM(p.amount_paid), 0) AS overdue_amount
FROM loans l
JOIN loan_repayment_schedule rs
  ON rs.loan_id = l.id
  AND rs.status IN ('PENDING', 'PARTIAL')
  AND rs.due_date < CURRENT_DATE       -- due dates in the past that are unpaid
LEFT JOIN emi_payments p ON p.schedule_id = rs.id
WHERE l.status = 'ACTIVE'
GROUP BY l.id, l.customer_id
HAVING MIN(rs.due_date) IS NOT NULL;   -- only loans with at least one unpaid past-due installment
```

**The partial payment edge case:**
A customer pays ₹4,000 against an EMI of ₹5,000. This is a partial payment — DPD does NOT reset to 0. The unpaid ₹1,000 keeps the installment overdue. DPD counts from the due date of that installment.

```sql
CREATE TABLE loan_repayment_schedule (
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  loan_id         UUID NOT NULL,
  emi_number      INT NOT NULL,
  due_date        DATE NOT NULL,
  principal       BIGINT NOT NULL,    -- paise
  interest        BIGINT NOT NULL,
  total_emi       BIGINT NOT NULL,
  amount_paid     BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  status          TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PENDING',
  -- PENDING, PARTIAL, PAID, WAIVED, WRITTEN_OFF
  paid_at         TIMESTAMPTZ,
  overdue_since   DATE GENERATED ALWAYS AS (
    CASE WHEN status IN ('PENDING', 'PARTIAL') AND due_date < CURRENT_DATE
         THEN due_date ELSE NULL END
  ) STORED
);
```

**Q4. What is the difference between DPD-based NPA and the "out of order" NPA for revolving credit?**
A: The 90-day DPD rule applies to term loans (where scheduled installments exist). For revolving credit facilities — overdraft (OD), cash credit (CC), working capital demand loan (WCDL) — a different trigger applies because there is no fixed installment schedule.

**Out of order definition (RBI):**
A CC/OD account is "out of order" if:
- The outstanding balance exceeds the sanctioned limit for more than 90 consecutive days, OR
- Credits in the account during the preceding 90 days are less than the interest debited during that period.

The second condition catches accounts where the customer is only covering interest by drawing down the facility — technically "current" on interest but in practice not reducing the principal.

```sql
-- Out-of-order detection for CC/OD accounts
CREATE VIEW cc_od_out_of_order AS
WITH daily_balance AS (
  SELECT
    account_id,
    balance_date,
    outstanding_balance,
    sanctioned_limit,
    outstanding_balance > sanctioned_limit AS is_over_limit
  FROM cc_od_daily_snapshots
),
consecutive_over_limit AS (
  SELECT
    account_id,
    MIN(balance_date) AS out_of_order_since,
    CURRENT_DATE - MIN(balance_date) AS consecutive_days
  FROM (
    SELECT
      account_id,
      balance_date,
      is_over_limit,
      balance_date - ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
        PARTITION BY account_id, is_over_limit ORDER BY balance_date
      ) * INTERVAL '1 day' AS grp
    FROM daily_balance
    WHERE is_over_limit
  ) grouped
  GROUP BY account_id, grp
  HAVING CURRENT_DATE - MIN(balance_date) >= 1
)
SELECT
  account_id,
  out_of_order_since,
  consecutive_days,
  consecutive_days >= 90 AS is_npa
FROM consecutive_over_limit;
```

**Q5. What is the "date of NPA" and why does it matter for provisioning and reporting?**
A: The date of NPA is the specific calendar date on which the account crossed the 90-day overdue threshold — the first date on which DPD = 91. This date is immutable once set.

Why it matters:
- **Provisioning start**: the provisioning requirement (15% of outstanding for sub-standard) begins accruing from the date of NPA. Backdating or forward-dating the NPA date changes the provisioning amount and the reported NPA ratio — a material accounting misstatement.
- **Income recognition**: from the date of NPA, interest income on that loan can no longer be recognised on an accrual basis. Any interest already accrued but uncollected must be reversed. This is the "stop accrual" rule.
- **CRILC reporting**: banks and NBFCs must report the date of NPA to RBI's CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits) for borrowers with aggregate exposure ≥ ₹5 crore. CRILC cross-references lenders — if one lender reports NPA, RBI can see the borrower's total credit.
- **Upgradation condition**: the date of NPA anchors the sub-standard / doubtful aging. An account can only be upgraded from NPA to standard after it has been regular for at least 12 months after the date of regularisation — not the date of NPA.

```sql
ALTER TABLE loans ADD COLUMN npa_date DATE;
ALTER TABLE loans ADD COLUMN npa_sub_category TEXT;
-- 'SUB_STANDARD', 'DOUBTFUL_1', 'DOUBTFUL_2', 'DOUBTFUL_3', 'LOSS'

-- Set NPA date — must be the actual 91st DPD day, not the processing date
UPDATE loans SET
  npa_date = earliest_unpaid_due_date + INTERVAL '90 days',
  npa_sub_category = 'SUB_STANDARD',
  status = 'NPA'
WHERE id = '<loan_id>'
  AND npa_date IS NULL;  -- idempotent; never overwrite an existing NPA date
```

## Section 2: Classification Pipeline

**Q6. How do you design a daily NPA classification pipeline that is correct, idempotent, and auditable?**
A: The pipeline must run once per business day, classify every active loan account, and record every classification change with an immutable audit trail.

**Pipeline schema:**

```sql
-- Snapshot of every loan's classification on every date
CREATE TABLE loan_classification_history (
  id                  UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  loan_id             UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES loans(id),
  classification_date DATE NOT NULL,
  dpd                 INT NOT NULL,
  sma_category        TEXT,               -- 'SMA_0', 'SMA_1', 'SMA_2', NULL
  asset_class         TEXT NOT NULL,      -- 'STANDARD', 'NPA'
  npa_sub_category    TEXT,               -- 'SUB_STANDARD', 'DOUBTFUL_1', etc.
  overdue_amount      BIGINT NOT NULL,    -- total overdue in paise
  outstanding_balance BIGINT NOT NULL,    -- total loan outstanding in paise
  provision_rate      NUMERIC(5,4),       -- e.g., 0.1500 for 15%
  provision_amount    BIGINT,             -- outstanding × provision_rate
  previous_asset_class TEXT,             -- for detecting upgrades/downgrades
  changed             BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,  -- true if class changed from yesterday
  PRIMARY KEY (loan_id, classification_date)  -- one row per loan per day
);

-- Pipeline run tracking
CREATE TABLE classification_pipeline_runs (
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  run_date        DATE NOT NULL UNIQUE,
  status          TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'RUNNING',
  loans_processed INT,
  new_npas        INT,
  upgrades        INT,
  downgrades      INT,
  started_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  completed_at    TIMESTAMPTZ
);
```

**Pipeline execution:**

```typescript
async function runDailyClassificationPipeline(runDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  // Idempotent: skip if already run for this date
  const existing = await db('classification_pipeline_runs')
    .where({ run_date: runDate, status: 'COMPLETED' }).first();
  if (existing) {
    logger.info({ runDate }, 'Classification already run for date — skipping');
    return;
  }

  const run = await upsertPipelineRun(runDate);
  const activeLoans = await db('loans').whereIn('status', ['ACTIVE', 'NPA', 'SMA']);

  let newNpas = 0, upgrades = 0, downgrades = 0;

  for (const loan of activeLoans) {
    const dpd = await computeDpd(loan.id, runDate);
    const classification = classifyAsset(loan, dpd, runDate);
    const previous = await getPreviousClassification(loan.id, runDate);

    await db('loan_classification_history').insert({
      loan_id: loan.id,
      classification_date: runDate,
      dpd,
      sma_category:     classification.smaCategory,
      asset_class:      classification.assetClass,
      npa_sub_category: classification.npaSubCategory,
      overdue_amount:   classification.overdueAmount,
      outstanding_balance: classification.outstandingBalance,
      provision_rate:   classification.provisionRate,
      provision_amount: classification.provisionAmount,
      previous_asset_class: previous?.asset_class ?? 'STANDARD',
      changed: previous?.asset_class !== classification.assetClass,
    }).onConflict(['loan_id', 'classification_date']).ignore();  // idempotent

    if (!previous || previous.asset_class !== classification.assetClass) {
      await handleClassificationChange(loan, previous, classification, runDate);
      if (classification.assetClass === 'NPA' && previous?.asset_class !== 'NPA') newNpas++;
      if (classification.assetClass === 'STANDARD' && previous?.asset_class === 'NPA') upgrades++;
      if (isDowngrade(previous?.npaSubCategory, classification.npaSubCategory)) downgrades++;
    }
  }

  await db('classification_pipeline_runs').where({ id: run.id }).update({
    status: 'COMPLETED',
    loans_processed: activeLoans.length,
    new_npas: newNpas,
    upgrades,
    downgrades,
    completed_at: new Date(),
  });
}
```

**Q7. How do you implement the asset classification function, including the NPA sub-category aging logic?**
A: The classification function takes a loan's current DPD and its `npa_date` (if already NPA) and returns the full classification:

```typescript
interface AssetClassification {
  assetClass:       'STANDARD' | 'NPA';
  smaCategory:      'SMA_0' | 'SMA_1' | 'SMA_2' | null;
  npaSubCategory:   'SUB_STANDARD' | 'DOUBTFUL_1' | 'DOUBTFUL_2' | 'DOUBTFUL_3' | 'LOSS' | null;
  provisionRate:    number;
  overdueAmount:    bigint;
  outstandingBalance: bigint;
  provisionAmount:  bigint;
}

function classifyAsset(
  loan: Loan,
  dpd: number,
  runDate: Date,
  overdueAmount: bigint,
  outstandingBalance: bigint,
  isSecured: boolean,
): AssetClassification {
  // Standard — no overdue
  if (dpd === 0) {
    return { assetClass: 'STANDARD', smaCategory: null, npaSubCategory: null,
             provisionRate: 0.004, provisionAmount: outstandingBalance * 4n / 1000n,  // 0.40% standard provisioning
             overdueAmount: 0n, outstandingBalance };
  }

  // SMA categories — 1 to 90 days
  if (dpd <= 30)  return buildSma('SMA_0', overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);
  if (dpd <= 60)  return buildSma('SMA_1', overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);
  if (dpd <= 90)  return buildSma('SMA_2', overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);

  // NPA — beyond 90 days
  // NPA sub-category based on months since npa_date (not since first overdue)
  const npaDate = loan.npa_date ?? addDays(loan.earliest_unpaid_due_date, 90);
  const monthsSinceNpa = differenceInMonths(runDate, npaDate);

  if (monthsSinceNpa <= 12) {
    const rate = isSecured ? 0.15 : 0.25;
    return buildNpa('SUB_STANDARD', rate, overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);
  }
  if (monthsSinceNpa <= 24) {
    const rate = isSecured ? 0.25 : 1.0;
    return buildNpa('DOUBTFUL_1', rate, overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);
  }
  if (monthsSinceNpa <= 36) {
    const rate = isSecured ? 0.40 : 1.0;
    return buildNpa('DOUBTFUL_2', rate, overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);
  }
  // > 36 months since NPA date
  return buildNpa('DOUBTFUL_3', 1.0, overdueAmount, outstandingBalance);
}

function buildNpa(
  subCategory: string,
  provisionRate: number,
  overdueAmount: bigint,
  outstandingBalance: bigint,
): AssetClassification {
  return {
    assetClass: 'NPA',
    smaCategory: null,
    npaSubCategory: subCategory,
    provisionRate,
    provisionAmount: BigInt(Math.ceil(Number(outstandingBalance) * provisionRate)),
    overdueAmount,
    outstandingBalance,
  };
}
```

**Q8. What is provisioning, and how do you compute and post provision entries in the ledger?**
A: Provisioning is the accounting practice of recognising an expected loss on a loan before the actual loss crystallises. It reduces reported profit immediately when an asset deteriorates — making financial statements more conservative and realistic.

RBI's provisioning rates (from Q2):
- Standard: 0.40% of outstanding (general provision)
- Sub-Standard: 15% (secured), 25% (unsecured)
- Doubtful-1: 25% (secured), 100% (unsecured)
- Doubtful-2: 40% (secured), 100% (unsecured)
- Doubtful-3 / Loss: 100%

**Ledger entries for provisioning:**

When a loan moves from Standard to Sub-Standard (NPA):
```
Dr  Provision for Loan Losses (Expense)     ₹15,000   ← 15% of ₹1,00,000 outstanding
  Cr  Allowance for Loan Losses (Asset contra)  ₹15,000
```

When the NPA ages from Sub-Standard to Doubtful-1 (additional provisioning):
```
Dr  Provision for Loan Losses (Expense)     ₹10,000   ← incremental (25% - 15% = 10%)
  Cr  Allowance for Loan Losses (Asset contra)  ₹10,000
```

When an NPA is upgraded back to Standard:
```
Dr  Allowance for Loan Losses (Asset contra)  ₹15,000   ← reversal of provision
  Cr  Provision for Loan Losses (Expense)        ₹15,000  ← gain on provision reversal
```

When a loan is written off:
```
Dr  Allowance for Loan Losses (Asset contra)  ₹1,00,000  ← consume the provision
  Cr  Loan Receivable (Asset)                   ₹1,00,000  ← remove loan from books
```

```typescript
async function postProvisioningEntries(
  loanId: string,
  previousProvision: bigint,
  newProvision: bigint,
  runDate: Date,
): Promise<void> {
  const delta = newProvision - previousProvision;  // positive = increase, negative = reversal
  if (delta === 0n) return;

  await postLedgerTransaction({
    idempotencyKey: `provision:${loanId}:${formatDate(runDate)}`,
    effectiveDate: runDate,
    entries: delta > 0n
      ? [
          { account: 'PROVISION_FOR_LOAN_LOSSES', type: 'DEBIT',  amount: delta },
          { account: 'ALLOWANCE_FOR_LOAN_LOSSES', type: 'CREDIT', amount: delta },
        ]
      : [
          { account: 'ALLOWANCE_FOR_LOAN_LOSSES', type: 'DEBIT',  amount: -delta },
          { account: 'PROVISION_FOR_LOAN_LOSSES', type: 'CREDIT', amount: -delta },
        ],
  });
}
```

## Section 3: Income Recognition and Interest Accrual

**Q9. What is the "stop accrual" rule for NPAs, and how do you implement it in the interest accrual engine?**
A: Under RBI's Income Recognition norms, interest on NPA accounts must NOT be recognised as income on an accrual basis. The rule:

- **Standard assets**: accrue interest daily; recognise as income as it accrues.
- **NPA accounts**: stop accruing interest to P&L. Any interest debited to the loan account must be credited to an "Interest Suspense" account (a contra-liability), not to income.
- **Existing accrued interest**: any interest accrued but uncollected at the time of NPA classification must be reversed from income.

Failing to implement stop-accrual is one of the most common regulatory findings in NBFC inspections — it overstates income and understates NPA impact.

**Interest accrual engine:**

```typescript
async function accrueInterestForLoan(loan: Loan, accrualDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  const classification = await getClassificationForDate(loan.id, accrualDate);
  const dailyInterest = computeDailyInterest(loan.outstanding_principal, loan.interest_rate);

  if (classification.assetClass === 'STANDARD') {
    // Normal accrual — recognise in P&L
    await postLedgerTransaction({
      idempotencyKey: `interest-accrual:${loan.id}:${formatDate(accrualDate)}`,
      effectiveDate: accrualDate,
      entries: [
        { account: 'INTEREST_RECEIVABLE',     type: 'DEBIT',  amount: dailyInterest },
        { account: 'INTEREST_INCOME',         type: 'CREDIT', amount: dailyInterest },
      ],
    });
  } else {
    // NPA — debit loan account but credit SUSPENSE, not income
    await postLedgerTransaction({
      idempotencyKey: `interest-suspense:${loan.id}:${formatDate(accrualDate)}`,
      effectiveDate: accrualDate,
      entries: [
        { account: 'INTEREST_RECEIVABLE',     type: 'DEBIT',  amount: dailyInterest },
        { account: 'INTEREST_SUSPENSE',       type: 'CREDIT', amount: dailyInterest },
        // Not credited to INTEREST_INCOME — income not recognised
      ],
    });
  }
}

async function reverseAccruedInterestOnNpa(loan: Loan, npaDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  // Find all interest accrued but uncollected as of npa_date
  const accruedUncollected = await db('ledger_entries')
    .where({ account: 'INTEREST_RECEIVABLE', loan_id: loan.id })
    .where('posted_at', '<=', npaDate)
    .sum('amount as total')
    .first();

  if (!accruedUncollected?.total) return;

  const amount = BigInt(accruedUncollected.total);

  // Reverse from income; move to Interest Suspense
  await postLedgerTransaction({
    idempotencyKey: `npa-interest-reversal:${loan.id}:${formatDate(npaDate)}`,
    effectiveDate: npaDate,
    entries: [
      { account: 'INTEREST_INCOME',         type: 'DEBIT',  amount },  // reverse income
      { account: 'INTEREST_RECEIVABLE',     type: 'CREDIT', amount },  // clear receivable
      { account: 'INTEREST_SUSPENSE',       type: 'CREDIT', amount },  // hold in suspense
      { account: 'INTEREST_RECEIVABLE',     type: 'DEBIT',  amount },  // re-record receivable
    ],
    // Net effect: income reversed; receivable remains on books; suspense records the contingent amount
  });
}
```

**Interest recovery on NPA accounts:**
When cash is received on an NPA account, the recovery order is: fees → interest → principal. The recovered interest is recognised as income only at the point of cash receipt:
```
Customer pays ₹5,000 on NPA account:
  Dr  Bank Account (Asset)             ₹5,000
    Cr  Interest Suspense (Liability)    ₹5,000   ← clear suspense
  Dr  Interest Suspense (Liability)    ₹5,000
    Cr  Interest Income (Revenue)        ₹5,000   ← recognise income on cash receipt
```

**Q10. How do you compute overdue interest and penalty interest, and how are they modelled differently in the ledger?**
A: Three types of interest exist on an overdue loan:

| Interest type | What it is | Accrual basis | Ledger account |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contractual interest** | Scheduled interest per loan agreement | Daily on outstanding principal | `INTEREST_RECEIVABLE` / `INTEREST_INCOME` |
| **Overdue interest** | Additional interest on overdue installments (penal rate applied to overdue amount) | Daily on overdue amount from due date | `OVERDUE_INTEREST_RECEIVABLE` |
| **Penal charges / late fees** | Fixed fee per missed payment (e.g., ₹500 per bounce) | Event-triggered (on each missed payment) | `PENAL_CHARGES_RECEIVABLE` |

```sql
CREATE TABLE loan_interest_accruals (
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  loan_id         UUID NOT NULL,
  accrual_date    DATE NOT NULL,
  accrual_type    TEXT NOT NULL,        -- 'CONTRACTUAL', 'OVERDUE', 'PENAL'
  principal_base  BIGINT NOT NULL,      -- amount on which interest is computed
  rate_per_day    NUMERIC(12,10) NOT NULL,
  amount          BIGINT NOT NULL,      -- interest for this day in paise
  asset_class     TEXT NOT NULL,        -- class at time of accrual (for income recognition)
  ledger_entry_id UUID REFERENCES ledger_entries(id),
  PRIMARY KEY (loan_id, accrual_date, accrual_type)
);
```

```typescript
async function accrueOverdueInterest(loan: Loan, accrualDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  const overdueAmount = await computeOverdueAmount(loan.id, accrualDate);
  if (overdueAmount === 0n) return;

  const OVERDUE_INTEREST_RATE = loan.penal_interest_rate ?? loan.interest_rate * 2;
  const dailyRate = OVERDUE_INTEREST_RATE / 365;
  const overdueInterest = BigInt(Math.ceil(Number(overdueAmount) * dailyRate));

  // Overdue interest on NPA accounts also goes to suspense
  const classification = await getClassificationForDate(loan.id, accrualDate);
  const creditAccount = classification.assetClass === 'NPA'
    ? 'OVERDUE_INTEREST_SUSPENSE'
    : 'OVERDUE_INTEREST_INCOME';

  await postLedgerTransaction({
    idempotencyKey: `overdue-interest:${loan.id}:${formatDate(accrualDate)}`,
    effectiveDate: accrualDate,
    entries: [
      { account: 'OVERDUE_INTEREST_RECEIVABLE', type: 'DEBIT',  amount: overdueInterest },
      { account: creditAccount,                  type: 'CREDIT', amount: overdueInterest },
    ],
  });
}
```

## Section 4: NPA Upgradation and Write-off

**Q11. Under what conditions can an NPA be upgraded back to Standard, and what is the engineering implementation?**
A: NPA upgradation (curing) is subject to strict RBI conditions — it is not sufficient for the customer to simply make one payment. The conditions:

**RBI upgradation conditions:**
- All overdues (principal + interest + fees) must be fully paid — not just the installments that triggered NPA.
- The account must remain regular (no new overdue) for at least **12 consecutive months** after full regularisation.
- For restructured accounts: a higher standard — often 12 months of satisfactory performance under the restructured terms.

**"Technically regularised" trap:** If a customer clears the overdue on March 31 (quarter-end) and then misses the April installment, the account should not be upgraded — the regularisation was not genuine. Many systems naively upgrade on receipt of payment; the correct approach is to defer upgrade until the 12-month observation period passes.

```sql
CREATE TABLE loan_regularisation_tracking (
  id                    UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  loan_id               UUID NOT NULL UNIQUE,
  regularised_at        DATE NOT NULL,          -- date all overdues cleared
  observation_end_date  DATE GENERATED ALWAYS AS (regularised_at + INTERVAL '12 months') STORED,
  is_upgrade_eligible   BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,
  upgraded_at           DATE,
  disqualified_at       DATE,                   -- if a new overdue occurred during observation
  disqualification_reason TEXT
);
```

```typescript
// Runs daily — checks if observation period has been maintained
async function evaluateNpaUpgrades(runDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  const candidates = await db('loan_regularisation_tracking')
    .where({ is_upgrade_eligible: false })
    .where('regularised_at', '<=', subMonths(runDate, 12))  // 12 months have passed
    .whereNull('disqualified_at');

  for (const candidate of candidates) {
    // Verify no new overdue occurred during observation period
    const newOverdue = await db('loan_repayment_schedule')
      .where({ loan_id: candidate.loan_id, status: ['PENDING', 'PARTIAL'] })
      .where('due_date', '>=', candidate.regularised_at)
      .where('due_date', '<', runDate)
      .first();

    if (newOverdue) {
      // Disqualified — new overdue during observation
      await db('loan_regularisation_tracking')
        .where({ loan_id: candidate.loan_id })
        .update({
          disqualified_at: runDate,
          disqualification_reason: `New overdue on EMI due ${newOverdue.due_date}`,
        });
    } else {
      // Eligible for upgrade
      await db('loan_regularisation_tracking')
        .where({ loan_id: candidate.loan_id })
        .update({ is_upgrade_eligible: true });

      await upgradeToStandard(candidate.loan_id, runDate);
    }
  }
}

async function upgradeToStandard(loanId: string, upgradeDate: Date): Promise<void> {
  await db.transaction(async (trx) => {
    // Update loan status
    await trx('loans').where({ id: loanId }).update({
      status: 'ACTIVE',
      npa_date: null,    // RBI: once upgraded, NPA date is cleared for current reporting
      npa_sub_category: null,
    });

    // Reverse interest suspense — now recognise as income
    await reverseInterestSuspense(loanId, upgradeDate, trx);

    // Reverse provision — no longer needed
    const currentProvision = await getCurrentProvision(loanId, trx);
    await postProvisioningEntries(loanId, currentProvision, 0n, upgradeDate);

    // Record in classification history
    await trx('loan_classification_history').insert({
      loan_id: loanId,
      classification_date: upgradeDate,
      dpd: 0,
      asset_class: 'STANDARD',
      sma_category: null,
      npa_sub_category: null,
      changed: true,
    });
  });
}
```

**Q12. What is a loan write-off, and how does it differ from a waiver? How are both implemented in the ledger?**
A: These are frequently confused but have completely different accounting and regulatory treatment:

**Write-off:**
- The lender removes the loan from its books (derecognises the asset) because collection is deemed unlikely.
- Does NOT extinguish the borrower's legal obligation — the lender can still pursue legal recovery after write-off.
- Required when: Doubtful-3 or Loss classification; RBI / auditor instruction; or internal policy after N years of NPA.
- Provisioning must be 100% before write-off (the provision absorbs the loss).

```
Write-off entry:
Dr  Allowance for Loan Losses (contra-Asset)  ₹1,00,000  ← consume provision
  Cr  Loan Receivable (Asset)                   ₹1,00,000  ← remove from books

Subsequent recovery (partial cash receipt after write-off):
Dr  Bank Account (Asset)                     ₹20,000
  Cr  Bad Debt Recovery Income (Revenue)       ₹20,000   ← directly to income
```

**Waiver:**
- The lender legally forgives part or all of the outstanding amount — the borrower's obligation is extinguished.
- Requires board / credit committee approval; must be disclosed in financial statements.
- Has income tax implications for the borrower (waived debt may be taxable income).
- Generates a write-off entry AND requires a credit to P&L for the waived amount (if not already provisioned).

```
Waiver of ₹50,000 on a loan with ₹30,000 provision:
Dr  Allowance for Loan Losses (contra-Asset)  ₹30,000  ← consume existing provision
Dr  Bad Debt Expense (Expense)                ₹20,000  ← additional loss (unprovisioned portion)
  Cr  Loan Receivable (Asset)                   ₹50,000  ← remove from books
```

**Q15. What is the RBI mandate on NPA divergence, and how does it affect your classification engine?**
A: RBI conducts Annual Supervisory Reviews (ASRs) / Annual Financial Inspections (AFIs) of banks and large NBFCs. One key assessment is NPA divergence — the difference between the NPA reported by the institution and the NPA identified by RBI inspectors.

**Disclosure threshold (RBI circular 2016, updated):**
If RBI's assessment of gross NPA exceeds the institution's reported gross NPA by:
- ≥ ₹500 crore, AND
- ≥ 10% of the institution's reported gross NPA

…the divergence must be disclosed in the annual report, along with additional provisioning.

This creates a direct engineering obligation: your classification engine must be conservative (err toward classifying more loans as NPA rather than fewer) and must not apply any manual overrides to delay NPA recognition. Any override must be:
- Logged with reason and approver.
- Reported separately in management information to the board's audit committee.
- Reversed if not substantiated.

**Q17. "A borrower paid all overdue amounts on June 30. Your system upgraded them to Standard on July 1. The RBI inspector says this is incorrect. Why?"**
A: This is the "technically regularised" problem — one of the most common RBI inspection findings.

The borrower cleared overdues on June 30, but that does not immediately qualify them for Standard classification. RBI's IRACP norms require that:
1. All overdues are cleared (satisfied on June 30 ✓).
2. The account demonstrates satisfactory performance for at least **12 months** after regularisation (not satisfied — only 1 day has passed).

Your system should have:
1. Set `regularised_at = June 30` in `loan_regularisation_tracking`.
2. Set loan status to a transitional state: `REGULARISED_UNDER_OBSERVATION` (or kept as NPA during observation).
3. Scheduled a check for `June 30 + 12 months = June 30 next year`.
4. Only upgraded to Standard if no new overdue occurred in the intervening 12 months.

By upgrading to Standard on July 1, your system:
- Understated gross NPA by the loan's outstanding amount.
- Reversed provisions prematurely (overstated profit).
- Sent incorrect classification to CRILC and credit bureaus.

The fix: add the `loan_regularisation_tracking` table and the 12-month observation period check shown in Q11. Add a database constraint that prevents direct transition from NPA to STANDARD without passing through the observation period:

```sql
-- Enforce via trigger: NPA → STANDARD requires an observation record
CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION enforce_npa_upgrade_observation()
RETURNS TRIGGER AS $$
BEGIN
  IF OLD.status = 'NPA' AND NEW.status = 'ACTIVE' THEN
    IF NOT EXISTS (
      SELECT 1 FROM loan_regularisation_tracking
      WHERE loan_id = NEW.id
        AND is_upgrade_eligible = TRUE
    ) THEN
      RAISE EXCEPTION 'NPA upgrade requires completed 12-month observation period for loan %', NEW.id;
    END IF;
  END IF;
  RETURN NEW;
END;
$$ LANGUAGE plpgsql;

CREATE TRIGGER enforce_npa_upgrade
  BEFORE UPDATE ON loans
  FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION enforce_npa_upgrade_observation();
```

**Q18. "How do you handle a loan where the customer has multiple overdue installments but makes a partial payment — which installment does the payment apply to?"**
A: Payment appropriation order is defined by RBI guidelines and the loan agreement. The standard RBI order for appropriation of collections:

```
1. Penal charges / bounce fees (if any)
2. Overdue interest (interest on overdue principal)
3. Principal overdue (oldest installment first — FIFO)
4. Current interest
5. Current principal
```

This matters because applying payment to the wrong component affects:
- DPD calculation (which installments are cleared determines the oldest unpaid due date).
- Interest accrual base (unpaid principal drives ongoing interest).
- Provisioning (overdue amount changes).

```typescript
async function appropriatePayment(
  loanId: string,
  paymentAmount: bigint,
  paymentDate: Date,
): Promise<AppropriationResult> {
  const loan = await db('loans').where({ id: loanId }).first();
  let remaining = paymentAmount;
  const appropriations: Appropriation[] = [];

  // Step 1: Penal charges (FIFO by charge date)
  const penalCharges = await db('loan_penal_charges')
    .where({ loan_id: loanId, status: 'OUTSTANDING' })
    .orderBy('charged_at', 'asc');

  for (const charge of penalCharges) {
    if (remaining <= 0n) break;
    const applied = remaining >= charge.amount ? charge.amount : remaining;
    appropriations.push({ type: 'PENAL', id: charge.id, applied });
    remaining -= applied;
    await db('loan_penal_charges').where({ id: charge.id })
      .update({ amount_paid: db.raw('amount_paid + ?', [applied]),
                status: applied >= charge.amount ? 'PAID' : 'PARTIAL' });
  }

  // Step 2: Overdue interest (FIFO by accrual date)
  const overdueInterest = await db('loan_interest_accruals')
    .where({ loan_id: loanId, accrual_type: 'OVERDUE', status: 'OUTSTANDING' })
    .orderBy('accrual_date', 'asc');

  for (const interest of overdueInterest) {
    if (remaining <= 0n) break;
    const applied = remaining >= interest.amount ? interest.amount : remaining;
    appropriations.push({ type: 'OVERDUE_INTEREST', id: interest.id, applied });
    remaining -= applied;
  }

  // Step 3: Overdue principal (FIFO by due date)
  const overdueInstallments = await db('loan_repayment_schedule')
    .where({ loan_id: loanId })
    .whereIn('status', ['PENDING', 'PARTIAL'])
    .where('due_date', '<', paymentDate)
    .orderBy('due_date', 'asc');

  for (const installment of overdueInstallments) {
    if (remaining <= 0n) break;
    const unpaid = installment.total_emi - installment.amount_paid;
    const applied = remaining >= unpaid ? unpaid : remaining;
    appropriations.push({ type: 'OVERDUE_PRINCIPAL', id: installment.id, applied });
    remaining -= applied;
    await db('loan_repayment_schedule').where({ id: installment.id })
      .update({
        amount_paid: db.raw('amount_paid + ?', [applied]),
        status: applied >= unpaid ? 'PAID' : 'PARTIAL',
        paid_at: applied >= unpaid ? paymentDate : null,
      });
  }

  // Step 4 & 5: Current interest and principal (current due installment)
  // ... similar pattern for current cycle

  await postPaymentLedgerEntries(loanId, appropriations, paymentDate);
  await triggerDpdRecalculation(loanId, paymentDate);
  return { totalApplied: paymentAmount - remaining, appropriations, unallocated: remaining };
}
```

**Q19. "Your NPA ratio jumped from 3.2% to 4.8% overnight. The CFO asks what happened. How do you investigate?"**
A: A 150 bps single-day jump in NPA ratio is significant — likely caused by a classification pipeline change, a data fix, or a genuine deterioration event. Systematic investigation:

**Step 1 — Quantify the change (10 minutes)**
```sql
-- Compare today's classification to yesterday's
SELECT
  today.asset_class AS current_class,
  yesterday.asset_class AS previous_class,
  COUNT(*) AS loan_count,
  SUM(today.outstanding_balance) AS outstanding_amount
FROM loan_classification_history today
JOIN loan_classification_history yesterday
  ON today.loan_id = yesterday.loan_id
  AND yesterday.classification_date = CURRENT_DATE - 1
WHERE today.classification_date = CURRENT_DATE
  AND today.asset_class <> yesterday.asset_class
GROUP BY today.asset_class, yesterday.asset_class
ORDER BY outstanding_amount DESC;
```

This shows all class changes (STANDARD → NPA, SMA_2 → NPA, etc.) and the outstanding balance involved.

**Step 2 — Identify the cohort (15 minutes)**

Were the newly classified NPAs all from one product, geography, disbursement vintage, or DPD band?

```sql
-- Newly classified NPAs — breakdown by cohort
SELECT
  l.product_type,
  l.disbursement_month,
  l.state,
  COUNT(*) AS new_npa_count,
  SUM(h.outstanding_balance) AS new_npa_amount,
  AVG(h.dpd) AS avg_dpd
FROM loan_classification_history h
JOIN loans l ON l.id = h.loan_id
WHERE h.classification_date = CURRENT_DATE
  AND h.asset_class = 'NPA'
  AND h.previous_asset_class <> 'NPA'
GROUP BY l.product_type, l.disbursement_month, l.state
ORDER BY new_npa_amount DESC;
```

**Step 3 — Root cause branches**

*DPD crossed 90 for a large cohort simultaneously*: a batch of loans disbursed in the same month had their 90th overdue day fall on the same date. Genuine deterioration — report to the board.

*Pipeline configuration change*: a change to the DPD computation (e.g., bug fix that now correctly counts holidays in DPD) reclassified accounts that were borderline. Check the pipeline's git history and deployment log for yesterday.

*Data fix applied*: overdue dates were corrected in a migration (e.g., a NACH file reprocessing updated `paid_at` dates). Check recent migrations and data corrections.

*Holiday catch-up*: DPD was not updated during a multi-day holiday; the pipeline ran for the first time in 3 days and caught up all three days simultaneously. The 90-day crossings are real but were delayed by the holiday.

**Step 4 — Communicate to CFO**
Provide: (a) the classification change table from Step 1, (b) the cohort breakdown from Step 2, (c) the root cause from Step 3, (d) whether the change is permanent (genuine deterioration) or a one-time catch-up.

**Q20. "How do you design a collections prioritisation system using NPA and DPD data?"**
A: Collections prioritisation determines which accounts a collections agent works on today. The input is the daily classification pipeline output; the goal is to maximise recovery within the available agent capacity.

**Priority scoring model:**

```typescript
function computeCollectionsPriority(loan: LoanWithClassification): number {
  let score = 0;

  // Base score by DPD band — higher DPD = higher urgency but lower recovery probability
  // Sweet spot: SMA_1 and SMA_2 — still recoverable, already delinquent
  const dpdScore: Record<string, number> = {
    'SMA_0':       30,   // Early — high recovery probability
    'SMA_1':       50,   // Medium — good recovery
    'SMA_2':       70,   // Urgent — approaching NPA
    'SUB_STANDARD': 60,  // NPA but fresh — still recoverable
    'DOUBTFUL_1':  40,
    'DOUBTFUL_2':  20,
    'DOUBTFUL_3':  10,   // Low — focus only if large amount
  };
  score += dpdScore[loan.smaCategory ?? loan.npaSubCategory] ?? 0;

  // Amount factor — larger outstanding = higher priority
  const amountScore = Math.min(40, Number(loan.outstanding_balance) / 100_000_00);
  score += amountScore;

  // Behaviour factor — customer who paid last month is better candidate than one silent for 6 months
  if (loan.days_since_last_payment <= 30) score += 20;
  else if (loan.days_since_last_payment <= 60) score += 10;

  // Re-presentation history — if NACH has already been tried 3 times, deprioritise NACH channel
  if (loan.nach_presentations_exhausted) score -= 15;

  // Approaching NPA boundary — SMA_2 loans crossing 91 DPD tomorrow get highest urgency
  if (loan.smaCategory === 'SMA_2' && loan.dpd >= 88) score += 30;

  return Math.min(100, score);
}
```

| **Technically regularised trap** | Payment on quarter-end, new overdue next month | Disqualify during observation period; do not upgrade |
| **Write-off vs waiver** | Write-off: off books, legal recovery continues; Waiver: obligation extinguished | Board approval required for both; separate ledger entries |
| **CRILC reporting** | Monthly by 21st; weekly for SMA-2/NPA ≥ ₹5 crore | Borrower PAN + DPD + classification + NPA date |
| **Credit bureau** | Monthly by 10th; all four bureaus; 7-year retention | CIBIL fixed-width format; DPD must be exact |
| **NPA divergence** | RBI flags if reported NPA < inspection NPA by ≥ ₹500 crore or ≥ 10% | Override audit log; board reporting; conservative classification |
| **Collections priority** | SMA_2 approaching 90 DPD = highest urgency | Score: DPD band + amount + recency of last payment |

---

**Tip for the interview:** Overdue and NPA questions at the 5 YOE level appear as "design a loan servicing system," "how do you compute DPD correctly," and "why did our NPA ratio change overnight." The detail that separates senior answers is the 12-month observation period for NPA upgrades — almost every candidate says "mark as Standard once the customer pays" which is exactly wrong and the most common RBI inspection finding. Know the stop-accrual rule and Interest Suspense account cold — it is the single most important accounting treatment unique to NPAs and every NBFC/bank system implements it. The payment appropriation order (penal → overdue interest → overdue principal → current) is a frequent follow-up question in lending fintech interviews. Finally, tie the classification pipeline to concrete outputs: CRILC, credit bureau files, provisioning entries, and the collections queue — that demonstrates you understand NPA classification as an operational system, not just a DPD threshold check.
