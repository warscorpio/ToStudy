## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is a collections workflow, and where does it sit in the lending lifecycle?**
A: A collections workflow is the structured, multi-stage process a lender uses to recover funds from borrowers who have missed scheduled payments. It begins the moment a payment is missed and ends when one of four terminal outcomes is reached: full recovery, partial recovery via settlement, legal judgment and enforcement, or write-off with third-party agency handoff.

Collections sits at the intersection of three disciplines:
- **Engineering** — state machines, communication orchestration, payment retry logic, bureau reporting pipelines, and data systems that track borrower behavior across every contact attempt.
- **Finance** — provisioning (reserving against expected losses), recovery accounting, write-off timing, and portfolio health metrics (roll rates, cure rates, vintage analysis).
- **Regulatory compliance** — every jurisdiction regulates how, when, and how many times a lender can contact a borrower, what language must appear in notices, and what remedies are available.

**Why collections is an engineering problem:**
A lender with 50,000 active loans and a 6% delinquency rate has 3,000 accounts in collections at any moment. Across a 90-day collections cycle, each account may generate 15–30 contact events (SMS, calls, emails, payment attempts). That is 45,000–90,000 events per month — each requiring correct sequencing, timing, channel selection, and outcome tracking. Manual management at this volume is impossible. The engineering system must automate the low-intensity stages entirely, route only genuinely complex cases to human agents, and maintain a complete audit trail of every action for regulatory examination.

**The collections funnel:**
```
All active loans
      │
      ▼ (payment missed)
Early Delinquency (DPD 1–30)      ← Automated: SMS, IVR, NACH retry
      │
      ▼ (no payment)
Soft Collections (DPD 31–60)       ← Semi-automated: agent outreach + digital nudges
      │
      ▼ (no payment)
Hard Collections (DPD 61–90)       ← Human-heavy: senior agents, settlement offers
      │
      ▼ (no payment)
NPA / Pre-legal (DPD 90–180)       ← Legal notices, agency assignment
      │
      ▼ (no payment)
Write-off / Agency (DPD 180+)      ← Third-party collections agency; internal write-off
      │
      ▼ (recovery)
Post-write-off Recovery            ← Any payment after write-off recognized as income
```

**Q2. What is the difference between DPD buckets, and why are the thresholds not arbitrary?**
A: DPD (Days Past Due) buckets are the industry-standard segmentation of delinquent loans by severity. The specific thresholds are anchored to regulatory classification rules, provisioning requirements, and empirical observation of borrower behavior at each stage.

**Why these specific thresholds:**

*DPD 1–30 (Special Mention / Watch List):*
- Regulatory significance: in most jurisdictions, loans < 30 DPD are still classified as "standard" on the lender's books — no additional provisioning required. At 30 DPD, some regulators require the loan to be reclassified as "special mention," triggering a 5–10% provision.
- Behavioral significance: most early delinquencies are caused by forgotten payments, incorrect bank details, or temporary cash flow issues — not inability to pay. Auto-communication and payment retry resolve 50–70% of DPD 1–30 accounts without human intervention.

*DPD 31–60 (Sub-standard / Soft Collections):*
- Regulatory significance: many regulators require reclassification to "sub-standard" at 60 DPD, with 15–25% provisioning.
- Behavioral significance: borrowers still reachable and willing to negotiate; cure rates drop sharply after 60 DPD. The 31–60 window is the highest-ROI period for human agent intervention.

*DPD 61–90 (Doubtful):*
- Regulatory significance: classification as "doubtful" in RBI's NPA framework at 90 DPD; 25–50% provisioning. GAAP requires similar treatment.
- Behavioral significance: borrowers who haven't paid by 90 DPD are typically in genuine financial distress or are intentionally avoiding payment. Settlement (accepting less than full outstanding) often becomes necessary.

*DPD 90+ (NPA / Non-Performing Asset):*
- Regulatory significance: RBI classifies any loan overdue for 90 days as an NPA. Interest accrual stops for income recognition purposes (interest moves to a suspense account). The lender must report NPAs to the regulator quarterly.
- Behavioral significance: most accounts at this stage require either a restructuring arrangement or legal action. Cure rates drop to 10–20%.

*DPD 180+ (Write-off Candidate):*
- Regulatory significance: most lenders write off at 180 DPD per internal policy, though the regulator allows up to 12 months before mandatory write-off for secured loans.
- Behavioral significance: empirical data shows recovery rates drop sharply after 6 months of non-payment; the cost of continued in-house collections often exceeds expected recovery.

**Q3. What are the terminal outcomes of a collections workflow, and how does each affect the system state?**
A: Every delinquent loan must eventually reach one of four terminal states:

**1. Full Recovery (Cure)**
The borrower repays all overdue amounts (principal, interest, fees, and penalties) and the loan returns to `active` status. The collections case is closed with outcome `cured`. The loan account is fully reinstated — the amortization schedule continues from the current installment. Bureau reporting is updated to reflect the cure (the delinquency history remains visible but the account shows current).

**2. Partial Recovery via Settlement (OTS — One-Time Settlement)**
The lender agrees to accept less than the full outstanding balance as complete satisfaction of the debt. Common in 90+ DPD cases where full recovery is unlikely. The settlement amount is determined by negotiation, discounted cash flow modeling, and the borrower's demonstrated ability to pay. Engineering must: generate a settlement offer letter, collect the agreed settlement payment, generate a settlement deed, update the loan account to `settled`, and report to the bureau as "settled for less than full amount" (which is a negative mark but better than "written off").

**3. Legal Recovery (Judgment and Enforcement)**
The lender obtains a court judgment against the borrower and enforces it through wage garnishment, bank account attachment, or property seizure (for secured loans). The timeline is long (12–36 months), the cost is high (legal fees), and the outcome is uncertain. Engineering must track: legal case filing date, court dates, judgment amount, enforcement actions taken, and amounts recovered via enforcement.

**4. Write-off with Agency Transfer**
The loan is removed from the lender's books as an uncollectable asset. The remaining outstanding balance (if any) is sold to or assigned to a third-party collections agency at a fraction of face value (typically 1–15 cents on the dollar). The agency then pursues the borrower independently. Engineering must: generate the agency transfer file (borrower data, payment history, contact history), record the write-off accounting entries, update loan status to `written_off`, and continue tracking any post-write-off recoveries (which are recognized as income).

## Section 2: Early Collections (DPD 1–30)

**Q4. How is the early collections communication cascade designed, and what makes it effective?**
A: The early collections communication cascade is a pre-defined sequence of automated touchpoints triggered by a missed payment. "Cascade" because each step escalates intensity if the previous step produced no payment response.

**Cascade design principles:**
- **Timing relative to due date, not calendar** — the cascade clock starts at the due date, not at the end of the month. A payment due on the 5th that is not received triggers Day 1 communications on the 6th, regardless of month boundaries.
- **Channel sequencing by cost and intrusiveness** — cheap, non-intrusive channels first (SMS, email); expensive, intrusive channels later (IVR calls, agent calls). This reduces cost for accounts that self-cure early.
- **Respect for regulatory communication windows** — most jurisdictions prohibit contacting borrowers before 8 AM or after 9 PM local time. The communication scheduler must enforce this.
- **Early stopping** — if a payment is received at any point, the entire cascade stops immediately. Do not send a collections SMS to a borrower who just paid.

**Cascade schedule (DPD 1–30 example):**

```
DPD 1 (Day after due date):
  08:00 AM  → SMS: "Your EMI of ₹8,500 was due on [date]. Please pay now to
                    avoid late fees. [Payment link]"
  06:00 PM  → Email: Friendly reminder with payment options and support contact

DPD 3:
  10:00 AM  → SMS: Second reminder with late fee notice
  02:00 PM  → WhatsApp message (if borrower opted in): personalized message
                with current outstanding (principal + accrued late fee)

DPD 5:
  09:00 AM  → NACH retry attempt (if first presentation returned)
  11:00 AM  → IVR call: automated voice call, option to speak to agent or
                    make payment via phone

DPD 7:
  09:30 AM  → Agent SMS (personalized, from agent's number, not shortcode):
                    higher response rate than shortcode
  02:00 PM  → IVR call: second attempt

DPD 10:
  10:00 AM  → Second NACH retry (if first retry also returned)
  Agent queue assignment: case enters soft collections queue for
                          human agent outreach starting DPD 15

DPD 15:
  Agent call: first human contact attempt
  If no answer: leave voicemail + send follow-up SMS

DPD 21:
  Agent call: second attempt
  Email: formal reminder with penal interest amount and
         escalation warning

DPD 30:
  Agent call: final DPD-30 attempt
  SMS: "Your account is now [30] days overdue. Failure to pay by [date]
       will result in bureau reporting and further action."
  → Transition to Soft Collections bucket (DPD 31+)
```

**Outcome tracking for each communication:**
Every communication event must record its outcome, not just its dispatch:

```python
class CommunicationOutcome(Enum):

    CALL_NO_ANSWER  = 'no_answer'      # rang out or voicemail

    PTP_MADE        = 'ptp'            # promise to pay on a specific date

    BOUNCE          = 'bounce'         # email bounced
```

Outcomes feed the next cascade decision: a `CLICKED` outcome without a subsequent `PAYMENT_MADE` suggests the borrower is aware of the debt but has a payment friction — the next touchpoint should offer a payment plan or alternative channel. A `CALL_REFUSED` suggests the borrower is avoiding — escalate faster.

**Q5. How does NACH / ACH retry work in the collections context, and what are the rules?**
A: NACH (National Automated Clearing House in India) and ACH (US) are bank mandate-based auto-debit systems. In collections, re-presenting a failed debit is one of the most effective early-stage recovery tools — a significant percentage of first-presentation failures are due to temporary insufficient funds that resolve within a few days.

**NACH retry mechanics:**
```
Day 0 (Due date):   First NACH presentation
                    → Result arrives T+1 (next business day)

Day 1:              NACH result received
  If SUCCESS:       Payment posted. Communications cascade stops.
  If RETURN R01     (Insufficient Funds):
    → Schedule retry per retry policy
  If RETURN R02/R03 (Account Closed / Invalid):
    → No retry. Flag for manual contact. Update account details.
  If RETURN R08     (Payment Stopped — borrower issued stop order):
    → No retry. This is a dispute signal. Escalate to agent immediately.

Day 5 (DPD 5):     First retry presentation
                    → Result arrives Day 6
  If SUCCESS:       Payment posted.
  If RETURN R01:    Schedule second retry.

Day 10 (DPD 10):   Second retry presentation
                    → Result arrives Day 11
  If SUCCESS:       Payment posted.
  If RETURN R01:    No further NACH retries. Transition to agent-led collections.
```

**NACHA / NPCI retry rules (non-negotiable compliance requirements):**

In the US, NACHA rules limit R01 (Insufficient Funds) and R09 (Uncollected Funds) retries to **2 retries within 180 days** of the original authorization. A third presentation is a NACHA violation, subject to fines.

In India, NPCI does not publish a fixed retry count limit, but RBI's Fair Practices Code effectively requires that repeated failed presentations be escalated to borrower communication rather than blind retry — presenting a mandate 10 times without borrower awareness is considered unfair practice.

**Retry timing strategy:**
Retry timing should be informed by the likely reason for failure:
- R01 (NSF): retry 4–7 days later (near typical salary credit dates — 1st and 15th of month for salaried borrowers).
- Network / system error: retry next business day.
- Never retry on the same day as the original failure — same-day retry almost never succeeds and generates unnecessary return file processing cost.

**Engineering idempotency for NACH retries:**
```python
def schedule_nach_retry(loan_account_id, original_presentation_id, retry_number):
    # Check retry count — enforce regulatory limit
    existing_retries = db.count("""
        SELECT COUNT(*) FROM nach_presentations
        WHERE loan_account_id = %s
        AND presentation_type = 'retry'
        AND original_presentation_id = %s
    """, [loan_account_id, original_presentation_id])
    
    if existing_retries >= MAX_RETRIES[jurisdiction]:
        flag_for_manual_collections(loan_account_id, reason='nach_retry_limit_reached')
        return
    
    retry_date = compute_retry_date(
        original_date=get_presentation_date(original_presentation_id),
        return_code=get_return_code(original_presentation_id),
        retry_number=retry_number
    )
    
    # Idempotent insert — don't double-schedule if this job runs twice
    db.execute("""
        INSERT INTO nach_presentations
          (loan_account_id, presentation_type, original_presentation_id,
           scheduled_date, retry_number, status)
        VALUES (%s, 'retry', %s, %s, %s, 'scheduled')
        ON CONFLICT (loan_account_id, original_presentation_id, retry_number)
        DO NOTHING
    """, [loan_account_id, original_presentation_id, retry_date, retry_number])
```

## Section 3: Soft and Hard Collections (DPD 31–90)

**Q6. How is agent workqueue management designed for collections operations?**
A: Agent workqueue management determines which accounts an agent works on, in what order, and with what information. At scale, naive approaches (assign all accounts to all agents) create chaos — agents cherry-pick easy accounts, complex cases are neglected, and priority accounts breach SLAs. A well-designed workqueue system drives both agent efficiency and recovery rate.

**Workqueue design principles:**

*Segmentation by bucket and intensity:*
Agents are specialized by delinquency bucket. An agent optimized for DPD 1–30 (friendly reminders, payment plan setup) is different from one working DPD 61–90 (negotiation, settlement authority, managing distressed borrowers). The workqueue engine assigns accounts to the appropriate agent pool based on current DPD.

*Priority scoring within bucket:*
Within a bucket, accounts are prioritized by a composite score:
```python
def compute_collection_priority(loan_account):
    score = 0
    
    # High outstanding amount → higher priority (more to recover)
    score += normalize(loan_account.overdue_amount, max_val=500_000) * 40
    
    # Recent PTP breach → higher priority (borrower made and broke commitment)
    if loan_account.last_ptp_broken_within_days(7):
        score += 25
    
    # Approaching DPD bucket threshold → higher priority
    # (DPD 28 account needs action before it becomes DPD 30 and triggers bureau reporting)
    days_to_next_threshold = next_dpd_threshold(loan_account.dpd) - loan_account.dpd
    score += max(0, (10 - days_to_next_threshold)) * 3
    
    # Recent engagement (clicked payment link, answered call) → higher priority
    # (borrower is reachable — strike while iron is hot)
    if loan_account.last_engagement_within_days(2):
        score += 20
    
    # Salary credit expected today (for salaried borrowers)
    if is_likely_salary_date(loan_account.borrower, today()):
        score += 15
    
    return score
```

*Agent capacity caps:*
Each agent has a maximum number of active cases (typically 80–150 for soft collections, 40–60 for hard collections where each case requires deeper engagement). The assignment engine respects capacity caps and queues overflow cases.

*Account-agent continuity:*
Once a borrower has spoken to an agent and made a PTP, the same agent should handle subsequent contacts. Continuity builds rapport and accountability — a borrower is less likely to break a PTP made to a specific person they've spoken to. The system must enforce agent-account binding within the same DPD cycle.

```sql
collection_assignments (
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  loan_account_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES loan_accounts(id),
  agent_id          VARCHAR(64) NOT NULL,
  bucket            VARCHAR(16) NOT NULL,
  assigned_at       TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  unassigned_at     TIMESTAMPTZ,             -- null while active
  unassign_reason   VARCHAR(32),            -- bucket_change, agent_left, manual_reassign
  cases_on_assign   INTEGER,                -- agent's load at assignment time
  outcome           VARCHAR(32)             -- cured, escalated, settled, written_off
)
```

**Q7. What is a Promise-to-Pay (PTP), and how does the system enforce it?**
A: A Promise-to-Pay (PTP) is a commitment made by a borrower during a collections contact to pay a specific amount by a specific date. It is the primary short-term outcome metric for collections agents — the ability to secure a PTP is a leading indicator of eventual recovery.

**PTP data model:**
```sql
collection_ptps (
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  case_id           UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES collection_cases(id),
  loan_account_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES loan_accounts(id),
  agent_id          VARCHAR(64) NOT NULL,
  promised_amount   NUMERIC(14,2) NOT NULL,
  promised_date     DATE NOT NULL,
  contact_channel   VARCHAR(16) NOT NULL,       -- call, whatsapp, email
  call_recording_id VARCHAR(128),               -- link to call recording (compliance)
  status            VARCHAR(16) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'open',
    -- open, kept, partially_kept, broken, cancelled
  actual_paid       NUMERIC(14,2),              -- populated when payment is received
  actual_paid_date  DATE,
  outcome_updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  created_at        TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
)
```

**PTP lifecycle automation:**

```python
def run_ptp_monitoring_job(run_date):
    # Check all open PTPs due today or overdue
    due_ptps = db.query("""
        SELECT p.*, la.overdue_amount, la.loan_account_id
        FROM collection_ptps p
        JOIN loan_accounts la ON la.id = p.loan_account_id
        WHERE p.status = 'open'
        AND p.promised_date <= %s
    """, [run_date])
    
    for ptp in due_ptps:
        payments_since_ptp = get_payments_after(ptp.loan_account_id, ptp.created_at)
        total_paid = sum(p.amount_received for p in payments_since_ptp)
        
        if total_paid >= ptp.promised_amount * Decimal('0.95'):  # 5% tolerance
            # PTP kept
            update_ptp_status(ptp.id, status='kept',
                              actual_paid=total_paid, actual_paid_date=run_date)
            notify_agent_ptp_kept(ptp)
        
        elif total_paid > 0:
            # Partially kept
            update_ptp_status(ptp.id, status='partially_kept',
                              actual_paid=total_paid)
            escalate_case(ptp.case_id, reason='ptp_partially_kept')
        
        elif ptp.promised_date < run_date:
            # PTP broken — borrower made no payment
            update_ptp_status(ptp.id, status='broken')
            escalate_case(ptp.case_id, reason='ptp_broken')
            increase_collections_intensity(ptp.loan_account_id)
            notify_agent_ptp_broken(ptp)
            
            # If second consecutive PTP broken → flag as high-risk / likely strategic default
            consecutive_broken = count_consecutive_broken_ptps(ptp.loan_account_id)
            if consecutive_broken >= 2:
                flag_for_legal_review(ptp.loan_account_id)
```

**PTP as a leading indicator:**
The PTP-to-kept ratio (what percentage of PTPs result in actual payment) is a key metric for:
- **Agent performance** — agents with low PTP-to-kept ratios are securing weak commitments (from borrowers who have no intention of paying). This indicates the agent needs coaching on qualifying commitments.
- **Portfolio quality** — a portfolio-level drop in PTP-to-kept ratio signals deteriorating borrower quality or changing economic conditions before it shows up in DPD metrics.

**Q8. How does a settlement (OTS) offer work, and how is the settlement amount computed?**
A: A One-Time Settlement (OTS) is an offer by the lender to accept a discounted lump-sum payment as full and final satisfaction of the loan. It is used when full recovery is unlikely (typically DPD 60+) and the expected recovery through continued collections or legal action is lower than the settlement amount.

**Settlement amount computation:**

The settlement floor is determined by the Net Present Value (NPV) of expected recovery under alternative scenarios:

```python
def compute_settlement_floor(loan_account_id, as_of_date):
    account = get_loan_account(loan_account_id)
    outstanding = compute_total_outstanding(loan_account_id, as_of_date)
    # outstanding = principal + accrued interest + fees + penalties
    
    # Scenario 1: continued collections (probability-weighted)
    collections_recovery = (
        outstanding *
        EXPECTED_RECOVERY_RATE[account.dpd_bucket] *
        DISCOUNT_FACTOR[account.dpd_bucket]   # NPV discount for time to recovery
    )
    
    # Scenario 2: legal action
    legal_recovery = (
        outstanding *
        LEGAL_RECOVERY_RATE[account.product_type] *
        DISCOUNT_FACTOR['legal'] -
        ESTIMATED_LEGAL_COST[account.loan_amount_band]
    )
    
    # Scenario 3: agency sale
    agency_price = outstanding * AGENCY_SALE_RATE[account.dpd_bucket]
    # typical agency sale rates: DPD 90 → 15–20¢/dollar; DPD 180 → 3–8¢/dollar
    
    # Settlement floor = best alternative scenario (BATNA)
    floor = max(collections_recovery, legal_recovery, agency_price)
    
    # Settlement ceiling = full outstanding (no point settling for more)
    ceiling = outstanding
    
    return SettlementRange(floor=floor, ceiling=ceiling,
                           recommended=floor * Decimal('1.15'))  # 15% buffer above floor
```

**Settlement workflow:**

```
1. Credit team approves settlement authority for this DPD bucket
   (e.g., agents can offer up to 40% waiver at DPD 60–90;
    credit committee approval required for > 50% waiver)

2. Agent discusses settlement with borrower; borrower counter-offers

3. System validates proposed settlement amount >= floor (automated check)
   If below floor: escalate to supervisor or credit committee

4. Settlement offer letter generated with:
   - Settlement amount
   - Payment deadline (typically 15–30 days from offer date)
   - Legal language confirming full and final settlement
   - Consequence of non-payment (offer lapses; full outstanding reinstated)

5. Borrower accepts and makes settlement payment

6. Settlement deed generated and signed by both parties

7. Loan account updated: status → 'settled'
   Outstanding balance → 0 (settlement amount credited to principal;
   waived amount booked as 'bad debt waived' expense)

8. Bureau reporting: account reported as 'settled' (not 'closed')
   — negative mark that persists 7 years (US) / varies by jurisdiction
```

**GL entries for settlement:**
```

Waiver:                       ₹40,000

Journal entries:
  DR: Cash/Bank                              ₹60,000
  DR: Loan Loss Provision (if provisioned)   ₹40,000 (or portion)
  DR: P&L — Bad Debt Waived                 (unprovisioned waiver amount)
  CR: Loan Receivable (principal)            ₹100,000
```

## Section 4: NPA Management and Legal Collections (DPD 90+)

**Q9. What happens at the NPA classification boundary, and what does the system need to do?**
A: The NPA (Non-Performing Asset) classification at 90 DPD is a regulatory event, not just a collections milestone. It triggers mandatory accounting changes, regulatory reporting obligations, and operational workflow changes.

**What happens at NPA classification:**

*Accounting changes (RBI / GAAP):*
```
Before NPA (active / delinquent):
  Interest income recognized monthly on accrual basis
  → DR: Interest Receivable, CR: Interest Income

At NPA classification:
  Stop recognizing interest income on accrual basis
  → All future interest accrual moves to suspense account (not P&L)
  → DR: Interest Receivable Suspense, CR: (nothing on P&L)
  → Reverse previously accrued but uncollected interest from P&L

Provisioning requirement (RBI substandard asset):
  → Create provision: DR: P&L — Provision Expense, CR: Loan Loss Reserve
  → Provision rate: 15% of outstanding for sub-standard (90–12 months DPD)
                    25%–50% for doubtful (12–36 months)
                    100% for loss assets (36+ months or deemed irrecoverable)
```

*System actions at NPA trigger:*
```python
def classify_as_npa(loan_account_id, classification_date):
    account = get_loan_account(loan_account_id)
    assert account.dpd >= 90, "NPA classification requires DPD >= 90"
    
    with db.transaction():
        # 1. Update loan account state
        update_loan_state(loan_account_id,
                          new_state='npa',
                          classification_date=classification_date)
        
        # 2. Stop interest accrual on P&L — move to suspense
        suspend_interest_accrual(loan_account_id, effective_date=classification_date)
        
        # 3. Reverse uncollected accrued interest from current period
        reverse_accrued_interest(loan_account_id, as_of=classification_date)
        
        # 4. Create initial NPA provision (15% for sub-standard)
        create_provision(
            loan_account_id=loan_account_id,
            provision_rate=PROVISION_RATES['sub_standard'],
            outstanding=account.outstanding_principal,
            created_at=classification_date
        )
        
        # 5. Generate NPA classification event for audit log
        log_state_transition(loan_account_id, 'delinquent', 'npa',
                             triggered_by='dpd_90_threshold',
                             metadata={'dpd': account.dpd,
                                       'outstanding': account.outstanding_principal})
        
        # 6. Queue for regulatory NPA reporting (next monthly submission)
        enqueue_npa_report(loan_account_id, reporting_date=next_reporting_date())
        
        # 7. Assign to dedicated NPA management team
        reassign_to_npa_team(loan_account_id)
```

*Regulatory reporting:*
RBI requires banks and NBFCs to report NPA portfolios quarterly (with monthly internal tracking). The NPA report must include: account number, borrower name, outstanding amount, date of NPA classification, asset classification (sub-standard / doubtful / loss), and provision amount held. Engineering must generate this report in the prescribed format (Excel template for smaller institutions; API submission for larger banks).

**Q10. What is loan restructuring, and how does it affect the amortization schedule?**
A: Loan restructuring is a modification of the original loan terms — interest rate, tenure, EMI amount, or principal (via partial waiver) — agreed between the lender and a borrower in financial distress. The goal is to make the loan serviceable again at the cost of some expected income, rather than losing more through default.

**Common restructuring types:**

*EMI reduction (tenure extension):*
The EMI is reduced to a level the borrower can afford; the tenure is extended to maintain total repayment. Example: ₹10,000/month EMI on a 24-month loan → ₹6,000/month on a 48-month restructured loan.

*Moratorium (repayment holiday):*
The borrower pays no EMI (or only interest) for a defined period (3–6 months), after which normal EMI resumes. The moratorium period's outstanding interest is typically capitalized (added to principal). COVID-19 restructuring schemes globally used this mechanism.

*Interest rate reduction:*
The lender agrees to reduce the interest rate for the remaining tenure. Used when the original rate is genuinely unaffordable but the principal is recoverable.

*Principal haircut (partial waiver):*
The lender waives a portion of the outstanding principal. Used only when the alternative (write-off) would result in less recovery. Typically requires credit committee or board approval for large amounts.

**Engineering restructuring:**

Restructuring creates a new version of the amortization schedule. The old schedule must be archived (for audit), not deleted:

```python
def restructure_loan(loan_account_id, restructure_params, effective_date, approver_id):
    account = get_loan_account(loan_account_id)
    current_schedule = get_active_schedule(loan_account_id)
    
    with db.transaction():
        # 1. Compute outstanding principal as of restructure date
        outstanding_principal = compute_outstanding_principal(loan_account_id, effective_date)
        
        # 2. Apply principal haircut if agreed
        new_principal = outstanding_principal - restructure_params.get('principal_waiver', 0)
        
        # 3. Capitalize moratorium interest if applicable
        if restructure_params.get('moratorium_months', 0) > 0:
            moratorium_interest = compute_moratorium_interest(
                new_principal,
                account.annual_rate,
                restructure_params['moratorium_months']
            )
            new_principal += moratorium_interest  # capitalize
        
        # 4. Build new amortization schedule
        new_tenure = restructure_params.get('new_tenure_months',
                                            account.remaining_tenure)
        new_rate   = restructure_params.get('new_annual_rate', account.annual_rate)
        
        new_schedule = build_amortization_schedule(
            principal=new_principal,
            annual_rate=new_rate,
            tenure_months=new_tenure,
            start_date=effective_date + timedelta(
                days=30 * restructure_params.get('moratorium_months', 0)
            )
        )
        
        # 5. Archive old schedule (never delete)
        archive_schedule(current_schedule.id, archived_at=effective_date,
                         archive_reason='restructuring')
        
        # 6. Store new schedule with incremented version
        new_version = current_schedule.version + 1
        store_schedule(loan_account_id, new_schedule, version=new_version,
                       effective_from=effective_date)
        
        # 7. Update loan account terms
        update_loan_account(loan_account_id,
                            annual_rate=new_rate,
                            emi_amount=new_schedule[0].emi_amount,
                            maturity_date=new_schedule[-1].due_date,
                            state='restructured')
        
        # 8. Record restructuring event
        log_restructuring(loan_account_id, original_terms=account,
                          new_terms=restructure_params, approver_id=approver_id,
                          effective_date=effective_date)
        
        # 9. Regulatory reporting: restructured loans must be separately reported
        enqueue_restructured_loan_report(loan_account_id)
    
    return new_schedule
```

**Regulatory classification of restructured loans:**
RBI requires restructured loans to be classified as "sub-standard" regardless of their pre-restructuring classification, and maintained in that classification for at least 12 months of satisfactory repayment before upgrading. This prevents lenders from using restructuring to cosmetically improve their NPA ratios.

## Section 5: Bureau Reporting in Collections

**Q12. How does bureau reporting work in the collections context, and what are the engineering requirements?**
A: Credit bureau reporting during collections is both a tool (negative reporting discourages strategic default) and a regulatory obligation (most central banks require regulated lenders to report delinquent accounts to bureaus). Inaccurate bureau reporting — reporting someone as delinquent when they are current, or failing to update a cured account — is a regulatory violation and creates legal liability.

**Reporting cycle:**
Most lenders report to credit bureaus monthly, typically within the first 5–10 business days of the following month. Every active loan (current and delinquent) must appear in the monthly submission. The bureau uses the current month's report to update each borrower's credit file.

**METRO 2 format (US — TransUnion, Equifax, Experian):**
```
METRO 2 Account Record — Key fields:
  Account Number (16 chars)
  Account Type Code (2 chars): 12=Installment, 15=Auto, 26=Mortgage
  Payment History Profile (24 months of payment status codes):
    '0' = Current
    '1' = 30 DPD
    '2' = 60 DPD
    '3' = 90 DPD
    '4' = 120 DPD
    '5' = 150 DPD
    '6' = 180+ DPD
    '7' = Making payments under wage earner plan or similar arrangement
    '8' = Repossession
    '9' = Charge-off
    'B' = No payment history available
    'D' = No payment history this month (data unavailable)
  Current Balance
  Amount Past Due
  Scheduled Monthly Payment Amount
  Account Status (11=Current, 13=Paid/Closed, 61=Account paid in full, 64=Settled, 97=Unpaid)
  Date of First Delinquency (critical — determines 7-year reporting clock)
  Date of Account Information (the report month)
```

**CIBIL format (India — TransUnion CIBIL):**
Similar structure; key fields include:
- DPD string (24-month history)
- Asset Classification (STD/SMA/SUB/DBT/LSS for Standard/Special Mention/Sub-Standard/Doubtful/Loss)
- Written-off amount
- Settlement amount (if settled)

**Engineering the bureau reporting pipeline:**

**Critical correctness requirements:**

*Date of First Delinquency (DOFD):* this date starts the 7-year clock after which the delinquency must be removed from the credit report (US FCRA). Once set, it must **never be changed** — even if the account is brought current, settled, or written off. A DOFD that changes between monthly submissions triggers a bureau dispute and regulatory scrutiny.

*Cured account update:* when a delinquent account is brought fully current (cured), the next month's bureau report must reflect the cure. The payment history string must be updated (the delinquent months remain in history, but subsequent months show '0' — current). Failing to update a cured account is a violation of FCRA (US) and causes the borrower unnecessary credit damage.

*Settled account:* must be reported as "settled" not "closed" (different status codes). The settled amount must be included. The distinction matters for future lenders reviewing the borrower's credit history.

## Section 6: Portfolio Analytics

**Q13. What are roll rates, and how do you use them to forecast collections outcomes?**
A: Roll rates measure the movement of loan balances between DPD buckets from one period to the next. They answer: "Of all the accounts that were in bucket X last month, what percentage moved to bucket Y (worse), stayed in X, or cured (improved) this month?"

**Roll rate matrix (monthly):**
```
                    This Month →
Last Month ↓   Current  DPD1-30  DPD31-60  DPD61-90  DPD90+  Closed
Current         92.5%     6.0%     1.0%      0.3%      0.1%    0.1%
DPD 1-30        45.0%    30.0%    20.0%      3.5%      1.0%    0.5%
DPD 31-60       20.0%    10.0%    35.0%     28.0%      5.0%    2.0%
DPD 61-90        8.0%     4.0%    12.0%     42.0%     30.0%    4.0%
DPD 90+          3.0%     2.0%     3.0%      5.0%     85.0%    2.0%

Interpretation:
- 45% of DPD 1-30 accounts cure (return to current) each month
- 20% of DPD 1-30 accounts worsen to DPD 31-60
- 85% of DPD 90+ accounts stay in DPD 90+ (very low cure rate)
```

**Using roll rates for forecasting:**

Roll rates enable balance-based loss forecasting. Given today's portfolio distribution across buckets and historical roll rates, you can project the portfolio state N months in the future:

```python
import numpy as np

def forecast_portfolio(current_distribution, roll_rate_matrix, months_ahead):
    """
    current_distribution: array [current, dpd1-30, dpd31-60, dpd61-90, dpd90+] balances
    roll_rate_matrix: 5x5 transition matrix (rows=from, cols=to)
    """
    distribution = np.array(current_distribution, dtype=float)
    
    forecasts = [distribution.copy()]
    for month in range(months_ahead):
        distribution = distribution @ roll_rate_matrix
        forecasts.append(distribution.copy())
    
    return forecasts

# Projected losses = projected balances in DPD 90+ bucket × expected recovery rate
def project_losses(forecasts, recovery_rate=0.20):
    return [f[4] * (1 - recovery_rate) for f in forecasts]
```

**Roll rate analysis for collections effectiveness:**
Compare roll rates before and after a collections initiative. If a new IVR campaign reduced the DPD 1-30 → DPD 31-60 roll rate from 20% to 15%, quantify the P&L impact: `improvement = 5% × DPD_1_30_balance × (1 - eventual_recovery_rate)`.

**Q14. What is vintage analysis, and how does it inform collections strategy?**
A: Vintage analysis groups loans by origination period (month or quarter) and tracks their cumulative default rates over time. Each "vintage" (origination cohort) has a performance curve showing how much of the original balance has defaulted at each month-since-origination (MSO).

**Why vintage analysis matters for collections:**

1. **Underwriting quality signal** — if the Q3 2024 vintage shows a 2× higher default rate at MSO 6 than the Q3 2023 vintage, something changed in underwriting (relaxed credit standards, new borrower segment, fraud). Collections must be intensified for the Q3 2024 vintage.

2. **Collections timing optimization** — vintage curves show when defaults peak. If most defaults occur between MSO 8 and MSO 14, the collections system can pre-position resources (more agent capacity, more aggressive early outreach) in that window.

3. **Forecasting reserve requirements** — the vintage curve extrapolated to maturity gives the expected lifetime default rate. This drives the loan loss provision calculation.

**Vintage curve construction:**
```sql
SELECT
    DATE_TRUNC('month', la.disbursal_date)      AS vintage_month,
    EXTRACT(MONTH FROM AGE(cb.npa_date,
                           la.disbursal_date))  AS months_since_origination,
    SUM(la.disbursed_amount) FILTER
        (WHERE la.state IN ('npa', 'written_off')) AS defaulted_balance,
    SUM(la.disbursed_amount)                    AS vintage_balance,
    SUM(la.disbursed_amount) FILTER
        (WHERE la.state IN ('npa', 'written_off'))
    / NULLIF(SUM(la.disbursed_amount), 0)       AS cumulative_default_rate
FROM loan_accounts la
LEFT JOIN (
    SELECT loan_account_id, MIN(classification_date) AS npa_date
    FROM loan_npa_events GROUP BY loan_account_id
) cb ON cb.loan_account_id = la.id
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 1, 2;
```

**Q17. "A borrower is claiming harassment — your system sent 12 SMS messages in one day. How do you investigate and fix it?"**
A: A harassment complaint is a regulatory and legal event, not just a customer service issue. In India, TRAI regulations limit commercial communications; in the US, TCPA violations carry $500–$1,500 per violation. A complaint must be treated with immediacy.

**Immediate containment (first 30 minutes):**
```python
def handle_harassment_complaint(borrower_id, complaint_date, complaint_channel):
    # 1. Immediately halt all outbound communications to this borrower
    add_to_communications_block_list(borrower_id, reason='harassment_complaint',
                                     blocked_by='compliance_system',
                                     blocked_at=utcnow())
    
    # 2. Pull complete communication log for this borrower for the complaint date
    events = db.query("""

        FROM collection_events ce
        JOIN collection_cases cc ON cc.id = ce.case_id
        WHERE ce.loan_account_id IN (
            SELECT id FROM loan_accounts WHERE borrower_id = %s
        )
        AND DATE(ce.event_time) = %s
        ORDER BY ce.event_time ASC
    """, [borrower_id, complaint_date])
    
    # 3. Alert compliance team immediately
    alert_compliance_team(borrower_id, complaint_date, events, complaint_channel)
    
    # 4. Assign a compliance officer as primary handler
    create_compliance_case(borrower_id, complaint_type='harassment',
                           complaint_date=complaint_date)
```

**Investigation — root cause analysis:**
```
Step 1 — Verify the count
  Query communication log for that borrower + date.
  If 12 SMS are confirmed: investigate why.
  If fewer: the borrower may be confused (received messages from a different sender);
            check if they have multiple loans at multiple lenders.

Step 2 — Trace each SMS
  For each of the 12 SMS events:
  → Which system triggered it? (collections orchestrator, NACH retry notification,
    payment reminder, agent-sent, third-party vendor?)
  → What template was used?
  → Was the frequency cap check run? Did it pass? If it passed incorrectly, why?

Step 3 — Common root causes:
  (a) Frequency cap not enforced across all sending systems
      → The collections orchestrator enforces the cap, but a separate NACH
        notification system sends independently without checking the cap.
      Fix: centralize ALL outbound communication through a single
           Communication Gateway that enforces the cap regardless of origin.

  (b) Race condition in the cap counter
      → Two parallel processes checked the cap simultaneously; both saw count=2
        (under limit) and both sent; actual count became 4 from one batch.
      Fix: use atomic increment (Redis INCR or DB row-level locking) for the
           cap counter rather than read-then-write.

  (c) Retry storm from failed deliveries
      → SMS vendor reported delivery failure; retry logic re-sent 8 times
        without checking the daily cap (retries were not counted).
      Fix: count retries in the frequency cap; do not retry failed deliveries
           indefinitely without a cap.

  (d) Multiple case IDs for the same borrower
      → Borrower has two loans; each loan's collection case generates
        independent communications; the cap is enforced per-case, not
        per-borrower.
      Fix: enforce frequency cap at borrower_id level, not loan_id level.
```

**Remediation:**
```
1. Formal written apology to borrower (required by most consumer protection regulations)
2. Compensation offer if required by regulation (TRAI/TCPA)
3. Fix the root cause in the codebase
4. Add integration test: simulate 4 SMS sends in a day; assert 5th is blocked
5. Audit: run the same query for all borrowers over the last 30 days;
   identify any others who received > daily limit; proactive outreach
6. Compliance report: document the incident, root cause, and fix for regulatory file
```

**Q18. "How would you build a system to automatically generate and send legal notices for loans in DPD 60+?"**
A: Legal notice generation is a high-stakes automation — an incorrectly addressed notice, incorrect outstanding amount, or missing regulatory language creates legal liability. The system must be correct-by-construction, with human review gates for high-value accounts.

**System design:**

```python
def legal_notice_generation_job(run_date):
    # Identify eligible accounts: DPD 60+, not already notified this cycle,
    # not in agency handoff, not in legal proceedings
    eligible = db.query("""
        SELECT la.id, la.account_number, la.borrower_id,
               la.outstanding_principal, la.dpd, la.overdue_amount
        FROM loan_accounts la
        LEFT JOIN legal_notices ln ON ln.loan_account_id = la.id
            AND ln.notice_type = 'pre_legal_demand'
            AND ln.issued_at >= NOW() - INTERVAL '90 days'
        WHERE la.dpd >= 60
        AND la.state NOT IN ('agency_collections', 'legal', 'written_off', 'closed')
        AND ln.id IS NULL   -- no recent notice
        ORDER BY la.outstanding_principal DESC
    """)
    
    for account in eligible:
        generate_and_issue_legal_notice(account, run_date)

def generate_and_issue_legal_notice(account, issue_date):
    borrower = get_borrower(account.borrower_id)
    outstanding = compute_total_outstanding(account.id, issue_date)
    
    # Compute notice type based on DPD
    if account.dpd < 90:
        notice_type = 'pre_legal_demand'          # Demand letter; no legal action yet
        template = 'demand_notice_v3'
        requires_review = outstanding.total > 500_000  # high-value: human review
    elif account.dpd < 180:
        notice_type = 'legal_notice_section_138'  # Legal notice under NI Act (India)
        template = 'legal_notice_ni_act_v2'
        requires_review = True                     # all legal notices require review
    else:
        notice_type = 'writ_petition_preparation'
        template = 'writ_preparation_v1'
        requires_review = True
    
    notice_data = {
        'borrower_name': borrower.legal_name,
        'borrower_address': borrower.registered_address,
        'loan_account_number': account.account_number,
        'original_loan_amount': account.disbursed_amount,
        'outstanding_principal': outstanding.principal,
        'accrued_interest': outstanding.interest,
        'fees_and_penalties': outstanding.fees,
        'total_outstanding': outstanding.total,
        'issue_date': issue_date,
        'response_deadline': issue_date + timedelta(days=15),
        'lender_name': LENDER_LEGAL_NAME,
        'lender_address': LENDER_REGISTERED_ADDRESS,
        'lender_signatory': get_authorized_signatory(),
    }
    
    # Render notice (PDF with digital signature)
    pdf = render_template(template, notice_data)
    signed_pdf = apply_digital_signature(pdf)
    
    # Store notice
    doc_id = document_store.save(signed_pdf, metadata={
        'notice_type': notice_type,
        'loan_account_id': account.id,
        'issue_date': issue_date
    })
    
    # Record in DB
    notice_id = db.insert('legal_notices', {
        'loan_account_id': account.id,
        'notice_type': notice_type,
        'template_version': template,
        'outstanding_at_issue': outstanding.total,
        'doc_id': doc_id,
        'status': 'pending_review' if requires_review else 'approved',
        'issued_at': issue_date if not requires_review else None,
    })
    
    if requires_review:
        # Queue for human review before dispatch
        create_review_task(notice_id, assigned_to='legal_team',
                           due_by=issue_date + timedelta(days=2))
    else:
        # Auto-dispatch for low-value DPD 60-90 demand notices
        dispatch_legal_notice(notice_id, borrower, doc_id)

def dispatch_legal_notice(notice_id, borrower, doc_id):
    # Send via registered post (legally required in most jurisdictions)
    postal_ref = registered_post_service.send(
        recipient_name=borrower.legal_name,
        recipient_address=borrower.registered_address,
        document=document_store.get(doc_id),
        acknowledgement_required=True
    )
    
    # Also send via email (secondary channel; not legally sufficient alone)
    email_service.send(
        to=borrower.email,
        subject=f"Demand Notice — Loan Account {borrower.account_number}",
        attachment=document_store.get(doc_id)
    )
    
    db.update('legal_notices', notice_id, {
        'status': 'dispatched',
        'issued_at': utcnow(),
        'postal_reference': postal_ref,
        'delivery_expected_by': utcnow() + timedelta(days=5)
    })
```
