# Lending Systems: Loan Status State Machine Design — States, Transitions & Invariants — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

> **Domain:** Fintech / Lending Systems Architecture

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is a state machine, and why is a loan lifecycle modeled as one?**

A: A **finite state machine (FSM)** is a computational model in which a system exists in exactly one of a finite number of states at any given time, and transitions between states are triggered by defined events under defined conditions (guards). The system cannot be in two states simultaneously, and it cannot transition to a state that isn't explicitly reachable from the current state.

A loan lifecycle is a natural FSM because:

- **Loans are stateful by definition.** A loan in `ACTIVE` state is fundamentally different from a loan in `NPA` state — different business rules apply, different collections logic runs, different regulatory treatments are required. The state is not metadata; it is the identity of what the loan currently is.
- **Not all transitions are valid.** A loan should never jump from `DISBURSED` directly to `CLOSED` without going through `ACTIVE` and `REPAID`. An FSM makes illegal transitions structurally impossible rather than requiring defensive checks scattered across the codebase.
- **Events drive transitions.** A disbursement event moves the loan from `APPROVED` to `DISBURSED`. An EMI payment event moves it toward `REPAID`. A missed payment moves it toward `OVERDUE`. The FSM makes these causal relationships explicit and auditable.
- **Regulatory requirement.** RBI's Fair Practices Code and IRACP (Income Recognition and Asset Classification and Provisioning) norms define specific states (Standard, Sub-Standard, Doubtful, Loss) with precise entry and exit conditions. These map directly to states in your FSM.

A loan without an explicit state machine is a loan whose status field is set by ad-hoc `UPDATE` statements across dozens of code paths — a maintenance nightmare that makes it impossible to answer "how did this loan get to NPA?" or "is this transition legal from a regulatory perspective?"

**Q2. What are the core states in a loan lifecycle, and what does each represent?**

A: The states vary by product (personal loan, BNPL, revolving credit) but a complete state machine for a term loan covers:

```
APPLICATION
  └─ The customer has submitted a loan application.
     Credit bureau checks, KYC, and BRE evaluation are in progress.
     No money has moved.

CREDIT_ASSESSMENT
  └─ Application passed initial screening.
     Detailed underwriting: income verification, bank statement analysis,
     CIBIL/Experian pull, fraud checks, debt-to-income ratio calculation.

APPROVED
  └─ Underwriting complete; loan offer generated.
     Sanction letter issued with approved amount, interest rate, tenure, EMI schedule.
     Customer has not yet accepted the offer.

OFFER_ACCEPTED
  └─ Customer accepted the loan offer (signed the loan agreement digitally).
     Disbursement is now pending (may require additional verification,
     escrow setup, or disbursement window).

DISBURSED
  └─ Principal amount has been transferred to the customer's account.
     Loan account is now live. EMI schedule begins.
     First EMI due date is set.

ACTIVE
  └─ Loan is in good standing. Regular EMI payments are being received.
     All EMIs are current (due dates met within the grace period).

OVERDUE
  └─ One or more EMIs have not been received by the due date + grace period.
     DPD (Days Past Due) > 0. Collections process begins.
     Sub-states: DPD 1–30, DPD 31–60, DPD 61–90.

NPA
  └─ Non-Performing Asset. DPD > 90 days.
     RBI IRACP classification: Sub-Standard (DPD 91–365),
     Doubtful (DPD 366–1095), Loss (DPD > 1095 or unrecoverable).
     Legal action / write-off territory.

REPAID
  └─ All EMIs collected. Principal + interest fully recovered.
     No outstanding balance. Loan is successfully closed from the customer side.

CLOSED
  └─ Terminal success state. NOC (No Objection Certificate) issued.
     Loan account closed in the system. CIBIL reported as "Closed".

REJECTED
  └─ Terminal failure state at underwriting stage.
     Application was denied. No disbursement occurred.

CANCELLED
  └─ Customer withdrew the application or declined the offer before disbursement.
     OR disbursement failed and was not retried.
     No financial obligation exists.

WRITTEN_OFF
  └─ Terminal state for unrecoverable loans.
     Loan removed from books as a loss. Collections may continue
     (a written-off loan can still be recovered — RECOVERED sub-state).

FORECLOSED
  └─ Customer repaid the entire outstanding principal before the scheduled tenure end.
     Early closure; foreclosure charges may apply per loan agreement.
```

**Q3. What is a state transition, and what three elements define every valid transition?**

A: A state transition is the movement from one state to another. Every valid transition is defined by exactly three elements:

1. **Source state:** The state the loan is currently in. A transition can only fire from its defined source state — not from any other.
2. **Event (trigger):** The business event that initiates the transition. Examples: `DISBURSEMENT_CONFIRMED`, `EMI_PAYMENT_RECEIVED`, `DPD_THRESHOLD_CROSSED`, `CUSTOMER_FORECLOSURE_REQUEST`.
3. **Guard (condition):** A boolean condition that must be true for the transition to execute. Guards prevent illegal transitions even when the event occurs. Example: `EMI_PAYMENT_RECEIVED` can only move a loan from `OVERDUE` → `ACTIVE` if the guard `all_overdue_emis_cleared = true` passes; a partial payment returns it to `OVERDUE` (with updated DPD count) or keeps it in `OVERDUE`.

Optionally, a transition may have:
- **Action (side effect):** Business logic executed as part of the transition — sending an NOC, generating a ledger entry, triggering a CIBIL report update, scheduling the next EMI.
- **Target state:** The state the loan enters after the transition.

**Formal notation:**

```
SOURCE_STATE --[EVENT / GUARD]--> TARGET_STATE / ACTION
```

Examples:
```
APPROVED      --[OFFER_ACCEPTED / customer_signed_agreement = true]--> OFFER_ACCEPTED / generate_loan_agreement_pdf
OFFER_ACCEPTED --[DISBURSEMENT_INITIATED / kyc_complete AND bank_account_verified]--> DISBURSED / create_ledger_entries, schedule_emi
ACTIVE        --[EMI_MISSED / dpd > grace_period_days]--> OVERDUE / trigger_collections_workflow
OVERDUE       --[DPD_UPDATED / dpd > 90]--> NPA / reclassify_in_rbi_reporting, increase_provision
NPA           --[FULL_PAYMENT_RECEIVED / outstanding_balance = 0]--> REPAID / close_npa_account
REPAID        --[CLOSURE_CONFIRMED / noc_generated = true]--> CLOSED / update_cibil, release_collateral
```

**Q4. What is the difference between a valid transition and a legal transition, and why does the distinction matter?**

A: This is a subtle but important distinction in financial state machine design:

**Valid transition:** A transition that is structurally permitted by the FSM definition — the source state exists, the event is recognized, and the guard passes. The state machine will execute it.

**Legal transition:** A transition that is also permissible from a regulatory, contractual, or business-rule perspective — it does not violate RBI guidelines, the loan agreement, or the platform's credit policy.

A transition can be **valid but not legal:**
- A loan in `ACTIVE` state can technically receive a `WRITE_OFF` event if the developer calls that code path — but writing off an active loan with zero DPD violates RBI's IRACP norms (which require 90+ DPD for NPA classification before write-off).
- A loan can technically be `FORECLOSED` if a foreclosure event fires — but if it fires before the lock-in period in the loan agreement, it violates the contract.

**Why the distinction matters:** Your FSM enforces structural validity (state + event + guard). Business legality must be enforced separately — either as richer guards that encode regulatory rules, or as a separate policy/rules engine layer that validates the event before presenting it to the FSM.

```typescript
// Structural guard — FSM layer
function canTransitionToNPA(loan: Loan): boolean {
  return loan.dpd > 90;  // FSM-level guard
}

// Legal guard — business rules / regulatory layer
function isNPATransitionRegulatorCompliant(loan: Loan): boolean {
  // RBI IRACP: asset can be classified as NPA only after 90 DPD
  // AND the loan must have been in OVERDUE state (not directly from ACTIVE)
  return loan.currentState === 'OVERDUE'
      && loan.dpd > 90
      && loan.lastCIBILReportDate !== null  // reporting obligation
      && loan.provisioningPercentage >= 0.10;  // 10% provisioning required at Sub-Standard
}
```

## Section 2: State Machine Implementation

**Q5. How do you implement a loan state machine in Node.js/TypeScript? What patterns work at scale?**

A: Three implementation patterns, in order of complexity and robustness:

**Pattern 1 — Enum + switch (simple, brittle at scale):**

```typescript
type LoanState =
  | 'APPLICATION' | 'CREDIT_ASSESSMENT' | 'APPROVED' | 'OFFER_ACCEPTED'
  | 'DISBURSED'   | 'ACTIVE'            | 'OVERDUE'  | 'NPA'
  | 'REPAID'      | 'CLOSED'            | 'REJECTED' | 'CANCELLED'
  | 'WRITTEN_OFF' | 'FORECLOSED';

type LoanEvent =
  | 'SUBMIT_APPLICATION'     | 'PASS_SCREENING'       | 'FAIL_SCREENING'
  | 'APPROVE_LOAN'           | 'REJECT_LOAN'          | 'ACCEPT_OFFER'
  | 'DECLINE_OFFER'          | 'CONFIRM_DISBURSEMENT' | 'FAIL_DISBURSEMENT'
  | 'EMI_RECEIVED'           | 'EMI_MISSED'           | 'DPD_EXCEEDED_90'
  | 'FULL_PAYMENT_RECEIVED'  | 'CONFIRM_CLOSURE'      | 'INITIATE_WRITEOFF'
  | 'FORECLOSE';

function transition(state: LoanState, event: LoanEvent): LoanState {
  switch (state) {
    case 'APPLICATION':
      if (event === 'PASS_SCREENING')  return 'CREDIT_ASSESSMENT';
      if (event === 'FAIL_SCREENING')  return 'REJECTED';
      break;
    case 'CREDIT_ASSESSMENT':
      if (event === 'APPROVE_LOAN')    return 'APPROVED';
      if (event === 'REJECT_LOAN')     return 'REJECTED';
      break;
    // ... and so on
  }
  throw new InvalidTransitionError(`No transition: ${state} + ${event}`);
}
```

Works for 5–8 states. Becomes unmanageable at 12+ states with guards.

**Pattern 2 — Transition table (recommended for production):**

```typescript
interface TransitionDefinition {
  targetState: LoanState;
  guard?:      (loan: Loan, context: TransitionContext) => boolean;
  action?:     (loan: Loan, context: TransitionContext) => Promise<void>;
}

type TransitionTable = {
  [S in LoanState]?: {
    [E in LoanEvent]?: TransitionDefinition;
  };
};

const TRANSITION_TABLE: TransitionTable = {
  APPLICATION: {
    PASS_SCREENING: {
      targetState: 'CREDIT_ASSESSMENT',
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.cibilService.pullReport(loan.loanId);
        await ctx.notificationService.send(loan.customerId, 'APPLICATION_UNDER_REVIEW');
      },
    },
    FAIL_SCREENING: {
      targetState: 'REJECTED',
      guard: (loan) => loan.screeningScore < SCREENING_THRESHOLD,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.notificationService.send(loan.customerId, 'APPLICATION_REJECTED');
      },
    },
  },
  CREDIT_ASSESSMENT: {
    APPROVE_LOAN: {
      targetState: 'APPROVED',
      guard: (loan) => loan.creditScore >= loan.product.minCreditScore
                    && loan.dtiRatio <= loan.product.maxDTI,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.sanctionLetterService.generate(loan.loanId);
      },
    },
    REJECT_LOAN: {
      targetState: 'REJECTED',
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.cibilService.reportInquiry(loan.loanId, 'REJECTED');
      },
    },
  },
  OFFER_ACCEPTED: {
    CONFIRM_DISBURSEMENT: {
      targetState: 'DISBURSED',
      guard: (loan) => loan.kycStatus === 'COMPLETE'
                    && loan.bankAccountVerified === true
                    && loan.agreementSigned === true,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.ledgerService.recordDisbursement(loan);
        await ctx.emiScheduler.createSchedule(loan);
        await ctx.payoutService.initiate(loan.disbursementAccountId, loan.approvedAmountPaise);
      },
    },
  },
  ACTIVE: {
    EMI_MISSED: {
      targetState: 'OVERDUE',
      guard: (loan, ctx) => ctx.dpd > loan.gracePeriodDays,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.collectionsService.openCase(loan.loanId);
        await ctx.notificationService.send(loan.customerId, 'EMI_OVERDUE');
      },
    },
    FORECLOSE: {
      targetState: 'FORECLOSED',
      guard: (loan) => loan.lockInPeriodComplete === true,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        const foreclosureCharges = await ctx.feeService.calculateForeclosure(loan);
        await ctx.ledgerService.recordForeclosure(loan, foreclosureCharges);
      },
    },
  },
  OVERDUE: {
    EMI_RECEIVED: {
      targetState: 'ACTIVE',
      guard: (loan) => loan.totalOutstandingOverduePaise === 0n,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.collectionsService.closeCase(loan.loanId);
        await ctx.cibilService.updateAccountStatus(loan.loanId, 'REGULAR');
      },
    },
    DPD_EXCEEDED_90: {
      targetState: 'NPA',
      guard: (loan, ctx) => ctx.dpd > 90,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.rbiReportingService.classifyNPA(loan.loanId);
        await ctx.provisioningService.setProvision(loan.loanId, 0.10);
      },
    },
  },
  REPAID: {
    CONFIRM_CLOSURE: {
      targetState: 'CLOSED',
      guard: (loan) => loan.outstandingBalancePaise === 0n,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.nocService.generate(loan.loanId);
        await ctx.cibilService.reportClosed(loan.loanId);
        if (loan.collateralId) await ctx.collateralService.release(loan.collateralId);
      },
    },
  },
};
```

**Pattern 3 — XState library (for complex hierarchical / parallel states):**

For loan products with sub-states (e.g., `OVERDUE` with nested `DPD_1_30`, `DPD_31_60`, `DPD_61_90`), the XState library provides hierarchical state machines with guards, actions, and services in a formalized way. Recommended when the state machine has 15+ states or requires parallel state regions (e.g., loan lifecycle AND collateral lifecycle running concurrently).

**Q6. How do you execute a state transition atomically in the database?**

A: A state transition in a loan system has multiple side effects — the state column changes, a transition history record is appended, ledger entries are written, notifications are queued. All of these must succeed or fail atomically, or the loan ends up in an inconsistent state (state changed but ledger not updated, or ledger updated but state not changed).

**Atomic transition with optimistic locking:**

```typescript
class LoanStateMachine {
  async transition(
    loanId:  string,
    event:   LoanEvent,
    context: TransitionContext,
  ): Promise<Loan> {
    return await db.transaction(async (trx) => {
      // 1. Load loan with a row-level lock — prevents concurrent transitions
      const loan = await trx('loans')
        .where({ loan_id: loanId })
        .forUpdate()   // SELECT ... FOR UPDATE
        .first();

      if (!loan) throw new LoanNotFoundError(loanId);

      // 2. Look up the transition definition
      const transitionDef = TRANSITION_TABLE[loan.status]?.[event];
      if (!transitionDef) {
        throw new InvalidTransitionError(
          `No transition defined: state=${loan.status} event=${event}`
        );
      }

      // 3. Evaluate guard
      if (transitionDef.guard && !transitionDef.guard(loan, context)) {
        throw new TransitionGuardFailedError(
          `Guard failed for: state=${loan.status} event=${event}`
        );
      }

      const previousState = loan.status;
      const nextState     = transitionDef.targetState;

      // 4. Update the loan state atomically
      await trx('loans')
        .where({ loan_id: loanId, status: previousState })  // optimistic check
        .update({
          status:           nextState,
          status_updated_at: new Date(),
          version:          loan.version + 1,
        });

      // 5. Append to transition history (immutable audit log)
      await trx('loan_state_transitions').insert({
        loan_id:        loanId,
        from_state:     previousState,
        to_state:       nextState,
        event:          event,
        triggered_by:   context.triggeredBy,    // 'SYSTEM' | 'USER' | 'AGENT'
        triggered_by_id: context.triggeredById,
        context_data:   JSON.stringify(context.data),  // DPD value, payment amount, etc.
        transitioned_at: new Date(),
      });

      // 6. Execute side-effect actions within the same transaction where possible
      // (ledger writes, fee calculations)
      // Queue external calls (notifications, CIBIL) for after-commit execution
      if (transitionDef.action) {
        await transitionDef.action({ ...loan, status: nextState }, context);
      }

      return { ...loan, status: nextState, version: loan.version + 1 };
    });
  }
}
```

**Why `SELECT FOR UPDATE`:** Without a row lock, two concurrent events (e.g., an EMI payment and an NPA classification job running simultaneously) can both read `OVERDUE`, both find valid transitions, and both write conflicting next states. The last write wins — one transition is silently lost. `FOR UPDATE` serializes access to this loan's row.

**Why the optimistic check in `UPDATE`:** Even with `FOR UPDATE`, the `WHERE status = previousState` in the update serves as a final consistency check — if another transaction somehow changed the state between the SELECT and the UPDATE (impossible with FOR UPDATE but defensive), this WHERE clause would match zero rows and the update would fail cleanly.

**Q7. What is a state transition history table, and what must it capture?**

A: The transition history is an **append-only audit log** of every state change a loan has ever undergone. It is not the current state (that's the `status` column on the `loans` table) — it is the complete lineage of how the loan arrived at its current state.

**Schema:**

```sql
CREATE TABLE loan_state_transitions (
    transition_id    UUID        PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    loan_id          UUID        NOT NULL REFERENCES loans(loan_id),
    from_state       TEXT        NOT NULL,
    to_state         TEXT        NOT NULL,
    event            TEXT        NOT NULL,
    triggered_by     TEXT        NOT NULL,   -- 'SYSTEM' | 'CUSTOMER' | 'AGENT' | 'SCHEDULER'
    triggered_by_id  TEXT,                   -- user_id, agent_id, or job_id
    context_data     JSONB,                  -- DPD at time of transition, payment amount, etc.
    transitioned_at  TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
    -- Prevent duplicate transitions from idempotency failures
    UNIQUE (loan_id, event, transitioned_at)
);

CREATE INDEX idx_transitions_loan_id   ON loan_state_transitions (loan_id, transitioned_at DESC);
CREATE INDEX idx_transitions_to_state  ON loan_state_transitions (to_state, transitioned_at DESC);
CREATE INDEX idx_transitions_event     ON loan_state_transitions (event, transitioned_at DESC);
```

**What to capture in `context_data`:**


**What the transition history enables:**
- **Audit:** "Show me every state this loan has been in and why" — answerable in one query.
- **Regulatory reporting:** RBI's SMA (Special Mention Account) reporting requires knowing the date a loan first crossed DPD thresholds — this is in the history table.
- **Debugging:** "The loan is in NPA but the customer claims they paid — show me every transition with timestamps" — the history table is the ground truth.
- **Analytics:** "What percentage of loans that entered OVERDUE returned to ACTIVE within 30 days?" — a pure query on the history table.

## Section 3: Complex Transition Scenarios

**Q8. How do you handle the OVERDUE state with sub-states for DPD buckets?**

A: `OVERDUE` is not a single homogeneous state — the regulatory treatment, provisioning requirements, and collections strategy differ significantly based on how many days past due the loan is. Two design approaches:

**Approach A — Flat states (simpler, less expressive):**

```
OVERDUE_DPD_1_30
OVERDUE_DPD_31_60
OVERDUE_DPD_61_90
NPA  (DPD > 90)
```

Each is a fully distinct state in the FSM. Transitions between them are driven by the daily DPD scheduler. Simple to query (`WHERE status = 'OVERDUE_DPD_31_60'`) but results in many states and many transition rows.

**Approach B — Single OVERDUE state with DPD as metadata (recommended):**

```sql
-- loans table
ALTER TABLE loans ADD COLUMN dpd INT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0;
ALTER TABLE loans ADD COLUMN sma_category TEXT;  -- NULL | 'SMA_0' | 'SMA_1' | 'SMA_2'
```

The FSM has one `OVERDUE` state. The `dpd` column and `sma_category` column are updated by a daily job without changing the state. State changes only when the loan crosses into `NPA` (DPD > 90) or returns to `ACTIVE` (DPD = 0).

```typescript
// Daily DPD update job — does NOT fire state transitions for DPD bucket changes
// Only fires DPD_EXCEEDED_90 when NPA threshold is crossed
async function updateDPDForLoan(loanId: string): Promise<void> {
  const loan = await db('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).first();

  if (!['OVERDUE', 'NPA'].includes(loan.status)) return;

  const dpd = calculateDPD(loan.firstOverdueDueDate, new Date());
  const smaCategory = getSMACategory(dpd);

  await db('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).update({ dpd, sma_category: smaCategory });

  // Only fire FSM transition at the NPA threshold
  if (dpd > 90 && loan.status === 'OVERDUE') {
    await loanStateMachine.transition(loanId, 'DPD_EXCEEDED_90', {
      triggeredBy:   'SYSTEM',
      triggeredById: 'dpd_scheduler',
      data: { dpd, smaCategory },
    });
  }
}

function getSMACategory(dpd: number): string | null {
  if (dpd === 0)       return null;          // Current / ACTIVE
  if (dpd <= 30)       return 'SMA_0';       // RBI SMA-0: 1–30 DPD
  if (dpd <= 60)       return 'SMA_1';       // RBI SMA-1: 31–60 DPD
  if (dpd <= 90)       return 'SMA_2';       // RBI SMA-2: 61–90 DPD
  return 'NPA';                              // > 90 DPD
}
```

**RBI SMA classification:**

| Category | DPD | Regulatory significance |
|---|---|---|
| SMA-0 | 1–30 | Early warning; lender must flag in CRILC reporting |
| SMA-1 | 31–60 | Heightened monitoring; collections escalation |
| SMA-2 | 61–90 | Pre-NPA; provisioning starts; regulatory scrutiny |
| Sub-Standard NPA | 91–365 | 15%+ provisioning; CIBIL tagged NPA |
| Doubtful NPA | 366–1095 | 25%–100% provisioning by age |
| Loss NPA | > 1095 or unrecoverable | 100% provisioning; write-off candidate |

**Q9. How do you handle a loan that receives a partial payment while in OVERDUE state?**

A: Partial payment is the most nuanced transition scenario because the loan's state depends on how much was received, not just the fact that a payment arrived.

**Payment application order:** Before determining the new state, the payment must be applied in the correct order per the loan agreement (typically interest-first):

```typescript
function applyPayment(
  loan:           LoanWithSchedule,
  paymentPaise:   bigint,
): PaymentAllocation {
  let remaining = paymentPaise;
  const allocation = {
    penaltyCleared:   0n,
    interestCleared:  0n,
    principalCleared: 0n,
    unallocated:      0n,
  };

  // Order: penalty charges → overdue interest → overdue principal → future interest → future principal
  const overdueCharges = calculateOverdueCharges(loan);

  if (remaining >= overdueCharges.penaltyPaise) {
    allocation.penaltyCleared = overdueCharges.penaltyPaise;
    remaining -= overdueCharges.penaltyPaise;
  } else {
    allocation.penaltyCleared = remaining;
    remaining = 0n;
  }

  if (remaining >= overdueCharges.interestPaise) {
    allocation.interestCleared = overdueCharges.interestPaise;
    remaining -= overdueCharges.interestPaise;
  } else {
    allocation.interestCleared = remaining;
    remaining = 0n;
  }

  if (remaining >= overdueCharges.principalPaise) {
    allocation.principalCleared = overdueCharges.principalPaise;
    remaining -= overdueCharges.principalPaise;
  } else {
    allocation.principalCleared = remaining;
    remaining = 0n;
  }

  allocation.unallocated = remaining;
  return allocation;
}
```

**State transition decision after payment:**

```typescript
async function handlePaymentReceived(
  loanId:       string,
  paymentPaise: bigint,
): Promise<void> {
  const loan = await db('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).forUpdate().first();
  const allocation = applyPayment(loan, paymentPaise);

  // Record the payment in ledger
  await ledgerService.recordEMIPayment(loanId, paymentPaise, allocation);

  // Recalculate outstanding overdue balance after this payment
  const remainingOverdue = await calculateTotalOverdue(loanId);
  const remainingPrincipal = await calculateOutstandingPrincipal(loanId);

  if (remainingPrincipal === 0n && remainingOverdue === 0n) {
    // Full loan repaid
    await loanStateMachine.transition(loanId, 'FULL_PAYMENT_RECEIVED', {
      triggeredBy: 'SYSTEM',
      data: { paymentPaise, allocation },
    });
  } else if (remainingOverdue === 0n && loan.status === 'OVERDUE') {
    // All overdue cleared — return to ACTIVE
    await loanStateMachine.transition(loanId, 'EMI_RECEIVED', {
      triggeredBy: 'SYSTEM',
      data: { paymentPaise, allocation, newDPD: 0 },
    });
  } else if (loan.status === 'OVERDUE' || loan.status === 'NPA') {
    // Partial payment — stay in current state, update DPD / overdue balance
    await db('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).update({
      overdue_amount_paise: remainingOverdue,
      dpd:                  loan.dpd,  // DPD unchanged by partial payment
      last_payment_date:    new Date(),
      last_payment_paise:   paymentPaise,
    });
    // No state transition — loan remains OVERDUE or NPA
    // Record payment event in transition history without state change
    await db('loan_payment_events').insert({
      loan_id:       loanId,
      payment_paise: paymentPaise,
      allocation:    allocation,
      loan_status:   loan.status,
      dpd_at_payment: loan.dpd,
      paid_at:       new Date(),
    });
  }
}
```

**Key rule:** DPD resets to zero only when all overdue EMIs (including penalties and accrued interest) are fully cleared. A partial payment that clears some overdue but not all does not reduce DPD — it only reduces the outstanding overdue balance.

**Q10. How do you handle the NPA → ACTIVE (or REPAID) transition, which requires going through intermediate states?**

A: A loan in `NPA` state can theoretically recover if the borrower pays all outstanding amounts. But jumping directly from `NPA` → `ACTIVE` violates RBI's IRACP upgrade norms — there is a mandatory observation period before an NPA can be upgraded to a Standard asset.

**RBI's NPA upgrade conditions:**

- A Sub-Standard account can be upgraded to Standard only if all outstanding overdue amounts (including penal interest and charges) are fully paid.
- The account must demonstrate satisfactory repayment behavior for a specified observation period after the overdue clearance (typically one full quarter of regular payments).
- The upgrade must be reported to CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits) and reflected in the next SMA/NPA report.

**State machine design for NPA recovery:**

```
NPA
 └─ [FULL_OVERDUE_CLEARED / all_overdue_paid = true] ──→ NPA_WATCHLIST
     └─ [OBSERVATION_PERIOD_COMPLETE / 3_consecutive_regular_emis = true] ──→ ACTIVE
         OR
     └─ [EMI_MISSED / dpd > grace_period] ──→ NPA  (re-classification)
```

**Implementation:**

```typescript
// NPA watchlist: intermediate state during observation period
const NPA_TRANSITIONS = {
  NPA: {
    FULL_OVERDUE_CLEARED: {
      targetState: 'NPA_WATCHLIST',
      guard: (loan) => loan.totalOutstandingOverduePaise === 0n,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.rbiReportingService.reportOverdueClearance(loan.loanId);
        await ctx.collectionsService.setWatchlistMode(loan.loanId);
        // Reset DPD to 0 — but NPA classification remains until observation complete
        await ctx.loanRepository.updateDPD(loan.loanId, 0);
      },
    },
    FULL_PAYMENT_RECEIVED: {
      // Exceptional: entire outstanding including principal paid in one shot
      targetState: 'REPAID',
      guard: (loan) => loan.outstandingPrincipalPaise === 0n
                    && loan.outstandingInterestPaise === 0n
                    && loan.penaltyPaise === 0n,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.rbiReportingService.reportNPARecovery(loan.loanId);
      },
    },
  },
  NPA_WATCHLIST: {
    OBSERVATION_COMPLETE: {
      targetState: 'ACTIVE',
      guard: (loan) => loan.consecutiveRegularEMIs >= 3  // 3 months of clean repayment
                    && loan.dpd === 0,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.cibilService.upgradeAccountStatus(loan.loanId, 'STANDARD');
        await ctx.rbiReportingService.reportUpgrade(loan.loanId, 'NPA_TO_STANDARD');
        await ctx.provisioningService.clearProvision(loan.loanId);
      },
    },
    EMI_MISSED: {
      targetState: 'NPA',   // Falls back to NPA immediately
      guard: (loan, ctx) => ctx.dpd > loan.gracePeriodDays,
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        await ctx.rbiReportingService.reportWatchlistFailure(loan.loanId);
      },
    },
  },
};
```

**Q11. How do you prevent concurrent state transitions from corrupting the loan state?**

A: Concurrent transitions are one of the most dangerous failure modes in a loan state machine. Consider a loan in `OVERDUE` state:

- **Event A:** An EMI payment arrives — should transition to `ACTIVE`.
- **Event B:** The daily DPD scheduler runs simultaneously — DPD > 90, should transition to `NPA`.

Without concurrency control, both transitions may read `OVERDUE`, both pass their guards, and the last write wins — the loan ends up in either `ACTIVE` or `NPA` depending on timing, not on which event actually happened first. Either outcome may be wrong.

**Three-layer defense:**

**Layer 1 — Database row lock (`SELECT FOR UPDATE`):**
```sql
-- Prevents concurrent reads of the same loan row during transition
SELECT * FROM loans WHERE loan_id = $1 FOR UPDATE;
```
Any other transaction attempting to read this row will block until the first transaction commits. This is the primary defense.

**Layer 2 — Optimistic version check:**
```sql
-- The UPDATE only succeeds if the version hasn't changed since we read it
UPDATE loans
SET status = $1, version = version + 1, status_updated_at = now()
WHERE loan_id = $2 AND version = $3 AND status = $4;
-- If rowCount = 0, another transaction won — throw ConcurrentModificationError
```

**Layer 3 — Application-level event deduplication:**
```typescript
// Use a Redis distributed lock before even entering the DB transaction
async function transitionWithLock(loanId: string, event: LoanEvent): Promise<void> {
  const lockKey  = `loan_transition_lock:${loanId}`;
  const lockToken = await redis.set(lockKey, '1', 'NX', 'PX', 30_000);  // 30s TTL

  if (!lockToken) {
    throw new TransitionAlreadyInProgressError(
      `Another transition is in progress for loan ${loanId}`
    );
  }

  try {
    await loanStateMachine.transition(loanId, event, context);
  } finally {
    await redis.del(lockKey);
  }
}
```

**Event ordering with a queue:** For event-driven architectures where loan events arrive via Kafka, configure the Kafka consumer to use `loan_id` as the partition key — all events for the same loan are processed by the same consumer in the order they were produced. This eliminates concurrent processing of the same loan at the application layer.

```typescript
// Kafka consumer: partition by loan_id ensures order and eliminates concurrent processing
consumer.subscribe({ topic: 'loan-events' });
// Events with the same loan_id always land in the same partition
// and are processed sequentially by the same consumer instance
```

## Section 4: Regulatory States & Reporting

**Q12. What are RBI's IRACP norms, and how do they map to states in your FSM?**

A: **IRACP (Income Recognition and Asset Classification and Provisioning)** are RBI guidelines that define how regulated lenders must classify loans based on their repayment performance and set aside provisions (capital reserves) against potential losses.

**Asset classification under IRACP:**

| RBI Classification | DPD | Your FSM State | Minimum Provisioning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard Asset | DPD = 0 | `ACTIVE` | 0.25% (general) |
| SMA-0 | 1–30 DPD | `OVERDUE` (sma_category = 'SMA_0') | 0.25% |
| SMA-1 | 31–60 DPD | `OVERDUE` (sma_category = 'SMA_1') | 0.25% |
| SMA-2 | 61–90 DPD | `OVERDUE` (sma_category = 'SMA_2') | 0.25% |
| Sub-Standard NPA | 91–365 DPD | `NPA` (npa_type = 'SUB_STANDARD') | 15% (secured) / 25% (unsecured) |
| Doubtful-1 NPA | 366–730 DPD | `NPA` (npa_type = 'DOUBTFUL_1') | 25% (secured) / 100% (unsecured) |
| Doubtful-2 NPA | 731–1095 DPD | `NPA` (npa_type = 'DOUBTFUL_2') | 40% (secured) / 100% (unsecured) |
| Doubtful-3 NPA | > 1095 DPD | `NPA` (npa_type = 'DOUBTFUL_3') | 100% |
| Loss Asset | Identified as unrecoverable | `WRITTEN_OFF` | 100% |

**CRILC reporting obligations triggered by state transitions:**

```typescript
// CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits) — mandatory for loans > ₹5 crore
// Reported to RBI on a monthly basis
async function generateCRILCReport(reportingDate: Date): Promise<CRILCReport> {
  const largeLoans = await db('loans')
    .where('sanctioned_amount_paise', '>=', 5_00_00_000n)  // ₹5 crore
    .where('status', '!=', 'CANCELLED');

  return largeLoans.map(loan => ({
    borrowerPAN:     loan.borrower_pan,
    loanId:          loan.loan_id,
    sanctionedAmount: loan.sanctioned_amount_paise,
    outstanding:     loan.outstanding_principal_paise,
    smaCategory:     loan.sma_category,
    npaType:         loan.npa_type,
    dpd:             loan.dpd,
    asOfDate:        reportingDate,
  }));
}
```

**Q13. What is the WRITTEN_OFF state, and why does it not mean the loan is gone?**

A: **Write-off** is an accounting action — the lender removes the loan from its books as an asset and recognizes it as a 100% loss. But write-off is **not** a legal waiver of the debt. The borrower still owes the money; the lender's legal right to recover it is unchanged.

**Why write off at all?**
- Regulatory capital: a 100% provisioned loan still occupies capital. Writing it off frees that capital for new lending.
- Balance sheet cleanliness: loss assets on the books distort asset quality metrics.
- Tax benefit: write-offs are tax-deductible in certain jurisdictions.

**Post-write-off states:**

```
WRITTEN_OFF
  └─ [RECOVERY_PAYMENT_RECEIVED] ──→ WRITTEN_OFF (partial recovery; balance updated)
  └─ [FULL_RECOVERY_COMPLETE]    ──→ RECOVERED   (all principal recovered; surprise outcome)
  └─ [LEGAL_SETTLEMENT]          ──→ SETTLED     (OTS — One Time Settlement agreed)
```

**Ledger treatment on write-off:**

```
Journal Entry: "Loan Write-Off"
Reference: loan_id = LOAN-001

  DEBIT   Loan Loss Reserve (contra-asset)      5,000,000 paise  [clear the provision]
  DEBIT   Loan Loss Expense (5003)                         0      [if under-provisioned: additional expense]
  CREDIT  Receivables — Loan Principal (1030)  5,000,000 paise   [remove from assets]

  Check: Debits = Credits ✓
```

**Post-write-off recovery entry:**

```
Journal Entry: "NPA Recovery — LOAN-001"
Reference: recovery_id = REC-001

  DEBIT   Bank Settlement Account (1001)        200,000 paise    [cash received from borrower]
  CREDIT  Loan Loss Recovery Income (4004)      200,000 paise    [income recognized on recovery]

  Note: does NOT touch Receivables — Loan Principal (already written off)
  Recovery is income, not a reversal of the original receivable
```

## Section 5: State Machine Testing

**Q14. How do you test a loan state machine comprehensively?**

A: A state machine has a finite, enumerable set of valid and invalid transitions. Testing it is methodical: enumerate all valid transitions, enumerate all invalid transitions, and test both.

**Test structure:**

```typescript
describe('LoanStateMachine', () => {

  // 1. Valid transitions: every row in the transition table
  describe('valid transitions', () => {
    it('APPLICATION → CREDIT_ASSESSMENT on PASS_SCREENING', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({ status: 'APPLICATION' });
      const result = await loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'PASS_SCREENING', mockContext);
      expect(result.status).toBe('CREDIT_ASSESSMENT');
      const history = await getTransitionHistory(loan.loanId);
      expect(history.at(-1)).toMatchObject({
        from_state: 'APPLICATION',
        to_state:   'CREDIT_ASSESSMENT',
        event:      'PASS_SCREENING',
      });
    });

    it('OVERDUE → ACTIVE on EMI_RECEIVED when all overdue cleared', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({ status: 'OVERDUE', totalOutstandingOverduePaise: 0n });
      const result = await loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'EMI_RECEIVED', mockContext);
      expect(result.status).toBe('ACTIVE');
    });
  });

  // 2. Invalid transitions: non-existent edges in the transition table
  describe('invalid transitions', () => {
    it('throws InvalidTransitionError: APPLICATION + DPD_EXCEEDED_90', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({ status: 'APPLICATION' });
      await expect(
        loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'DPD_EXCEEDED_90', mockContext)
      ).rejects.toThrow(InvalidTransitionError);
      // State must remain unchanged
      const unchanged = await getLoan(loan.loanId);
      expect(unchanged.status).toBe('APPLICATION');
    });

    it('throws InvalidTransitionError: CLOSED + EMI_MISSED', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({ status: 'CLOSED' });
      await expect(
        loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'EMI_MISSED', mockContext)
      ).rejects.toThrow(InvalidTransitionError);
    });
  });

  // 3. Guard failures: valid edge but guard condition not met
  describe('guard failures', () => {
    it('throws TransitionGuardFailedError: OFFER_ACCEPTED → DISBURSED when KYC incomplete', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({
        status:              'OFFER_ACCEPTED',
        kycStatus:           'PENDING',          // guard fails
        bankAccountVerified: true,
        agreementSigned:     true,
      });
      await expect(
        loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'CONFIRM_DISBURSEMENT', mockContext)
      ).rejects.toThrow(TransitionGuardFailedError);
    });

    it('stays OVERDUE (not ACTIVE) on partial payment', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({
        status: 'OVERDUE',
        totalOutstandingOverduePaise: 100_000n,
      });
      // Partial payment clears 50% — guard (all_overdue_cleared) fails
      await handlePaymentReceived(loan.loanId, 50_000n);
      const updated = await getLoan(loan.loanId);
      expect(updated.status).toBe('OVERDUE');   // not ACTIVE
      expect(updated.overdue_amount_paise).toBe(50_000n);
    });
  });

  // 4. Concurrency tests: two simultaneous transitions
  describe('concurrent transition safety', () => {
    it('prevents double-transition under concurrency', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoan({ status: 'ACTIVE' });

      // Fire two conflicting events simultaneously
      const [result1, result2] = await Promise.allSettled([
        loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'EMI_MISSED', { ...mockContext, dpd: 5 }),
        loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loanId, 'EMI_MISSED', { ...mockContext, dpd: 5 }),
      ]);

      // Exactly one must succeed
      const successes = [result1, result2].filter(r => r.status === 'fulfilled');
      const failures  = [result1, result2].filter(r => r.status === 'rejected');
      expect(successes).toHaveLength(1);
      expect(failures).toHaveLength(1);

      // Exactly one history entry
      const history = await getTransitionHistory(loan.loanId);
      const emiMissedEntries = history.filter(h => h.event === 'EMI_MISSED');
      expect(emiMissedEntries).toHaveLength(1);
    });
  });

  // 5. Property test: transition history must always be consistent with current state
  describe('invariants', () => {
    it('loan.status always equals the to_state of the last transition', async () => {
      const loan = await createTestLoanWithHistory();
      const history = await getTransitionHistory(loan.loanId);
      const lastTransition = history.at(-1);
      expect(loan.status).toBe(lastTransition?.to_state ?? 'APPLICATION');
    });
  });
});
```

**Transition coverage matrix (generate from transition table):**

## Section 6: System Design Scenario Questions

**Q15. "Design the state machine for a BNPL (Buy Now Pay Later) product with a 3-installment repayment."**

A: BNPL differs from a term loan in that the tenure is very short (typically 15–90 days), the amounts are small (₹500–₹50,000), the approval is near-instant (BRE-driven, no manual underwriting), and the customer expects a seamless in-checkout experience.

**BNPL-specific states:**

```
CHECKOUT_INITIATED   Customer clicked "Pay with BNPL" at merchant checkout
ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED  BRE ran; customer eligible; offer generated in < 2 seconds
OFFER_PRESENTED      Offer shown in merchant checkout; customer reviewing
OFFER_ACCEPTED       Customer accepted; T&C signed; mandate created
ORDER_CONFIRMED      Merchant payment disbursed (merchant receives funds immediately)
INSTALLMENT_1_PENDING  First installment due (typically at T+0 or T+15)
INSTALLMENT_1_PAID
INSTALLMENT_2_PENDING
INSTALLMENT_2_PAID
INSTALLMENT_3_PENDING
INSTALLMENT_3_PAID     ← Terminal success: loan fully repaid
OVERDUE              Any installment missed beyond grace period
DEFAULTED            DPD > 60 (BNPL products have shorter NPA thresholds)
WRITTEN_OFF
CLOSED
```

**Key differences from term loan FSM:**

```typescript
const BNPL_TRANSITIONS = {

      guard: (loan, ctx) => ctx.breDecision === 'APPROVED'
                         && ctx.breLatencyMs < 2000,
    },
    ELIGIBILITY_FAILED: {
      targetState: 'REJECTED',
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        // Do not expose rejection reason at checkout — just say "not available"
        await ctx.merchantCallbackService.notifyDecline(loan.merchantOrderId);
      },
    },
  },
  ORDER_CONFIRMED: {
    // Automatically move to first installment pending after merchant disbursement
    DISBURSEMENT_COMPLETE: {
      targetState: 'INSTALLMENT_1_PENDING',
      action: async (loan, ctx) => {
        // Schedule installment due dates
        await ctx.emiScheduler.scheduleInstallments(loan.loanId, [
          { installmentNumber: 1, dueDate: addDays(new Date(), 0)  },  // due immediately or T+15
          { installmentNumber: 2, dueDate: addDays(new Date(), 30) },
          { installmentNumber: 3, dueDate: addDays(new Date(), 60) },
        ]);
        // Set up UPI AutoPay mandate for auto-debit
        await ctx.upiMandateService.setup(loan.loanId, loan.customerId);
      },
    },
  },
  // Installment transitions are symmetric: PENDING → PAID → next PENDING
  INSTALLMENT_1_PAID: {
    NEXT_INSTALLMENT: {
      targetState: 'INSTALLMENT_2_PENDING',
    },
  },
  INSTALLMENT_2_PAID: {
    NEXT_INSTALLMENT: {
      targetState: 'INSTALLMENT_3_PENDING',
    },
  },
```

**Q16. "A loan is stuck in DISBURSED state — the disbursement payout was initiated but the bank confirmation never arrived. How do you handle this?"**

A: A loan stuck in `DISBURSED` state without bank confirmation is a limbo state — the customer may or may not have received the money, but your system can't prove either way. This is the loan equivalent of the "deemed transaction" problem in UPI.

**Root causes:**
- Payout API call timed out after the bank accepted the request.
- Bank webhook with confirmation was delivered but your webhook handler threw an exception.
- Network partition between your system and the bank API during confirmation.

**Handling strategy:**

```typescript
// Scheduled job: find loans stuck in DISBURSED state > 30 minutes
async function reconcileStuckDisbursements(): Promise<void> {
  const stuckLoans = await db('loans')
    .where({ status: 'DISBURSED' })
    .where('status_updated_at', '<', new Date(Date.now() - 30 * 60 * 1000));

  for (const loan of stuckLoans) {
    const payout = await db('payouts').where({ loan_id: loan.loan_id }).first();

    if (!payout) {
      // No payout record — disbursement never initiated
      await loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loan_id, 'DISBURSEMENT_FAILED', {
        triggeredBy: 'RECONCILIATION_JOB',
        data: { reason: 'NO_PAYOUT_RECORD' },
      });
      continue;
    }

    // Query bank API for the payout status using RRN / UTR
    const bankStatus = await bankClient.queryPayoutStatus({
      rrn:    payout.bank_rrn,
      amount: payout.amount_paise,
    });

    switch (bankStatus.status) {
      case 'SUCCESS':
        // Money reached customer — move to ACTIVE
        await loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loan_id, 'DISBURSEMENT_CONFIRMED', {
          triggeredBy: 'RECONCILIATION_JOB',
          data: { utr: bankStatus.utr, settledAt: bankStatus.settledAt },
        });
        break;

      case 'FAILED':
      case 'RETURNED':
        // Money did not reach customer — cancel loan, reverse ledger
        await loanStateMachine.transition(loan.loan_id, 'DISBURSEMENT_FAILED', {
          triggeredBy: 'RECONCILIATION_JOB',
          data: { reason: bankStatus.failureReason },
        });
        await ledgerService.reverseDisbursement(loan.loan_id);
        break;

      case 'PENDING':
        // Still in transit — check again in next run
        logger.warn({ loanId: loan.loan_id }, 'Disbursement still pending');
        break;
    }
  }
}
```

**The state machine addition for disbursement failure:**

```
DISBURSED
  └─ [DISBURSEMENT_CONFIRMED / bank_utr_received = true] ──→ ACTIVE
  └─ [DISBURSEMENT_FAILED / bank_returned_funds = true]  ──→ CANCELLED
```

`CANCELLED` is the correct terminal state here — not `REJECTED` (which implies underwriting failure) and not `CLOSED` (which implies successful repayment). The loan was approved and attempted but never actually disbursed.

**Q17. "Walk me through how you'd audit every loan that transitioned to NPA in Q1 2024."**

A: This is a pure query on the transition history table — one of the core value propositions of having an explicit FSM with an append-only history log.

```sql
-- All loans that entered NPA state in Q1 2024
SELECT
    lt.loan_id,
    lt.from_state,
    lt.to_state,
    lt.event,
    lt.triggered_by,
    lt.context_data,
    lt.transitioned_at,
    -- How long was the loan in OVERDUE before becoming NPA?
    lt.transitioned_at - prev_lt.transitioned_at AS time_in_overdue,
    -- Current loan state (may have recovered since)
    l.status                                      AS current_status,
    l.dpd                                         AS current_dpd,
    -- Loan details
    l.sanctioned_amount_paise,
    l.outstanding_principal_paise,
    l.borrower_id
FROM loan_state_transitions lt
JOIN loan_state_transitions prev_lt
  ON prev_lt.loan_id  = lt.loan_id
  AND prev_lt.to_state = 'OVERDUE'
  -- Most recent OVERDUE entry before the NPA transition
  AND prev_lt.transitioned_at = (
    SELECT MAX(transitioned_at)
    FROM loan_state_transitions
    WHERE loan_id = lt.loan_id AND to_state = 'OVERDUE'
      AND transitioned_at < lt.transitioned_at
  )
JOIN loans l ON l.loan_id = lt.loan_id
WHERE lt.to_state         = 'NPA'
  AND lt.transitioned_at >= '2024-01-01'
  AND lt.transitioned_at <  '2024-04-01'
ORDER BY lt.transitioned_at;

-- Aggregate: how many NPAs came from each source state, broken by trigger
SELECT
    from_state,
    event,
    triggered_by,
    COUNT(*)                                      AS count,
    AVG((context_data->>'dpd')::INT)             AS avg_dpd_at_npa,
    SUM(l.sanctioned_amount_paise) / 100         AS total_sanctioned_rupees
FROM loan_state_transitions lt
JOIN loans l USING (loan_id)
WHERE lt.to_state         = 'NPA'
  AND lt.transitioned_at >= '2024-01-01'
  AND lt.transitioned_at <  '2024-04-01'
GROUP BY from_state, event, triggered_by
ORDER BY count DESC;
```

This is only possible because:
- Every transition was recorded at the time it happened (not reconstructed from current state).
- `context_data` captured the DPD value at the moment of transition.
- The `triggered_by` field identifies whether the NPA classification was system-driven (scheduler), agent-driven (collections team), or manual override.

Without a transition history table, answering this question would require reconstructing loan history from payment records, EMI schedules, and bank statements — a multi-day forensic exercise.

| **Testing approach** | All valid transitions + all invalid transitions + guard failures + concurrent transition safety + invariant: `status = last history to_state` |

**Tip for the interview:** At the 5 YOE level in a lending fintech, state machine questions appear in three forms: (1) "design a loan lifecycle" — they expect you to enumerate states, name terminal states correctly, and identify the OVERDUE → NPA threshold at 90 DPD per RBI IRACP; (2) "how do you prevent a loan from being in two states" — the answer is `SELECT FOR UPDATE` + a version check in the `UPDATE WHERE status = currentState` clause, not application-level locking alone; (3) "how do you audit which loans became NPA last quarter" — the answer is a transition history table, and you should be able to write the query on a whiteboard. Know the difference between `CANCELLED` (never disbursed) and `WRITTEN_OFF` (disbursed, never recovered) and `CLOSED` (disbursed, fully repaid) — confusing them suggests you haven't worked with a real lending system. Know that write-off is an accounting action, not debt forgiveness — recovery after write-off is income, not a reversal. And always connect the state machine design to its regulatory obligation: RBI's IRACP norms are not optional annotations on your FSM, they are the reason the states exist.
