# Lending Systems: Prepayment & Foreclosure Handling — Calculations, Charges & System Design — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

> **Domain:** Fintech / Lending Systems Architecture

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is prepayment, and how does it differ from a regular EMI payment?**

A: A **prepayment** is any payment made toward a loan's outstanding principal that is over and above the scheduled EMI amount. It reduces the principal balance ahead of schedule, which in turn affects the remaining interest obligation and the overall loan timeline.

A regular EMI payment is **scheduled** — it covers the exact interest accrued since the last payment date plus a portion of principal, as defined by the original amortization schedule. It does not change the loan's tenure or future EMI amounts (in a fixed-EMI product).

A prepayment is **unscheduled** — it is initiated by the borrower outside the regular EMI cycle, for any amount above zero (subject to minimum prepayment limits set in the loan agreement). Its effects depend on which of two options the borrower chooses:

- **Reduce EMI:** The outstanding principal is reduced; the amortization schedule is recalculated so that the same number of remaining installments now have a lower EMI. The tenure stays the same.
- **Reduce tenure:** The outstanding principal is reduced; the EMI amount stays the same, but fewer installments are needed to fully repay the loan. The tenure shortens.

The distinction between these two options is not a UI preference — it requires rebuilding the amortization schedule from scratch with different inputs, generating new ledger entries, and (in regulated products) issuing an amended repayment schedule to the borrower. The system must explicitly record which option the borrower chose, because it governs every future payment calculation.

---

**Q2. What is foreclosure, and how does it differ from prepayment?**

A: **Foreclosure** (also called full prepayment or pre-closure) is the act of repaying the entire outstanding loan balance in a single payment before the scheduled end of the tenure. After a successful foreclosure, the loan moves to a `FORECLOSED` or `CLOSED` terminal state, an NOC (No Objection Certificate) is issued, and no further EMIs are due.

The key differences:

| Property | Partial Prepayment | Foreclosure |
|---|---|---|
| **Amount** | A portion of outstanding principal | Entire outstanding balance (principal + accrued interest + foreclosure charges) |
| **Loan status after** | Remains `ACTIVE`; schedule rebuilt | `FORECLOSED` → `CLOSED` (terminal) |
| **EMI schedule** | Rebuilt with new tenure or new EMI | Cancelled — no future EMIs |
| **NOC issued** | No | Yes |
| **CIBIL reporting** | `ACTIVE` with updated outstanding | `Closed` / `Settled` |
| **Charges** | Prepayment fee (if applicable) | Foreclosure fee (if applicable; may differ from prepayment fee) |
| **Accrued interest** | Up to last EMI date (future interest not due) | Up to foreclosure date (exact daily accrual) |

**Regulatory dimension (RBI):** For floating-rate loans to individual borrowers, RBI's guidelines prohibit foreclosure charges — lenders cannot penalize a borrower for prepaying a floating-rate loan. For fixed-rate loans, foreclosure charges are permitted but must be disclosed in the Key Fact Statement (KFS) at the time of loan sanction. Failing to follow this exposes the lender to regulatory penalties.

**Q3. What is the outstanding balance at foreclosure, and how is it computed?**

A: The **foreclosure outstanding** is the total amount the borrower must pay to fully extinguish their debt on a specific date. It has up to five components:

```
Foreclosure Outstanding =
    Outstanding Principal
  + Accrued Interest (from last EMI date to foreclosure date)
  + Overdue EMIs (if any — principal + interest portions not yet paid)
  + Penal / Late Payment Charges (if in overdue)
  + Foreclosure Fee (if applicable per loan agreement)
  - Prepayments Already Made (already applied to principal)
```

**Accrued interest calculation (daily accrual method):**

```
Accrued Interest = Outstanding Principal × (Annual Rate / 365) × Days Since Last EMI
```

This is critical: the borrower does not owe interest for the *remaining* tenure — only for the days since the last EMI was paid up to the foreclosure date. Future EMIs included interest that is no longer going to accrue because the principal will be extinguished. Charging future interest on foreclosure is an error that constitutes unjust enrichment.

**Example:**

```
Loan: ₹5,00,000 principal, 12% annual interest, 24-month tenure
Last EMI paid: 1 Feb 2024
Foreclosure date: 20 Feb 2024 (19 days of accrued interest)
Outstanding principal after last EMI: ₹3,20,456

Accrued interest:
  = 3,20,456 × (0.12 / 365) × 19
  = 3,20,456 × 0.000328767 × 19
  = 3,20,456 × 0.006246573
  = 2,001.45 → rounded to 2,001 paise using ROUND_HALF_UP

Foreclosure fee (2% of outstanding principal, fixed-rate loan):
  = 3,20,456 × 0.02 = 6,409.12 → 6,409 paise

Foreclosure outstanding:
  = 3,20,456 + 2,001 + 6,409
  = 3,28,866 paise = ₹3,288.66
```

All arithmetic uses `decimal.js` or `BigDecimal` — never floating-point. Accrued interest is computed in paise from the start, not computed in rupees and converted.

**Q4. What is an amortization schedule, and why must it be rebuilt on every prepayment?**

A: An **amortization schedule** is a complete table of all future payments for a loan, showing for each installment: the due date, total EMI amount, interest component, principal component, and outstanding balance after payment. It is the contractual repayment plan — what the borrower agreed to pay and when.

**Why the schedule must be rebuilt on prepayment:**

A standard fixed-rate reducing-balance loan's EMI is calculated from three inputs: principal (`P`), monthly interest rate (`r`), and number of installments (`n`). The formula:

```
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
```

When a prepayment reduces `P` while `n` or the EMI amount changes (depending on which option the borrower chose), the formula must be reapplied with the new inputs. Every remaining installment's interest component, principal component, and due balance changes — because each installment's interest is computed on the *outstanding balance after the previous installment*, which is now different.

**Example of why a partial rebuild is wrong:**

Original schedule, EMI 5 of 24:
- Outstanding before EMI 5: ₹3,50,000
- EMI 5 interest: ₹3,500 (at 12% annual = 1%/month)
- EMI 5 principal: ₹12,500

After a ₹50,000 prepayment on the EMI 5 date:
- New outstanding after prepayment: ₹2,87,500 (not ₹3,37,500 as in the old schedule)
- EMI 6 interest must be computed on ₹2,87,500, not ₹3,37,500
- Difference: ₹500/month in interest — ₹500 × remaining 19 months = ₹9,500 total interest saved

If the old schedule is kept without rebuilding, the borrower is over-charged ₹9,500 in total interest. This is not a rounding error — it is a systematic calculation defect.

## Section 2: Prepayment Calculations

**Q5. How do you implement partial prepayment with the "reduce tenure" option?**

A: Reduce-tenure prepayment keeps the EMI constant and recalculates how many installments remain. The new tenure is the number of periods needed to repay the new outstanding principal at the same EMI and rate.

```typescript
import Decimal from 'decimal.js';

interface PrepaymentResult {
  newOutstandingPaise:   bigint;
  newTenureMonths:       number;
  emiPaise:              bigint;          // unchanged in reduce-tenure
  interestSavedPaise:    bigint;
  newSchedule:           AmortizationRow[];
}

interface AmortizationRow {
  installmentNumber: number;
  dueDate:           Date;
  emiPaise:          bigint;
  interestPaise:     bigint;
  principalPaise:    bigint;
  balanceAfterPaise: bigint;
}

function applyPrepaymentReduceTenure(
  outstandingPaiseBefore:  bigint,    // principal outstanding before prepayment
  prepaymentAmountPaise:   bigint,    // extra amount paid
  annualRatePercent:       string,    // e.g. "12.5" — string to avoid float
  currentEMIPaise:         bigint,    // existing EMI amount
  nextDueDate:             Date,      // date of the next scheduled EMI
): PrepaymentResult {
  const newOutstanding = outstandingPaiseBefore - prepaymentAmountPaise;

  if (newOutstanding <= 0n) {
    throw new Error('Prepayment amount exceeds outstanding principal — use foreclosure flow');
  }

  const monthlyRate = new Decimal(annualRatePercent).div(100).div(12);
  const emi         = new Decimal(currentEMIPaise.toString());
  const P           = new Decimal(newOutstanding.toString());

  // Solve for n: n = -ln(1 - P*r/EMI) / ln(1 + r)
  // This is the exact formula for number of periods given P, r, EMI
  const Pr_over_EMI = P.mul(monthlyRate).div(emi);

  if (Pr_over_EMI.gte(1)) {
    // EMI is not enough to cover even the monthly interest — reject
    throw new Error('Current EMI insufficient to repay new outstanding at given rate');
  }

  const nDecimal = Decimal.ln(new Decimal(1).sub(Pr_over_EMI)).neg()
                     .div(Decimal.ln(new Decimal(1).add(monthlyRate)));

  // Round up — always take the ceiling (last installment will be smaller)
  const newTenure = Math.ceil(Number(nDecimal.toFixed(6)));

  // Build the new amortization schedule
  const newSchedule = buildAmortizationSchedule(
    newOutstanding,
    currentEMIPaise,
    annualRatePercent,
    newTenure,
    nextDueDate,
  );

  // Interest saved = (old remaining interest) - (new remaining interest)
  const oldRemainingInterest = calculateRemainingInterest(
    outstandingPaiseBefore, currentEMIPaise, annualRatePercent
  );
  const newRemainingInterest = newSchedule.reduce(
    (sum, row) => sum + row.interestPaise, 0n
  );
  const interestSaved = oldRemainingInterest - newRemainingInterest;

  return {
    newOutstandingPaise:  newOutstanding,
    newTenureMonths:      newTenure,
    emiPaise:             currentEMIPaise,
    interestSavedPaise:   interestSaved,
    newSchedule,
  };
}

function buildAmortizationSchedule(
  principalPaise:    bigint,
  emiPaise:          bigint,
  annualRatePercent: string,
  tenureMonths:      number,
  firstDueDate:      Date,
): AmortizationRow[] {
  const monthlyRate = new Decimal(annualRatePercent).div(100).div(12);
  const schedule:   AmortizationRow[] = [];
  let   balance =   principalPaise;

  for (let i = 1; i <= tenureMonths; i++) {
    const interestPaise = BigInt(
      new Decimal(balance.toString())
        .mul(monthlyRate)
        .toDecimalPlaces(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_UP)
        .toFixed(0)
    );

    // Last installment: pay exact remaining balance + interest (handles rounding remainder)
    const isLast = i === tenureMonths;
    const principalPortion = isLast
      ? balance
      : emiPaise - interestPaise;

    const emiForRow = isLast
      ? balance + interestPaise
      : emiPaise;

    const balanceAfter = balance - principalPortion;

    schedule.push({
      installmentNumber: i,
      dueDate:           addMonths(firstDueDate, i - 1),
      emiPaise:          emiForRow,
      interestPaise,
      principalPaise:    principalPortion,
      balanceAfterPaise: balanceAfter < 0n ? 0n : balanceAfter,
    });

    balance = balanceAfter < 0n ? 0n : balanceAfter;
  }

  // Invariant: sum of all principal portions must equal the original principal
  const totalPrincipal = schedule.reduce((s, r) => s + r.principalPaise, 0n);
  console.assert(
    totalPrincipal === principalPaise,
    `Schedule principal mismatch: expected ${principalPaise}, got ${totalPrincipal}`
  );

  return schedule;
}
```

**Q6. How do you implement partial prepayment with the "reduce EMI" option?**

A: Reduce-EMI prepayment keeps the tenure constant (same number of remaining installments) and recalculates the EMI using the new lower outstanding principal.

```typescript
function applyPrepaymentReduceEMI(
  outstandingPaiseBefore: bigint,
  prepaymentAmountPaise:  bigint,
  annualRatePercent:      string,
  remainingTenureMonths:  number,
  nextDueDate:            Date,
): PrepaymentResult {
  const newOutstanding = outstandingPaiseBefore - prepaymentAmountPaise;

  if (newOutstanding <= 0n) {
    throw new Error('Prepayment exceeds outstanding — use foreclosure flow');
  }

  const P = new Decimal(newOutstanding.toString());
  const r = new Decimal(annualRatePercent).div(100).div(12);
  const n = new Decimal(remainingTenureMonths);

  // Standard EMI formula: P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1)
  const onePlusR     = r.add(1);
  const onePlusRPowN = onePlusR.pow(n);
  const newEMI       = P.mul(r).mul(onePlusRPowN)
                        .div(onePlusRPowN.sub(1))
                        .toDecimalPlaces(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_UP);

  const newEMIPaise = BigInt(newEMI.toFixed(0));

  const newSchedule = buildAmortizationSchedule(
    newOutstanding,
    newEMIPaise,
    annualRatePercent,
    remainingTenureMonths,
    nextDueDate,
  );

  const oldRemainingInterest = calculateRemainingInterest(
    outstandingPaiseBefore,
    newEMIPaise,  // compare at new EMI to isolate principal effect
    annualRatePercent,
  );
  const newRemainingInterest = newSchedule.reduce(
    (sum, row) => sum + row.interestPaise, 0n
  );

  return {
    newOutstandingPaise:  newOutstanding,
    newTenureMonths:      remainingTenureMonths,  // unchanged
    emiPaise:             newEMIPaise,
    interestSavedPaise:   oldRemainingInterest - newRemainingInterest,
    newSchedule,
  };
}
```

**Which option saves more interest?**

Both options save the same total interest relative to the same prepayment amount — mathematically, the time value of the interest saved is identical because the same principal is being extinguished. The *practical* difference is cash flow:

- **Reduce tenure:** Customer pays the same EMI but for fewer months. Better if they have stable cash flows and want to clear the debt faster.
- **Reduce EMI:** Customer pays a smaller EMI for the same duration. Better if they want to reduce monthly cash outflow — useful for borrowers managing tight monthly budgets.

The interview question "which saves more?" is a trap: the total interest saved is the same. The difference is timing and cash flow preference.

**Q7. How do you apply a prepayment payment to the loan — in what order are components settled?**

A: When a prepayment amount arrives, it must be allocated in a defined order. The order is specified in the loan agreement and must be followed exactly — applying in the wrong order is a regulatory and contractual breach.

**Standard payment application order (RBI-aligned, most common):**

```
1. Penal charges / late payment fees (oldest first)
2. Overdue interest (oldest first)
3. Overdue principal (oldest first)
4. Prepayment fee / foreclosure fee (if applicable)
5. Current period accrued interest (from last EMI date to today)
6. Principal prepayment (the remainder, applied to outstanding principal)
```

```typescript
interface PrepaymentAllocation {
  penalChargesCleared:    bigint;
  overdueInterestCleared: bigint;
  overduePrincipalCleared: bigint;
  prepaymentFeeCleared:   bigint;
  accruedInterestCleared: bigint;
  principalPrepaid:       bigint;
  unallocated:            bigint;   // should be 0; non-zero means overpayment
}

function allocatePrepayment(
  paymentPaise:      bigint,
  loan:              LoanWithCharges,
  prepaymentDate:    Date,
): PrepaymentAllocation {
  let remaining = paymentPaise;
  const alloc: PrepaymentAllocation = {
    penalChargesCleared:    0n,
    overdueInterestCleared: 0n,
    overduePrincipalCleared: 0n,
    prepaymentFeeCleared:   0n,
    accruedInterestCleared: 0n,
    principalPrepaid:       0n,
    unallocated:            0n,
  };

  const apply = (available: bigint): bigint => {
    const applied = remaining >= available ? available : remaining;
    remaining -= applied;
    return applied;
  };

  alloc.penalChargesCleared     = apply(loan.totalPenalChargesPaise);
  alloc.overdueInterestCleared  = apply(loan.totalOverdueInterestPaise);
  alloc.overduePrincipalCleared = apply(loan.totalOverduePrincipalPaise);
  alloc.prepaymentFeeCleared    = apply(calculatePrepaymentFee(loan, remaining));
  alloc.accruedInterestCleared  = apply(
    calculateAccruedInterest(loan.outstandingPrincipalPaise, loan.annualRatePercent, loan.lastEMIDate, prepaymentDate)
  );
  alloc.principalPrepaid        = apply(loan.outstandingPrincipalPaise - loan.totalOverduePrincipalPaise);
  alloc.unallocated             = remaining;

  return alloc;
}
```

**Critical rule — never apply prepayment directly to principal if there are outstanding charges.** A common bug is a "fast path" that deducts the prepayment amount from `outstanding_principal` without checking for overdue charges. This leaves the charges on the books, understates the loan balance, and causes reconciliation failures when the next EMI is processed.

## Section 3: Foreclosure Calculations

**Q8. Walk through the complete foreclosure calculation for a loan mid-tenure.**

A: **Scenario:** ₹5,00,000 loan, 12% annual interest (1% per month), 24-month tenure, EMI = ₹23,537. Customer is foreclosing on day 15 of month 10 (9 EMIs paid). Foreclosure fee: 2% of outstanding principal.

**Step 1 — Determine outstanding principal after last EMI:**

```typescript
// Run 9 iterations of the amortization schedule to find the outstanding after EMI 9
const schedule = buildAmortizationSchedule(
  50_000_000n,  // ₹5,00,000 in paise
  2_353_700n,   // ₹23,537 in paise
  '12',
  24,
  new Date('2023-05-01'),
);

const outstandingAfterEMI9 = schedule[8].balanceAfterPaise;
// Let's say: 3_10_45_678n paise = ₹3,10,456.78 → we'll use 31_045_678n paise
```

**Step 2 — Calculate accrued interest (daily, from last EMI date to foreclosure date):**

```typescript
import Decimal from 'decimal.js';

function calculateDailyAccruedInterest(
  outstandingPaise: bigint,
  annualRatePercent: string,
  lastEMIDate:       Date,
  foreclosureDate:   Date,
): bigint {
  const days = differenceInCalendarDays(foreclosureDate, lastEMIDate);

  const accrued = new Decimal(outstandingPaise.toString())
    .mul(new Decimal(annualRatePercent).div(100).div(365))
    .mul(days)
    .toDecimalPlaces(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_UP);

  return BigInt(accrued.toFixed(0));
}

const lastEMIDate    = new Date('2024-01-01');  // EMI 9 paid date
const foreclosureDate = new Date('2024-01-16'); // 15 days later
const outstandingPaise = 31_045_678n;

const accruedInterest = calculateDailyAccruedInterest(
  outstandingPaise, '12', lastEMIDate, foreclosureDate
);
// = 31_045_678 × (0.12 / 365) × 15
// = 31_045_678 × 0.000328767 × 15
// = 153,070 paise ≈ ₹1,530.70 → 153_070n paise
```

**Step 3 — Calculate foreclosure fee:**

```typescript
function calculateForeclosureFee(
  outstandingPaise: bigint,
  feeRatePercent:   string,
): bigint {
  return BigInt(
    new Decimal(outstandingPaise.toString())
      .mul(new Decimal(feeRatePercent).div(100))
      .toDecimalPlaces(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_UP)
      .toFixed(0)
  );
}

const foreclosureFee = calculateForeclosureFee(outstandingPaise, '2');
// = 31_045_678 × 0.02 = 620_914 paise ≈ ₹6,209.14 → 620_914n paise
```

**Step 4 — Total foreclosure amount:**

```typescript
const totalForeclosureAmountPaise =
  outstandingPaise      // 31_045_678n
  + accruedInterest     // 153_070n
  + foreclosureFee;     // 620_914n
  // = 31_819_662n paise = ₹3,18,196.62
```

**Step 5 — Verify: no future interest charged:**

```typescript
// The original schedule shows EMIs 10–24 would have totalled:
const remainingEMITotal = schedule.slice(9).reduce(
  (sum, row) => sum + row.emiPaise, 0n
);
const remainingPrincipalInEMIs = schedule.slice(9).reduce(
  (sum, row) => sum + row.principalPaise, 0n
);
const futureInterestAvoided = remainingEMITotal - remainingPrincipalInEMIs;
// This amount must NOT appear in the foreclosure amount
// It is the customer's saving — the entire benefit of foreclosing early
```

**Q9. How do you handle the GST on foreclosure charges?**

A: Foreclosure fees are a financial service, and GST at 18% applies to the fee component (not to the principal or interest repayment). This is a common omission that causes incorrect invoicing and GST liability mismatches.

```typescript
interface ForeclosureChargesBreakdown {
  outstandingPrincipalPaise: bigint;
  accruedInterestPaise:      bigint;
  foreclosureFeePreGSTPaise: bigint;
  gstOnFeePaise:             bigint;    // 18% of foreclosure fee
  totalForeclosurePaise:     bigint;    // sum of all above
}

function calculateForeclosureWithGST(
  outstandingPaise:  bigint,
  annualRatePercent: string,
  lastEMIDate:       Date,
  foreclosureDate:   Date,
  feeRatePercent:    string,
  gstRatePercent:    string = '18',
): ForeclosureChargesBreakdown {
  const accruedInterest     = calculateDailyAccruedInterest(
    outstandingPaise, annualRatePercent, lastEMIDate, foreclosureDate
  );
  const foreclosureFeePreGST = calculateForeclosureFee(outstandingPaise, feeRatePercent);
  const gstOnFee             = BigInt(
    new Decimal(foreclosureFeePreGST.toString())
      .mul(new Decimal(gstRatePercent).div(100))
      .toDecimalPlaces(0, Decimal.ROUND_HALF_UP)
      .toFixed(0)
  );

  return {
    outstandingPrincipalPaise: outstandingPaise,
    accruedInterestPaise:      accruedInterest,
    foreclosureFeePreGSTPaise: foreclosureFeePreGST,
    gstOnFeePaise:             gstOnFee,
    totalForeclosurePaise:
      outstandingPaise + accruedInterest + foreclosureFeePreGST + gstOnFee,
  };
}
```

**GST accounting entries on foreclosure:**

```
Journal Entry: "Foreclosure Fee + GST"
Reference: foreclosure_id = FCL-001

  DEBIT   Bank Settlement Account (1001)         
            = outstanding + accrued + fee + gst    [cash received]
  CREDIT  Receivables — Loan Principal (1030)    [outstanding principal cleared]
  CREDIT  Interest Income (4003)                 [accrued interest = revenue]
  CREDIT  Fee Revenue — Foreclosure Fee (4005)   [pre-GST fee = revenue]
  CREDIT  GST Payable (2020)                     [GST collected, owed to government]

  Check: Debits = Credits ✓
```

**Q10. What is a foreclosure quote, and how long is it valid?**

A: A **foreclosure quote** (also called a foreclosure statement or pre-closure statement) is a document issued to the borrower that states exactly how much they must pay to foreclose the loan, valid until a specific expiry date.

The quote has a validity window because the accrued interest component grows daily — a quote valid for 7 days would become incorrect on day 8 (the accrued interest amount would be higher). After the validity window, a new quote must be generated.

**Why the validity window matters for your system:**

```typescript
interface ForeclosureQuote {
  quoteId:                   string;
  loanId:                    string;
  generatedAt:               Date;
  validUntil:                Date;          // typically 3–7 days from generatedAt
  outstandingPrincipalPaise: bigint;
  accruedInterestPaise:      bigint;
  foreclosureFeePreGSTPaise: bigint;
  gstOnFeePaise:             bigint;
  totalPayablePaise:         bigint;
  quoteStatus:               'ACTIVE' | 'EXPIRED' | 'USED';
}

async function generateForeclosureQuote(
  loanId: string,
  requestDate: Date,
): Promise<ForeclosureQuote> {
  const loan = await db('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).first();

  if (!['ACTIVE', 'OVERDUE'].includes(loan.status)) {
    throw new InvalidOperationError(`Cannot foreclose loan in ${loan.status} state`);
  }

  const validityDays = 3;  // configurable per loan product
  const validUntil   = addDays(requestDate, validityDays);

  // Quote is computed for 'validUntil' date — the max the customer could wait to pay
  const charges = calculateForeclosureWithGST(
    loan.outstanding_principal_paise,
    loan.annual_rate_percent,
    loan.last_emi_date,
    validUntil,    // accrue to the end of the validity window
    loan.foreclosure_fee_rate_percent,
  );

  const quote: ForeclosureQuote = {
    quoteId:                   generateUUID(),
    loanId,
    generatedAt:               requestDate,
    validUntil,
    ...charges,
    quoteStatus:               'ACTIVE',
  };

  await db('foreclosure_quotes').insert(quote);
  return quote;
}
```

**Why accrue to `validUntil` rather than `requestDate`:**

If the customer pays on day 1 of a 3-day validity window, they pay slightly more than the exact accrual on that day — the excess goes toward principal reduction, and the system must handle the small surplus correctly (as a micro-prepayment). Some platforms compute the quote for the current day and require payment on the same day. Either approach is correct; choose one, document it in the KFS, and implement consistently.

## Section 4: Regulatory Rules

**Q11. What are RBI's guidelines on foreclosure charges, and how do they differ by loan type?**

A: RBI's stance on foreclosure charges has evolved and differs by borrower type and loan type. These rules directly govern what your system may and may not compute as a foreclosure fee:

**Individual borrowers — floating-rate loans:**
- RBI circular (2014, reaffirmed 2023): Banks and NBFCs **cannot levy prepayment/foreclosure charges** on floating-rate term loans sanctioned to individual borrowers (for purposes other than business). This includes home loans, personal loans, and auto loans where the rate is linked to an external benchmark (repo rate, MCLR).
- Your system must check `loan.rate_type` before calculating the foreclosure fee. If `rate_type === 'FLOATING'` and `borrower_type === 'INDIVIDUAL'`, the fee must be `0`.

**Individual borrowers — fixed-rate loans:**
- Foreclosure charges are permitted but must be disclosed in the KFS at sanction.
- Maximum charge is not regulated by RBI (it is a lender policy decision) but must be "reasonable and not penal in nature" per the Fair Practices Code.
- Typical range: 1%–5% of outstanding principal.

**Business / MSME loans:**
- Foreclosure charges apply regardless of rate type.
- RBI has not prohibited foreclosure charges for business loans.

**Lock-in period:**
- Many loan agreements specify a lock-in period (typically 6–12 months for personal loans) during which foreclosure is not permitted at all — the borrower cannot foreclose even if they want to.
- Your state machine guard must check: `foreclosure_date >= loan_start_date + lock_in_months`.

```typescript
function validateForeclosureEligibility(
  loan:             Loan,
  foreclosureDate:  Date,
): { eligible: boolean; reason?: string } {
  // Check lock-in period
  const lockInEndDate = addMonths(loan.disbursement_date, loan.lock_in_months);
  if (foreclosureDate < lockInEndDate) {
    return {
      eligible: false,
      reason:   `Loan cannot be foreclosed before ${format(lockInEndDate, 'dd MMM yyyy')} (lock-in period)`,
    };
  }

  // Check minimum DPD — some lenders don't allow foreclosure if overdue > 90 DPD (NPA)
  // (must go through settlement / OTS process instead)
  if (loan.status === 'NPA') {
    return {
      eligible: false,
      reason:   'NPA loans require OTS (One Time Settlement) — contact collections team',
    };
  }

  return { eligible: true };
}

function calculateForeclosureFeeRegCompliant(
  loan:             Loan,
  outstandingPaise: bigint,
): bigint {
  // Floating rate + individual borrower → no foreclosure fee (RBI mandate)
  if (loan.rate_type === 'FLOATING' && loan.borrower_type === 'INDIVIDUAL') {
    return 0n;
  }

  // Fixed rate or business loan → apply contractual fee
  return calculateForeclosureFee(outstandingPaise, loan.foreclosure_fee_rate_percent);
}
```

**Q12. What is an OTS (One Time Settlement), and how does it differ from foreclosure?**

A: A **One Time Settlement (OTS)** is a negotiated settlement between the lender and a borrower who is unable to repay the full outstanding amount. The lender agrees to accept a lump-sum amount less than the total outstanding (principal + accrued interest + charges) in full and final settlement of the loan.

OTS is used for NPA loans where full recovery is unlikely — it is a collections tool, not a voluntary early closure. The economic trade-off: accept ₹X now (less than the full outstanding) rather than spend more on legal recovery with an uncertain outcome.

| Property | Foreclosure | OTS |
|---|---|---|
| **Borrower financial state** | Healthy — has the money | Distressed — cannot pay full amount |
| **Amount** | Full outstanding (principal + accrued + fee) | Negotiated — typically 40%–80% of outstanding |
| **Loan status after** | `FORECLOSED` → `CLOSED` | `SETTLED` (not `CLOSED` — different CIBIL impact) |
| **CIBIL reporting** | `Closed` (positive) | `Settled` (negative — indicates partial recovery) |
| **Write-off impact** | No write-off (full recovery) | Remaining outstanding written off post-settlement |
| **Who initiates** | Borrower | Collections team / legal team |
| **Regulatory approval** | Not required | May require board approval above certain amounts |

**OTS ledger entries:**

```
Loan outstanding (NPA): ₹3,00,000
OTS agreed amount:      ₹1,80,000
Write-off on settlement: ₹1,20,000

Journal Entry: "OTS Settlement — LOAN-001"

  DEBIT   Bank Settlement Account              1,80,00,000 paise  [cash received]
  DEBIT   Loan Loss Reserve (contra-asset)     1,20,00,000 paise  [provision utilized]
  CREDIT  Receivables — Loan Principal         3,00,00,000 paise  [receivable extinguished]

  Check: Debits = Credits ✓
```

**Q13. How does a prepayment or foreclosure change the loan's state machine?**

```typescript
const PREPAYMENT_FORECLOSURE_TRANSITIONS = {
```

**Version management for EMI schedule:**

```typescript
async function rebuildEMISchedule(
  trx:         Knex.Transaction,
  loanId:      string,
  newSchedule: AmortizationRow[],
  reason:      string,
): Promise<void> {
  // Get current max version
  const { max_version } = await trx('emi_schedule')
    .where({ loan_id: loanId })
    .max('schedule_version as max_version')
    .first();

  const newVersion = (max_version ?? 0) + 1;

  // Mark all current PENDING entries as SUPERSEDED
  await trx('emi_schedule')
    .where({ loan_id: loanId, status: 'PENDING' })
    .update({
      status:               'SUPERSEDED',
      superseded_by_version: newVersion,
    });

  // Insert new schedule at new version
  await trx('emi_schedule').insert(
    newSchedule.map((row, idx) => ({
      loan_id:            loanId,
      schedule_version:   newVersion,
      installment_number: idx + 1,
      due_date:           row.dueDate,
      emi_paise:          row.emiPaise,
      interest_paise:     row.interestPaise,
      principal_paise:    row.principalPaise,
      balance_after_paise: row.balanceAfterPaise,
      status:             'PENDING',
    }))
  );
}
```

**Q15. How do you handle a foreclosure payment that arrives slightly above or below the quoted amount?**

A: Payment amount mismatches are extremely common in foreclosure flows — bank transfer fees, rounding in NEFT/IMPS, or the customer manually typing the amount incorrectly. The system must handle both over-payment and under-payment gracefully.

**Over-payment (payment > foreclosure quote):**

```typescript
async function processForeclosurePayment(
  loanId:       string,
  quoteId:      string,
  receivedPaise: bigint,
): Promise<void> {
  const quote = await db('foreclosure_quotes').where({ quote_id: quoteId }).first();

  if (quote.quote_status !== 'ACTIVE' || new Date() > quote.valid_until) {
    throw new QuoteExpiredError('Foreclosure quote has expired — generate a new quote');
  }

  const tolerance = 500n;  // ₹5 tolerance (configurable per product)

  if (receivedPaise >= quote.total_payable_paise) {
    // Over-payment or exact payment — proceed with foreclosure
    const surplus = receivedPaise - quote.total_payable_paise;

    await loanStateMachine.transition(loanId, 'FORECLOSURE_PAYMENT_RECEIVED', {
      triggeredBy:           'PAYMENT_GATEWAY',
      paymentAmountPaise:    receivedPaise,
      activeForeclosureQuote: quote,
      data: { surplus },
    });

    if (surplus > 0n) {
      // Refund the surplus back to the customer
      await payoutService.initiate({
        userId:       quote.customer_id,
        amountPaise:  surplus,
        reason:       `Foreclosure surplus refund — Loan ${loanId}`,
        rail:         'UPI',
      });
    }

  } else if (quote.total_payable_paise - receivedPaise <= tolerance) {
    // Under-payment within tolerance — absorb as a platform write-off (small amount)
    // Common when NEFT deducts a ₹2–₹5 bank charge from the transfer
    await loanStateMachine.transition(loanId, 'FORECLOSURE_PAYMENT_RECEIVED', {
      triggeredBy:           'PAYMENT_GATEWAY',
      paymentAmountPaise:    receivedPaise,
      activeForeclosureQuote: quote,
      data: {
        shortfall:        quote.total_payable_paise - receivedPaise,
        absorbedAsWaiver: true,
      },
    });

    // Record the waiver as a ledger entry (minor write-off)
    await ledgerService.recordForeclosureWaiver(
      loanId,
      quote.total_payable_paise - receivedPaise,
    );

  } else {
    // Under-payment outside tolerance — partial payment; cannot foreclose
    // Apply as a prepayment to the loan instead
    await handlePaymentReceived(loanId, receivedPaise);
    throw new InsufficientForeclosureAmountError(
      `Received ${receivedPaise} paise; foreclosure requires ${quote.total_payable_paise} paise. ` +
      `Payment applied as prepayment.`
    );
  }
}
```

## Section 6: System Design Scenario Questions

**Q16. "Design the end-to-end prepayment flow for a lending platform — from customer request to updated EMI schedule."**

A: The flow has five phases: eligibility check, quote generation, payment collection, schedule rebuild, and confirmation.

```
Phase 1 — Eligibility Check
  Customer: "I want to make a prepayment of ₹50,000"
  System checks:
    1. Loan is in ACTIVE or OVERDUE state
    2. Loan has passed the lock-in period
    3. Prepayment amount > minimum prepayment amount (e.g., ₹1,000 or 1 EMI)
    4. Prepayment amount < outstanding principal (else route to foreclosure)
    5. No pending foreclosure quote already active for this loan

Phase 2 — Prepayment Quote Generation
  System generates:
    - Current outstanding principal
    - Prepayment fee (if applicable)
    - GST on prepayment fee
    - Net principal reduction after fee
    - Projected new EMI (reduce-EMI option) OR new tenure (reduce-tenure option)
    - Interest saved under each option
    - Quote valid for 24 hours (prepayment quotes have shorter validity than foreclosure)
  Customer sees both options, selects one

Phase 3 — Payment Collection
  Payment collected via UPI/IMPS/NEFT
  Idempotency: prepayment_event_id is the idempotency key for the payment
  Status: PENDING until payment gateway confirms

Phase 4 — Schedule Rebuild (within payment confirmation transaction)
  DB transaction:
    1. Verify idempotency: prepayment_event_id not already processed
    2. Allocate payment: penal charges → overdue → fee → accrued interest → principal
    3. Build new amortization schedule (version N+1)
    4. Mark old PENDING schedule entries as SUPERSEDED
    5. Insert new schedule entries
    6. Update loans table: outstanding_principal, current_emi, remaining_tenure
    7. Record prepayment_event with before/after snapshots
    8. Record ledger entries (double-entry)
    9. Transition loan state (ACTIVE → ACTIVE, but version incremented)
  COMMIT

Phase 5 — Confirmation
  Send customer:
    - New EMI amount (or same EMI with new tenure)
    - Updated repayment schedule (PDF)
    - Total interest saved
    - Next EMI due date and amount
  Update CIBIL: no change required for partial prepayment
    (CIBIL is updated on NPA/close events, not prepayments)
```

**Idempotency for prepayment:**

```typescript
async function processPrepayment(
  loanId:          string,
  prepaymentId:    string,   // idempotency key — generated client-side
  amountPaise:     bigint,
  option:          'REDUCE_TENURE' | 'REDUCE_EMI',
): Promise<void> {
  await db.transaction(async (trx) => {
    // Idempotency check: if this prepayment was already processed, return early
    const existing = await trx('prepayment_events')
      .where({ prepayment_id: prepaymentId })
      .first();
    if (existing) {
      logger.info({ prepaymentId }, 'Duplicate prepayment request — skipping');
      return;
    }

    const loan = await trx('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).forUpdate().first();
    const allocation = allocatePrepayment(amountPaise, loan, new Date());
    const principalReduced = allocation.principalPrepaid;

    const result = option === 'REDUCE_TENURE'
      ? applyPrepaymentReduceTenure(
          loan.outstanding_principal_paise,
          principalReduced,
          loan.annual_rate_percent,
          loan.current_emi_paise,
          loan.next_emi_due_date,
        )
      : applyPrepaymentReduceEMI(
          loan.outstanding_principal_paise,
          principalReduced,
          loan.annual_rate_percent,
          loan.remaining_tenure_months,
          loan.next_emi_due_date,
        );

    await rebuildEMISchedule(trx, loanId, result.newSchedule, 'PREPAYMENT');
    await recordPrepaymentEvent(trx, loanId, prepaymentId, loan, result, allocation, option);
    await appendPrepaymentLedgerEntries(trx, loanId, amountPaise, allocation);
    await trx('loans').where({ loan_id: loanId }).update({
      outstanding_principal_paise: result.newOutstandingPaise,
      remaining_tenure_months:     result.newTenureMonths,
      current_emi_paise:           result.emiPaise,
      version:                     loan.version + 1,
    });
  });
}
```

**Step 2 — Check interest saved calculation:**

The `interest_saved_paise` in the record is the value that was computed and shown to the customer at the time. Recompute it independently to verify:

```typescript
async function auditInterestSaved(prepaymentId: string): Promise<void> {
  const event = await db('prepayment_events').where({ prepayment_id: prepaymentId }).first();
  const loan  = await db('loans').where({ loan_id: event.loan_id }).first();

  // Recompute the old remaining interest (before prepayment)
  const oldSchedule = await db('emi_schedule')
    .where({ loan_id: event.loan_id })
    .where('schedule_version', '<', event.new_schedule_version)
    .where('due_date', '>=', event.prepayment_date)
    .where('status', 'SUPERSEDED');

  const oldRemainingInterest = oldSchedule.reduce(
    (sum, row) => sum + BigInt(row.interest_paise), 0n
  );

  // Recompute new remaining interest (after prepayment)
  const newSchedule = await db('emi_schedule')
    .where({ loan_id: event.loan_id, schedule_version: event.new_schedule_version });

  const newRemainingInterest = newSchedule.reduce(
    (sum, row) => sum + BigInt(row.interest_paise), 0n
  );

  const recomputedSaving = oldRemainingInterest - newRemainingInterest;

  console.log({
    storedSaving:      event.interest_saved_paise,
    recomputedSaving,
    discrepancy:       recomputedSaving - event.interest_saved_paise,
  });
}
```

**Common root causes of the discrepancy:**

| Root cause | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued interest was deducted from prepayment before applying to principal | Less principal was actually prepaid than the customer assumed | Show the allocation breakdown explicitly: "₹X applied to accrued interest, ₹Y applied to principal" |
| Prepayment fee was not shown in the quote but was deducted from the payment | Effective principal reduction was lower | Fee must be disclosed in the quote; deducted before principal |
| Interest saved was computed pre-fee (on the full payment amount) but actual principal reduction was post-fee | The saving shown was optimistic | Compute interest saved on the net principal reduction, not the gross payment |
| Rounding mode mismatch between quote engine and schedule builder | Small discrepancy (< ₹100) | Standardize `ROUND_HALF_UP` across all modules |
| Quote showed saving based on "reduce tenure" but customer chose "reduce EMI" | Two different savings figures | Show both options with their respective savings before customer selects |

**Resolution:** If the discrepancy is due to a system error (fee not disclosed, wrong allocation order), the platform must compensate the customer for the difference. If it is due to a misunderstanding of allocation order (accrued interest deducted first), the resolution is improved disclosure — show the full allocation breakdown at the time of prepayment confirmation.

## Quick Reference Table

| Concept | Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Partial prepayment** | Payment above EMI; reduces principal ahead of schedule | Reduces future interest; requires schedule rebuild |
| **Foreclosure** | Full outstanding repayment; terminates the loan | NOC issued; CIBIL updated to `Closed` |
| **OTS** | Negotiated partial settlement for NPA loans | CIBIL shows `Settled` (negative mark); remaining written off |
| **Reduce tenure** | Same EMI, fewer installments | Better for stable income borrowers |
| **Reduce EMI** | Same installments, lower EMI amount | Better for cash-flow-constrained borrowers |
| **Interest saved (both options)** | Mathematically identical | Difference is timing/cash-flow preference, not total cost |
| **Payment allocation order** | Penal → overdue interest → overdue principal → fee → accrued interest → principal | Wrong order = regulatory breach + reconciliation failure |
| **Accrued interest** | Daily: P × (r/365) × days since last EMI | Only charge to foreclosure date — not future interest |
| **RBI: floating rate individual** | No foreclosure/prepayment charges permitted | Hard rule; `rate_type === FLOATING && borrower_type === INDIVIDUAL` → fee = 0 |
| **Lock-in period** | Foreclosure not permitted during lock-in | State machine guard: `foreclosure_date >= disbursement_date + lock_in_months` |
| **Foreclosure quote validity** | 3–7 days; accrued interest grows daily | Quote computed to validity end date; new quote needed after expiry |
| **GST on foreclosure fee** | 18% on fee (not on principal or interest) | Separate ledger entry; reported in GST return |
| **Schedule versioning** | Each rebuild = new version; old entries marked `SUPERSEDED` | Audit: see every schedule the loan has ever had |
| **Idempotency** | `prepayment_id` = idempotency key; `UNIQUE` constraint on table | Prevents double-prepayment from payment gateway retries |
| **Tolerance on payment mismatch** | ±₹5 tolerance; absorb small shortfall as waiver | NEFT/IMPS bank charges cause ₹2–₹5 shortfalls routinely |

---

**Tip for the interview:** At the 5 YOE level in a lending fintech, prepayment and foreclosure questions surface in three forms: (1) "a customer wants to foreclose their loan — what do you show them and how do you compute it?" — the answer must include: outstanding principal + accrued interest (daily, only up to today) + fee + GST; never future interest; never apply foreclosure to an NPA without OTS; (2) "what happens to the EMI schedule after a prepayment?" — they expect you to say the schedule is completely rebuilt, not patched; know the reduce-tenure vs. reduce-EMI options and that the total interest saved is mathematically identical between them; (3) "how do you prevent a duplicate prepayment if the payment gateway retries?" — the answer is idempotency: a `prepayment_id` generated before the payment is initiated, stored as a `UNIQUE` constraint in the `prepayment_events` table, and checked at the start of the processing transaction. Know the RBI rule on foreclosure charges cold: floating rate + individual borrower = zero fee, always. Confusing this in an interview at a lending company signals unfamiliarity with the regulatory environment your code must operate in.
