# CIBIL / Credit Bureau API Integration Patterns — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is a credit bureau, and what role does it play in the Indian lending ecosystem?**
A: A credit bureau (Credit Information Company, or CIC, in Indian regulatory terminology) is a licensed entity that collects, maintains, and distributes credit information about borrowers. It aggregates repayment history from all member credit institutions — banks, NBFCs, HFCs, MFIs, credit card companies — and makes this information available to lenders for credit decisioning.

India has four RBI-licensed credit bureaus:

| Bureau | Full name | Parent | Market position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CIBIL** | TransUnion CIBIL Ltd | TransUnion (US) | Oldest (2000); largest market share; most lenders check CIBIL first |
| **Experian** | Experian Credit Information Company | Experian (UK/Ireland) | Second largest; strong in south India |
| **Equifax** | Equifax Credit Information Services | Equifax (US) | Significant share; used by many PSU banks |
| **CRIF Highmark** | CRIF High Mark Credit Information Services | CRIF (Italy) | Strong in MFI/rural lending; NBFC-focused |

**Data flow in the credit ecosystem:**

```
Credit Institutions (banks, NBFCs, HFCs)
      ↓ monthly submission (by 10th of month)
Credit Bureaus (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF)
      ↓ real-time API (on lender request)
Lenders (during loan origination / monitoring)
      ↓ score + report used in credit decision
Borrower (has right to access their own report)
```

The Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 (CICRA) governs bureaus. Key provisions:
- Every credit institution must be a member of all four bureaus (RBI mandate since 2019).
- Every credit institution must submit data to all four bureaus monthly.
- Bureaus must provide data only to members and to individuals about themselves.
- Data must be retained for 7 years from the last activity.

**Q2. What does a credit bureau report contain, and what fields matter most for credit decisioning?**
A: A credit bureau report (Credit Information Report, CIR) is a structured document containing a borrower's complete credit history across all lenders. Key sections:

**Section 1 — Identity**
- Name, date of birth, PAN, Aadhaar (masked), passport number, voter ID.
- Addresses (current and past), phone numbers, email.
- Used for: identity verification, deduplication, KYC confirmation.

**Section 2 — Score**
- CIBIL Score: 300–900 (higher is better). Score < 650: high risk; 650–750: medium; 750+: good.
- Score version (v1, v2, v3 — different models for different segments).
- Score date (when computed — may lag by a day).

**Section 3 — Account Summary**
- Total accounts, active accounts, closed accounts.
- Total credit limit (across all revolving accounts).
- Total current balance.
- Total overdue amount.
- Oldest account age (indicates credit history length).
- Number of accounts opened in last 12 months (velocity indicator).

**Section 4 — Account Details (per account)**
For each loan or credit card account:

| Field | Description | Decisioning use |
|---|---|---|
| `member_name` | Lender name | Identify existing lender relationships |
| `account_type` | Auto Loan, Personal Loan, Credit Card, Home Loan, etc. | Understand debt composition |
| `ownership_type` | Individual, Joint, Guarantor | Guarantor obligations count against borrower |
| `account_status` | Active / Closed / Written Off / Settled | Written Off / Settled = major negative |
| `open_date` | Account open date | Credit age computation |
| `credit_limit` | Sanctioned limit (for revolving) | Credit utilisation calculation |
| `current_balance` | Outstanding as of last reported date | Debt burden |
| `amount_overdue` | Total overdue amount | Immediate risk signal |
| `dpd_history` | Last 36 months of DPD per month (0, 30, 60, 90, SMA labels) | Most important field for decisioning |
| `date_of_npa` | NPA classification date if applicable | Hard negative; most lenders reject on any NPA |
| `written_off_settled_amount` | Amount written off or settled | Significant derogatory |

**Section 5 — Enquiries**
Every time a lender pulls a credit report, it creates an "enquiry" record. The enquiry section shows:
- Enquiry date, enquiring member name, loan amount enquired for, purpose.
- Multiple enquiries in a short period (bureau shopping) signal desperation — a risk flag.

**Q3. What is the difference between a hard enquiry and a soft enquiry, and how do they affect the CIBIL score?**
A: Both types involve pulling a credit report from the bureau, but they differ in purpose and score impact:

| Property | Hard enquiry | Soft enquiry |
|---|---|---|
| **Triggered by** | Borrower applying for credit (lender pulls report for underwriting) | Borrower checking own score; pre-approved offer screening; account review |
| **Visible to** | All lenders who pull the report | Only the entity that performed it (not visible to other lenders) |
| **Score impact** | Negative — each hard enquiry typically reduces score by 2–10 points | No score impact |
| **Decay** | Impact diminishes after 6 months; enquiry stays on report for 2 years | Stays on record but not visible to lenders |
| **In India** | All bureau pulls by lenders during origination are hard enquiries | Available via some bureau APIs; less common |

**Enquiry velocity risk:**
5+ hard enquiries in 30 days signals the borrower is desperately seeking credit from multiple lenders — a strong predictor of distress. Most underwriting engines auto-decline above a threshold (typically 3–4 enquiries in 90 days for unsecured loans).

```typescript
function assessEnquiryVelocity(enquiries: BureauEnquiry[]): EnquiryRisk {
  const last30Days  = enquiries.filter(e => differenceInDays(new Date(), e.date) <= 30).length;
  const last90Days  = enquiries.filter(e => differenceInDays(new Date(), e.date) <= 90).length;
  const last180Days = enquiries.filter(e => differenceInDays(new Date(), e.date) <= 180).length;

  if (last30Days >= 5 || last90Days >= 8)  return { level: 'HIGH',   decline: true  };
  if (last30Days >= 3 || last90Days >= 5)  return { level: 'MEDIUM', decline: false };
  return                                          { level: 'LOW',    decline: false };
}
```

**Q4. What is the CIBIL score model, and what factors drive it?**
A: The CIBIL TransUnion Score v2.0 (the most widely used model) is a 3-digit number from 300 to 900. It is a logistic regression model trained on historical repayment data. The approximate factor weights:

| Factor | Weight | Key signals |
|---|---|---|
| **Payment history** | ~35% | DPD history across all accounts; any 90+ DPD, NPA, write-off, or settlement is highly negative |
| **Credit utilisation** | ~30% | Outstanding balance / credit limit for revolving accounts; > 30% utilisation hurts score |
| **Credit age** | ~15% | Age of oldest account; average age of all accounts; closing old accounts hurts score |
| **Credit mix** | ~10% | Combination of secured (home, auto) and unsecured (personal, credit card) loans — diversity is positive |
| **New credit** | ~10% | Recent hard enquiries; new accounts opened recently; rapid credit-seeking is negative |

**Score segments used in Indian underwriting:**

| CIBIL Score | Risk segment | Typical lender action |
|---|---|---|
| 750–900 | Prime | Standard approval; best rates |
| 700–749 | Near-prime | Approval with conditions; slightly higher rate |
| 650–699 | Sub-prime | Higher rates; additional collateral or co-applicant |
| 600–649 | Deep sub-prime | Most NBFCs decline; some MFIs accept |
| 300–599 | Very high risk | Rejection by most regulated lenders |
| -1 (NH) | No Hit — no bureau record | New-to-credit (NTC) borrower; no history |
| 0 (NA) | Not Applicable — insufficient history | Too few accounts or too recent |

The NH (No Hit) and NA (Not Applicable) scores are important edge cases — they are not the same as a low score. Many fintechs specifically target NTC borrowers (e.g., young professionals, first-time borrowers) with alternate data scoring.

**Q5. What are the four bureau APIs that an NBFC integrates with, and what does each provide?**
A: Each bureau offers a family of APIs. The core APIs common across all four:

| API | Purpose | Typical latency | When called |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Credit Report / CIR API** | Full credit information report — all accounts, enquiries, score | 1–3 seconds | Loan application (hard enquiry) |
| **Score API** | Score only (faster, cheaper than full report) | < 1 second | Pre-screening; soft check before full pull |
| **Consumer Dispute API** | Borrower-initiated dispute submission | Async | When borrower flags incorrect data |
| **Consent API** | Digital consent capture and submission | < 500ms | Before pulling report (CICRA requirement) |
| **Match API** | Identity verification against bureau records | < 1 second | KYC; deduplication |
| **Portfolio Review / Batch API** | Monthly submission of your loan book to the bureau | Async batch | Monthly credit reporting obligation |

Beyond the core APIs, CIBIL specifically offers:

- **CIBIL Commercial Bureau API** — for MSME/business loans; reports on company CIN/GSTIN rather than individual PAN.
- **CIBIL Rank** — 1–10 ranking for commercial entities (analogous to score for companies).
- **CIBIL Mortgage Check** — specifically for home loan applications; includes co-applicant and property information.

## Section 2: Integration Architecture

**Q6. How do you design a credit bureau integration that is resilient, idempotent, and compliant with consent requirements?**
A: A credit bureau pull involves: consent capture → API call → response storage → downstream consumption. Each step has failure modes and compliance obligations.

**High-level architecture:**

```
Loan Application API
      ↓
Consent Service
  ├── Capture consent (purpose, scope, timestamp, IP)
  ├── Store consent record
  └── Return consent_id
      ↓
Bureau Pull Orchestrator
  ├── Check cache (has a recent report been pulled?)
  ├── Select bureau(s) to query
  ├── Call Bureau API (with timeout + retry)
  ├── Store raw response (immutable)
  ├── Parse and normalise response
  └── Return parsed report
      ↓
Credit Decision Engine
  ├── Score assessment
  ├── Rule evaluation
  └── Decision (approve / reject / refer)
      ↓
Reporting Service (async)
  └── Monthly submission of loan data back to bureaus
```

**Schema:**

```sql
-- Consent record — mandatory before every bureau pull
CREATE TABLE bureau_consents (
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  application_id  UUID NOT NULL,
  customer_id     UUID NOT NULL,
  pan             CHAR(10) NOT NULL,
  purpose         TEXT NOT NULL,          -- 'LOAN_ORIGINATION', 'ACCOUNT_REVIEW', 'COLLECTIONS'
  consent_text    TEXT NOT NULL,          -- exact text shown to borrower
  consent_version TEXT NOT NULL,          -- version of consent template
  channel         TEXT NOT NULL,          -- 'WEB', 'MOBILE', 'BRANCH', 'API'
  ip_address      INET,
  device_id       TEXT,
  consented_at    TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  expires_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,   -- consent valid for 6 months (RBI guidance)
  is_withdrawn    BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,
  withdrawn_at    TIMESTAMPTZ
);

-- Bureau pull record — one per bureau per application
CREATE TABLE bureau_pulls (
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  consent_id      UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bureau_consents(id),
  application_id  UUID NOT NULL,
  customer_id     UUID NOT NULL,
  bureau          TEXT NOT NULL,          -- 'CIBIL', 'EXPERIAN', 'EQUIFAX', 'CRIF'
  pull_type       TEXT NOT NULL,          -- 'HARD', 'SOFT'
  purpose         TEXT NOT NULL,
  request_payload JSONB NOT NULL,         -- request sent to bureau (for audit)
  raw_response    TEXT,                   -- raw XML/JSON from bureau (immutable; encrypted at rest)
  parsed_score    INT,                    -- extracted score (300–900 or -1 for NH)
  parsed_at       TIMESTAMPTZ,
  status          TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PENDING',
  -- PENDING, SUCCESS, FAILED, TIMEOUT, BUREAU_ERROR, NO_MATCH
  error_code      TEXT,
  error_message   TEXT,
  bureau_ref_id   TEXT,                   -- bureau's own reference number for the pull
  pulled_at       TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  expires_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL    -- bureau reports are valid for 30–90 days; re-pull after
);

-- Parsed report data — normalised from bureau-specific format
CREATE TABLE bureau_report_accounts (
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  bureau_pull_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bureau_pulls(id),
  member_name       TEXT NOT NULL,
  account_type      TEXT NOT NULL,
  account_number    TEXT,                 -- masked
  ownership_type    TEXT NOT NULL,        -- 'INDIVIDUAL', 'JOINT', 'GUARANTOR'
  open_date         DATE,
  close_date        DATE,
  account_status    TEXT NOT NULL,
  credit_limit      BIGINT,              -- paise; NULL for non-revolving
  current_balance   BIGINT NOT NULL,
  amount_overdue    BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  dpd_24_months     INT[],              -- array of 24 DPD values (most recent first)
  date_of_npa       DATE,
  written_off_amount BIGINT,
  settled_amount    BIGINT,
  suit_filed        BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE
);
```

**Q7. How do you handle bureau API failures, timeouts, and the multi-bureau fallback strategy?**
A: Bureau APIs are external HTTP dependencies with real SLA variability. CIBIL's API has a stated SLA of 3 seconds but can spike to 10–15 seconds during peak hours. Your integration must handle this without blocking the loan application flow.

**Timeout and retry configuration:**

```typescript
// bureau-client.ts
const BUREAU_CONFIG: Record<string, BureauConfig> = {
  CIBIL: {
    baseUrl:       'https://api.cibil.com/v3',
    connectTimeout: 3_000,    // 3s to establish connection
    readTimeout:    8_000,    // 8s for response (CIBIL SLA is 3s; buffer for spikes)
    maxRetries:     2,
    retryDelay:     1_000,    // 1s between retries
    retryOn:        [408, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504],
  },
  EXPERIAN: {
    baseUrl:       'https://api.experian.in/v2',
    connectTimeout: 3_000,
    readTimeout:    8_000,
    maxRetries:     2,
    retryDelay:     1_500,
    retryOn:        [408, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504],
  },
};

async function pullBureauReport(
  bureau: string,
  request: BureauRequest,
  pullId: string,
): Promise<BureauResponse> {
  const config = BUREAU_CONFIG[bureau];
  let lastError: Error | null = null;

  for (let attempt = 0; attempt <= config.maxRetries; attempt++) {
    try {
      const response = await axios.post(
        `${config.baseUrl}/credit-report`,
        buildBureauPayload(bureau, request),
        {
          timeout: config.readTimeout,
          headers: buildHeaders(bureau),
        },
      );

      await db('bureau_pulls').where({ id: pullId }).update({
        status: 'SUCCESS',
        raw_response: encrypt(JSON.stringify(response.data)),
        bureau_ref_id: response.data.referenceId,
        parsed_at: new Date(),
      });

      return parseBureauResponse(bureau, response.data);

    } catch (err: any) {
      lastError = err;
      const isRetryable = config.retryOn.includes(err.response?.status) || err.code === 'ECONNABORTED';

      if (!isRetryable || attempt === config.maxRetries) break;
      await sleep(config.retryDelay * Math.pow(2, attempt));  // exponential backoff
    }
  }

  const errorCode = classifyBureauError(lastError);
  await db('bureau_pulls').where({ id: pullId }).update({
    status: errorCode,
    error_code: lastError?.response?.data?.errorCode ?? lastError?.code,
    error_message: lastError?.message,
  });

  throw new BureauPullFailedError(bureau, pullId, lastError);
}
```

**Multi-bureau fallback strategy:**

Not all borrowers appear in all bureaus — a borrower who has only taken loans from lenders that report to CRIF but not CIBIL will show as NH in CIBIL. The fallback strategy:

```typescript
async function pullWithFallback(
  request: BureauRequest,
  applicationId: string,
): Promise<BureauReport> {
  const PRIMARY   = 'CIBIL';
  const FALLBACKS = ['EXPERIAN', 'EQUIFAX', 'CRIF'];

  // Always pull primary
  try {
    const primaryReport = await pullBureauReport(PRIMARY, request, applicationId);
    if (primaryReport.score !== -1 && primaryReport.score !== 0) {
      // Good data from primary — no need for fallback
      return primaryReport;
    }
    // NH or NA — try fallbacks for richer data
  } catch (err) {
    logger.warn({ bureau: PRIMARY, err }, 'Primary bureau pull failed — trying fallbacks');
  }

  // Try fallbacks sequentially (or parallel for speed — tradeoff: cost vs latency)
  for (const bureau of FALLBACKS) {
    try {
      const report = await pullBureauReport(bureau, request, applicationId);
      if (report.score !== -1) return report;
    } catch (err) {
      logger.warn({ bureau, err }, 'Fallback bureau pull failed');
    }
  }

  // All bureaus failed or NH — return NTC (New To Credit) signal
  return { score: -1, scoreLabel: 'NH', accounts: [], enquiries: [] };
}
```

**Cost consideration:** Each bureau pull costs ₹15–₹60 (varies by bureau and volume commitment). Pulling all four for every application at scale (say, 10,000 applications/day) costs ₹6–24 lakh/day. The fallback strategy (pull primary; fall back only if NH/failure) optimises cost while maximising data coverage.

**Q8. How do you cache bureau responses, and what are the cache validity rules?**
A: Bureau reports are expensive (cost) and latency-sensitive (3–8 seconds per pull). Caching is mandatory at scale. But a stale report can lead to a credit decision based on outdated data — a compliance and risk issue.

**Cache validity rules by use case:**

| Use case | Cache validity | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Same application, same step retry | Until application expires | Idempotent — same pull, same report |
| Different loan product, same customer, same week | 7 days | Customer's credit profile unlikely to change in 7 days |
| Same customer, after 90 days | Expired — re-pull | Bureau report > 90 days old is considered stale by most lenders |
| Adverse event (NACH bounce, payment default) | Invalidate immediately | Credit profile may have deteriorated |
| Post-moratorium / restructuring review | Always re-pull | Significant event; cached report meaningless |

```typescript
async function getCachedOrFreshReport(
  customerId: string,
  pan: string,
  bureau: string,
  purpose: string,
): Promise<{ report: BureauReport; fromCache: boolean }> {
  const CACHE_TTL_DAYS = 30;  // conservative; many lenders use 30–90 days

  // Look for a recent successful pull for this customer + bureau
  const cached = await db('bureau_pulls')
    .where({
      customer_id: customerId,
      bureau,
      status: 'SUCCESS',
    })
    .where('pulled_at', '>', subDays(new Date(), CACHE_TTL_DAYS))
    .where('expires_at', '>', new Date())
    .orderBy('pulled_at', 'desc')
    .first();

  if (cached) {
    const report = parseBureauResponse(bureau, JSON.parse(decrypt(cached.raw_response)));
    return { report, fromCache: true };
  }

  // Cache miss — pull fresh report
  const consentId = await getValidConsent(customerId, purpose);
  const pullId = await createPullRecord(customerId, bureau, purpose, consentId);
  const report = await pullBureauReport(bureau, { pan }, pullId);
  return { report, fromCache: false };
}
```

**Consent linkage:** The cached report must still be linked to a valid consent for the current purpose. A consent granted for "loan origination" in January cannot be reused to pull a bureau report for "collections monitoring" in March — different purpose, different consent required.

**Q9. How do you parse and normalise the bureau XML/JSON response across four different formats?**
A: Each bureau has a different response format. CIBIL uses XML; Experian and Equifax use JSON; CRIF Highmark uses a proprietary format. Normalisation into a common internal model is essential.

**Common internal model:**

```typescript
interface NormalisedBureauReport {
  bureau:         string;
  pullId:         string;
  reportDate:     Date;
  score:          number;          // 300–900, or -1 (NH), 0 (NA)
  scoreLabel:     string;          // 'PRIME', 'NEAR_PRIME', 'SUB_PRIME', 'NH', 'NA'
  identity: {
    name:         string;
    pan:          string;
    dob:          Date | null;
  };
  summary: {
    totalAccounts:      number;
    activeAccounts:     number;
    closedAccounts:     number;
    totalBalance:       bigint;    // paise
    totalOverdue:       bigint;
    totalCreditLimit:   bigint;
    oldestAccountAge:   number;    // months
    recentEnquiries90d: number;
  };
  accounts:  NormalisedAccount[];
  enquiries: NormalisedEnquiry[];
  flags: {
    hasNpa:          boolean;
    hasWriteOff:     boolean;
    hasSettlement:   boolean;
    hasSuitFiled:    boolean;
    isWilfulDefault: boolean;
  };
}

interface NormalisedAccount {
  memberName:      string;
  accountType:     AccountType;  // enum: AUTO_LOAN, PERSONAL_LOAN, HOME_LOAN, CREDIT_CARD, ...
  ownershipType:   'INDIVIDUAL' | 'JOINT' | 'GUARANTOR' | 'CO_BORROWER';
  status:          AccountStatus;
  openDate:        Date | null;
  closeDate:       Date | null;
  creditLimit:     bigint | null;
  currentBalance:  bigint;
  amountOverdue:   bigint;
  dpd36Months:     number[];    // 36 values, index 0 = most recent month
  dateOfNpa:       Date | null;
  writtenOffAmt:   bigint | null;
  settledAmt:      bigint | null;
}
```

**Bureau-specific parsers:**

```typescript
// Parser registry — add a new parser for each bureau format
const BUREAU_PARSERS: Record<string, BureauParser> = {
  CIBIL:   new CibilXmlParser(),
  EXPERIAN: new ExperianJsonParser(),
  EQUIFAX:  new EquifaxJsonParser(),
  CRIF:     new CrifHighmarkParser(),
};

class CibilXmlParser implements BureauParser {
  parse(raw: string): NormalisedBureauReport {
    const xml = parseXML(raw);  // e.g., fast-xml-parser

    const scoreNode = xml['INProfileResponse']['SCORE']['BureauScore'];
    const score = parseInt(scoreNode, 10);

    const accounts = xml['INProfileResponse']['CAIS_Account_DETAILS']?.map(
      (acc: any): NormalisedAccount => ({
        memberName:    acc['Subscriber_Name'],
        accountType:   mapCibilAccountType(acc['Account_Type']),
        ownershipType: mapCibilOwnership(acc['Ownership_Indicator']),
        status:        mapCibilStatus(acc['Account_Status']),
        openDate:      parseCibilDate(acc['Open_Date']),
        closeDate:     parseCibilDate(acc['Date_Closed']),
        creditLimit:   acc['Credit_Limit_Amount']
                         ? BigInt(acc['Credit_Limit_Amount']) * 100n  // CIBIL returns rupees
                         : null,
        currentBalance: BigInt(acc['Current_Balance'] ?? '0') * 100n,
        amountOverdue:  BigInt(acc['Amount_Past_Due'] ?? '0') * 100n,
        dpd36Months:   parseCibilDpdHistory(acc['CAIS_Holder_Details']),
        dateOfNpa:     parseCibilDate(acc['Date_of_NPA']),
        writtenOffAmt: acc['Written_off_Amt_Total']
                         ? BigInt(acc['Written_off_Amt_Total']) * 100n
                         : null,
        settledAmt:    acc['Settlement_Amount']
                         ? BigInt(acc['Settlement_Amount']) * 100n
                         : null,
      })
    ) ?? [];

    return {
      bureau: 'CIBIL',
      score,
      scoreLabel: scoreToLabel(score),
      accounts,
      enquiries: parseCibilEnquiries(xml),
      flags: computeFlags(accounts),
      // ... other fields
    };
  }
}
```

**Version handling:** Bureau response formats change without deprecation notice. Store the raw response alongside the parsed data — when the parser is updated, you can re-parse historical responses without re-pulling from the bureau.

## Section 3: Credit Decisioning Integration

**Q10. How do you integrate bureau data into a rules-based credit decisioning engine?**
A: The bureau report is one input into a multi-layer credit decision. The decisioning engine applies rules in sequence — hard cutoffs first (instant decline), then soft rules (refer for manual review), then score-based pricing:

```typescript
interface CreditDecision {
  decision:       'APPROVE' | 'DECLINE' | 'REFER';
  declineReasons: string[];
  referReasons:   string[];
  approvedAmount: bigint | null;
  interestRate:   number | null;
  conditions:     string[];
}

async function makeCreditDecision(
  application: LoanApplication,
  bureauReport: NormalisedBureauReport,
): Promise<CreditDecision> {
  const declineReasons: string[] = [];
  const referReasons:   string[] = [];

  // ── HARD DECLINE RULES (any one = instant decline) ──────────────────────────

  // Rule H1: NPA on any account
  if (bureauReport.flags.hasNpa) {
    declineReasons.push('ACTIVE_NPA');
  }

  // Rule H2: Write-off in last 36 months
  const recentWriteOff = bureauReport.accounts.some(
    a => a.writtenOffAmt && a.writtenOffAmt > 0n && isWithinMonths(a.closeDate, 36)
  );
  if (recentWriteOff) declineReasons.push('RECENT_WRITE_OFF');

  // Rule H3: Settlement in last 24 months
  const recentSettlement = bureauReport.accounts.some(
    a => a.settledAmt && a.settledAmt > 0n && isWithinMonths(a.closeDate, 24)
  );
  if (recentSettlement) declineReasons.push('RECENT_SETTLEMENT');

  // Rule H4: Score below hard floor (300–599 = decline)
  if (bureauReport.score > 0 && bureauReport.score < 600) {
    declineReasons.push('SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM');
  }

  // Rule H5: Enquiry velocity — more than 4 hard enquiries in 30 days
  const enquiryRisk = assessEnquiryVelocity(bureauReport.enquiries);
  if (enquiryRisk.decline) declineReasons.push('HIGH_ENQUIRY_VELOCITY');

  // Rule H6: Total overdue above threshold
  if (bureauReport.summary.totalOverdue > 5_000_00n) {  // > ₹5,000
    declineReasons.push('EXISTING_OVERDUE');
  }

  // Rule H7: Suit filed
  if (bureauReport.flags.hasSuitFiled) declineReasons.push('SUIT_FILED');

  if (declineReasons.length > 0) {
    return { decision: 'DECLINE', declineReasons, referReasons, approvedAmount: null,
             interestRate: null, conditions: [] };
  }

  // ── SOFT RULES (any one = refer to credit team) ──────────────────────────────

  // Rule S1: Score in sub-prime range
  if (bureauReport.score > 0 && bureauReport.score < 700) {
    referReasons.push('SCORE_600_700_RANGE');
  }

  // Rule S2: 60+ DPD in last 12 months on any account
  const has60DpdLast12 = bureauReport.accounts.some(
    a => a.dpd36Months.slice(0, 12).some(dpd => dpd >= 60)
  );
  if (has60DpdLast12) referReasons.push('60_DPD_LAST_12_MONTHS');

  // Rule S3: High credit utilisation on revolving accounts
  const revolving = bureauReport.accounts.filter(a => a.creditLimit && a.creditLimit > 0n);
  if (revolving.length > 0) {
    const utilisation = revolving.reduce((s, a) => s + a.currentBalance, 0n)
                        * 100n
                        / revolving.reduce((s, a) => s + (a.creditLimit ?? 0n), 0n);
    if (utilisation > 70n) referReasons.push('HIGH_CREDIT_UTILISATION');
  }

  // Rule S4: More than 2 unsecured loans active simultaneously
  const activeUnsecured = bureauReport.accounts.filter(
    a => a.status === 'ACTIVE' && isUnsecured(a.accountType)
  ).length;
  if (activeUnsecured > 2) referReasons.push('MULTIPLE_ACTIVE_UNSECURED_LOANS');

  if (referReasons.length > 0) {
    return { decision: 'REFER', declineReasons: [], referReasons,
             approvedAmount: null, interestRate: null, conditions: [] };
  }

  // ── APPROVAL + PRICING ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  const { amount, rate } = computeApprovalTerms(application, bureauReport);
  return {
    decision: 'APPROVE',
    declineReasons: [],
    referReasons: [],
    approvedAmount: amount,
    interestRate: rate,
    conditions: buildConditions(application, bureauReport),
  };
}
```

**Q11. How do you compute the debt-to-income (DTI) ratio using bureau data, and why is it an underwriting critical metric?**
A: DTI (Debt-to-Income ratio) measures how much of a borrower's gross monthly income is already committed to debt repayments. A high DTI means the borrower has little capacity to absorb a new EMI — even if their CIBIL score is good.

```
DTI = (Total existing monthly EMI obligations) / (Gross monthly income) × 100
```

**Computing existing obligations from bureau:**

```typescript
function computeExistingEmiObligations(accounts: NormalisedAccount[]): bigint {
  return accounts
    .filter(a => a.status === 'ACTIVE')
    .reduce((total, account) => {
      // For term loans: approximate monthly EMI from outstanding balance
      // Bureau doesn't always provide EMI directly — estimate from balance and remaining tenure
      const estimatedEmi = estimateMonthlyObligation(account);
      return total + estimatedEmi;
    }, 0n);
}

function estimateMonthlyObligation(account: NormalisedAccount): bigint {
  // If it's a credit card, minimum payment is typically 5% of outstanding
  if (account.accountType === 'CREDIT_CARD') {
    return account.currentBalance * 5n / 100n;
  }

  // For term loans: use rough approximation if EMI not directly available
  // EMI ≈ outstanding_balance / remaining_months (simplified — ignores interest)
  // Better: use the current balance and a standard rate assumption
  const assumedMonthlyRate = new Decimal('0.015');  // 18% p.a. assumption
  const assumedRemainingMonths = 24;  // default if not available
  const emi = computeEmiDecimal(account.currentBalance, assumedMonthlyRate, assumedRemainingMonths);
  return emi;
}
```

**DTI thresholds (typical Indian NBFC underwriting):**

| DTI | Risk level | Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 30% | Low | Approve; full requested amount |
| 30–40% | Medium | Approve; may reduce amount |
| 40–50% | High | Refer; strong income verification required |
| > 50% | Very high | Decline for unsecured; conditional for secured |
| > 60% | Critical | Decline regardless of score |

**Including the proposed loan in DTI calculation:**
DTI must include the proposed new EMI — not just existing obligations. A borrower with 35% DTI from existing loans who requests a new loan that would bring DTI to 55% should be declined even though their existing DTI was within limits.

```typescript
function computeForwardDti(
  existingObligations: bigint,
  proposedEmi: bigint,
  monthlyIncome: bigint,
): number {
  const totalObligations = existingObligations + proposedEmi;
  return Number(totalObligations * 100n / monthlyIncome);
}
```

**Q12. How do you handle the New-to-Credit (NTC) borrower — score is -1 (No Hit)?**
A: NH (No Hit) means the bureau has no record for this PAN. This is common for:
- First-time borrowers (students, recent graduates, rural borrowers).
- Borrowers who have only used informal credit (moneylenders, family).
- Borrowers whose lenders don't report to the queried bureau.

NTC borrowers represent both a risk (no history to assess) and an opportunity (many fintechs specifically target them with alternate data models).

**Handling strategy:**

```typescript
async function handleNtcBorrower(
  application: LoanApplication,
  ntcReport: NormalisedBureauReport,
): Promise<CreditDecision> {
  // Step 1: Try all four bureaus — borrower may appear in one but not another
  const allBureauReports = await pullAllBureaus(application.pan);
  const hitReport = allBureauReports.find(r => r.score > 0);
  if (hitReport) return makeCreditDecision(application, hitReport);

  // Step 2: Confirmed NTC — evaluate alternate data signals
  const alternateScore = await computeAlternateDataScore(application);

  // Alternate data sources:
  // - Bank statement analysis (income stability, spending patterns, savings behaviour)
  // - GST return data (for self-employed / MSME borrowers)
  // - Telco data (payment of mobile bills)
  // - E-commerce / UPI transaction history (with consent)
  // - Education / employment verification

  if (alternateScore.confidence === 'HIGH' && alternateScore.score >= 65) {
    return {
      decision: 'APPROVE',
      approvedAmount: computeNtcLoanAmount(application, alternateScore),
      interestRate:   NTC_INTEREST_RATE_PREMIUM,  // higher rate for NTC risk
      conditions:     ['SALARY_ACCOUNT_LINKAGE', 'NACH_MANDATORY', 'REDUCED_TENURE'],
    };
  }

  if (alternateScore.confidence === 'MEDIUM') {
    return { decision: 'REFER', referReasons: ['NTC_ALTERNATE_DATA_INCONCLUSIVE'] };
  }

  return { decision: 'DECLINE', declineReasons: ['NTC_INSUFFICIENT_ALTERNATE_DATA'] };
}
```

**Thin-file strategy:**
Some lenders offer a "credit builder" product — a small secured loan (against FD) or credit card — to NTC borrowers specifically to establish a bureau record. After 6–12 months of on-time payments, the borrower graduates to standard products.

```typescript
async function runMonthlyBureauReporting(reportMonth: Date): Promise<void> {
  const monthEnd = endOfMonth(reportMonth);

  // Step 1: Extract all accounts active during the report month
  const accounts = await db('loans')
    .leftJoin('loan_repayment_schedule as lrs', q =>
      q.on('lrs.loan_id', 'loans.id')
       .andOnVal('lrs.due_date', '<=', monthEnd)
       .andOnVal('lrs.due_date', '>=', startOfMonth(reportMonth))
    )
    .whereIn('loans.status', ['ACTIVE', 'CLOSED', 'NPA', 'WRITTEN_OFF'])
    .where('loans.disbursement_date', '<=', monthEnd)
    .select(
      'loans.*',
      db.raw('MIN(lrs.due_date) as earliest_due_this_month'),
      db.raw('SUM(lrs.total_emi - lrs.amount_paid) FILTER (WHERE lrs.due_date < CURRENT_DATE) as overdue_amount'),
    )
    .groupBy('loans.id');

  // Step 2: Build bureau records
  const records: BureauMonthlyRecord[] = await Promise.all(
    accounts.map(a => buildMonthlyRecord(a, monthEnd))

  const validationErrors = validateBureauRecords(records);

    throw new BureauReportingError(validationErrors);

  const bureaus = ['CIBIL', 'EXPERIAN', 'EQUIFAX', 'CRIF'];
  for (const bureau of bureaus) {
    await submitToBureau(bureau, records, reportMonth);
  }
}
```

**Q14. How do you handle bureau data disputes raised by borrowers, and what is the engineering obligation?**
A: CICRA gives borrowers the right to dispute incorrect information in their bureau report. When a borrower disputes data, the process is:

1. Borrower raises dispute with the bureau (online or via lender).
2. Bureau notifies the lender (the member who submitted the data).
3. Lender must investigate and respond within 30 days (CICRA Section 21).
4. If data is confirmed correct: bureau maintains it; borrower is notified.
5. If data is incorrect: lender submits a correction to the bureau; bureau updates the report.

```typescript
      return { resolution: 'REJECTED', correctionRequired: false };
    }
  }
```

## Section 5: Security, Compliance & Data Handling

**Q15. What are the data security requirements for bureau data, and how do you implement them?**
A: Bureau data (credit reports) contains highly sensitive PII — financial history, PAN, address, phone. Multiple regulations govern its handling:

**CICRA requirements:**
- Bureau data may only be used for the purpose stated in the consent.
- Data must not be shared with third parties without separate consent.
- Bureau reports must be protected against unauthorised access.
- Access logs must be maintained for audit.

**PCI-DSS / RBI IT Framework requirements:**
- PAN and Aadhaar must be masked in all non-production environments.
- Raw bureau responses must be encrypted at rest.
- Access to raw bureau data must be role-based and logged.

**Implementation:**

```typescript
// Encryption at rest — raw bureau response stored encrypted
const ENCRYPTION_KEY = process.env.BUREAU_DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY;  // AES-256

function encrypt(plaintext: string): string {
  const iv = crypto.randomBytes(16);
  const cipher = crypto.createCipheriv('aes-256-gcm', Buffer.from(ENCRYPTION_KEY, 'hex'), iv);
  const encrypted = Buffer.concat([cipher.update(plaintext, 'utf8'), cipher.final()]);
  const authTag = cipher.getAuthTag();
  return `${iv.toString('hex')}:${authTag.toString('hex')}:${encrypted.toString('hex')}`;
}

// PAN masking — for logs and non-production
function maskPan(pan: string): string {
  return `${pan.slice(0, 2)}XXXXXX${pan.slice(-2)}`;   // e.g., AB1234567C → ABXXXXXXXX7C
}
```

**Q16. What is the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 impact on bureau integrations?**
A: The DPDP Act (India's privacy law, effective 2024) introduces obligations beyond CICRA for credit bureau integrations:

**Key DPDP provisions affecting bureau pulls:**

| Provision | Impact | Engineering action |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose limitation** | Data collected for loan origination cannot be used for marketing or collections without separate consent | Separate `purpose` field on each bureau pull; enforce at query layer |
| **Data minimisation** | Collect only what's needed for the stated purpose | Request only required fields from bureau API; don't log full PAN |

## Section 6: Scenario / System Design Questions

**Q17. "Design a credit bureau integration system for an NBFC processing 20,000 loan applications per day."**
A: At 20,000 applications/day, bureau pulls are a high-volume, cost-sensitive, latency-critical operation. Each pull costs ₹20–₹50 and takes 2–8 seconds.

**Architecture:**

```
Loan Application API
      ↓ (async — return application ID immediately)
Application Queue (SQS / Kafka)
      ↓
Bureau Pull Service (horizontally scaled — 20 workers)
  ├── Consent validation
  ├── Cache check (30-day TTL)
  ├── Primary bureau pull (CIBIL) with 8s timeout
  ├── Fallback pull (Experian) if NH or failure
  ├── Store raw response (encrypted, S3 + DB)
  ├── Parse + normalise
  └── Publish to Credit Decision Queue
      ↓
Credit Decision Engine
  ├── Rule evaluation
  ├── DTI computation
  └── Approval / Decline / Refer
      ↓
Application State Update + Notification
```

**Cost optimisation at scale:**
- 20,000 applications/day × ₹35/pull = ₹7 lakh/day if every application pulls from CIBIL.
- Cache hit rate target: 15–20% (repeat applicants, re-applications within 30 days).
- Pre-screening: before bureau pull, reject obviously ineligible applications (age, income < minimum, blacklisted PIN codes) to reduce unnecessary pulls.

```typescript
async function preScreenBeforeBureauPull(application: LoanApplication): Promise<PreScreenResult> {
  // Hard cutoffs that don't require bureau data

    return { eligible: false, reason: 'AGE_OUT_OF_RANGE' };
  }
```

**Bureau pull worker pool sizing:**
- 20,000 applications/day = ~14 applications/minute = ~0.23/second.
- Each pull takes ~4 seconds average.
- Workers needed: `0.23 req/s × 4 s/req = ~1 concurrent` (single-threaded easily handles this).
- But with retry spikes and latency variability: 20 workers is a safe pool size.
- Auto-scale: add workers if queue depth > 100.

**Q18. "A lender says your system pulled bureau reports without valid consent for 500 customers. How do you investigate and remediate?"**
A: This is a CICRA violation with potential regulatory consequences. Systematic investigation:

**Step 1 — Confirm the scope (30 minutes)**
```sql
-- Find bureau pulls with no corresponding consent, or with expired/withdrawn consent
SELECT bp.id, bp.customer_id, bp.pulled_at, bp.bureau, bp.purpose,
       bc.id AS consent_id, bc.consented_at, bc.expires_at, bc.is_withdrawn
FROM bureau_pulls bp
LEFT JOIN bureau_consents bc ON bc.id = bp.consent_id
WHERE bp.pulled_at BETWEEN '<start_date>' AND '<end_date>'
  AND (
    bc.id IS NULL                          -- no consent linked
    OR bc.is_withdrawn = TRUE              -- consent was withdrawn before pull
    OR bc.expires_at < bp.pulled_at        -- consent expired before pull
    OR bc.purpose <> bp.purpose            -- purpose mismatch
  );
```

**Step 2 — Classify the failures**
- Missing consent: pull happened but no consent record was created (likely a bug in the consent capture → pull linkage).
- Expired consent: consent was created but pull happened after the 6-month validity window.
- Purpose mismatch: consent was for "loan origination" but pull was for "collections monitoring."

**Step 3 — Root cause**

Most common causes:
- A code path that pulls bureau without going through the consent validation middleware (e.g., a background job, a retry mechanism, a new feature that bypassed the existing consent check).
- A race condition: consent captured asynchronously, bureau pull initiated immediately — consent record not yet written to DB when pull validation ran.
- An expired consent that wasn't flagged as invalid in the cache check.

**Step 4 — Remediation**
```typescript
// Add a hard gate in the bureau pull function — not just middleware
async function pullBureauReportWithConsentValidation(
  customerId: string,
  bureau: string,
  purpose: string,
): Promise<BureauReport> {
  // Validate consent INSIDE the function — cannot be bypassed
  const consent = await db('bureau_consents')
    .where({ customer_id: customerId, purpose, is_withdrawn: false })
    .where('expires_at', '>', new Date())
    .orderBy('consented_at', 'desc')
    .first();

  if (!consent) {
    throw new ConsentNotFoundError(
      `No valid consent for customer ${customerId}, purpose ${purpose}`
    );
  }

  // Proceed with pull, linking to consent
  return pullBureauReport(bureau, { customerId }, consent.id);
}
```

**Step 5 — Regulatory notification**
If the consent violation affected more than 500 customers or involved a systematic failure (not a one-off), RBI and the affected bureaus must be notified. Work with the compliance and legal team to draft the notification. The self-reporting is mandatory and viewed more favorably than a bureau or RBI discovering it independently.

```typescript
// Monthly portfolio review job — runs 1st of each month
async function runMonthlyPortfolioReview(reviewMonth: Date): Promise<void> {
  const activeLoans = await db('loans')
    .where({ status: 'ACTIVE' })
    .whereNotExists(  // not already reviewed this month
      db('portfolio_monitoring_schedule')
        .where('scheduled_month', startOfMonth(reviewMonth))
        .whereColumn('loan_id', 'loans.id')
    );

  // Process in batches to manage bureau API rate limits and cost
  const BATCH_SIZE = 100;
  for (const batch of chunk(activeLoans, BATCH_SIZE)) {
    await Promise.all(batch.map(async (loan) => {
      const report = await getCachedOrFreshReport(
        loan.customer_id, loan.pan, 'CIBIL', 'ACCOUNT_REVIEW'
      );

      const deterioration = detectCreditDeterioration(loan, report);

        loan_id: loan.id,
        customer_id: loan.customer_id,
        scheduled_month: startOfMonth(reviewMonth),
        status: 'COMPLETED',
        pulled_at: new Date(),
        deterioration_detected: deterioration.detected,
      });

      if (deterioration.detected) {
        await escalateDeteriorationAlert(loan, deterioration);
      }
    }));

function detectCreditDeterioration(
  loan: Loan,
  currentReport: NormalisedBureauReport,
): DeteriorationAssessment {
  const signals: string[] = [];

  // Signal 1: Score drop > 50 points since last review
  if (loan.last_bureau_score && currentReport.score > 0) {
    const scoreDrop = loan.last_bureau_score - currentReport.score;
    if (scoreDrop > 50) signals.push(`SCORE_DROP_${scoreDrop}`);
  }

  // Signal 2: New NPA or write-off on any account
  if (currentReport.flags.hasNpa && !loan.known_npa_flag) {
    signals.push('NEW_NPA_DETECTED');
  }

  // Signal 3: New overdue > ₹5,000 on any account with another lender
  const newOverdue = currentReport.summary.totalOverdue > 5_000_00n;
  if (newOverdue) signals.push('NEW_EXTERNAL_OVERDUE');

  // Signal 4: Enquiry spike — 3+ new enquiries since last month
  const recentEnquiries = currentReport.enquiries
    .filter(e => isAfter(e.date, subMonths(new Date(), 1))).length;
  if (recentEnquiries >= 3) signals.push('ENQUIRY_SPIKE');

  // Signal 5: New unsecured loan opened (increased leverage)
  const newAccounts = currentReport.accounts
    .filter(a => a.openDate && isAfter(a.openDate, subMonths(new Date(), 1)));
  if (newAccounts.length > 0) signals.push('NEW_EXTERNAL_LOAN');

  return {
    detected: signals.length > 0,
    signals,
    severity: signals.includes('NEW_NPA_DETECTED') ? 'CRITICAL'
              : signals.length >= 2 ? 'HIGH' : 'MEDIUM',
  };
}
```

**Tip for the interview:** CIBIL integration questions at the 5 YOE level appear as "how do you integrate with credit bureaus," "design a credit decisioning system," and "what happens when a bureau pull fails during loan origination." The detail that separates senior answers is consent architecture — most candidates describe pulling a bureau report but completely ignore the CICRA consent requirement. Know that every pull must be linked to a purpose-specific consent, that consent has a validity window, and that withdrawn consent must block future pulls but not retroactively delete existing data (CICRA retention vs DPDP erasure tension). The multi-bureau fallback strategy (pull CIBIL; fall back on NH or failure) with cost analysis (₹35/pull × 20,000 applications/day) demonstrates operational awareness. The NTC path (NH score) and the portfolio monitoring monthly review are the two scenarios most candidates overlook — both are extremely common in Indian fintech and distinguish someone who has operated a lending system from someone who has only read about it.
