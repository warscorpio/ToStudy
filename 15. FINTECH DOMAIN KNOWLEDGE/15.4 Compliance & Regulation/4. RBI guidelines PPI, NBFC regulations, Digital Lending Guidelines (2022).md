## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is the RBI's regulatory architecture for fintech, and how do PPIs, NBFCs, and Digital Lending fit within it?**
A: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is the apex financial regulator in India, exercising authority over banks, non-bank financial companies, payment systems, and credit markets. Its regulatory architecture for fintech operates across three overlapping frameworks:

**Payments regulation (Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 — PSS Act):**
The PSS Act gives RBI exclusive authority to regulate and supervise payment systems in India. Under it, the RBI issues licenses and guidelines for:
- Payment System Operators (PSOs): UPI, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS (operated by NPCI and RBI)
- Payment Aggregators (PAs): entities that facilitate card/net banking payments for merchants
- Payment Gateways (PGs): technology providers (regulated only if they hold funds)
- **Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs)**: wallets, prepaid cards, gift cards — governed by the Master Directions on PPIs

**Credit regulation (Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 + RBI Act amendments):**
Any entity that lends money to the public (other than banks) must be registered as an NBFC under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act. NBFCs are governed by:
- NBFC Master Directions (general framework)
- Specific category directions (NBFC-MFI, NBFC-P2P, NBFC-AA, etc.)
- Prudential norms (income recognition, asset classification, provisioning)
- Fair Practices Code

**Digital Lending (2022 Guidelines):**
The Digital Lending Guidelines (September 2022) are not a separate license category — they overlay the NBFC and bank framework and govern how digital lending must be conducted. They were prompted by predatory practices of Loan Service Providers (LSPs) and digital lending apps.

**How they intersect:**
A fintech company building a lending product may simultaneously need:
- A PPI license (if they issue a wallet for disbursing or repaying loans)
- An NBFC license (if they lend their own capital)
- Compliance with Digital Lending Guidelines (if the origination is app/web-based)
- A PA license (if they process payments for merchants)

Each framework has distinct compliance requirements, and a single product can fall under multiple frameworks simultaneously.

**Q2. What are the key RBI regulatory categories, and what does each license permit?**
A: The RBI issues distinct authorizations for different financial activities:

| License / Authorization | What it permits | Capital requirement | Regulator |
|--|--|--|--|
| **Scheduled Commercial Bank** | Full banking — deposits, lending, payments | ₹500 crore min paid-up capital | RBI |
| **Small Finance Bank** | Retail banking focus; 75% to underserved | ₹200 crore | RBI |
| **Payment Bank** | Deposits (up to ₹2 lakh/customer), payments, remittances — NO lending | ₹100 crore | RBI |
| **NBFC-Investment & Credit (ICC)** | Lending, investment — cannot take public deposits | ₹2 crore (min); ₹10 crore for systemically important | RBI |
| **NBFC-P2P** | Peer-to-peer lending platform | ₹2 crore | RBI |
| **NBFC-Account Aggregator (AA)** | Consent-based financial data sharing — cannot lend or hold funds | ₹2 crore | RBI |
| **NBFC-MFI** | Microfinance — min 85% qualifying assets to low-income | ₹5 crore (₹2 crore NE states) | RBI |
| **PPI — Small** | Closed/semi-closed wallets up to ₹10,000 balance, min KYC | None specified | RBI |
| **PPI — Full-KYC** | Wallets up to ₹2 lakh balance, interoperability | ₹15 crore net worth | RBI |
| **Payment Aggregator** | Facilitate online payments between merchants and customers | ₹15 crore (existing); ₹25 crore by March 2026 | RBI |

**Engineering relevance:** each license has ongoing compliance obligations that must be engineered into the product:
- Capital adequacy ratios require real-time computation of risk-weighted assets.
- PPI balance limits require enforcement at the transaction level.
- PA merchant onboarding requires KYC workflows before the merchant can go live.
- NBFC fair practices require specific disclosures in the loan origination flow.

## Section 2: Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs)

**Q3. What is a PPI, what are its categories, and what are the key regulatory constraints on each?**
A: A Prepaid Payment Instrument is any instrument that allows a person to access a pre-loaded monetary value stored on it for the purchase of goods and services, remittance, and fund transfers. The Master Directions on PPIs (updated most recently in 2021) define three categories:

**1. Closed System PPIs**
Issued by entities for purchase of their own goods and services only. The issuer does not permit cash withdrawal or transfer to third parties.
- Examples: Flipkart gift cards (redeemable only on Flipkart), OLA Money on OLA platform (pre-2019 interoperability).
- **No RBI authorization required** — closed system PPIs are outside the PSS Act's purview as long as funds cannot leave the issuer's platform.
- Balance limit: no RBI cap (issuer's commercial decision).
- Engineering constraint: the system must technically prevent fund transfer to external accounts or cash withdrawal — it is not merely a contractual restriction.

**2. Semi-Closed System PPIs**
Issued by RBI-authorized entities; can be used at a group of clearly identified merchants/establishments that have a specific contract with the issuer. Cannot be used for cash withdrawal at ATMs.
- Examples: Paytm Wallet (before full-KYC), PhonePe wallet, prepaid gift cards usable at partner merchants.
- **Requires RBI authorization** under the PSS Act.
- Two sub-types based on KYC:

| Property | Minimum KYC PPI | Full-KYC PPI |
|--|--|--|
| KYC required | Mobile number + self-declaration | Full KYC (Aadhaar OTP / V-KYC / physical) |
| Maximum balance | ₹10,000 | ₹2,00,000 |
| Loading limit | ₹10,000/month | No monthly limit |
| Permitted debits | Merchant payments | Merchant payments + fund transfers to bank/other PPIs |
| Cash withdrawal | Not permitted | Permitted (with full-KYC) |
| Validity | 1 year | 2 years (renewable) |
| Interoperability | Not mandatory | Mandatory (UPI-linked) |

**3. Open System PPIs**
Issued only by banks; can be used for all payment transactions including ATM withdrawal, PoS, online, and fund transfers.
- Examples: Visa prepaid cards issued by banks, RuPay prepaid cards.
- **Only banks** can issue open system PPIs — NBFCs and fintechs cannot.
- Effectively functions like a debit card on a prepaid account.

**Engineering constraints on PPIs:**

*Balance limits:* the system must enforce the ₹10,000 (min KYC) and ₹2,00,000 (full KYC) balance caps at transaction time, not just at loading time. If a customer has ₹9,500 balance and receives a cashback of ₹600, the balance would breach the min-KYC limit. The system must either block the cashback or trigger a KYC upgrade prompt.

*KYC upgrade flow:* a customer who reaches the min-KYC loading limit (₹10,000/month) must be prompted to complete full KYC to continue using the wallet. The engineering system must track cumulative monthly loads, block further loading when the limit is reached, and offer a seamless KYC upgrade flow that converts the account to full-KYC without disrupting the existing balance.

*Expiry and dormancy:* PPIs that are inactive for 1 year (min KYC) or 2 years (full KYC) are considered expired. The system must notify customers before expiry, handle the expiry event (blocking transactions), and process the refund of the outstanding balance to the customer's linked bank account within the prescribed timeframe.

**Q4. What are the RBI's KYC requirements for PPIs, and how do you engineer the KYC workflow?**
A: KYC (Know Your Customer) requirements for PPIs are governed by the RBI's Master Direction on KYC (2016, updated 2023) and the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA). Engineering the KYC workflow is one of the most complex aspects of building a PPI product.

**KYC hierarchy for PPIs:**

**Level 1 — Minimum KYC (wallet creation):**
- Mobile number verified via OTP.
- Name and self-declared identification (PAN number entered by customer, not verified against NSDL at this stage for small PPIs).
- Enables: ₹10,000 balance, ₹10,000/month loading, merchant payments only.
- No documents uploaded; no third-party verification at this stage.

**Level 2 — Basic KYC (intermediate tier):**
- Mobile number + PAN verification against NSDL/Income Tax department API.
- Or: Aadhaar number entered + OTP-based e-KYC via UIDAI API.
- Enables: higher loading limits (varies by issuer's risk policy).

**Level 3 — Full KYC (complete tier):**
Four accepted methods under RBI's Master Direction on KYC:
1. **Aadhaar OTP e-KYC**: Aadhaar number + OTP consent → UIDAI returns name, address, photo. Instant, fully digital. RBI imposes a per-entity annual limit on Aadhaar authentications.
2. **Video KYC (V-KYC)**: Live video call with a trained agent; customer shows original documents; agent captures live photo and document images. Fully digital but requires human agent and scheduling. RBI guidelines for V-KYC are stringent (liveness check, agent location, recording retention).
3. **Physical / Branch KYC**: customer visits a branch or authorized agent with original documents. Slowest; used as fallback.
4. **DigiLocker-based KYC**: documents pulled from DigiLocker (government document repository). Emerging; not yet universally accepted for all PPI types.

**Engineering the KYC workflow:**

```python
class PPIKYCWorkflow:
    def initiate_kyc(self, ppi_account_id, kyc_method):
        account = get_ppi_account(ppi_account_id)
        
        if kyc_method == 'aadhaar_otp':
            # Aadhaar OTP e-KYC flow
            # Step 1: Collect Aadhaar number
            # Step 2: Send OTP consent request to UIDAI
            # Step 3: Customer enters OTP
            # Step 4: UIDAI returns demographic data + photo
            # Step 5: Store encrypted KYC data; DO NOT store Aadhaar number
            #         (prohibited by Aadhaar Act — store only last 4 digits)
            return initiate_aadhaar_otp_kyc(account)
        
        elif kyc_method == 'video_kyc':
            # V-KYC flow
            # Step 1: Schedule video call slot
            # Step 2: Customer joins at scheduled time
            # Step 3: Agent conducts liveliness check (random gesture/word)
            # Step 4: Agent captures ID document + photo
            # Step 5: OCR + face match against ID document
            # Step 6: Agent approves / rejects
            # Step 7: Record stored for 10 years (RBI requirement)
            return schedule_video_kyc(account)
    
    def post_kyc_upgrade(self, ppi_account_id, kyc_result):
        if kyc_result.status == 'approved':
            upgrade_account_tier(ppi_account_id, tier='full_kyc')
            update_balance_limit(ppi_account_id, new_limit=200_000)
            enable_fund_transfer(ppi_account_id)
            enable_cash_withdrawal(ppi_account_id)
            send_kyc_completion_notification(ppi_account_id)
        
        elif kyc_result.status == 'rejected':
            record_kyc_failure(ppi_account_id, reason=kyc_result.rejection_reason)
            notify_customer_kyc_failed(ppi_account_id, kyc_result.rejection_reason)
            # Account remains at min-KYC tier; customer can retry after 24 hours
```

**Critical data storage constraints (Aadhaar Act, 2016):**
- The full 12-digit Aadhaar number **must not be stored** by any entity other than UIDAI. Store only the last 4 digits plus the Virtual ID (VID) used for the authentication.
- The biometric data returned by UIDAI (photo) must be stored in encrypted form with access controls.
- Aadhaar authentication logs must be retained for a minimum of 6 months.
- Any breach of Aadhaar data is a criminal offense under the Aadhaar Act — not merely a regulatory violation.

**Q5. What are the interoperability requirements for full-KYC PPIs, and what do they mean for engineering?**
A: RBI's 2021 PPI guidelines mandated full interoperability for full-KYC PPIs. This means:

**Mandatory interoperability requirements:**
1. **UPI interoperability**: all full-KYC PPIs must be linked to UPI. The PPI issuer must integrate with NPCI's UPI infrastructure to allow UPI-based payments from the wallet.
2. **Card interoperability**: full-KYC PPIs issued in card form (physical or virtual) must run on Visa, Mastercard, or RuPay networks.
3. **Fund transfer interoperability**: full-KYC PPI holders must be able to transfer funds to any bank account in India (IMPS/NEFT) and to other PPI wallets.

**Engineering implications:**

*UPI integration for PPI:*
The PPI issuer must become a Prepaid Payment Instrument member of NPCI (different from a bank's UPI membership). The technical integration requires:
- Implementing UPI APIs (collect, pay, mandate) with the PPI's VPA (Virtual Payment Address) format: `mobilenumber@issuername`.
- Handling UPI push (customer-initiated payment from wallet) and UPI pull (merchant-initiated collect request).
- Real-time balance checks against the PPI balance before authorizing any UPI transaction.
- The NPCI system does not hold the PPI balance — the issuer's system does. The issuer must respond to UPI authorization requests in real time (< 5 seconds per NPCI SLA).

*Fund transfer limits:*
Post interoperability, the following limits apply to full-KYC PPIs:
- Fund transfer to bank: up to ₹2 lakh per transaction (within overall balance limit).
- Fund transfer to other PPIs: up to ₹2 lakh per transaction.
- No limit on number of transactions per day (subject to issuer's internal risk limits).

*Reporting to NPCI:*
PPI issuers must submit daily settlement files to NPCI for UPI transactions and reconcile against NPCI's settlement. The technical format is specified in NPCI's UPI Technical Specifications document. Engineering must build the settlement file generation and automated reconciliation pipeline.

## Section 3: NBFC Regulations

**Q6. What are the key NBFC categories in fintech, and what are the regulatory requirements for each?**
A: RBI has created specialized NBFC categories for fintech-relevant activities. The most important for engineers to understand:

**NBFC-Investment and Credit Company (NBFC-ICC)**
The general-purpose lending NBFC. Most digital lenders (consumer lending apps, BNPL companies, personal loan fintechs) operate as NBFC-ICCs.

Key requirements:
- **Net Owned Funds (NOF)**: minimum ₹2 crore for registration; ₹10 crore for Systemically Important NBFCs (assets > ₹500 crore).
- **Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)**: minimum 15% (Tier 1 + Tier 2 capital as a percentage of risk-weighted assets). Banks need only 9%; NBFCs have a higher buffer requirement.
- **NPA Norms**: loans overdue > 90 days classified as NPA (same as banks post-2021 harmonization). Prior to 2021, NBFCs used a 180-day NPA recognition threshold — the harmonization to 90 days significantly tightened NPA recognition.
- **Asset Classification**: Standard → SMA-0 (1-30 DPD) → SMA-1 (31-60 DPD) → SMA-2 (61-90 DPD) → NPA (90+ DPD).
- **Provisioning**: Standard: 0.25–1%; Sub-standard: 10%; Doubtful: 20–50%; Loss: 100%.
- **Leverage**: Net leverage cap of 7× NOF for non-deposit-taking NBFCs.

**NBFC-MFI (Microfinance Institution)**
Specialized NBFCs focused on microfinance lending to low-income households.
Key regulatory requirements:
- Minimum 85% of qualifying assets must be microfinance loans (loans to households with annual income ≤ ₹3 lakh in rural areas, ≤ ₹3 lakh in urban/semi-urban areas).
- Loan size caps: up to ₹1.25 lakh per individual borrower (across all lenders).
- Repayment frequency: weekly, fortnightly, or monthly — borrower's choice.
- **Pricing ceiling**: the interest rate cap is calculated as the lower of: (a) cost of funds + 10% margin for large MFIs (loan portfolio > ₹100 crore) or (b) cost of funds + 12% for smaller MFIs.
- No processing fee > 1% of loan amount.
- **Household indebtedness cap**: a household cannot borrow more than ₹3 lakh in total across all MFIs. MFIs must check this via credit bureau before disbursing.

Engineering implication: the MFI must pull credit bureau data before every disbursal and validate that the total outstanding (across all lenders) + proposed loan ≤ ₹3 lakh. This is a hard gate — the disbursal must not proceed if the cap would be breached.

**NBFC-P2P (Peer-to-Peer Lending Platform)**
Technology platforms that facilitate lending directly between individual lenders (investors) and borrowers, without the NBFC itself taking credit risk.
Key regulatory requirements:
- **Balance sheet lending prohibited**: the platform cannot lend its own funds. All loans must be funded by registered lenders on the platform.
- **Lender limits**: any single lender across all P2P platforms combined: maximum ₹50 lakh total lent.
- **Borrower limits**: any single borrower across all P2P platforms: maximum ₹50 lakh outstanding.
- **Loan tenure**: maximum 36 months.
- **Funds must flow via escrow**: borrower repayments and lender disbursements must flow through a bank-held escrow account — the platform cannot commingle funds.
- **NDA (Net Asset Value) disclosure**: lenders must receive clear disclosure of the return earned and principal at risk.

Engineering implication: the escrow architecture is a core system design constraint. The platform cannot hold funds in its own account. Every rupee must flow:
```
Lender bank account → Escrow bank account → Borrower bank account (disbursal)
Borrower bank account → Escrow bank account → Lender bank account (repayment)
```
The engineering system must maintain a ledger of each lender's committed and disbursed amounts, each borrower's repayment allocation across lenders, and escrow account reconciliation.

**NBFC-Account Aggregator (AA)**
Account Aggregators are consent managers — they enable customers to share their financial data (bank statements, mutual fund holdings, insurance policies, tax returns) with Financial Information Users (FIUs, typically lenders) with explicit, revocable consent.

Key regulatory requirements:
- **Cannot hold funds**: the AA is purely a data conduit.
- **Cannot share data without consent**: every data share requires a fresh, specific consent from the customer (specifying what data, for how long, for what purpose, with whom).
- **Consent Artefact**: the consent is represented as a digitally signed JSON artifact containing: purpose, data range, frequency, expiry, and the data types consented to. The AA stores this artefact and provides it to FIUs on request.
- **Technical standard**: AA ecosystem operates on the Sahamati network, using the ReBIT-defined API specifications (Account Aggregator Technical Standards).

Engineering implication: if you are building a lending product that uses AA data for underwriting, your system must:
1. Request consent via the AA framework (not directly from the customer).
2. Accept and validate the digitally signed consent artefact.
3. Fetch the consented data via the AA's FIP (Financial Information Provider) connector.
4. Use the data only within the consented purpose and timeframe.
5. Delete the data after the consented period expires.
This is a significant engineering contract — the data you receive via AA has a legal expiry.

**Q7. What are the key ongoing compliance requirements for NBFCs, and how are they engineered?**
A: NBFCs must maintain continuous compliance across several dimensions — not one-time registration, but ongoing monitoring and reporting obligations.

**1. Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) monitoring:**

```python
def compute_car(as_of_date):
    # Tier 1 Capital: equity share capital + retained earnings - intangible assets
    tier1 = (
        get_equity_share_capital(as_of_date) +
        get_retained_earnings(as_of_date) -
        get_intangible_assets(as_of_date) -
        get_accumulated_losses(as_of_date)
    )
    
    # Tier 2 Capital: subordinated debt + general provisions (capped at 1.25% of RWA)
    tier2 = min(
        get_subordinated_debt(as_of_date) + get_general_provisions(as_of_date),
        get_risk_weighted_assets(as_of_date) * Decimal('0.0125')
    )
    
    total_capital = tier1 + tier2
    
    # Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA): each asset class × its risk weight
    rwa = compute_rwa(as_of_date)
    # Risk weights (Basel-inspired, RBI-adjusted):
    # Cash and government securities: 0%
    # Claims on banks: 20%
    # Retail loans (consumer credit): 100%
    # Commercial real estate: 100%
    # NPA (outstanding net of provisions): 150%
    
    car = (total_capital / rwa) * 100
    
    return CARResult(car=car, tier1=tier1, tier2=tier2, rwa=rwa,
                     compliant=(car >= Decimal('15.0')))

# Run daily; alert if CAR approaches 15% (minimum threshold)
# Alert at 16% (1% buffer); escalate to board at 15.5%
```

**2. NPA and Provisioning computation:**

```python
def compute_portfolio_provisions(as_of_date):
    loans = get_all_active_loans(as_of_date)
    total_provisions = Decimal('0')
    
    for loan in loans:
        dpd = compute_dpd(loan.id, as_of_date)
        outstanding = compute_outstanding_principal(loan.id, as_of_date)
        
        if dpd == 0:
            provision_rate = Decimal('0.0025')    # 0.25% — standard asset
        elif dpd <= 30:
            provision_rate = Decimal('0.0025')    # SMA-0 — still standard provisioning
        elif dpd <= 60:
            provision_rate = Decimal('0.0025')    # SMA-1 — still standard
        elif dpd <= 90:
            provision_rate = Decimal('0.0025')    # SMA-2 — still standard (last chance)
        elif dpd <= 365:
            provision_rate = Decimal('0.10')      # Sub-standard (NPA 90-365 DPD)
        elif dpd <= 730:
            provision_rate = Decimal('0.40')      # Doubtful-1 (NPA 1-3 years)
        elif dpd <= 1095:
            provision_rate = Decimal('1.00')      # Doubtful-2/3 / Loss
        else:
            provision_rate = Decimal('1.00')      # Loss asset — fully provisioned
        
        loan_provision = outstanding * provision_rate
        total_provisions += loan_provision
        
        update_loan_provision(loan.id, provision_rate, loan_provision, as_of_date)
    
    return total_provisions
```

| **DNBS-02** | Quarterly | NPA details, provisioning | Automated from loan management system |

A separate "regulatory reporting database" that diverges from operational data is a compliance risk — discrepancies between reported figures and operational figures constitute mis-reporting to the regulator.

**Q8. What is the NBFC Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) framework introduced in 2021, and why does it matter?**
A: The Scale-Based Regulation framework, effective October 2022, replaced the earlier binary classification (systemically important / non-systemically important) with a four-layer pyramid based on asset size and systemic risk. Each layer has progressively stricter regulatory requirements.

**SBR Pyramid:**

```
              ┌─────────────────┐
              │   NBFC-Upper    │   (Top Layer)
              │  Layer (NBFC-UL)│   Asset size: ≥ ₹50,000 crore
              │                 │   Top 10 NBFCs by asset size
              │  Near-bank      │   Requirements: near-bank regulation,
              │  regulation     │   mandatory listing within 3 years
              └────────┬────────┘
              ┌────────┴────────┐
              │  NBFC-Middle    │   (Third Layer)
              │  Layer (NBFC-ML)│   Asset size: ≥ ₹1,000 crore
              │                 │   Requirements: enhanced disclosure,
              │  Enhanced       │   board governance, large exposure limits
              │  regulation     │
              └────────┬────────┘
              ┌────────┴────────┐
              │   NBFC-Base     │   (Second Layer)
              │  Layer (NBFC-BL)│   Asset size: < ₹1,000 crore
              │                 │   Most digital lenders start here
              │  Standard       │   Requirements: standard NBFC norms
              │  regulation     │   + Digital Lending Guidelines
              └────────┬────────┘
              ┌────────┴────────┐
              │  NBFC-Non-      │   (Base Layer)
              │  Systemically   │   Asset size: < ₹500 crore (non-deposit)
              │  Important (BL) │   Reduced compliance burden
              └─────────────────┘
```

**Key changes at NBFC-ML and above:**
- **Large Exposure Framework**: no single borrower or group can account for > 25% of the NBFC's Tier 1 capital (NBFC-ML).
- **Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP)**: NBFC-ML must formally assess whether their capital is adequate for their risk profile beyond the minimum CAR.
- **Board-level Risk Management**: a dedicated Risk Management Committee at the board level is mandatory for NBFC-ML.
- **Mandatory listing**: NBFC-UL entities must list on stock exchanges within 3 years of being classified as upper layer.

**Engineering implication of SBR:**
As a fintech NBFC grows its asset book, it crosses regulatory thresholds that trigger new compliance requirements. The engineering system must:
- Track asset size in real time and alert when approaching ₹500 crore and ₹1,000 crore thresholds.
- Automatically generate the additional regulatory reports required at each layer.
- Implement the Large Exposure Framework as a hard limit in the loan origination system when crossing NBFC-ML threshold.

## Section 4: Digital Lending Guidelines (2022)

**Q9. What are the RBI Digital Lending Guidelines (2022), and what problem were they designed to solve?**
A: The RBI issued the Digital Lending Guidelines on September 2, 2022, implementing recommendations from the Working Group on Digital Lending (November 2021). They address widespread malpractices in the digital lending ecosystem that caused significant consumer harm:

**Problems the guidelines address:**

1. **Unauthorized data collection**: lending apps were accessing contacts, photos, and call logs beyond what was necessary. These were used to harass borrowers and their contacts during collections.

2. **Predatory pricing opacity**: annualized interest rates were not disclosed — apps showed only "1% per day" without converting to APR. Borrowers received ₹9,000 on a ₹10,000 loan after upfront fee deduction, but owed the full ₹10,000.

3. **Illegal recovery practices**: apps threatened to share edited/morphed photos of borrowers with their contacts if they missed payments. Agents called borrowers' employers and family members.

4. **Unregulated Loan Service Providers (LSPs)**: technology companies were running end-to-end lending operations — KYC, underwriting, collections — through a regulated NBFC or bank as a "pass-through." The LSP bore no regulatory scrutiny but controlled the customer experience.

5. **Proliferation of unregistered apps**: apps not associated with any regulated entity were offering loans. When borrowers defaulted, the apps would use criminal intimidation tactics.

**The core regulatory philosophy of the 2022 guidelines:**
All digital lending — regardless of which technology company facilitates it — must ultimately be conducted by a Regulated Entity (RE: bank or NBFC). The RE cannot outsource its regulatory obligations. Every significant customer touchpoint must be attributable to and controlled by the RE.

**Q10. What are the key requirements of the Digital Lending Guidelines, and what does each mean for system design?**
A: The 2022 guidelines impose requirements across the full loan lifecycle. The most significant engineering implications:

**Requirement 1: Funds must flow only through regulated entity accounts**

*Regulation:* loan disbursements must be made only to the borrower's bank account — not to a third-party account, not to a wallet, not to the LSP's account. Repayments must be collected directly into the RE's account.

*Why it matters:* previously, many lending apps would disburse to their own wallet and collect repayments into their own account, then transfer net amounts to the NBFC. The NBFC had no visibility into individual transactions.

*Engineering implication:*
```
PROHIBITED flow:
  NBFC → LSP's bank account → Borrower's wallet → Merchant

REQUIRED flow:
  NBFC's nodal/escrow account → Borrower's bank account directly (IMPS/NEFT)
  Borrower's bank account → NACH mandate → NBFC's collection account directly

The LSP may not intermediate the fund flow. The LSP's role is limited to:
  - Sourcing / marketing
  - KYC assistance (but RE must verify)
  - Technical platform for application
  - Collections communication (but actual mandate is in RE's name)
```

**Requirement 2: Key Fact Statement (KFS) mandatory before loan execution**

*Regulation:* the borrower must receive a standardized Key Fact Statement in a simple, readable format (vernacular language option) before accepting the loan. The KFS must contain in a prescribed format:
- Annual Percentage Rate (APR) — all-in cost including fees and charges
- All fees and charges (processing fee, insurance, documentation)
- Repayment schedule (full amortization table)
- Cooling-off period (see below)
- Grievance redressal officer name and contact

*Engineering implication:*
The KFS is a pre-contract disclosure — the loan agreement e-sign flow must include a step where the KFS is presented and the borrower explicitly acknowledges it (checkbox or separate e-sign). The KFS must be generated dynamically from the actual loan terms, not a static template. Storing the KFS version shown to each borrower (with timestamp) is a compliance requirement — in case of a dispute, the RE must produce the exact KFS the borrower saw.

```python
def generate_kfs(loan_application_id):
    app = get_loan_application(loan_application_id)
    schedule = compute_amortization_schedule(app)
    
    # APR computation — must include ALL charges, not just interest
    # APR = IRR of cash flows (disbursement + all charges in, EMIs out)
    all_charges = (
        app.processing_fee +
        app.insurance_premium +
        app.documentation_charges +
        app.stamp_duty
    )
    net_disbursement = app.approved_amount - all_charges
    
    apr = compute_irr(
        disbursement=net_disbursement,
        payments=[(inst.due_date, inst.emi_amount) for inst in schedule],
        as_annual_rate=True
    )
    
    kfs = KeyFactStatement(
        lender_name=app.re_name,               # Regulated Entity name
        lender_contact=app.re_grievance_contact,
        lsp_name=app.lsp_name,                 # Loan Service Provider (if any)
        loan_amount=app.approved_amount,
        net_disbursement=net_disbursement,
        tenure=app.approved_tenure,
        interest_rate_annual=app.annual_rate,
        apr=apr,
        processing_fee=app.processing_fee,
        insurance=app.insurance_premium,
        other_charges=app.documentation_charges + app.stamp_duty,
        total_repayment_amount=sum(i.emi_amount for i in schedule),
        emi_amount=schedule[0].emi_amount,
        repayment_schedule=schedule,
        cooling_off_days=app.cooling_off_period,
        grievance_officer=app.re_grievance_officer,
        generated_at=utcnow()
    )
    
    # Store immutably — this is a regulatory artifact
    kfs_doc_id = document_store.save(
        render_kfs_pdf(kfs),
        metadata={'loan_application_id': loan_application_id,
                  'document_type': 'KFS',
                  'generated_at': kfs.generated_at},
        immutable=True
    )
    
    return kfs, kfs_doc_id
```

**Requirement 3: Cooling-off period**

*Regulation:* borrowers must be given a minimum cooling-off period (lookback window) after loan disbursement during which they can exit the loan by returning the principal without any prepayment penalty. The duration varies by loan tenure:
- Loans ≤ 7 days tenure: 1-day cooling-off
- Loans > 7 days tenure: 3-day cooling-off

*Engineering implication:*
The loan origination system must:
1. Set `cooling_off_expires_at = disbursal_timestamp + cooling_off_days`.
2. The borrower must be able to initiate a cooling-off exit via the app — a prominent "Cancel Loan" button is required during the cooling-off period.
3. On exit: the system initiates an immediate refund of the disbursed amount to the borrower's bank account. No prepayment penalty is levied. Interest accrued during the cooling-off period may be charged (only interest, no other charges).
4. The loan account transitions to `cancelled_cooling_off` terminal state.
5. After `cooling_off_expires_at`: the "Cancel Loan" option is removed; prepayment terms apply.

**Requirement 4: Data collection restrictions**

*Regulation:* digital lending apps may collect only the data that is necessary for the purpose for which consent is sought. Specifically prohibited: access to contacts list, call logs, photos/media, and location for loan processing (these data types may be accessed only if genuinely required for a specific, documented purpose with explicit consent).

*Engineering implication:* the Android/iOS app must request only the minimum permission set:
```
PERMITTED (with explicit consent):
  - Camera: for document capture (ID front, ID back, selfie)
  - Storage: for document upload
  - Phone number: for KYC verification
  
PROHIBITED for loan processing:
  - Contacts: accessing the borrower's phonebook
  - Call logs: history of calls made/received
  - Gallery/Photos: existing photos on the device
  - Location: real-time location tracking
  - Microphone: beyond video KYC use
```

The permission request must be accompanied by a plain-language explanation of why the permission is needed. A "grant all permissions" screen that bundles prohibited permissions with permitted ones is a compliance violation.

**Requirement 5: Grievance redressal and Nodal Officer**

*Regulation:* every RE must designate a Nodal Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) specifically for digital lending complaints. The GRO's name and contact must appear:
- On the RE's website
- In the KFS
- In the loan agreement
- In the digital lending app (dedicated Help/Support section)

Complaints must be resolved within 30 days. If unresolved or unsatisfactorily resolved, the borrower can escalate to the RBI Ombudsman (under the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme).

*Engineering implication:*
The app must have a dedicated complaint flow that:
1. Allows complaint submission with category tagging (disbursement, collection, data privacy, charges, etc.).
2. Issues a complaint reference number immediately.
3. Tracks resolution within the 30-day SLA.
4. Escalates automatically to senior team if unresolved in 25 days.
5. Generates the RBI Ombudsman format export if the borrower requests escalation.

**Requirement 6: Reporting to credit bureaus and CKYCR**

*Regulation:* all digital lenders must report loan data to credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark). Additionally, KYC records must be submitted to the Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) — a centralized repository maintained by CERSAI under the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act.

*CKYCR process:*
```
First-time borrower:
  1. RE performs KYC (Aadhaar OTP, V-KYC, etc.)
  2. RE submits KYC record to CKYCR via API
  3. CKYCR issues a 14-digit KYC Identifier (CKYC number)
  4. RE stores CKYC number against borrower record

Returning borrower (at another RE):
  1. Borrower provides CKYC number
  2. New RE fetches KYC record from CKYCR using the number
  3. No fresh KYC needed if record is less than 3 years old
  4. New RE submits updated record if any information has changed
```

The engineering system must integrate with CKYCR's API (operated by CERSAI), handle both submission (new KYC) and fetch (returning customer) flows, and store the CKYC number as a persistent identifier in the borrower master record.

**Q11. What is the Loan Service Provider (LSP) framework under the 2022 guidelines, and what are its system implications?**
A: A Loan Service Provider is any entity that provides services to a Regulated Entity (bank or NBFC) at any stage of the lending lifecycle — customer acquisition, underwriting support, loan servicing, collections. The 2022 guidelines establish a formal framework for this relationship.

**Key LSP requirements:**

*RE accountability:*
The RE cannot delegate its regulatory obligations to the LSP. The RE is fully accountable for every action taken by the LSP on its behalf. If the LSP violates the guidelines (e.g., illegal collection practices), the RE is the entity the RBI holds responsible.

*Formal agreement:*
Every RE-LSP relationship must be governed by a written agreement that includes:
- Scope of services the LSP can perform.
- Data sharing restrictions (LSP cannot use borrower data for purposes other than the RE's loan).
- Audit rights (RE must be able to audit LSP systems).
- Prohibition on sub-outsourcing without RE approval.
- Mandatory disclosure to borrowers that an LSP is involved.

*Borrower disclosure:*
The borrower must be informed of which LSP(s) are involved in their loan. The KFS and the loan agreement must name the LSP. The RE cannot be a silent principal.

*LSP-RE data flows — engineering constraints:*
```
PERMITTED:
  Borrower → LSP app (data collection) → RE system (data transmitted to RE)
  RE system (decision) → LSP app (decision communicated to borrower)
  RE NACH mandate → LSP triggers collection notification → Borrower
  
PROHIBITED:
  Borrower repayment → LSP account (LSP cannot hold funds)
  RE → LSP account → Borrower (disbursement must not pass through LSP)
  LSP stores raw Aadhaar / PAN data beyond processing (data minimization)
```

*Technical audit trail for RE-LSP accountability:*
Every action performed by the LSP on behalf of the RE must be logged with:
- Timestamp
- LSP entity identifier
- Action type (KYC verified, loan offer generated, collection SMS sent)
- Borrower account reference
- RE approval or instruction that authorized the action

The RE must be able to produce this log for any loan in a regulatory examination.

**Q14. What is the PMLA / AML compliance framework for fintechs, and how is it engineered?**
A: The Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA, 2002) and the rules under it impose Know Your Customer (KYC), Customer Due Diligence (CDD), and Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR) obligations on every regulated entity — banks, NBFCs, and PPI issuers.

**AML engineering components:**

**1. Customer Risk Scoring:**
Every customer must be assigned a risk rating (Low / Medium / High) based on:
- Source of funds (salaried vs. business owner vs. politically exposed person)
- Geographic risk (customer from high-risk jurisdiction)
- Transaction patterns (large cash equivalent transactions, rapid inflows and outflows)
- PEP status (Politically Exposed Person — requires enhanced due diligence)

```python
def compute_customer_risk_score(customer_id):
    customer = get_customer(customer_id)
    
    score = 0
    
    # PEP / Sanctions check — must check at onboarding AND periodically
    if is_pep(customer):
        score += 40              # PEPs are always high-risk per PMLA
    if is_sanctioned(customer):
        return RiskRating.BLOCKED    # Cannot onboard sanctioned individuals
    
    # Geographic risk
    if customer.nationality in HIGH_RISK_COUNTRIES:  # FATF blacklist/greylist
        score += 20
    
    # Occupation / business type
    if customer.occupation in HIGH_RISK_OCCUPATIONS:  # cash-intensive businesses
        score += 15
    
    # Transaction velocity (from historical data)
    if customer.monthly_transaction_volume > VOLUME_THRESHOLD:
        score += 10
    
    if score >= 40:
        return RiskRating.HIGH      # Enhanced Due Diligence required
    elif score >= 20:
        return RiskRating.MEDIUM    # Standard CDD
    else:
        return RiskRating.LOW       # Simplified CDD (if permitted by regulation)
```

**2. Transaction Monitoring (TM):**
Every transaction is evaluated against rules that flag suspicious patterns:

```python
TRANSACTION_MONITORING_RULES = [
    # Structuring: multiple transactions just below reporting threshold
    Rule('structuring_detection',
         condition=lambda txns: (
             len([t for t in txns if t.amount > 450_000 and t.amount < 500_000]) > 3
             and sum(t.amount for t in txns) > 1_500_000
         ),
         alert_type='STR'),
    
    # Rapid round-tripping: funds in and out within 24 hours
    Rule('round_tripping',
         condition=lambda account: (
             account.inflows_24h > 100_000 and
             account.outflows_24h > account.inflows_24h * 0.80
         ),
         alert_type='STR'),
    
    # Unusual geographic pattern
    Rule('geo_anomaly',
         condition=lambda txn, customer: (
             txn.counterparty_country not in customer.usual_countries and
             txn.amount > 50_000
         ),
         alert_type='review'),
    
    # Large cash equivalent (PPI wallet loads via cash agents)
    Rule('large_cash_equivalent',
         condition=lambda txn: (
             txn.channel == 'cash_agent' and txn.amount > 200_000
         ),
         alert_type='CTR')  # Cash Transaction Report — mandatory filing
]
```

**3. Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs):**
When a suspicious transaction is flagged and reviewed, and the compliance officer determines it is suspicious, an STR must be filed with the Financial Intelligence Unit — India (FIU-IND) within 7 days of confirming the suspicion. STRs are filed via the FIU-IND's reporting platform (DARPAN). The filing is confidential — the customer must not be informed that an STR has been filed ("tipping off" is a criminal offense under PMLA).

**Engineering constraint:** the STR generation system must be completely siloed from customer-facing systems. A customer must be able to log in and use their account normally even if an STR has been filed on their transactions. Any system alert, notification, or account action that could tip off the customer is prohibited.

    current_balance = get_wallet_balance_with_lock(wallet_id)

    max_balance = {
        'min_kyc': Decimal('10000'),
        'full_kyc': Decimal('200000')

**Tip for the interview:** At the 5 YOE level in Indian fintech, "what is an NBFC" or "what is a PPI" are entry-level questions — the depth interviewers probe is whether you understand the *engineering consequences*: why the ₹10,000 PPI balance limit must be enforced with a strongly consistent database read (not eventual consistency — a race condition between two concurrent loads can breach the regulatory limit and expose the issuer to RBI action), why NACH mandates must be in the RE's name and not the LSP's (otherwise the fund flow violates Digital Lending Guidelines), why Aadhaar numbers cannot be stored (it is a criminal offense under the Aadhaar Act, not merely a policy violation), why the KFS must be stored immutably with the exact APR shown to that specific borrower (in a dispute, the RE must produce what was disclosed), and why the STR filing system must be completely siloed from customer-facing systems (tipping off is a PMLA criminal offense). Have a concrete story — a KYC upgrade flow you designed, a UPI authorization latency issue you debugged (NPCI's 5-second SLA is a hard cutoff), a CAR computation system you built, or an AML transaction monitoring pipeline you implemented. The ability to connect RBI regulatory requirements to system design choices (strongly consistent balance checks, immutable consent ledgers, append-only transaction records, atomic escrow flows) is what distinguishes a compliant fintech engineer from someone who has read the RBI circulars.
