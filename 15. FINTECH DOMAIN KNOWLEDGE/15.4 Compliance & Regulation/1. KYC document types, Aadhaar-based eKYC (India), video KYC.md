## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is KYC, why is it mandated, and who enforces it in India?**
A: KYC (Know Your Customer) is the process by which a regulated financial entity verifies the identity and address of a customer before onboarding them and periodically thereafter. It is the foundational layer of AML (Anti-Money Laundering) and CFT (Countering the Financing of Terrorism) compliance — you cannot launder money or finance terrorism through a financial system that knows exactly who every account holder is.

**Who enforces it in India:**

| Regulator | Entities Regulated | KYC Framework |
|---|---|---|
| RBI | Banks, NBFCs, Payment Aggregators, Prepaid Instrument issuers | Master Direction — KYC (2016, updated 2023) |
| SEBI | Brokers, Mutual Funds, Depositories | KYC Registration Agency (KRA) framework |
| IRDAI | Insurance companies | KYC norms under Insurance Act |
| PFRDA | NPS service providers | KYC under PFRDA guidelines |
| PMLA | All of the above — umbrella law | Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 |

The **Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002** is the primary statute. The **RBI Master Direction on KYC** operationalizes PMLA requirements for RBI-regulated entities. Non-compliance attracts: monetary penalties (up to ₹1 crore per violation), suspension of business activities, and in extreme cases, license revocation.

For a fintech engineer, KYC is not a product feature — it is a legal obligation. Decisions about what data to collect, how long to store it, when to re-verify, and how to handle failures are all governed by regulatory requirements, not product discretion.

**Q2. What are the four pillars of KYC, and how do they map to engineering requirements?**
A: The RBI Master Direction defines KYC through four policy elements, each with direct engineering implications:

- **Customer Acceptance Policy (CAP)** — defines which customers can be onboarded, what risk categories they fall into, and what enhanced due diligence is required for high-risk customers. Engineering: customer risk scoring system, category-based document requirements engine.

- **Customer Identification Procedures (CIP)** — defines what documents and data points must be collected to establish identity and address. Engineering: document collection workflow, OCR extraction, face match, liveness detection.

- **Monitoring of Transactions** — ongoing surveillance of customer transactions post-onboarding to detect suspicious patterns. Engineering: transaction monitoring system, STR (Suspicious Transaction Report) filing pipeline to FIU-IND.

- **Risk Management** — periodic re-KYC (re-verification) based on customer risk category; enhanced due diligence for Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and high-risk jurisdictions. Engineering: KYC expiry tracking, re-KYC notification and workflow, PEP/sanctions screening integration.

**Q3. What is the KYC document hierarchy in India, and what constitutes a valid OVD (Officially Valid Document)?**
A: The Prevention of Money Laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005 defines six **Officially Valid Documents (OVDs)** that can satisfy KYC identity verification:

| OVD | Verifies Identity | Verifies Address | Authority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aadhaar Card | Yes | Yes | UIDAI | Preferred — enables eKYC via UIDAI API |
| Passport | Yes | Yes | Ministry of External Affairs | Gold standard for international customers |
| Voter ID (EPIC) | Yes | Yes | Election Commission of India | Address may be outdated |
| Driving Licence | Yes | Yes | State RTO | Address validity varies by state |
| NREGA Job Card | Yes | Yes | Ministry of Rural Development | Used for rural/low-income segments |
| National Population Register letter | Yes | Yes | Registrar General | Rarely used in practice |

**PAN Card** — not an OVD but mandatory for financial transactions above thresholds (₹50,000 cash, all account openings). Verifies identity and tax identity; does not verify address.

For **address-only documents** (when the OVD address is outdated): utility bills, bank statements, rent agreements — accepted only as supporting address proof, not as standalone OVDs.

Engineering implication: the KYC document collection engine must maintain a product-rule mapping — which product requires which combination of OVDs, whether a single OVD is sufficient or two are required, and whether the OVD must be less than N years old (e.g., Driving Licence validity date must be in the future).

**Q4. What are the three KYC risk categories, and how do they determine document requirements and monitoring intensity?**
A: RBI's risk-based KYC framework classifies customers into three categories, each with proportional verification and monitoring requirements:

**Low Risk:**
- Salaried individuals with salary accounts, senior citizens, small-value customers.
- Document requirement: one OVD (identity + address). Simplified KYC.
- Re-KYC: every 10 years.
- Transaction monitoring: standard.
- Example product: Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account (BSBDA) — zero-balance account with annual transaction limit of ₹1L.

**Medium Risk:**
- Self-employed individuals, small businesses, traders.
- Document requirement: two OVDs or one OVD + supporting address proof. Standard KYC.
- Re-KYC: every 8 years.
- Transaction monitoring: enhanced.

**High Risk:**
- PEPs (Politically Exposed Persons) and their family members, non-resident Indians (NRIs) dealing in high-value transactions, cash-intensive businesses, customers from high-risk jurisdictions (FATF grey/black list).
- Document requirement: two OVDs + enhanced due diligence (source of funds, business purpose, senior management approval).
- Re-KYC: every 2 years.
- Transaction monitoring: intensive; all transactions above threshold reviewed.
- Additional: PEP/sanctions screening against OFAC, UN, and India's domestic list.

```typescript
// kyc/risk/customer-risk-classifier.ts
export type RiskCategory = 'low' | 'medium' | 'high';

export interface RiskClassificationInput {
  customerType: 'individual' | 'sole_proprietor' | 'company' | 'trust';
  occupation: string;
  annualIncome?: number;
  isNri: boolean;
  isPep: boolean;
  isPepFamily: boolean;
  countryOfResidence: string;
  productType: string;
}

export class CustomerRiskClassifier {
  classify(input: RiskClassificationInput): RiskCategory {
    // High risk — any one condition triggers high risk
    if (input.isPep || input.isPepFamily) return 'high';
    if (this.isHighRiskJurisdiction(input.countryOfResidence)) return 'high';
    if (input.customerType === 'trust') return 'high'; // trusts always high risk per RBI
    if (input.isNri && (input.annualIncome ?? 0) > 5_000_000) return 'high';

    // Low risk — all conditions must be met
    if (
      input.customerType === 'individual' &&
      !input.isNri &&
      (input.annualIncome ?? 0) < 1_000_000 &&
      this.isLowRiskOccupation(input.occupation)
    ) return 'low';

    return 'medium';
  }

  private isHighRiskJurisdiction(country: string): boolean {
    // FATF grey list + RBI-specified high risk countries
    const HIGH_RISK = new Set(['IR', 'KP', 'MM', 'SY']); // Iran, N Korea, Myanmar, Syria
    return HIGH_RISK.has(country);
  }

  private isLowRiskOccupation(occupation: string): boolean {
    const LOW_RISK = new Set(['salaried', 'pensioner', 'student', 'homemaker']);
    return LOW_RISK.has(occupation.toLowerCase());
  }
}
```

**Q5. What is the KYC Central Registry (CKYCR), and how does it eliminate duplicate KYC across financial institutions?**
A: CKYCRR (Central KYC Records Registry) is a centralized repository of KYC records maintained by CERSAI (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India) under the authority of the Ministry of Finance. Every regulated financial entity must upload customer KYC data to CKYCR and search CKYCR before performing fresh KYC.

**How it eliminates duplication:**
1. Customer completes KYC at Bank A. Bank A uploads the KYC record to CKYCR; CKYCR issues a 14-digit **KYC Identifier (KIN)**.
2. Same customer opens an account at NBFC B. NBFC B searches CKYCR using the customer's PAN or Aadhaar (masked). CKYCR returns the KIN and the KYC record.
3. NBFC B downloads the KYC record — identity and address documents already verified by Bank A. NBFC B does not need to collect documents again.
4. NBFC B links the KIN to the customer's account; marks KYC as satisfied from CKYCR.

Engineering integration:

```typescript
  }
```

## Section 2: Aadhaar-Based eKYC In Depth

**Q6. What is Aadhaar-based eKYC, and what are the two modes of eKYC delivery?**
A: Aadhaar eKYC is a paperless KYC mechanism where a customer's identity and address data are fetched directly from UIDAI's (Unique Identification Authority of India) database using their 12-digit Aadhaar number and an OTP or biometric authentication. Since UIDAI holds authoritative demographic and biometric data for 1.3+ billion Indians, a UIDAI-authenticated eKYC is legally equivalent to in-person document verification.

**Two eKYC modes:**

**OTP-based eKYC (most common in fintech):**
1. Customer enters Aadhaar number on the fintech platform.
2. UIDAI sends OTP to the mobile number registered with Aadhaar.
3. Customer enters OTP; fintech forwards OTP to UIDAI via AUA (Authentication User Agency) API.
4. UIDAI authenticates and returns demographic data (name, DOB, gender, address, photograph) in encrypted XML — the **eKYC XML**.
5. The XML is signed by UIDAI's digital signature — fintech can verify it has not been tampered with.

**Biometric eKYC:**
1. Customer provides fingerprint or iris scan via a UIDAI-certified biometric device.
2. Biometric template encrypted and sent to UIDAI via authentication API.
3. UIDAI authenticates and returns eKYC XML.
4. Used primarily at bank branches, CSC (Common Service Centres), and government offices — not suited for mobile-only flows.

**Aadhaar eKYC data returned by UIDAI:**

**Q7. What is the AUA/KUA architecture, and how does a fintech connect to UIDAI for eKYC?**
A: UIDAI does not allow direct API access by individual fintech companies. Access is mediated through a layered architecture:

```
Fintech (Sub-AUA / Sub-KUA)
      │  eKYC request (encrypted, signed)
      ▼
AUA (Authentication User Agency) / KUA (KYC User Agency)
      │  AUA aggregates requests from multiple sub-AUAs
      │  Manages UIDAI API credentials and rate limits
      ▼
ASA (Authentication Service Agency)
      │  Network-level connectivity to UIDAI
      │  Typically a bank or TSP (Technology Service Provider)
      ▼
UIDAI Authentication Server
      │  Authenticates request; returns eKYC XML
      ▼
Response propagates back through ASA → AUA → Fintech
```

- **AUA/KUA** — licensed by UIDAI. Banks (SBI, HDFC) and large fintech companies (Razorpay, NSDL) are AUAs. Most fintechs operate as **Sub-AUAs** under an AUA's license, avoiding the cost and complexity of obtaining their own AUA license.
- **KUA** — specifically licensed for eKYC (demographic data retrieval). An AUA can only do authentication (yes/no — does this OTP match this Aadhaar?); a KUA can retrieve the demographic data.
- **ASA** — pure network connectivity; typically a bank or certified TSP.

Engineering integration as a Sub-KUA under a KUA partner (e.g., Digio, NSDL, Perfios):

```typescript
// kyc/aadhaar/aadhaar-ekyc.service.ts
@Injectable()
export class AadhaarEkycService {
  constructor(
    private readonly kuaClient: KuaApiClient,  // e.g., Digio SDK / NSDL KUA API
    private readonly encryptionService: AadhaarEncryptionService,
    private readonly kycRepo: KycRepository,
  ) {}

  async initiateOtpEkyc(
    customerId: string,
    aadhaarNumber: string,   // never log raw Aadhaar — mask before logging
  ): Promise<{ txnId: string }> {
    // Validate Aadhaar format (12 digits, Verhoeff check digit)
    this.validateAadhaarFormat(aadhaarNumber);

    // Encrypt Aadhaar before sending to KUA (mandatory — UIDAI requires PID block encryption)
    const encryptedPid = await this.encryptionService.encryptAadhaar(aadhaarNumber);

    const response = await this.kuaClient.sendOtp({
      uid: encryptedPid,
      txnId: generateUUID(),
      subAuaCode: this.config.subAuaCode,
    });

    // Store transaction for OTP verification
    await this.kycRepo.createEkycSession({
      customerId,
      txnId: response.txnId,
      maskedAadhaar: this.maskAadhaar(aadhaarNumber),  // store masked only
      expiresAt: addMinutes(new Date(), 10),            // OTP valid for 10 minutes
    });

    return { txnId: response.txnId };
  }

  async verifyOtpAndFetchData(
    customerId: string,
    txnId: string,
    otp: string,
  ): Promise<AadhaarEkycData> {
    const session = await this.kycRepo.getEkycSession(txnId);
    if (!session || session.expiresAt < new Date()) {
      throw new EkycSessionExpiredError('eKYC session expired. Please restart.');
    }

    const response = await this.kuaClient.verifyOtpAndGetEkyc({
      txnId,
      otp: await this.encryptionService.encryptOtp(otp),
      subAuaCode: this.config.subAuaCode,
    });

    // Validate UIDAI's digital signature on eKYC XML
    const isValid = await this.encryptionService.validateUidaiSignature(response.ekycXml);
    if (!isValid) throw new EkycSignatureError('UIDAI signature validation failed');

    // Decrypt and parse eKYC XML
    const ekycData = await this.encryptionService.decryptAndParseEkyc(response.ekycXml);

    // Store KYC record — store maskedAadhaar, not raw; photo separately encrypted
    await this.kycRepo.saveAadhaarEkyc(customerId, {
      maskedAadhaar: ekycData.maskedAadhaar,
      name: ekycData.name,
      dob: ekycData.dob,
      gender: ekycData.gender,
      address: ekycData.address,
      photoEncrypted: await this.encryptionService.encryptPhoto(ekycData.photo),
      ekycXmlHash: sha256(response.ekycXml), // hash for audit, not raw XML
      completedAt: new Date(),
      source: 'aadhaar_otp',
    });

    await this.kycRepo.closeEkycSession(txnId);
    return ekycData;
  }

  private validateAadhaarFormat(aadhaar: string): void {
    if (!/^\d{12}$/.test(aadhaar)) throw new InvalidAadhaarError('Must be 12 digits');
    if (!this.verhoeffCheck(aadhaar)) throw new InvalidAadhaarError('Invalid check digit');
    if (['0', '1'].includes(aadhaar[0])) throw new InvalidAadhaarError('Cannot start with 0 or 1');
  }

  private maskAadhaar(aadhaar: string): string {
    return `XXXX XXXX ${aadhaar.slice(8)}`; // retain last 4 digits
  }
}
```

**Q8. What are the critical data storage and privacy rules for Aadhaar data under the Aadhaar Act?**
A: The Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act, 2016 and the UIDAI's regulations impose strict rules on how entities can handle Aadhaar data. Violations attract penalties up to ₹1 crore and imprisonment up to 3 years:

**What CAN be stored:**
- Masked Aadhaar number (last 4 digits visible: XXXX XXXX 1234).
- Reference number (transaction ID from UIDAI for the eKYC transaction).
- eKYC demographic data (name, DOB, address) — since the customer has consented to share this.
- Photograph received via eKYC — must be stored encrypted.

**What CANNOT be stored:**
- Raw (unmasked) 12-digit Aadhaar number — prohibited under Section 29(1) of the Aadhaar Act.
- Biometric data (fingerprint, iris) — Section 29(2) strictly prohibits any storage of biometrics received via authentication.
- eKYC XML in raw form — contains UIDAI's encrypted data; should be decrypted, used, and discarded. Storing the raw encrypted XML is a grey area; storing it in decrypted form is prohibited.

**Consent requirements:**
- Explicit, informed consent must be obtained before initiating Aadhaar authentication. The consent must specify: the purpose of authentication, what data will be received, and who it will be shared with.
- Consent must be stored with a timestamp and the exact consent text presented to the user.
- Consent cannot be a pre-ticked checkbox or buried in terms of service.

**Aadhaar data purge requirements:**
- eKYC data must not be retained beyond the purpose for which it was obtained.
- For loan origination: retain for the loan tenure + 8 years (PMLA record-keeping requirement) post-loan closure.
- Aadhaar-related data in logs must be masked or purged within 24–48 hours.

**Q9. What is the Virtual ID (VID), and how does it protect customer privacy in Aadhaar authentication?**
A: A Virtual ID (VID) is a temporary, revocable 16-digit number generated by the Aadhaar holder on UIDAI's portal or mAadhaar app. It maps internally to the Aadhaar number within UIDAI but reveals the Aadhaar number to no one — not even the requesting entity.

**How it protects privacy:**
- A customer shares their VID (not their Aadhaar number) with a fintech for eKYC.
- The fintech submits the VID to UIDAI for authentication.
- UIDAI internally resolves VID → Aadhaar and returns the eKYC data.
- The fintech receives demographic data but never learns the actual 12-digit Aadhaar number.
- The customer can revoke the VID and generate a new one at any time — without losing their Aadhaar identity.

**Engineering implications:**
- The eKYC flow must support both Aadhaar number input and VID input. The validation regex differs — Aadhaar is 12 digits (starting with 2–9); VID is 16 digits.
- After eKYC, the system stores the masked Aadhaar (received from UIDAI in the eKYC XML) — not the VID (which is ephemeral) and not the raw Aadhaar (which was never known).
- The masked Aadhaar in the eKYC XML always shows the actual last 4 digits of the Aadhaar number, even when a VID was used for authentication — this is intentional for re-identification purposes if required by a court.

```typescript
function validateAadhaarOrVid(input: string): 'aadhaar' | 'vid' | 'invalid' {
  const cleaned = input.replace(/\s/g, '');
  if (/^\d{12}$/.test(cleaned) && !['0', '1'].includes(cleaned[0])) {
    return verhoeffCheck(cleaned) ? 'aadhaar' : 'invalid';
  }
  if (/^\d{16}$/.test(cleaned)) {
    return 'vid'; // VIDs don't have a public check digit algorithm
  }
  return 'invalid';
}
```

## Section 3: Video KYC In Depth

**Q10. What is Video KYC (V-CIP), and what regulatory framework governs it in India?**
A: Video KYC — officially called **Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP)** — is a real-time, audio-visual interaction between a regulated entity's trained agent and the customer for customer identification. It was introduced by RBI via its January 2020 circular (Master Direction on KYC — amendment) as a fully valid alternative to in-person KYC.

V-CIP is legally equivalent to face-to-face KYC. A customer onboarded via V-CIP is treated identically to one who walked into a branch, allowing full banking products (savings accounts, loans, credit cards) — unlike certain eKYC-onboarded customers who face transaction limits.

**Regulatory requirements (RBI V-CIP circular, updated 2021):**

- **Live interaction** — must be a real-time video call, not a recorded video. The agent must verify liveness during the call.
- **Geotagging** — the customer's location must be captured during V-CIP and must be within India. V-CIP for NRIs must be handled separately with additional documentation.
- **Document capture** — agent must ask the customer to hold up original OVD to the camera; agent captures a still image or video frame of the document for verification.
- **PAN verification** — agent must display/collect PAN details and verify against NSDL/UTI database in real-time during the call.
- **Facial match** — the customer's face during the video call must be matched against the photograph on the OVD and (if Aadhaar eKYC is done in conjunction) the UIDAI photograph.
- **Randomized questions** — agent must ask randomized questions to prevent scripted/coached responses — a liveline test.
- **Recording and storage** — the full video recording must be stored for at least 5 years. Recording must be tamper-proof with a cryptographic hash. Audio and video must be synchronized.
- **Agent qualification** — agents must be trained and certified; the fintech must maintain training records.
- **Audit trail** — full logs of: agent ID, customer ID, session start/end time, geotag coordinates, documents captured, questions asked, facial match score, PAN verification result, final KYC decision.

**Q11. How do you architect a V-CIP (Video KYC) system end-to-end?**
A: A production V-CIP system has six technical components:

```
Customer Mobile App
    │  WebRTC video stream
    ▼
Video KYC Platform (in-house or vendor: Hyperverge, IDfy, Perfios)
    │
    ├── Session Manager
    │     Creates session, assigns agent, manages queue
    │
    ├── WebRTC Server (Janus / Kurento / Twilio)
    │     Manages real-time video streams between customer and agent
    │     Low-latency bidirectional video/audio
    │
    ├── Agent Dashboard (Web App)
    │     Real-time video feed from customer
    │     Document capture button (still frame from stream)
    │     AI-assisted liveness score overlay
    │     PAN verification widget (live NSDL lookup)
    │     Facial match score (customer face vs OVD photo)
    │     Random question prompt (system-generated, randomized)
    │     Approve / Reject / Defer controls
    │
    ├── AI/ML Services
    │     Liveness detection (anti-spoofing — checks blink, head movement)
    │     OCR on document frames (extract PAN, name, DOB, address)
    │     Facial recognition (customer face vs document photo)
    │     Quality scoring (lighting, angle, document readability)
    │
    ├── Recording Service
    │     Records full session (audio + video)
    │     Computes SHA-256 hash of recording immediately after session
    │     Stores hash in immutable audit log (separate from recording)
    │     Uploads to tamper-evident storage (S3 with Object Lock)
    │
    └── Compliance Logger
          Geotag capture (GPS + IP geolocation)
          Timestamps for every agent action
          Session metadata export for regulatory audit
```

```typescript
// kyc/vkyc/vkyc-session.service.ts
@Injectable()
export class VkycSessionService {
  async createSession(
    customerId: string,
    deviceInfo: DeviceInfo,
    geoLocation: GeoLocation,
  ): Promise<VkycSession> {
    // Validate geolocation — must be within India
    if (!this.isWithinIndia(geoLocation)) {
      throw new VkycGeoRestrictionError(
        'Video KYC must be completed from within India. NRI customers must use alternative KYC.',
      );
    }

    // Assign agent from queue (round-robin or skill-based routing)
    const agent = await this.agentQueueService.assignAgent({
      language: deviceInfo.preferredLanguage,
      productType: 'standard',
    });

    const session = await this.vkycRepo.create({
      customerId,
      agentId: agent.id,
      status: 'waiting',
      geolat: geoLocation.latitude,
      geolong: geoLocation.longitude,
      geoAccuracy: geoLocation.accuracy,
      ipAddress: deviceInfo.ipAddress,
      deviceId: deviceInfo.deviceId,
      createdAt: new Date(),
    });

    // Create WebRTC room
    const room = await this.webrtcService.createRoom(session.id);

    return { ...session, roomToken: room.customerToken, agentToken: room.agentToken };
  }

  async completeSession(
    sessionId: string,
    agentId: string,
    outcome: VkycOutcome,
  ): Promise<void> {
    const session = await this.vkycRepo.findById(sessionId);

    // Finalize recording — get recording URL from WebRTC server
    const recordingUrl = await this.webrtcService.finalizeRecording(session.id);

    // Compute tamper-proof hash of recording
    const recordingHash = await this.hashRecording(recordingUrl);

    // Store hash in immutable audit log BEFORE updating session
    await this.auditLogRepo.create({
      sessionId,
      recordingUrl,
      recordingHash,
      hashAlgorithm: 'SHA-256',
      hashedAt: new Date(),
    });

    // Upload to S3 with Object Lock (WORM — Write Once Read Many)
    await this.storageService.uploadWithObjectLock(recordingUrl, {
      retentionYears: 5,
      legalHold: false,
    });

    await this.vkycRepo.update(sessionId, {
      status: outcome.approved ? 'approved' : 'rejected',
      agentDecision: outcome.decision,
      agentNotes: outcome.notes,
      facialMatchScore: outcome.facialMatchScore,
      panVerified: outcome.panVerified,
      documentsCaptures: outcome.capturedDocuments,
      completedAt: new Date(),
    });

    if (outcome.approved) {
      await this.kycOrchestrator.markKycComplete(session.customerId, 'vkyc', sessionId);
    }
  }

  private isWithinIndia(geo: GeoLocation): boolean {
    // Bounding box for India (approximate)
    return (
      geo.latitude >= 6.0 && geo.latitude <= 37.0 &&
      geo.longitude >= 68.0 && geo.longitude <= 98.0
    );
  }
}
```

**Q12. How do liveness detection and facial match work in a V-CIP flow, and what are their failure modes?**
A: Liveness detection and facial match are the two AI components that make V-CIP fraud-resistant:

**Liveness Detection:**
Prevents presentation attacks — a fraudster showing a photograph, video replay, or 3D mask of the legitimate customer. Two approaches:

- **Active liveness** — the system or agent instructs the customer to perform specific actions: blink, turn left, smile, say a phrase. The system checks that the instructions were followed in real-time. More secure; higher friction.
- **Passive liveness** — AI analyzes a single frame or short clip for signs of a real face: skin texture, micro-movements, depth cues, specular reflection. Lower friction; lower detection accuracy against sophisticated attacks.

In V-CIP, liveness is a hybrid: the agent performing randomized questions provides active liveness; additional passive liveness AI runs in the background.

**Failure modes:**
- Poor lighting → passive liveness fails even for genuine customers (high false rejection rate). Mitigation: real-time lighting quality feedback to customer before session starts.
- Low camera resolution → facial details insufficient for liveness. Mitigation: minimum camera quality check at session start.
- Identical twins → facial match passes for fraudster. Mitigation: document verification + address verification + PAN verification together make this impractical.
- Deep fake video injection → advanced attack where the WebRTC stream is replaced with AI-generated video. Mitigation: WebRTC stack must use DTLS-SRTP encryption; client app must be integrity-verified (certificate pinning + root detection).

**Facial Match:**
Compares the customer's face during the video call against the photograph on their OVD:

```typescript
// kyc/vkyc/facial-match.service.ts
@Injectable()
export class FacialMatchService {
  constructor(private readonly aiClient: FacialRecognitionClient) {}

  async matchFaces(
    liveFrameBase64: string,     // captured still from video stream
    documentPhotoBase64: string, // extracted from OVD via OCR/document parser
    aadhaarPhotoBase64?: string, // from eKYC XML — additional reference
  ): Promise<FacialMatchResult> {
    const results = await Promise.all([
      this.aiClient.compare(liveFrameBase64, documentPhotoBase64),
      aadhaarPhotoBase64
        ? this.aiClient.compare(liveFrameBase64, aadhaarPhotoBase64)
        : Promise.resolve(null),
    ]);

    const docMatchScore = results[0].similarity;       // 0–100
    const aadhaarMatchScore = results[1]?.similarity;

    // RBI does not specify a minimum threshold; lenders set their own
    // Industry standard: >75% for approval; 60–75% for manual review; <60% reject
    const APPROVAL_THRESHOLD = 75;
    const REVIEW_THRESHOLD = 60;

    const primaryScore = Math.max(docMatchScore, aadhaarMatchScore ?? 0);

    return {
      docMatchScore,
      aadhaarMatchScore,
      primaryScore,
      decision: primaryScore >= APPROVAL_THRESHOLD
        ? 'approved'
        : primaryScore >= REVIEW_THRESHOLD
          ? 'manual_review'
          : 'rejected',
      requiresAgentReview: primaryScore < APPROVAL_THRESHOLD,
    };
  }
}
```

**Q13. What are the key differences between Aadhaar eKYC and Video KYC — when does a fintech use each?**
A: The choice between eKYC and V-CIP depends on product requirements, customer risk profile, and regulatory constraints:

| Dimension | Aadhaar OTP eKYC | Video KYC (V-CIP) |
|---|---|---|
| Customer effort | Low (OTP only) | Medium (live video session, 5–10 min) |
| Agent required | No — fully automated | Yes — trained human agent |
| Operational cost | Very low (~₹2–5 per eKYC via KUA) | Higher (~₹50–200 per session — agent cost) |
| Turn-around time | Seconds | 5–30 minutes (queue dependent) |
| Products supported | Capped products (wallet ₹1L, BSBDA) | Full KYC — all products including credit cards, large loans |
| RBI transaction limits (eKYC-only accounts) | ₹1L/year inflow for PPIs; loan limits per NBFC guidelines | No limits — equivalent to in-person |
| Fraud resistance | Moderate — OTP can be socially engineered | High — liveness + agent judgment + document check |
| Aadhaar requirement | Mandatory — Aadhaar number + UIDAI OTP | Not mandatory — any OVD works |
| NRI customers | Not permitted | Permitted with additional documentation |
| Works without Aadhaar | No | Yes |
| Suitable for | High-volume low-value onboarding (BNPL, wallets, small loans) | High-value accounts, loans, credit products |
| Re-KYC | Supported | Supported |

**Decision tree for a fintech lending product:**
```
Is the product a wallet or PPI?
  → Yes: Aadhaar OTP eKYC (if customer has Aadhaar) → sufficient up to ₹1L limit
  → No: Is loan amount > ₹60,000?
      → Yes: Full KYC required → V-CIP or in-person
      → No: eKYC may suffice per NBFC's board-approved policy
            (RBI does not prescribe a specific loan amount threshold for full KYC)
```

## Section 4: KYC Orchestration, Re-KYC, and Failure Handling

**Q14. How do you design a KYC orchestration engine that handles multiple KYC methods, fallbacks, and partial completions?**
A: A production KYC engine must be method-agnostic, resumable (customer can continue a partially completed KYC), and auditable at every step:

```typescript
// kyc/orchestration/kyc-orchestrator.service.ts
export type KycMethod = 'aadhaar_otp' | 'aadhaar_biometric' | 'vkyc' | 'offline_xml' | 'document_upload';
export type KycStatus = 'not_started' | 'in_progress' | 'pending_review' | 'completed' | 'rejected' | 'expired';

export interface KycStep {
  stepName: string;
  status: 'pending' | 'completed' | 'failed' | 'skipped';
  completedAt?: Date;
  data?: Record<string, unknown>;
}

@Injectable()
export class KycOrchestrator {
  async initializeKyc(
    customerId: string,
    productType: string,
    preferredMethod: KycMethod,
  ): Promise<KycWorkflow> {
    const riskCategory = await this.riskClassifier.classify(customerId);
    const requiredSteps = this.buildRequiredSteps(productType, riskCategory, preferredMethod);

    return this.kycRepo.createWorkflow({
      customerId,
      productType,
      riskCategory,
      method: preferredMethod,
      steps: requiredSteps,
      status: 'in_progress',
      expiresAt: addHours(new Date(), 48),  // session expires if not completed in 48h
    });
  }

  private buildRequiredSteps(
    productType: string,
    risk: RiskCategory,
    method: KycMethod,
  ): KycStep[] {
    const steps: KycStep[] = [
      { stepName: 'consent_collection', status: 'pending' },
      { stepName: 'pan_collection', status: 'pending' },
      { stepName: 'pan_verification', status: 'pending' },   // NSDL/UTI API
      { stepName: 'ckyc_search', status: 'pending' },        // check CKYCR first
    ];

    if (method === 'aadhaar_otp') {
      steps.push(
        { stepName: 'aadhaar_otp_initiation', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'aadhaar_otp_verification', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'face_match', status: 'pending' },       // selfie vs Aadhaar photo
      );
    } else if (method === 'vkyc') {
      steps.push(
        { stepName: 'document_upload', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'vkyc_session', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'agent_review', status: 'pending' },
      );
    }

    // High-risk customers require additional steps
    if (risk === 'high') {
      steps.push(
        { stepName: 'pep_screening', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'sanctions_screening', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'source_of_funds_declaration', status: 'pending' },
        { stepName: 'senior_manager_approval', status: 'pending' },
      );
    }

    steps.push({ stepName: 'ckyc_upload', status: 'pending' }); // upload to CKYCR post-approval

    return steps;
  }

  async markStepComplete(
    workflowId: string,
    stepName: string,
    data: Record<string, unknown>,
  ): Promise<KycWorkflow> {
    const workflow = await this.kycRepo.findWorkflow(workflowId);

    await this.kycRepo.updateStep(workflowId, stepName, {
      status: 'completed',
      completedAt: new Date(),
      data,
    });

    // Check if all required steps complete
    const updatedWorkflow = await this.kycRepo.findWorkflow(workflowId);
    const allComplete = updatedWorkflow.steps
      .filter(s => s.status !== 'skipped')
      .every(s => s.status === 'completed');

    if (allComplete) {
      await this.finalizeKyc(workflowId);
    }

    return updatedWorkflow;
  }

  // Fallback: if preferred method fails, offer alternatives
  async handleMethodFailure(
    workflowId: string,
    failedMethod: KycMethod,
    reason: string,
  ): Promise<KycFallback> {
    const FALLBACK_MAP: Record<KycMethod, KycMethod[]> = {
      aadhaar_otp: ['vkyc', 'offline_xml'],
      aadhaar_biometric: ['aadhaar_otp', 'vkyc'],
      vkyc: ['offline_xml'],         // no further fallback for V-CIP failure
      offline_xml: [],               // terminal — escalate to manual
      document_upload: ['vkyc'],
    };

    const fallbacks = FALLBACK_MAP[failedMethod];
    if (fallbacks.length === 0) {
      await this.kycRepo.updateWorkflowStatus(workflowId, 'rejected', reason);
      return { hasAlternative: false };
    }

    const nextMethod = fallbacks[0];
    await this.kycRepo.updateWorkflowMethod(workflowId, nextMethod, failedMethod);
    return { hasAlternative: true, nextMethod };
  }
}
```

**Q15. How do you implement re-KYC, and what happens to customers who fail to complete re-KYC?**
A: Re-KYC is the periodic reverification of a customer's identity and address. RBI mandates re-KYC at intervals determined by the customer's risk category (see Q4). Banks and NBFCs that fail to perform re-KYC on time face regulatory penalties; those that allow re-KYC-overdue accounts to continue transacting without restriction face additional scrutiny.

```typescript
// kyc/rekyc/rekyc-scheduler.service.ts
@Injectable()
export class RekycSchedulerService {
  private readonly REKYC_INTERVALS: Record<RiskCategory, number> = {
    low: 10 * 365,    // 10 years in days
    medium: 8 * 365,  // 8 years
    high: 2 * 365,    // 2 years
  };

  // Cron: daily — identify customers due for re-KYC
  @Cron('0 6 * * *')  // 6am daily
  async identifyRekycDue(): Promise<void> {
    const today = new Date();
    const categories: RiskCategory[] = ['high', 'medium', 'low'];

    for (const category of categories) {
      const intervalDays = this.REKYC_INTERVALS[category];
      const cutoffDate = subDays(today, intervalDays);

      const overdueCustomers = await this.kycRepo.findByLastKycBefore(
        cutoffDate,
        category,
        { status: 'completed' },
      );

      for (const customer of overdueCustomers) {
        await this.initiateRekycFlow(customer, category);
      }
    }
  }

  private async initiateRekycFlow(
    customer: Customer,
    riskCategory: RiskCategory,
  ): Promise<void> {
    const daysSinceKyc = differenceInDays(new Date(), customer.lastKycCompletedAt);
    const intervalDays = this.REKYC_INTERVALS[riskCategory];

    // Notification schedule based on urgency
    if (daysSinceKyc >= intervalDays) {
      // Overdue — immediate restriction + notification
      await this.restrictAccount(customer.id, 'rekyc_overdue');
      await this.notifyRekyc(customer, 'overdue_restriction');
    } else if (daysSinceKyc >= intervalDays - 90) {
      // 90 days warning
      await this.notifyRekyc(customer, 'warning_90_days');
    } else if (daysSinceKyc >= intervalDays - 30) {
      // 30 days warning
      await this.notifyRekyc(customer, 'warning_30_days');
    }
  }

  private async restrictAccount(
    customerId: string,
    reason: string,
  ): Promise<void> {
    // RBI guidance: re-KYC-overdue accounts must have restrictions
    // Debit freeze is the standard — customer can receive money but cannot send
    await this.accountService.applyRestriction(customerId, {
      restrictionType: 'debit_freeze',
      reason,
      appliedAt: new Date(),
      // Restriction lifted automatically when re-KYC is completed
    });

    await this.notificationService.sendMultiChannel(customerId, {
      channels: ['email', 'sms', 'push', 'in_app'],
      template: 'rekyc_account_restricted',
      data: {
        rekycLink: this.generateRekycLink(customerId),
        supportContact: this.config.supportNumber,
      },
    });
  }
}
```

**Q16. "Design the complete KYC system for an NBFC launching a personal loan product — from onboarding to re-KYC."**
A: A complete KYC system for a personal loan NBFC, end-to-end:

```
Customer Journey:

1. App Download + Registration
   └── Collect: mobile (OTP verified), email, basic profile

2. Loan Application Initiation
   └── Collect: PAN → verify with NSDL/UTI API → check CIBIL/Experian

3. KYC Selection
   ├── Aadhaar eKYC (preferred — instant, low cost)
   └── V-CIP (fallback — no Aadhaar, NRI, high loan amount)

4. Aadhaar eKYC Flow (for standard cases):
   a. Consent recording (store exact consent text + timestamp + IP)
   b. Aadhaar / VID input → format + Verhoeff validation
   c. OTP → UIDAI via KUA → eKYC XML returned
   d. Signature validation → decrypt → extract demographic data
   e. Face match: selfie vs Aadhaar photo (threshold: ≥75%)
   f. Liveness check on selfie (passive liveness — blink detection)
   g. PAN-Aadhaar linkage verification (UIDAI seeding status)
   h. CKYC search → if found, download record → skip to step 5
   i. Store: maskedAadhaar, name, DOB, address (encrypted), photo (encrypted)

5. Document Upload (for address if Aadhaar address outdated):
   └── OVD upload → OCR → address extraction → quality check

6. Risk Classification:
   └── Risk engine → low/medium/high → document requirements determined

7. Credit Underwriting (parallel with KYC steps 5–6):
   └── Bureau pull → BRE → offer generation

8. Loan Agreement + Mandate:
   └── Digital signing (e-sign via Aadhaar OTP or DSC)
   └── NACH mandate registration (e-mandate via Aadhaar)

9. CKYC Upload:
   └── Upload KYC record to CKYCR → store KIN against customer

10. Disbursement:
    └── KYC status = 'completed' is a gate → loan cannot disburse without it

Re-KYC (annual for high-risk, every 8 years for medium, every 10 for low):
    └── Automated notification 90/30 days before due
    └── Debit freeze at due date if not completed
    └── Re-KYC via same flow → update CKYCR record
```

**Q17. "A customer's Aadhaar eKYC succeeded but the face match score is 68% (below your 75% threshold). How do you handle this?"**
A: A borderline face match score is one of the most operationally nuanced KYC decisions — rejecting it loses a potentially valid customer; accepting it creates fraud risk. The handling must be deterministic, auditable, and consistent:

**Decision framework for 60–75% face match:**

```typescript
async function handleBorderlineFaceMatch(
  workflowId: string,
  ekycData: AadhaarEkycData,
  selfieBase64: string,
  faceMatchResult: FacialMatchResult,
): Promise<BorderlineDecision> {
  const HARD_REJECT_THRESHOLD = 60;
  const AUTO_APPROVE_THRESHOLD = 75;
  const score = faceMatchResult.primaryScore;

  if (score < HARD_REJECT_THRESHOLD) {
    // Hard reject — faces are clearly different
    await kycRepo.rejectWorkflow(workflowId, 'face_match_failed', { score });
    return { action: 'reject', reason: 'Face does not match identity document' };
  }

  if (score >= AUTO_APPROVE_THRESHOLD) {
    // Auto-approve — above threshold
    await kycOrchestrator.markStepComplete(workflowId, 'face_match', { score, decision: 'approved' });
    return { action: 'approve' };
  }

  // 60–75%: borderline — trigger additional verification steps
  const additionalChecks: string[] = [];

  // 1. Check if Aadhaar photo quality is low (older Aadhaar enrolments had poor photo capture)
  const aadhaarPhotoQuality = await photoQualityService.score(ekycData.photo);
  if (aadhaarPhotoQuality < 50) {
    // Low-quality Aadhaar photo is a known cause of borderline scores — allow with note
    additionalChecks.push('low_quality_aadhaar_photo');
  }

  // 2. Check selfie quality
  const selfieQuality = await photoQualityService.score(selfieBase64);
  if (selfieQuality < 60) {
    // Poor selfie — ask customer to retake
    return {
      action: 'request_selfie_retake',
      reason: 'Please take a clearer selfie in good lighting.',
      attemptsRemaining: 2,
    };
  }

  // 3. Route to manual review queue if quality issues ruled out
  await kycRepo.updateWorkflowStatus(workflowId, 'pending_review');
  await manualReviewQueue.enqueue({
    workflowId,
    reviewType: 'face_match_borderline',
    faceMatchScore: score,
    additionalChecks,
    ekycData: { maskedAadhaar: ekycData.maskedAadhaar, name: ekycData.name },
    // Do NOT include raw Aadhaar number or photo in queue message
    priority: score < 65 ? 'high' : 'normal',
  });

  return {
    action: 'pending_manual_review',
    estimatedWaitMinutes: 30,
  };
}
```

**Manual reviewer decision options:**
- Approve: reviewer is satisfied the customer is genuine (e.g., older Aadhaar photo vs current appearance).
- Reject: reviewer believes the faces are different — genuine fraud attempt.
- Request V-CIP: escalate to video KYC for a live interaction — effectively a re-verification.

**Audit trail requirement:** every borderline decision must log: the score, the threshold applied, the additional checks performed, the reviewer ID (for manual review), and the final decision rationale. This is the audit artifact if the customer disputes a rejection or if a fraud incident requires investigation.

**Q18. "How do you build a PAN verification service that is resilient, cached, and handles NSDL/UTI API downtime?"**
A: PAN verification is a mandatory KYC step — it verifies that the PAN exists, belongs to the correct person (name + DOB match), and is linked to Aadhaar (as required by Income Tax Act Section 139AA). It must be resilient because NSDL/UTI APIs have periodic downtime:

```typescript
// kyc/pan/pan-verification.service.ts
@Injectable()
export class PanVerificationService {
  private readonly CACHE_TTL_SECONDS = 24 * 60 * 60; // 24h — PAN status rarely changes
  private readonly NEGATIVE_CACHE_TTL = 60 * 60;     // 1h for invalid PANs

  async verifyPan(
    pan: string,
    name: string,
    dob: Date,
  ): Promise<PanVerificationResult> {
    // 1. Format validation — no API call needed
    if (!this.isValidPanFormat(pan)) {
      return { valid: false, reason: 'Invalid PAN format' };
    }

    // 2. Cache lookup — avoid re-calling API for recently verified PANs
    const cacheKey = `pan:verify:${pan}`;
    const cached = await this.redis.get(cacheKey);
    if (cached) {
      const cachedResult = JSON.parse(cached) as PanVerificationResult;
      // Even for cached result, re-run name match with current input
      return { ...cachedResult, nameMatch: this.fuzzyNameMatch(name, cachedResult.registeredName) };
    }

    // 3. Primary: NSDL API
    try {
      const result = await this.callWithTimeout(
        () => this.nsdlClient.verify(pan, name, format(dob, 'dd/MM/yyyy')),
        5000, // 5 second timeout
      );
      await this.redis.setex(cacheKey, this.CACHE_TTL_SECONDS, JSON.stringify(result));
      return result;

    } catch (nsdlError) {
      // 4. Fallback: UTI Infrastructure Technology (UTIITSL) API
      try {
        const result = await this.callWithTimeout(
          () => this.utiClient.verify(pan),
          5000,
        );
        await this.redis.setex(cacheKey, this.CACHE_TTL_SECONDS, JSON.stringify(result));
        return result;

      } catch (utiError) {
        // 5. Both down — soft failure with grace window
        await this.alertingService.raiseApiDownAlert('pan_verification', { nsdlError, utiError });

        // Store as pending — allow onboarding to continue with a flag
        // Re-verify when APIs recover (background job)
        await this.pendingVerificationRepo.create({
          pan,
          name,
          dob,
          customerId: this.currentCustomerId,
          reason: 'api_unavailable',
          retryAfter: addMinutes(new Date(), 30),
        });

        return {
          valid: null,  // null = unknown (not invalid) — distinct from false
          reason: 'Verification service temporarily unavailable. Will be verified automatically.',
          pendingVerification: true,
        };
      }
    }
  }

  // Fuzzy name match — handles common variations
  private fuzzyNameMatch(inputName: string, registeredName: string): NameMatchResult {
    const normalize = (s: string) =>
      s.toUpperCase().replace(/[^A-Z\s]/g, '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim();

    const input = normalize(inputName);
    const registered = normalize(registeredName);

    if (input === registered) return { matched: true, confidence: 100 };

    // Handle common patterns: initials, middle name absent, name order swap
    const inputParts = input.split(' ');
    const regParts = registered.split(' ');

    const firstLastMatch =
      inputParts[0] === regParts[0] &&
      inputParts[inputParts.length - 1] === regParts[regParts.length - 1];

    if (firstLastMatch) return { matched: true, confidence: 85 };

    // Levenshtein distance for minor spelling variations
    const distance = this.levenshteinDistance(input, registered);
    const maxLen = Math.max(input.length, registered.length);
    const similarity = ((maxLen - distance) / maxLen) * 100;

    return {
      matched: similarity >= 80,
      confidence: Math.round(similarity),
    };
  }

  isValidPanFormat(pan: string): boolean {
    // Format: AAAAA9999A — 5 alpha, 4 numeric, 1 alpha
    // 4th char indicates taxpayer type: P=person, C=company, H=HUF, F=firm, etc.
    return /^[A-Z]{5}[0-9]{4}[A-Z]{1}$/.test(pan);
  }
}
```

**Tip for the interview:** At the 5 YOE level, KYC questions surface in three forms: system design ("design the KYC system for an NBFC"), domain knowledge ("what is the difference between eKYC and V-CIP?"), and debugging/compliance scenarios ("a customer's face match is 68% — what do you do?"). The senior differentiator is demonstrating that you understand the **legal architecture** behind KYC engineering — that the Aadhaar Act prohibits raw Aadhaar storage and is enforced with criminal penalties, that UIDAI requires explicit informed consent stored verbatim (not a checkbox tick), that V-CIP recordings must be cryptographically hashed and stored on WORM storage for 5 years, and that a re-KYC-overdue account must have a debit freeze applied. Most engineers know how to call a face-match API; the senior knows why the threshold is set where it is, what happens at the boundary, and how every decision becomes an audit record for a potential RBI inspection. Drop these specifics naturally and you have demonstrated fintech compliance depth that interview panels at banks, NBFCs, and payment companies actively look for.
