# NACH / e-NACH for Recurring Mandates — Interview Q&A (5 YOE Level)

---

## Section 1: Fundamentals

**Q1. What is NACH, and how does it differ from ECS, the system it replaced?**
A: NACH (National Automated Clearing House) is a centralised electronic payment system operated by NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) for processing bulk, repetitive debit and credit transactions. It went live in 2013 and fully replaced ECS (Electronic Clearing Service), which was operated by RBI through regional clearing houses.

Key differences:

| Property | ECS (old) | NACH (current) |
|---|---|---|
| **Operator** | RBI, via regional clearing houses | NPCI, single centralised platform |
| **Reach** | Limited to cities with clearing houses (~89) | Pan-India, all bank branches on CBS |
| **Settlement** | T+1 or T+2, region-dependent | T+1 nationally standardised |
| **Mandate registration** | Paper form, physical delivery to bank | Digital (e-NACH) or paper |
| **Rejection reporting** | Manual, slow | Standardised rejection codes, same day |
| **Transaction limit** | No enforced cap | Configurable per mandate |
| **Sponsor bank** | Not required | Mandatory — the biller's bank sponsors the mandate |

NACH operates two distinct services:
- **NACH Debit** — for recurring collections from customers (loan EMIs, insurance premiums, SIP investments, subscription fees). The biller initiates a debit from the customer's bank account.
- **NACH Credit** — for bulk disbursements to many accounts (salary credits, dividend payments, government DBT transfers). Less relevant to most fintech products but architecturally similar.

**Q2. What is e-NACH, and what are its registration channels?**
A: e-NACH (electronic NACH) is the digital mandate registration flow — the customer authorises recurring debits without signing a physical paper form. It eliminates the 15–30 day physical mandate registration lag that made ECS impractical for digital businesses.

NPCI-approved e-NACH registration channels:

| Channel | How it works | Typical use case |
|---|---|---|
| **Aadhaar OTP** | Customer enters Aadhaar number; OTP sent to Aadhaar-linked mobile; NPCI verifies via UIDAI | Lending apps, insurers — highest NPA coverage |
| **Aadhaar Biometric** | Customer's fingerprint/iris scanned at a CSP (Customer Service Point) | Rural, low-digital-literacy segments |
| **Debit card** | Customer enters debit card number + CVV + OTP (bank 2FA); bank validates card ownership | E-commerce subscriptions, SaaS |
| **Net banking** | Customer redirected to their bank's net banking portal; authenticates and approves mandate | Premium customers; higher success rate |
| **UPI** | Customer approves mandate via UPI app (VPA-based); NPCI links UPI ID to bank account | Fastest growing; UPI AutoPay (discussed separately) |

Each channel has a different mandate success rate in production:
- Net banking: ~85–90% (customer actively authenticates at their bank).
- Debit card: ~70–80% (card details must match; OTP required).
- Aadhaar OTP: ~60–75% (depends on Aadhaar-mobile linkage completeness).

**Q3. What is the role of each party in the NACH ecosystem, and what does each do in a recurring debit?**
A: Five parties participate in every NACH debit transaction:

| Party | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer** | Account holder whose bank account is debited | Borrower paying loan EMI |
| **Biller / Destination** | The business collecting the recurring payment | NBFC, insurer, SaaS company |
| **Sponsor bank** | The biller's bank — submits mandate registrations and debit files to NPCI on the biller's behalf | HDFC Bank (if the NBFC banks with HDFC) |
| **Destination bank (customer's bank)** | The bank that holds the customer's account; processes the debit instruction | SBI, Kotak, ICICI |
| **NPCI** | Central clearinghouse — receives files from sponsor bank, routes debit instructions to destination banks, returns responses | NPCI NACH platform |

The flow for a mandate registration:
```
Biller → Sponsor Bank → NPCI → Destination Bank → Customer's Account
         (mandate file)         (mandate validation)  (mandate approval/rejection)
```

The flow for a debit execution (after mandate is live):
```
Biller → Sponsor Bank → NPCI → Destination Bank → Debit customer account → Return status
         (debit file,           (routes to         (honors or rejects
          D-1 day)               destination)       per mandate terms)
```

The sponsor bank relationship is critical — a biller cannot participate in NACH directly. Every biller must have a sponsor bank that has an NPCI NACH membership. This is why fintechs that want NACH capability either bank with a NACH-member bank or use a payment aggregator (Razorpay, Cashfree, Digio) that has a sponsor bank relationship.

**Q4. What is a NACH mandate, and what fields define it?**
A: A NACH mandate is the authorization record that permits the biller to debit the customer's account under defined terms. It is registered with NPCI and stored at both the sponsor bank and the destination bank.

Mandate fields:

| Field | Description | Constraints |
|---|---|---|
| `UMRN` | Unique Mandate Reference Number — assigned by NPCI on registration | 20-character alphanumeric; primary key for all subsequent operations |
| `customer_account` | Customer's bank account number | Masked in storage after registration |
| `ifsc` | Customer's bank branch IFSC code | 11-character; used to route to destination bank |
| `account_type` | Savings / Current / Cash Credit / Overdraft | Determines debit rules |
| `amount` | Maximum debit amount per cycle | Can debit up to this amount; not fixed — actual debit can be less |
| `frequency` | How often debits are allowed | ADHO (as and when presented), DAIL, WEEK, MNTH, QURT, MIAN (half-yearly), YEAR |
| `debit_type` | Fixed or Maximum | Fixed: same amount every time; Maximum: up to the registered amount |
| `start_date` | First date the mandate is valid | Cannot be before registration date |
| `end_date` | Last date the mandate is valid | Can be set to a far future date (e.g., 2099-12-31) for open-ended mandates |
| `purpose` | Reason for mandate | Standardised codes: LOANS, INSURANCE, MUTUAL_FUNDS, UTILITY, SUBSCRIPTION, etc. |
| `sponsor_bank_ifsc` | Sponsor bank's IFSC | Routes the mandate to the correct sponsor bank |

The `UMRN` is the single most important field in your data model — every debit file, cancellation, and amendment references it. It must be stored and indexed immediately on mandate registration response from NPCI.

**Q5. What is the difference between NACH and UPI AutoPay, and when would you choose each?**
A: UPI AutoPay (launched 2020) is NPCI's UPI-based recurring payment system. It competes with NACH Debit for recurring collection use cases.

| Property | NACH Debit | UPI AutoPay |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration** | e-NACH flow (net banking/debit card/Aadhaar) | UPI app approval (Paytm, PhonePe, GPay) |
| **Registration time** | 2–5 business days (mandate activation) | Real-time (T+0) |
| **Pre-debit notification** | Mandatory 30 days before first debit | Not required for amounts ≤ ₹15,000 |
| **Per-transaction limit** | No regulatory cap (mandate amount defines limit) | ₹1 lakh per transaction (₹15,000 for auto-execution without OTP) |
| **Debit execution** | D-1 file submission; T+1 settlement | Real-time or scheduled |
| **Failure handling** | NPCI return file next day; re-presentation rules | Immediate failure notification |
| **Cancellation** | NPCI mandate cancellation (1–2 days) | Instant via UPI app |
| **Best for** | Loan EMIs, insurance premiums, large amounts | Subscriptions, OTT, small recurring amounts |
| **Penetration** | All bank accounts with NACH capability | UPI-enabled smartphones only |

Choose NACH when: loan EMI amounts exceed ₹1 lakh, customer base skews older or rural (lower UPI adoption), or the use case requires debit from current/CC/OD accounts (UPI AutoPay only supports savings accounts).

Choose UPI AutoPay when: subscription amounts are below ₹15,000, you want T+0 mandate registration (no 2–5 day wait), or your customer base is smartphone-first.

Most production systems implement both and route based on customer profile and amount.

## Section 2: Mandate Lifecycle

**Q6. What are the states of a NACH mandate, and what triggers each transition?**
A: The mandate state machine:

```
INITIATED → PENDING_REGISTRATION → ACTIVE → PAUSED → CANCELLED
                ↘ REJECTED                   ↗ (re-activate)
                ↘ EXPIRED (if not activated within window)
         ACTIVE → EXPIRED (end_date reached)
         ACTIVE → CANCELLED (by customer or biller)
```

| State | Meaning | Triggered by |
|---|---|---|
| `INITIATED` | Customer started the e-NACH flow; not yet submitted to NPCI | User click on "Set up auto-pay" |
| `PENDING_REGISTRATION` | Mandate submitted to NPCI via sponsor bank; awaiting activation | Sponsor bank file submission (D-day) |
| `ACTIVE` | NPCI confirmed mandate; debits can be presented | NPCI activation response (D+2 to D+5) |
| `REJECTED` | Destination bank rejected the mandate | Bank reasons: account closed, signature mismatch, invalid IFSC |
| `PAUSED` | Temporarily suspended — debits will be returned | Biller-initiated (e.g., payment dispute) |
| `CANCELLED` | Permanently cancelled — no further debits possible | Customer via bank / biller via sponsor bank |
| `EXPIRED` | End date passed or activation window expired | System job; `end_date < today` |

```sql
CREATE TYPE mandate_status AS ENUM (
  'INITIATED', 'PENDING_REGISTRATION', 'ACTIVE',
  'REJECTED', 'PAUSED', 'CANCELLED', 'EXPIRED'
);

CREATE TABLE nach_mandates (
  id                    UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  customer_id           UUID NOT NULL,
  loan_id               UUID,           -- or subscription_id, policy_id
  umrn                  TEXT UNIQUE,    -- populated on NPCI registration; NULL until then
  status                mandate_status NOT NULL DEFAULT 'INITIATED',
  amount                BIGINT NOT NULL,              -- maximum debit amount in paise
  debit_type            TEXT NOT NULL,                -- 'FIXED' or 'MAXIMUM'
  frequency             TEXT NOT NULL,                -- 'MNTH', 'QURT', etc.
  purpose               TEXT NOT NULL,                -- 'LOANS', 'INSURANCE', etc.
  start_date            DATE NOT NULL,
  end_date              DATE NOT NULL,
  bank_account_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bank_accounts(id),
  sponsor_bank_ifsc     TEXT NOT NULL,
  registration_channel  TEXT NOT NULL,               -- 'NET_BANKING', 'DEBIT_CARD', 'AADHAAR_OTP'
  registered_at         TIMESTAMPTZ,                  -- when NPCI confirmed activation
  cancelled_at          TIMESTAMPTZ,
  cancellation_reason   TEXT,
  created_at            TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  updated_at            TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);

CREATE TABLE mandate_events (
  id            UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  mandate_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES nach_mandates(id),
  from_status   mandate_status NOT NULL,
  to_status     mandate_status NOT NULL,
  triggered_by  TEXT NOT NULL,    -- 'npci_webhook', 'customer', 'biller', 'system_job'
  reason_code   TEXT,             -- NPCI rejection code
  reason_desc   TEXT,
  occurred_at   TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);
```

**Q7. What happens between mandate submission and mandate activation, and how do you handle the registration lag?**
A: The registration gap (D+2 to D+5) is the most operationally complex part of NACH from an engineering perspective — the customer has completed the e-NACH flow but no debits can be collected yet.

**Day-by-day sequence:**

```
D+0  Customer completes e-NACH on your platform
     → Mandate record created: status = PENDING_REGISTRATION
     → Sponsor bank receives the mandate file (either same-day or next-day batch)

D+1  Sponsor bank submits mandate file to NPCI
     → NPCI routes to destination bank

D+2  Destination bank validates mandate (account exists, customer details match)
     → Accepted: NPCI sends activation confirmation to sponsor bank
     → Rejected: NPCI sends rejection with reason code

D+3  Sponsor bank notifies your system via webhook or file
     → Accepted: update mandate status to ACTIVE; UMRN populated
     → Rejected: update mandate status to REJECTED; notify customer
```

**Engineering challenges during the lag:**

1. **First EMI collection timing**: if a loan is disbursed on D+0, the first EMI is due in 30 days, but the mandate takes 5 days to activate. Your scheduler must not attempt a NACH debit until the mandate is `ACTIVE`.

2. **Duplicate mandate prevention**: the customer may restart the e-NACH flow if they don't see immediate confirmation. Your system must check for an existing `PENDING_REGISTRATION` mandate before creating a new one.

3. **Activation timeout**: if no response from NPCI within 10 business days, the mandate should be transitioned to `REJECTED` (likely lost in the file) and the customer notified to re-register.

```typescript
// Mandate activation timeout job — runs daily
async function checkMandateActivationTimeouts(): Promise<void> {
  const ACTIVATION_TIMEOUT_DAYS = 10;

  const timedOut = await db('nach_mandates')
    .where({ status: 'PENDING_REGISTRATION' })
    .where('created_at', '<', subBusinessDays(new Date(), ACTIVATION_TIMEOUT_DAYS));

  for (const mandate of timedOut) {
    await transitionMandate(mandate.id, 'REJECTED', {
      triggered_by: 'system_job',
      reason_code: 'ACTIVATION_TIMEOUT',
      reason_desc: `No NPCI response within ${ACTIVATION_TIMEOUT_DAYS} business days`,
    });
    await notifyCustomer(mandate.customer_id, 'MANDATE_REGISTRATION_FAILED');
  }
}
```

**Q8. How does NACH debit file submission work, and what is the D-1 cutoff?**
A: NACH debit execution is batch-based, not real-time. The biller must submit a debit file to the sponsor bank by a cutoff time (D-1) for settlement the next business day (D+0, the due date).

**File submission timeline:**
```
D-1 (day before due date)
  10:00 AM: Generate debit file for all due EMIs
  12:00 PM: Submit file to sponsor bank (via SFTP / API)
  14:00 PM: Sponsor bank cutoff (varies by bank; typically 14:00–16:00)
  16:00 PM: Sponsor bank forwards file to NPCI

D+0 (due date — settlement day)
  07:00 AM: NPCI routes debit instructions to destination banks
  11:00 AM: Destination banks process debits
  14:00 PM: NPCI returns response file (successes + rejections)
  17:00 PM: Sponsor bank forwards return file to biller

D+1
  Settlement: net amounts credited/debited to sponsor bank's NPCI account
```

**Debit file format (NPCI NACH specification):**

NPCI specifies a fixed-width or CSV format. Each row in the debit file:

```
UMRN | Destination bank IFSC | Account number | Amount | Debit date | Purpose | Sequence type | Biller reference
```

`Sequence type` is important:
- `FRST` — First presentation (first EMI on this mandate).
- `RCUR` — Recurring (subsequent EMIs).
- `FNAL` — Final presentation (last EMI).
- `OOFF` — One-off (single debit on an otherwise recurring mandate).

```typescript
function generateNachDebitFile(debits: NachDebit[]): string {
  const header = buildFileHeader({ totalRecords: debits.length, totalAmount: sumAmounts(debits) });
  const rows = debits.map(d => [
    d.umrn.padEnd(20),
    d.destination_ifsc.padEnd(11),
    d.account_number.padEnd(35),
    d.amount.toString().padStart(13, '0'),   // paise, zero-padded
    format(d.debit_date, 'ddMMyyyy'),
    d.purpose_code.padEnd(10),
    d.sequence_type.padEnd(4),              // FRST/RCUR/FNAL/OOFF
    d.biller_reference.padEnd(20),
  ].join('|'));

  return [header, ...rows].join('\n');
}
```

**Q9. What are NACH rejection codes, and how should your system handle each?**
A: NPCI defines standardised rejection reason codes. Your system must handle each differently — some are permanent (don't retry), some are transient (retry with changes), and some require customer action.

| Code | Description | Category | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| `01` | Account closed | Permanent | Cancel mandate; notify customer to update bank account |
| `02` | Account does not exist | Permanent | Cancel mandate; verify account details |
| `03` | Account frozen | Temporary | Retry after customer resolves freeze |
| `04` | Insufficient funds | Temporary | Retry per re-presentation rules (see Q10) |
| `05` | Stop payment instruction | Customer-initiated | Contact customer; possibly mandate cancellation |
| `06` | Amount exceeds mandate limit | Biller error | Re-submit with correct amount ≤ mandate amount |
| `07` | Mandate not registered | System error | Check UMRN; re-register if mandate was lost |
| `08` | Mandate cancelled | Permanent | Cannot present; create new mandate if needed |
| `09` | Mandate expired | Permanent | End date passed; renew mandate |
| `10` | Duplicate presentation | Biller error | Already debited this cycle; do not retry |
| `11` | Invalid IFSC | Data error | Update IFSC; re-register mandate |
| `12` | Debit date mismatch | Biller error | Debit date doesn't match mandate frequency |
| `13` | Account type mismatch | Data error | Account type in file ≠ registered account type |

```typescript
async function handleNachReturn(returnRow: NachReturnRow): Promise<void> {
  const mandate = await db('nach_mandates').where({ umrn: returnRow.umrn }).first();
  const debit = await db('nach_debits').where({ biller_reference: returnRow.biller_reference }).first();

  await db('nach_debits').where({ id: debit.id }).update({
    status: 'RETURNED',
    return_code: returnRow.reason_code,
    returned_at: new Date(),
  });

  const handling = getRejectionHandling(returnRow.reason_code);

  if (handling.cancelMandate) {
    await transitionMandate(mandate.id, 'CANCELLED', {
      triggered_by: 'npci_return',
      reason_code: returnRow.reason_code,
    });
  }

  if (handling.notifyCustomer) {
    await notifyCustomer(mandate.customer_id, 'EMI_BOUNCE', {
      amount: debit.amount,
      reason: handling.customerMessage,
      nextSteps: handling.nextSteps,
    });
  }

  if (handling.scheduleRetry && debit.presentation_count < MAX_PRESENTATIONS) {
    await scheduleRepresentation(debit.id, addBusinessDays(new Date(), 3));
  }
}

function getRejectionHandling(code: string): RejectionHandling {
  const PERMANENT = { cancelMandate: true, notifyCustomer: true, scheduleRetry: false };
  const TRANSIENT  = { cancelMandate: false, notifyCustomer: true, scheduleRetry: true };
  const BILLER_ERR = { cancelMandate: false, notifyCustomer: false, scheduleRetry: false };

  const map: Record<string, RejectionHandling> = {
    '01': { ...PERMANENT, customerMessage: 'Your bank account is closed. Please update.' },
    '02': { ...PERMANENT, customerMessage: 'Account not found. Please verify details.' },
    '04': { ...TRANSIENT, customerMessage: 'Insufficient funds. Retry will be attempted.' },
    '06': BILLER_ERR,
    '08': { ...PERMANENT, customerMessage: 'Mandate has been cancelled.' },
    '10': BILLER_ERR,
  };
  return map[code] ?? { cancelMandate: false, notifyCustomer: true, scheduleRetry: false };
}
```

## Section 3: Debit Scheduling and Re-presentation

**Q10. What are NACH re-presentation rules, and how do you implement a compliant retry scheduler?**
A: When a NACH debit is returned (e.g., insufficient funds), the biller is permitted to re-present the debit a limited number of times. NPCI and RBI define the rules:

**NPCI re-presentation rules:**
- Maximum **3 presentations** per debit cycle (original + 2 re-presentations).
- Re-presentation must be **at least 3 business days** after the previous return.
- Re-presentation must be within the **same mandate frequency cycle** — you cannot carry a monthly EMI into the next month's cycle as a re-presentation.
- Re-presentation must use sequence type `RCUR` (not `FRST` again, even if it was the first).

**RBI guidelines on bounce charges:**
- Destination banks may charge a bounce fee (typically ₹500–₹750) to the customer on each failed debit.
- The biller may also charge a bounce penalty per their loan/insurance agreement.
- Both must be disclosed in the mandate registration and loan agreement.

```sql
CREATE TABLE nach_debits (
  id                    UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  mandate_id            UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES nach_mandates(id),
  due_date              DATE NOT NULL,
  debit_date            DATE NOT NULL,         -- actual date submitted to NPCI
  amount                BIGINT NOT NULL,        -- paise
  sequence_type         TEXT NOT NULL,          -- FRST/RCUR/FNAL/OOFF
  presentation_number   INT NOT NULL DEFAULT 1, -- 1, 2, or 3
  status                TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'SCHEDULED',
  -- SCHEDULED, SUBMITTED, SETTLED, RETURNED, CANCELLED
  biller_reference      TEXT UNIQUE NOT NULL,   -- your unique ref per presentation
  npci_reference        TEXT,                   -- NPCI's reference (from return file)
  return_code           TEXT,
  submitted_at          TIMESTAMPTZ,
  settled_at            TIMESTAMPTZ,
  returned_at           TIMESTAMPTZ,
  parent_debit_id       UUID REFERENCES nach_debits(id)  -- for re-presentations
);
```

```typescript
async function scheduleRepresentation(originalDebitId: string): Promise<void> {
  const original = await db('nach_debits').where({ id: originalDebitId }).first();
  const mandate  = await db('nach_mandates').where({ id: original.mandate_id }).first();

  // Validate re-presentation eligibility
  if (original.presentation_number >= 3) {
    await markDebitAsFinallyFailed(originalDebitId);
    await triggerLoanDefaultWorkflow(mandate.loan_id);
    return;
  }

  // Must be at least 3 business days after return
  const earliestRepresentationDate = addBusinessDays(original.returned_at, 3);

  // Must be within the same cycle (due_date's month for monthly mandates)
  const cycleEnd = endOfMonth(original.due_date);
  if (isAfter(earliestRepresentationDate, cycleEnd)) {
    // Cannot re-present within the same cycle — mark as failed for this cycle
    await markDebitCycleFailed(originalDebitId);
    await scheduleNextCycleDebit(mandate);  // will try again next month
    return;
  }

  const representationDate = earliestRepresentationDate;

  await db('nach_debits').insert({
    mandate_id:          original.mandate_id,
    due_date:            original.due_date,
    debit_date:          representationDate,
    amount:              original.amount,
    sequence_type:       'RCUR',
    presentation_number: original.presentation_number + 1,
    biller_reference:    generateBillerReference(),
    parent_debit_id:     originalDebitId,
    status:              'SCHEDULED',
  });
}
```

**Q11. How do you design a NACH debit scheduler that handles holidays, weekends, and bank-specific cutoffs?**
A: NACH settlement only happens on business days — weekends, national holidays, and RBI-declared bank holidays cause debits to shift. Incorrect scheduling results in missed cut-offs or unintended early/late debits.

**Holiday calendar management:**
```sql
CREATE TABLE bank_holidays (
  holiday_date  DATE NOT NULL,
  description   TEXT NOT NULL,         -- 'Republic Day', 'Diwali', etc.
  holiday_type  TEXT NOT NULL,         -- 'NATIONAL', 'STATE', 'RBI'
  applicable_to TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'ALL',  -- 'ALL', 'MAHARASHTRA', etc.
  PRIMARY KEY (holiday_date, applicable_to)
);
```

NPCI publishes the NACH holiday calendar annually. Load it into `bank_holidays` at the start of each year; monitor NPCI advisories for mid-year additions.

```typescript
async function getNextNachBusinessDay(date: Date): Promise<Date> {
  let candidate = date;
  const MAX_ITERATIONS = 10;

  for (let i = 0; i < MAX_ITERATIONS; i++) {
    const isWeekend = [0, 6].includes(candidate.getDay());  // Sunday=0, Saturday=6
    const isHoliday = await db('bank_holidays')
      .where({ holiday_date: formatDate(candidate) })
      .whereIn('applicable_to', ['ALL'])
      .first();

    if (!isWeekend && !isHoliday) return candidate;
    candidate = addDays(candidate, 1);
  }
  throw new Error('Could not find a NACH business day within 10 days');
}

async function scheduleNachDebitForDueDate(dueDate: Date, mandate: NachMandate): Promise<Date> {
  // D-1: submit file the day before settlement
  const targetSettlementDate = await getNextNachBusinessDay(dueDate);
  const fileSubmissionDate = await getPreviousNachBusinessDay(targetSettlementDate);

  // If due date is itself a holiday, settle on the next business day (not before)
  // Loan agreements typically specify: "if due date is a holiday, debit on next business day"
  return fileSubmissionDate;
}
```

**Sponsor bank cutoff time:**
Each sponsor bank has a specific file submission cutoff (typically 12:00–16:00). Your debit scheduler must submit the file well before the cutoff:

```typescript
const SPONSOR_BANK_CUTOFFS: Record<string, string> = {
  'HDFC0000001': '14:00',
  'ICIC0000001': '13:00',
  'KKBK0000001': '15:00',
};

async function submitDebitFileToBankIfBeforeCutoff(
  sponsorBankIfsc: string,
  file: NachDebitFile,
): Promise<SubmissionResult> {
  const cutoff = SPONSOR_BANK_CUTOFFS[sponsorBankIfsc];
  const now = format(new Date(), 'HH:mm');

  if (now >= cutoff) {
    // Missed cutoff — file will be processed next business day
    logger.warn({ sponsorBankIfsc, cutoff, now }, 'Missed NACH submission cutoff');
    await alertOpsTeam('NACH_CUTOFF_MISSED', { sponsorBankIfsc, file });
    return { submitted: false, reason: 'CUTOFF_MISSED', nextAttemptDate: nextBusinessDay() };
  }

  return await bankSftpClient.upload(file);
}
```

**Q12. How do you handle the pre-debit notification requirement for NACH?**
A: NPCI and RBI mandate that billers notify customers before debiting their account. The requirements:

- **Notification timing**: at least **30 days before the first debit** and **adequate notice** (typically 3–5 days) before each subsequent debit.
- **Notification channels**: email and/or SMS to the customer's registered contact details.
- **Content**: must include the debit amount, debit date, and UMRN.
- **Opt-out**: the notification must give the customer a way to contact the biller if they want to dispute or cancel.

Failure to send pre-debit notifications is a regulatory violation — RBI has penalised multiple lenders for this.

```sql
CREATE TABLE nach_debit_notifications (
```

```typescript
async function schedulePreDebitNotifications(debit: NachDebit): Promise<void> {
  const mandate = await db('nach_mandates').where({ id: debit.mandate_id }).first();
  const notifyDate = subBusinessDays(debit.debit_date, 3);  // 3 business days before debit

  // SMS notification
  await db('nach_debit_notifications').insert({
    debit_id: debit.id,
    customer_id: mandate.customer_id,
    channel: 'SMS',
    scheduled_at: setTime(notifyDate, '09:00'),
    content: {
      template: 'NACH_PRE_DEBIT',
      params: {
        amount: formatCurrency(debit.amount, 'INR'),
        debit_date: format(debit.debit_date, 'dd MMM yyyy'),
        umrn: mandate.umrn,
```

## Section 4: Indian Fintech & Regulatory Context

**Q13. What RBI and NPCI regulations govern NACH mandates, and what are the key compliance obligations?**
A: The regulatory framework:

**NPCI NACH Operational Guidelines** — the technical and operational specification. Covers file formats, timelines, rejection codes, re-presentation rules, and member bank obligations. Updated periodically; billers must subscribe to NPCI circulars.

**RBI Master Direction on Payment Aggregators (2020)** — PAs facilitating NACH mandate registration must comply with KYC, data storage, and audit requirements. Biller onboarding must include due diligence.

**RBI Circular on pre-debit notifications (2019, updated 2021)** — mandates pre-debit notification at least 30 days before first debit and 3–5 working days before each subsequent debit. Non-compliance penalty: up to ₹1 crore per instance.

**Key compliance obligations for engineering:**

| Obligation | Requirement | Engineering implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **UMRN storage** | Must be stored and queryable for 8 years | Non-deletable; append-only mandate table |
| **Consent audit trail** | Proof of customer's e-NACH consent must be stored | Consent timestamp, IP, channel, device ID in `mandate_events` |
| **Pre-debit notification** | 30 days (first); 3–5 days (recurring) | Notification scheduler; delivery receipt tracking |
| **Bounce charge disclosure** | Must be disclosed at mandate registration | Stored in mandate agreement text; version-controlled |
| **Re-presentation cap** | Max 3 presentations per cycle | `presentation_number` constraint; enforced in scheduler |
| **Mandate cancellation** | Must be processed within 2 business days of customer request | Cancellation SLA tracker; NPCI file submission |
| **Data localisation** | All mandate and debit data must be stored in India | Cloud region: `ap-south-1` (Mumbai) only for mandate data |

**Q14. How does the NACH mandate amendment process work, and what changes require a new mandate vs. an amendment?**
A: Mandates can be amended (modified in place) for certain field changes, but other changes require cancellation and re-registration.

**Amendable fields (amendment, no re-registration):**
- Amount (can only be increased, not decreased, without re-registration in practice)
- End date (extension)
- Frequency (requires sponsor bank approval)

**Fields requiring new mandate:**
- Bank account number (customer changed banks)
- IFSC (branch transfer)
- Account type
- Start date

Amendment process:
```
Biller submits amendment file to sponsor bank
→ Sponsor bank forwards to NPCI
→ NPCI routes to destination bank
→ Destination bank validates and accepts/rejects
→ Response file returned (D+1 to D+3)
```

```sql
CREATE TABLE mandate_amendments (
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  mandate_id        UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES nach_mandates(id),
  amendment_type    TEXT NOT NULL,      -- 'AMOUNT_CHANGE', 'END_DATE_EXTENSION', 'FREQUENCY_CHANGE'
  old_value         JSONB NOT NULL,
  new_value         JSONB NOT NULL,
  status            TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PENDING',
  -- PENDING, SUBMITTED, ACCEPTED, REJECTED
  submitted_at      TIMESTAMPTZ,
  accepted_at       TIMESTAMPTZ,
  rejected_at       TIMESTAMPTZ,
  rejection_reason  TEXT,
  requested_by      TEXT NOT NULL       -- 'CUSTOMER', 'BILLER', 'SYSTEM'
);
```

An accepted amendment updates `nach_mandates` with the new field values; a rejected amendment leaves the mandate unchanged. The amendment history in `mandate_amendments` is the audit trail.

**Q15. What is the eNACH via API (API-based mandate) flow offered by aggregators like Razorpay and Digio, and how does it differ from direct NPCI integration?**

| Property | Direct NPCI integration | Via aggregator (Razorpay, Digio) |

| **Control** | Full control over timing, file format, retry logic | Dependent on aggregator's SLAs |

## Section 5: Failure Handling & Edge Cases

**Q16. How do you handle a NACH debit that is returned after the funds have already been credited to the downstream system (e.g., loan already disbursed)?**
A: This is the most dangerous NACH failure scenario — you have disbursed a loan based on a NACH mandate, but the first EMI debit bounces. The money has already left your books.

This is called a **credit-before-collection risk** and is common in:
- Loan disbursement (loan disbursed on D+0; first EMI collected on D+30 via NACH).
- Insurance premium (policy activated; first premium debit bounces a month later).

**Handling flow:**
```typescript
async function handleBounceAfterDisbursement(debit: NachDebit, returnCode: string): Promise<void> {
  const mandate = await db('nach_mandates').where({ id: debit.mandate_id }).first();
  const loan = await db('loans').where({ id: mandate.loan_id }).first();

  // 1. Post bounce to loan account (increases outstanding balance)
  await postLedgerTransaction({
    idempotencyKey: `nach-bounce:${debit.id}`,
    entries: [
      { account: 'LOAN_RECEIVABLE', type: 'DEBIT', amount: debit.amount,
        description: `EMI bounce - presentation ${debit.presentation_number}` },
      { account: 'EMI_COLLECTIONS', type: 'CREDIT', amount: debit.amount,
        description: 'Bounce reversal' },
    ],
  });

  // 2. Apply bounce charge to loan (if in loan agreement)
  if (loan.bounce_charge_applicable) {
    await applyBounceCharge(loan.id, BOUNCE_CHARGE_AMOUNT);
  }

  // 3. Schedule re-presentation if eligible
  const canRepresent = debit.presentation_number < 3
    && !PERMANENT_REJECTION_CODES.includes(returnCode)
    && isWithinSameCycle(debit.due_date, addBusinessDays(new Date(), 3));

  if (canRepresent) {
    await scheduleRepresentation(debit.id);
  } else {
    // Final failure — escalate to collections
    await escalateToCollections(loan.id, {
      amount: debit.amount,
      due_date: debit.due_date,
      bounce_count: debit.presentation_number,
      return_code: returnCode,
    });
    await updateLoanNpaStatus(loan.id);
  }

  // 4. Notify customer at every stage
  await notifyCustomer(mandate.customer_id, 'EMI_BOUNCE', {
    amount: debit.amount,
    due_date: debit.due_date,
    return_reason: getCustomerFacingReason(returnCode),
    action_required: canRepresent ? 'ENSURE_FUNDS' : 'CONTACT_SUPPORT',
  });
}
```

**Q17. How do you design an NPA (Non-Performing Asset) detection pipeline using NACH bounce data?**
A: In lending, a loan becomes NPA when payments are overdue by more than 90 days (RBI NPA classification norms). NACH bounce data is the primary signal for early NPA detection.

```sql
-- Bounce history aggregated per loan
CREATE VIEW loan_bounce_summary AS
SELECT
  m.loan_id,
  COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE d.status = 'RETURNED') AS total_bounces,
  COUNT(DISTINCT DATE_TRUNC('month', d.due_date))
    FILTER (WHERE d.status = 'RETURNED') AS months_with_bounces,
  MAX(d.due_date) FILTER (WHERE d.status IN ('RETURNED', 'CANCELLED')) AS last_failed_due_date,
  MAX(d.settled_at) AS last_successful_settlement,
  CURRENT_DATE - MAX(d.due_date) FILTER (WHERE d.status = 'RETURNED') AS days_since_last_bounce,
  CURRENT_DATE - MAX(d.settled_at) AS days_since_last_payment
FROM nach_mandates m
JOIN nach_debits d ON d.mandate_id = m.id
WHERE m.status IN ('ACTIVE', 'PAUSED')
GROUP BY m.loan_id;
```

```typescript
// Early warning system — runs nightly
async function detectEarlyNpaSignals(): Promise<void> {
  const riskLoans = await db('loan_bounce_summary')
    .where('days_since_last_payment', '>=', 30)
    .orWhere('months_with_bounces', '>=', 2)
    .orWhere('total_bounces', '>=', 3);

  for (const loan of riskLoans) {
    const riskLevel = classifyNpaRisk(loan);

    await db('loan_risk_flags').insert({
      loan_id: loan.loan_id,
      risk_level: riskLevel,          // 'SMA_0', 'SMA_1', 'SMA_2', 'NPA'
      flagged_at: new Date(),
      reason: buildRiskReason(loan),
    }).onConflict('loan_id').merge();

    if (riskLevel === 'NPA' || riskLevel === 'SMA_2') {
      await notifyCollectionsTeam(loan.loan_id, riskLevel);
    }
  }
}

function classifyNpaRisk(loan: LoanBounceSummary): string {
  if (loan.days_since_last_payment >= 90) return 'NPA';
  if (loan.days_since_last_payment >= 60) return 'SMA_2';
  if (loan.days_since_last_payment >= 30) return 'SMA_1';
  if (loan.total_bounces >= 1)            return 'SMA_0';
  return 'STANDARD';
}
```

RBI SMA (Special Mention Account) classification: SMA-0 (30 days), SMA-1 (30–60 days), SMA-2 (60–90 days), NPA (> 90 days). Banks and NBFCs must report SMA and NPA accounts to CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits) monthly.

**Q18. How do you handle mandate cancellation requests, and what are the timing constraints?**
A: Mandate cancellation is initiated either by the customer (via their bank or your platform) or by the biller (via the sponsor bank).

**Customer-initiated cancellation (via their bank):**
- The customer walks into their bank branch or uses net banking to cancel the mandate.
- The destination bank informs NPCI.
- NPCI sends a cancellation notification to the sponsor bank.
- Sponsor bank notifies your system via webhook or file.
- Your system receives this as an unsolicited `MANDATE_CANCELLED` event.

```typescript
// Handle unsolicited bank-initiated cancellation
async function handleUnsolicitedCancellation(umrn: string, bankNotification: BankCancellationNotice): Promise<void> {
  const mandate = await db('nach_mandates').where({ umrn }).first();
  if (!mandate) {
    logger.error({ umrn }, 'Received cancellation for unknown UMRN');
    return;
  }

  await transitionMandate(mandate.id, 'CANCELLED', {
    triggered_by: 'destination_bank',
    reason_code: 'CUSTOMER_INITIATED',
    reason_desc: bankNotification.reason,
  });

  // Cancel any scheduled future debits for this mandate
  await db('nach_debits')
    .where({ mandate_id: mandate.id, status: 'SCHEDULED' })
    .update({ status: 'CANCELLED', cancelled_at: new Date() });

  // Notify biller's operations team
  await notifyOpsTeam('MANDATE_CANCELLED_BY_CUSTOMER', { mandate, loan_id: mandate.loan_id });
}
```

**Biller-initiated cancellation:**
```typescript
async function cancelMandateByBiller(mandateId: string, reason: string): Promise<void> {
  const mandate = await db('nach_mandates').where({ id: mandateId }).first();

  if (!['ACTIVE', 'PAUSED'].includes(mandate.status)) {
    throw new Error(`Cannot cancel mandate in ${mandate.status} state`);
  }

  // Check: are there scheduled debits within the next 3 business days?
  // These may already be in-flight with the sponsor bank
  const inFlightDebits = await db('nach_debits')
    .where({ mandate_id: mandateId, status: 'SCHEDULED' })
    .where('debit_date', '<=', addBusinessDays(new Date(), 3));

  if (inFlightDebits.length > 0) {
    // These debits may already be submitted to NPCI — cancellation may not stop them
    await alertOpsTeam('CANCELLATION_WITH_INFLIGHT_DEBITS', { mandateId, inFlightDebits });
  }

  // Submit cancellation file to sponsor bank
  await sponsorBankClient.submitCancellation({
    umrn: mandate.umrn,
    reason_code: mapReasonCode(reason),
  });

  await transitionMandate(mandateId, 'CANCELLED', {
    triggered_by: 'biller',
    reason_code: reason,
  });
}
```

## Section 6: Scenario / System Design Questions

**Q19. "Design the NACH mandate and debit management system for an NBFC that disburses 10,000 loans per month, each with a 36-month tenure."**
A: At 10,000 new loans/month with 36-month tenure, at steady state: 10,000 × 36 = 3,60,000 active mandates; ~3,60,000 debit presentations per month; ~12,000 per business day.

**System components:**

```
Loan Origination System
      ↓ (loan sanctioned → trigger mandate registration)
Mandate Registration Service
      ↓ (e-NACH flow → customer completes online)
Aggregator / Sponsor Bank API
      ↓ (UMRN received on activation)
Mandate Store (nach_mandates table)
      ↓ (ACTIVE mandate triggers debit scheduler)
Debit Scheduler (cron: D-1, 08:00 AM)
      ↓ (generates debit file for next business day's due dates)
File Generator → Sponsor Bank SFTP
      ↓ (D+0: return file received)
Return File Processor
      ↓ (SETTLED: update loan; RETURNED: handle bounce)
Notification Service (pre-debit D-3; bounce notification same day)
      ↓
NPA Detection Pipeline (nightly)
      ↓
Collections System (SMA/NPA escalation)
```

**Key schema additions for NBFC context:**

```sql
-- Link mandates to loan repayment schedule
```

```typescript
// Runs daily at 08:00; generates file for next business day's due dates
async function runDailyDebitScheduler(): Promise<void> {
```

**Q20. "A merchant complains that a customer's NACH debit succeeded but the loan EMI is still showing as unpaid in the customer's account. What do you investigate?"**
A: This is a classic ledger posting failure — NACH settled, but the downstream system wasn't updated.

**Step 1 — Verify NACH settlement (5 minutes)**
```sql
SELECT d.status, d.settled_at, d.npci_reference, d.amount, d.biller_reference
FROM nach_debits d
JOIN nach_mandates m ON m.id = d.mandate_id
JOIN loans l ON l.id = m.loan_id
WHERE l.id = '<loan_id>'
  AND d.due_date = '<reported_emi_date>';
```

If `status = SETTLED` and `settled_at` is populated — NACH definitely succeeded. The problem is downstream.

**Step 2 — Check ledger entries**
```sql
SELECT * FROM ledger_entries
WHERE reference_id = '<nach_debit_biller_reference>'
ORDER BY posted_at;
```

If no ledger entries — the return file processor ran but the ledger posting job failed. Check job logs for the settlement date.

**Step 3 — Check loan repayment schedule**
```sql
SELECT * FROM loan_repayment_schedule
WHERE loan_id = '<loan_id>' AND due_date = '<emi_date>';
```

If `status = PENDING` — the EMI status update job failed after NACH return file processing. The job must be idempotent: re-running it with the same `nach_debit_id` should be safe.

**Step 4 — Re-trigger the posting (with idempotency)**
```typescript
async function repostNachSettlement(nachDebitId: string): Promise<void> {
  const debit = await db('nach_debits').where({ id: nachDebitId, status: 'SETTLED' }).first();
  if (!debit) throw new Error('Debit not found or not settled');

  await postLedgerTransaction({
    idempotencyKey: `nach-settlement:${nachDebitId}`,  // idempotent: safe to re-run
    entries: buildEmiCollectionEntries(debit),
  });

  await db('loan_repayment_schedule')
    .where({ nach_debit_id: nachDebitId })
    .update({ status: 'COLLECTED', collected_at: debit.settled_at });
}
```

**Step 5 — Root cause and prevent recurrence**
- Check if the return file processor crashed mid-run on that date. Look for a gap in `reconciliation_runs`.
- Add a nightly check: any `nach_debits.status = SETTLED` with no corresponding ledger entry is an anomaly — alert immediately.
- Ensure the return file processor is transactional: ledger posting + EMI status update must be in a single DB transaction.

## Quick Reference Table

| Concept | Detail | Engineering action |
|---|---|---|
| **UMRN** | NPCI-assigned 20-char ID; primary key for all mandate operations | Store immediately on activation; non-nullable once received |
| **Mandate states** | INITIATED → PENDING → ACTIVE → CANCELLED/EXPIRED | State machine with append-only `mandate_events` |
| **Activation lag** | D+2 to D+5 after submission | Do not schedule debits until status = ACTIVE |
| **D-1 file submission** | Debit file must reach sponsor bank by cutoff (12:00–16:00) | Submit by 10:00 to be safe; alert on missed cutoff |
| **Sequence type** | FRST / RCUR / FNAL / OOFF | FRST on first presentation only; RCUR on re-presentations |
| **Re-presentation rules** | Max 3 presentations; min 3 business days apart; same cycle | `presentation_number` ≤ 3; cycle boundary check |
| **Rejection code 01/02** | Account closed / not found — permanent | Cancel mandate immediately; notify customer |
| **Rejection code 04** | Insufficient funds — transient | Schedule re-presentation; max 2 retries |
| **Rejection code 06/10** | Biller error (amount exceeded / duplicate) | Fix data; do not notify customer as a bounce |
| **Pre-debit notification** | 30 days before first; 3–5 days before each recurring | Regulatory mandate; track delivery receipt |
| **Holiday calendar** | NPCI NACH holiday list; load annually | `bank_holidays` table; shift debit dates accordingly |
| **Unsolicited cancellation** | Customer cancels at their bank; arrives as inbound event | Cancel scheduled debits; notify ops team |
| **NPA signals** | SMA-0 (30d), SMA-1 (60d), SMA-2 (90d), NPA (>90d) | Nightly pipeline on bounce data; CRILC reporting |
| **Credit-before-collection risk** | Loan disbursed before first EMI collected | Bounce triggers collections escalation, not disbursal reversal |
| **UPI AutoPay vs NACH** | AutoPay: real-time, ≤ ₹1L, smartphone-only; NACH: batch, higher limits, pan-India | Route by amount + customer segment |
| **Aggregator vs direct** | < 50,000 mandates: aggregator; > 50,000: direct integration | Direct saves ₹5–15/debit but requires 3–6 months integration |
